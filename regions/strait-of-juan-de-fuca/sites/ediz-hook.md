# Ediz Hook (Inner Harbor)

Ediz Hook is the long sand spit that curls out from the base of Port Angeles and encloses Port Angeles Harbor, sheltering it from the open Strait of Juan de Fuca on the far side. This entry dives the inside, harbor facing shore partway out the spit, an easy walk in beach dive over a shallow, colorful shelf that drops away quickly beyond the reef edge into an open, current free slope thick with sunken log debris. Its headline hazard is not current, which stays negligible here, but the slope itself: past the reef edge the bottom keeps going with nothing to mark a stopping point, past recreational depths, over a jumble of decaying, precariously stacked logs.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A gravel pull-off along Ediz Hook Road, with a restroom across the street. Walk down onto the beach for a direct entry; the bottom shelves gently through the shallows, so at low tide it can be more of a trudge over an exposed flat than a swim.
* **Parking.** Free, at the gravel pull-off. This is separate from Harborview Park, the developed park with its own parking near the spit's outer end, closer to the Coast Guard station; this entry sits further back down the road, roughly between the park and a cell tower along the route.
* **Access.** Reached from Highway 101 through Port Angeles: follow Front Street west past the marina and along the edge of the paper mill, currently McKinley Paper on the site of the former Nippon mill, onto Ediz Hook Road, then a couple of miles further out the spit.
* **Facilities.** Restroom across the street from the pull-off. Older reports mention informal fresh water taps near a salmon net-pen shed and a kayak club picnic shed further along the road, but treat these as unreliable and not guaranteed.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shallows.** Sand and scattered rock close to shore, holding most of the site's colorful life. Deepens gradually from the beach.
* **The reef edge.** Around 18.5 m below MLLW, where the shallow shelf gives way and the bottom drops away more steeply.
* **The log slope.** Beyond the edge, decaying logs, some sunk decades ago, litter the bottom in stacks several layers deep, increasingly so with depth. Life thins out here and the mood shifts; move carefully, some logs are precariously balanced on others.
* **Working the site.** The productive shallow diving runs along a stretch of the inside shore between Harborview Park, toward the spit's outer end, and a cell tower further back down the road; this entry sits within that stretch. Conditions can differ site to site along it.
* **The Rockpile.** Further out toward the Coast Guard Air Station, a separate reef loaded with large rockfish. Boat access only since 2001; not reachable from this shore entry.

* **At the harbor mouth.** The governing station sees real flow, peak flood and ebb both capable of exceeding 0.5 m/s.
* **At the dive site.** Sheltered well inside the harbor basin, current here stays negligible through the whole tidal cycle; this is not a slack tide dive.
* **Diveable window.** Any time. This is one of the few sites in the region where current genuinely does not govern the plan, a useful fallback when current rules out a dive elsewhere on the strait.

## Hazards

* **The open slope.** Past the reef edge the bottom keeps dropping with no natural stopping feature, well past recreational depths within a few hundred metres of shore. Plan the depth and watch it; the slope itself will not tell you to turn around.
* **Log debris.** Decaying logs, stacked several layers deep in places, cover the deeper slope. Some sit precariously on others; move carefully to avoid dislodging one, and watch for entanglement in submerged branches and cable.
* **Reduced visibility and low oxygen at depth.** Reports describe clearer water in the shallows fading to hazy, silty conditions deeper, with patches of bacterial mat around the wood debris consistent with low dissolved oxygen where the logs are decomposing.
* **Harbor sediment.** Port Angeles Harbor carries a legacy of contaminated sediment from over a century of mill and marine industry, including wood waste burned by the historic pulp mills; the western harbor near the foot of the spit is an active Washington Department of Ecology cleanup site. Avoid unnecessary bottom contact and stirring silt.
* **Boat traffic.** Shared water with the Port Angeles Boat Haven marina and general harbor traffic; stay visible and watch for boats working close to shore.
* **Low tide footing.** The shallow entry flat can be slippery and, at a low spring tide, mostly exposed, lengthening the walk to swimmable water.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The inside shore faces roughly south into Port Angeles Harbor, sheltered from the open strait by the spit itself.
* **Bad.** South through southwest, blowing the length of the harbor basin, though fetch is limited by the harbor's own size.
* **Fine.** North through east, blocked entirely by the spit.
* **Wind against current.** Not a real factor here; current at the dive site stays negligible regardless of tidal state.

## Visibility

* **By depth.** Consistently better in the shallows than at depth, clear water over the shelf fading to hazy and silty on the log slope below.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 6 to 9 °C, matching the strait generally, though the sheltered harbor may run slightly warmer than the open strait shore. Standard drysuit protocol year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens late in summer, per the region generally.

## Marine life

* **The shallows.** Abundant and colorful: ling cod, crabs, nudibranchs and anemones work the sand and scattered rock close to shore, along with assorted debris, lost golf balls among it.
* **The log slope.** Giant Pacific octopus den among the logs and debris. Life thins with depth as the bottom turns to stacked, decaying wood, with bacterial mats in places near the wood itself.
