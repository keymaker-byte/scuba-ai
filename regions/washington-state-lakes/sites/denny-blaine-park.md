# Denny Blaine Park

A shore dive off a small Olmsted park on the west shore of Lake Washington in Seattle, between Madison Park and Madrona. A pebble beach below the park's old stone seawall slopes gently out to about 14 m, where the bottom steepens into a wall to about 26 m and a steep slope below it that runs past 40 m before levelling out on the lake floor at 50 to 59 m. The lake holds still at depth, with wind driving only a surface drift, and its water is cold at depth, silty and modest in visibility. The headline hazards are boat traffic over the dive area through the boating season and a slope that keeps going well past sport diving depth.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the parking, cross the upper lawn to one of the two sets of stairs built into the old seawall, walk down to the lower lawn and the beach, and enter over small, smooth river pebble. The bottom slopes gently: 2.5 m at 20 m out, 5.8 m at 40 m, 9.3 m at 60 m and 13.6 m at 80 m, where the wall begins. The dive area is a 116 m swim out, east, 94°, over 25.1 m of water.
* **Parking.** A small lot at the top of the park at 200 Lake Washington Blvd E, with limited street parking nearby. Park only in the marked spaces and keep clear of private driveways; illegal parking is ticketed and towed. Arrive early on a summer day. The parking cost is not established.
* **Access.** Seattle Parks, open 6 AM to 10 PM. Seattle's water rules restrict diving to daylight hours and require the dive site to be marked with a diver's flag,. The park is divided by a visual barrier: clothing is required in the upper area, west of the barrier and the stairs, and clothing-optional use is allowed in the lower areas east of them, the beach included. Toplessness is allowed throughout the park. Dogs are barred from the beach, smoking is prohibited, and so is alcohol without a permit. The beach is unguarded.
* **Facilities.** Lawns, trash receptacles and the stairs down to the beach.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The seawall.** The stone wall around the beach marked the lakeshore until the lake was lowered 2.7 m as the Lake Washington Ship Canal opened. It is the landmark to aim for on the way back in.
* **The slope.** Straight out from the beach the bottom runs a gentle, silty slope to about 14 m at 80 m out.
* **The wall.** From about 14 m the bottom steepens into the wall, which runs to about 26 m off the beach and continues as a steep slope below it: 26.8 m at 118 m out, 33.5 m at 138 m, 40.9 m at 158 m and 47.1 m at 178 m.
* **North and south.** Deeper walls and ledges run along the shore in both directions. To the north the deep water comes closer to shore, reaching 20.9 m about 80 m out and 27.6 m about 100 m out. To the south the slope sits farther out, reaching 21.7 m about 120 m out.
* **The lake floor.** Past about 200 m out the slope levels out at 50 to 59 m, beyond sport diving depth.

## Current

* **Type.** None: the water holds still at depth, and wind drives only a surface drift.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | east-northeast, 78° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | east-northeast, 78° |

* **Bad.** North-northeast (NNE), onshore over 9.3 km of open water up the lake, and east-southeast (ESE), onshore over 5.1 km. A sustained wind from either builds chop onto the pebble beach and makes the exit rough.
* **Short fetch.** Northeast through east (NE-E), onshore over 2.7 to 4.1 km, and southeast (SE), onshore over 3.8 km. A sustained wind from here puts a chop on the beach, enough to make the exit uncomfortable in a stiff breeze.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast through south (SSE-S), along the shore over 5.3 to 8.4 km. South is the lake's prevailing wind, and it strengthens through fall and winter; it runs chop along the beach and past the entry.
* **Fine.** South-southwest clockwise through north (SSW-N): offshore from south-southwest through northwest, blowing off the park's own shore, and cross-shore from north-northwest and north over 1 km or less.

## Visibility

* **What drives it.** An algae bloom in early spring and blooms again through summer cloud the water, with a clearer spell between them in late spring, around May and June. The bottom is fine silt that clouds around a diver who stirs it.
* **Range.** Modest by salt water standards; about 7 to 8 m counts as a good day in the lake.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** The lake stratifies from late March or early April to early December, and the water below the thermocline stays about 10 °C or colder through summer. After the lake mixes in December it cools top to bottom, coldest in late January and February, and runs below 10 °C through winter; the winter low at depth is not established. Drysuit protocol applies year round.
* **Surface layer.** The warm surface layer runs about 10 m deep over a thermocline near 10 to 12 m. It peaks at 22 to 23 °C in August and tops 20 °C on more than 40 days each summer.

## Marine life

* **Crayfish.** Signal crayfish live under logs and debris on the slope, with occasional ones out in the open.
* **Fish.** Sculpin on the bottom, and bass and yellow perch in the water above it.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** The site's main hazard. Motorboats run the lake all through the boating season, and Lake Washington's speed limit of 13 km/h holds only within about 90 m of shore, so the dive area, 116 m out, lies in open water past it. Tow a diver's flag on a float, stay under it, and listen for engines before ascending. Winter brings far less traffic.
* **Depth.** The wall and the slope below it run past 40 m with nothing to stop a descent, and the light fades fast with depth in the lake's silty water. Plan a turn depth and watch the computer.
* **Silt.** The fine silt bottom clouds the water quickly; keep fins off the bottom, and carry a compass for the return to the beach.
* **Weighting.** Fresh water gives less lift than salt water, and a diver weighted for the sea sinks fast down the wall. Drop weight for fresh water and check buoyancy in the shallows before heading out.
* **Cold at depth.** The water below the thermocline stays near 10 °C or colder even in summer, well below the warm surface, and the whole lake runs cold through winter.
* **Wind chop.** A sustained wind from the north-northeast or east-southeast rolls chop onto the beach and makes the exit rough.
* **No lifeguard.** The beach is unguarded, and swimmers share the shallows on summer days.
