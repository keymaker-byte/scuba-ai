# Sledgehammer Point

A wall dive straight off US 101 at the east end of Lake Crescent in Olympic National Park, entered from the pullout at the Lake Crescent welcome sign. The shore drops almost at once into a steep wall that runs past 90 m before it levels out, in the same exceptionally clear, low-nutrient water as the rest of the lake. The point gives its name to the Sledgehammer Point Rockslide, about 7.2 million m³ of rock that fell from Mount Storm King into water at least 140 m deep about 3,100 years ago. The headline hazard is depth: the wall offers nothing to stop a descent, and the clear water makes depth easy to misjudge.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the pullout, look over the guardrail for three or four worn paths down to the water, about 12 m west of the parking. Walk down with gear and enter off the rocks; the bottom drops away within a few metres, and the dive area is a 32 m swim out, west, 271°, over 27.7 m of water.
* **Parking.** The pullout at the Lake Crescent welcome sign on US 101, the first lakeside turnout heading west along the lake. Limited space.
* **Access.** Inside Olympic National Park; the park requires an entrance pass of all visitors. Park rules bar swimming more than 46 m from the Lake Crescent shoreline unless a vessel stays within 9 m of the swimmer, so keep surface swims close to shore.
* **Facilities.** None beyond the pullout itself.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry.** The paths reach the water on a short stretch of shore facing west-northwest. The bottom drops away within a few metres of the waterline.
* **Ledges to the right.** Heading right from the entry, toward the north-northeast, the wall breaks into a series of ledges, the gentler side of the site.
* **The wall to the left.** Heading left, toward the south-southwest and on along the shore west, the shore is a sheer wall, most impressive the farther west it runs.
* **Straight out.** Off the entry the bottom runs about 18 m deep 20 m from shore, 27.7 m at the dive area, 40 m at 55 m out and 67 m at 80 m out, reaching about 87 m deep at 105 m out and levelling out at 93 to 96 m beyond 120 m.
* **The truck.** A truck lies at about 49 m off this shore, past sport diving depth.

## Current

* **Type.** None: the water holds still at depth, and wind drives only a surface drift.

* **Bad.** West-southwest (WSW), onshore over 6.5 km of open water, the longest run of water in reach of the entry. A sustained wind from here builds chop onto the shore, which makes the scramble out over rock below the guardrail awkward.
* **Short fetch.** Southwest (SW), and west through north-northwest (W-NNW), onshore over 1.2 to 2.6 km. A sustained wind from here puts a chop on the entry, enough to make the exit uncomfortable in a stiff breeze.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southwest (N-SSW): offshore from northeast through south-southeast, and cross-shore from north, north-northeast, south and south-southwest, running along the shore over 2.4 km or less.
* **Afternoon wind.** Wind on the lake often rises in the afternoon and quickly builds waves of 30 cm or more; mornings are calmest.

## Visibility

* **Clarity.** Routinely excellent, commonly 9 to 15 m and reaching well past 20 m on the best days.
* **What lowers it.** The lake's low nitrogen level keeps algae from clouding it, so visibility holds through the year, with no seasonal pattern established. Rain and wind lower it for a time, and fine silt on the ledges clouds the water around a diver who stirs it.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Cold year round. In summer the water below the thermocline runs about 7 to 11 °C at 30 m and about 6 °C below 40 m. Through winter and spring the lake reads about 6 to 7 °C from the surface to depth. Drysuit protocol applies year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms to about 18 to 20 °C by late summer, over a thermocline that sits around 15 to 20 m, and cools back toward the deep water's temperature through the fall.

## Marine life

* **Fish.** Sparse along the open wall. Small sculpin hide in cracks in the rock, and trout, including the lake's endemic Beardslee and Crescenti strains, are seen along the wall.
* **Invertebrates.** Signal crayfish live in the cracks and under ledges, with freshwater sponge on the rock.

## Hazards

* **Depth.** The site's defining hazard. The wall runs from the shallows to past 90 m with nothing to stop a descent, and the clear water lets light reach depths where a diver used to salt water would expect it to be dark. Plan a turn depth and watch the computer, not the light.
* **Weighting.** Fresh water gives less lift than salt water, and a diver weighted for the sea sinks fast down the wall. Drop weight for fresh water and check buoyancy in the shallows before heading out.
* **The highway.** The pullout sits directly on US 101, with traffic close by; gear up on the lake side of the car and cross the guardrail with care. The paths down to the water are steep and worn.
* **Rockfall.** The slopes above this stretch of US 101 shed rock, and slides have blocked the highway along the lake.
* **Cold at depth.** The water below the thermocline stays near 6 to 11 °C even in summer, cold enough that hypothermia builds faster than the clear water and calm surface suggest.
* **Boat traffic.** Motorboats and water skiers use Lake Crescent. Boats are held to flat wake within about 90 m of shore. Surface with a dive flag or marker buoy deployed, and listen for engines before ascending.
* **Snags.** Old fishing line and abandoned dive guidelines lie in the lake in places. Carry a cutting tool.
* **Remoteness.** The nearest hospital is in Port Angeles, about 29 km east by road, and the nearest chamber is farther still.
