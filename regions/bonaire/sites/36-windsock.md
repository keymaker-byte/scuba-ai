# 36. Windsock

A public beach on EEG Boulevard opposite the seaward end of Flamingo Airport's runway, about 2 km south of Kralendijk on Bonaire's west coast, named for the airport windsocks beside it. A short swim crosses a sand and rubble terrace at 4 to 6 m to its edge at about 11 m, where a reef of hard and soft corals slopes away to sand from about 32 m. Calm, shallow and close to shore, it suits snorkelers and newer divers, with rays and turtles on the sand and a relaxed night dive. The headline hazards are the airport's jet fuel pier, where tankers berth, and traffic on the boulevard beside the parking.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Follow the path from the yellow marker stone to the beach and wade in over sand. A narrow shelf of rubble and ironshore lines the waterline in places, so footing on the first steps is uneven; take them slowly. Swim 72 m northwest, straight out from the beach, across the terrace to the dive area at its edge. Exit at the same beach.
* **Parking.** At the roadside on EEG Boulevard, 32 m east-southeast of the entry, among the trees behind the beach. Free. The space is small and fills on weekends and holidays, so arrive early.
* **Access.** A public beach, open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Keep clear of the jet fuel pier whenever a tanker is alongside it.
* **Facilities.** Shade trees, barbecue pits and rubbish bins on the beach.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach.** A small sandy beach with patches of shade, its yellow marker stone at the roadside above the entry. The runway of Flamingo Airport ends across the road, and aircraft pass low over the beach on approach.
* **The fuel pier.** A T-shaped pier serving the airport's jet fuel tanks runs out from the shore toward the north end of the beach. Fish school in its shade.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble shelving from the beach to 4 to 6 m and holding there for the first 40 m out, with scattered coral heads, then dropping to about 11 m at its outer edge. The shallows near the beach carry storm-broken rubble and dead coral that rise close to the surface in places; pick a sandy line through them.
* **The reef slope.** Beyond the terrace edge a reef of alternating hard and soft corals slopes away at a gentle angle to sand from about 32 m, with deeper water beyond. The reef runs on north and south along the coast, so a dive can follow it either way and turn back.
* **The return.** The beach lies 72 m southeast of the dive area, straight back across the terrace.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift. What sets its direction on a given day is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight or absent, at times limited to moderate along the reef. An entry near the middle of the beach leaves room to start the dive into the current whichever way it runs. Check its direction before entering.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | northwest, 319° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northwest, 319° |

The swim out runs 72 m northwest from the entry, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** West (W), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea past the south end of Klein Bonaire. Wind from this direction puts waves on the beach and makes the swim out across the shallows hard work.
* **Short fetch.** West-northwest through north-northeast (WNW-NNE), onshore over 1.9 to just under 5 km across the bay toward Kralendijk and Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** Southwest through west-southwest (SW-WSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through south-southwest (NE-SSW): offshore from east through south-southwest, off the land, and cross-shore over 290 m to 1.1 km from the northeast and east-northeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 20 to 30 m, at times down to about 10 m, best in the calm early morning and off the deeper slope.
* **Wind and rain.** Strong wind and runoff after rain, mostly October to January, cloud the shallow terrace for a time, while the slope stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths, with a thermocline at times on calm days. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Southern stingrays rest on the sand, and green and hawksbill turtles graze the shallows. Schools of juvenile fish, trumpetfish, burrfish and trunkfish work the rubble and coral heads.
* **Reef slope.** Parrotfish and wrasse in numbers, French and queen angelfish, grunts and the odd barracuda or tarpon along the slope, with green, spotted and goldentail morays in the coral and cleaning stations on the reef. Spotted eagle rays pass along the slope at times. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Macro.** Squid over the reef, octopus, scorpionfish, spotted drum, shrimp and the occasional seahorse, rewarding a slow search.
* **Night.** Octopus, spiny lobster and morays forage over the reef, and parrotfish sleep in the coral.

## Hazards

* **The fuel pier.** Tankers berth at the pier to discharge jet fuel. Keep well clear of it whenever a tanker is alongside, and enter toward the middle or south of the beach.
* **Road traffic.** Cars come fast around the corner on EEG Boulevard beside the roadside parking. Watch the road while kitting up.
* **Car break-ins.** Leave nothing of value in the car, and lock it.
* **The shallows.** Rubble and dead coral rise close to the surface between the beach and the terrace; take a sandy line through them, and mark it before a night dive.
* **Depth.** The slope continues past 30 m to sand from about 32 m and deeper water beyond. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from the west (W) puts waves on the beach, and from west-northwest through north-northeast (WNW-NNE) a chop; choose another site when any of these blows fresh.
