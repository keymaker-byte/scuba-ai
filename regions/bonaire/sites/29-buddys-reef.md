# 29. Buddy's Reef

Buddy Dive Resort's house reef on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, entered from the resort pier and open to divers around the clock. A shallow terrace of sand and coral rubble runs about 65 m out from the pier to a drop-off at about 8 m, where black crinoids perch on the coral heads and the reef slopes steeply away. It is one of the island's easiest shore dives and a favourite night dive, when resident tarpon hunt in divers' lights. The headline hazards are the dive boats using the pier and the slope below the drop-off, which leads well past the maximum operating depth of EAN32.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Through Buddy Dive Resort to its pier, down a ladder into about 4.5 m of water. The dive area lies 64 m west-southwest of the pier, straight out across the terrace to the drop-off. Exit by the same ladders.
* **Parking.** In the shaded Buddy Dive Resort lot beside the coastal road, Kaya Gobernador N. Debrot 85, about 110 m from the pier, free and open to non-guests.
* **Access.** On private resort property, open to divers 24 hours a day. Non-guests check in at the Buddy Dive shop before gearing up. Confirm status, and the site's current standing on STINAPA's SCTLD map, before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** The pier with its ladders, rinse tanks, showers, changing rooms and lockers.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pier.** The resort's pier stands in about 4.5 m of water. Guide ropes run from it out across the terrace toward the reef.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble with small, scattered coral heads, holding at about 4.5 m for the first 20 m out from the pier and deepening gradually to about 7 m some 50 m out. Rows of planted staghorn and elkhorn coral stand in its shallower parts.
* **The nursery.** Coral nursery trees hung with staghorn and elkhorn fragments stand on the terrace in front of the pier at about 6 m.
* **The drop-off.** About 65 m west-southwest of the pier the terrace ends at about 8 m and the reef slopes steeply away, a 45 degree slope of hard coral, sponges and some gorgonians. The dive area sits on its lip.
* **The reef slope.** The slope runs on below 40 m. The reef continues unbroken north and south along the coast, so a dive can follow it either way from the drop-off and turn back.
* **The return.** The pier lies 64 m east-northeast of the dive area; follow the ropes back across the terrace to the ladders.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What sets its direction is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight or absent. At times a light to moderate current runs along the reef, north or south, and is stronger along the drop-off than on the terrace. Start the dive into whatever current is running and let it carry you back toward the pier.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 247° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 246° |

The swim out runs 64 m west-southwest from the pier, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South (S), onshore over 5.2 km down the coast, and west through west-northwest (W-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises waves at the pier and stirs up the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), onshore over 1.1 to 1.3 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the pier.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over 6.7 km, running a swell along the shore past the pier.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 200 to 570 m from the north-northwest, southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the pier calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on a typical day, best in the morning.
* **Wind.** Wind from south through northwest stirs up the sandy terrace and cuts visibility in the shallows.
* **The pier.** Boats coming and going and training classes on the terrace stir up the sand near the pier, most of all in the middle of the day.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Queen angelfish hold territories around the coral heads, and sergeant majors guard their eggs on them. Peacock flounder lie on the sand, and green and hawksbill turtles graze the shallows.
* **Drop-off and slope.** Black crinoids perch on the coral heads at the top of the drop-off. French angelfish, stoplight parrotfish and trumpetfish are common along the slope, with barracuda off its edge, and cleaning stations draw parrotfish and angelfish. Seahorses and frogfish live on the reef and take searching to find. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Restoration.** Staghorn and elkhorn fragments grow on the nursery trees and in the planted rows on the terrace, part of the island's coral restoration effort.
* **Tarpon.** A resident group of tarpon, 1 to 1.5 m long, patrols the reef.
* **Night.** The tarpon hunt in divers' lights, often passing very close. Octopus and lobster come out to forage over the reef.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats run in and out from the pier divers enter and exit by, busiest through the middle of the day, and pass close to the ladders. Stay down until back at the ladders, look up before surfacing, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Depth.** The slope below the drop-off leads on past 40 m, below the maximum operating depth of EAN32 and outside a no-decompression plan. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Box jellyfish.** Box jellyfish swarm in the shallows about 8 to 10 nights after the full moon and swim toward light, gathering under the pier lights on a night dive; the sting is very painful. Wear a full wetsuit on those nights, keep lights off the surface, and leave the water if any are seen.
* **Restoration structures.** The nursery trees and planted rows are fragile; keep fins and gauges clear of them, and use the guide ropes as a reference only, never a handhold.
* **Current.** Usually slight, and occasionally moderate along the reef; keep enough gas to return to the pier against it.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from south (S) and from west through west-northwest (W-WNW) puts waves on the pier, from south-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW) a chop, and from the northwest (NW) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
