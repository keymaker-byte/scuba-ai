# 29. Buddy's Reef

Buddy Dive Resort's house reef and one of the island's most accessible sites, a gently sloping reef that suits a relaxed dive at any level. Black crinoids perch on coral heads in the shallows, and a resident tarpon greets most night divers, often hunting close by in the beam of a dive light.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A straightforward, gradual shore entry from the resort.
* **Parking.** At Buddy Dive Resort.
* **Access.** Open, the resort's own house reef.
* **Facilities.** Full resort facilities on site, including a drive-through fill station.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The drop-off.** Begins around 8 m, marking the shift from the shallow shelf to the working reef.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Logged dives here found slack to negligible current, consistent with the site's reputation as a relaxed, minimal current dive.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The shallow shelf runs to about 8 m before the drop-off; logged dives here have reached 30.3 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 247° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 246° |

* **Bad.** South (S), onshore over 5.2 km down the coast, and west through west-northwest (W-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from these quarters raises swell at the entry.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), onshore over 1.1 to 1.4 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over 6.7 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 100 to 370 m from the north-northwest, southeast and south-southeast.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast; logged day dives here have rated it 5 out of 5 (excellent).

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October; logged dives here measured 29 C. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Black crinoids perched on coral heads.
* **Night.** A resident tarpon greets most night divers and hunts in the light beam.
