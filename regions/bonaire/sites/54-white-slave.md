# 54. White Slave

White Slave is a shore site on EEG Boulevard on Bonaire's southwest coast, opposite the white-painted slave huts and the white obelisk that once marked the Witte Pan salt pond for trading ships. An ironshore entry gives onto a broad sand plain where stingrays and eagle rays feed, and a swim of about 190 m crosses it to the start of the reef at about 12.6 m, where gorgonians, soft coral and sponges run down a gentle slope past 30 m. Turtles are nearly always seen. The site sits on a bend in the coast, and its headline hazards are a current that varies in strength and direction, sometimes within a single dive, and the long swim out and back.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk 22 m west-southwest from the yellow stone, across loose coral rubble, to the ironshore below the huts. The rock ends in a ledge that drops straight into about 4 m of water, so step off carefully and put fins on in the water. Swim 190 m west-southwest across the sand plain to the start of the reef, and exit at the same ledge.
* **Parking.** The wide sandy shoulder of EEG Boulevard beside the huts, free. The soft sand can trap a car, especially in the rainy season.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. The huts are a protected monument and a memorial to slavery on Bonaire; treat them with respect.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The huts and the obelisk.** The row of small white coral stone huts stands on the shore above the entry, with the white obelisk nearby. Both are visible from the water and serve as the reference for the exit, by day and at night.
* **The sand plain.** About 4 m deep for the first 50 m from the entry, sloping to 8 m at 80 m out and 10 m at 140 m. Rays feed on the sand here.
* **The start of the reef.** At the dive area, 190 m west-southwest of the entry at about 12.6 m, an underwater buoy floating at about 10 m marks where the coral begins. Use it as the reference to start and end the dive.
* **The outer reef.** Coral heads, gorgonians and sponges on a terrace of 9 to 13 m running about 150 m farther west-southwest, then a gentle slope down past 30 m. The reef runs lusher and holds more fish to the north than to the south.
* **The return.** Come back up the slope to the buoy and head east-northeast, 190 m across the sand to the ledge below the huts.

## Current

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The site sits on a bend in the coast, where the current changes direction from day to day and runs strong at times.
* **Change during the dive.** The current can build or turn while divers are down.
* **A southerly set.** A current running south sets away from the entry toward the island's southern tip; choose another site when it sets south at the entry.
* **A northerly set.** A current running north carries a diver north of the huts on the swim home. Check its direction before entering, start the dive heading south into it so the return drifts back toward the entry, and keep enough gas for the crossing.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 240° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 238° |

The swim out runs 190 m west-southwest from the entry, 5° off the shore's facing, straight out.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the ironshore, puts surf and surge on the entry ledge and stirs up the sand plain.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast (SSE), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and northwest through north-northwest (NW-NNW), cross-shore over 11.3 to more than 30 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through southeast (N-SE): offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 2.7 km from the southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land or along the shore and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** About 20 m on average and much the same year round, best on calm mornings.
* **The sand plain.** Waves from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) stir up the sand near the entry and cut visibility there, while the reef farther out stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Sand plain.** Southern stingrays burying themselves in the sand, spotted eagle rays, sand divers, flounder and garden eels. Tarpon pass over the sand at times.
* **Reef.** Turtles, nearly always present. Dense gorgonians and sea fans, soft coral and sponges, with French angelfish, parrotfish, blue tang, schoolmaster snapper, cowfish, trumpetfish, scorpionfish and green morays. Barracuda and spotted eagle rays along the deeper slope.
* **Night.** Lobster and octopus out on the reef.
* **Coral.** Gorgonians and soft coral dominate. Hard coral along the whole leeward coast has suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Variable in strength and direction, sometimes strong, and able to change during the dive. Choose another site when it sets south, and start the dive into a northerly set.
* **The entry and exit.** Loose coral rubble on the walk down and an ironshore ledge that drops into 4 m of water, with surf and surge on it when wind or swell come from south through west-northwest (S-WNW). Wear sturdy boots and help a buddy over the ledge. Choose another site when a swell is running.
* **The swim and gas.** The 190 m swim across the sand to the reef and the same back, plus the terrace beyond, use a large share of a cylinder, and more against a current. Turn on gas with the swim home across the sand still to come.
* **Depth.** The slope runs on past 30 m, close to EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar. Watch the computer on the gentle slope, and keep within the no-decompression limit.
* **Night diving.** Dive the site by day first. The ledge is hard to find and climb in the dark; take a bearing back to the huts and carry a surface marker buoy.
