# 45. Alice in Wonderland

Alice in Wonderland is a shore site on EEG Boulevard south of Kralendijk, entered just before the Cargill salt works sign driving south, and one of the clearest examples of Bonaire's double reef. A sandy terrace with scattered coral heads leads about 110 m out to the first reef, which slopes from 8 to 10 m down to about 22 m above a sand channel at 24 to 30 m, with a second reef rising beyond it out of sight of the first. The first reef suits any diver; the crossing and the second reef are deep and call for a compass. The headline hazard is depth on the crossing, where the sand pulls a diver down toward 30 m without a reef in view.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Follow the marked path from the lot over rocks and coral rubble down to a small sandy beach, and wade in over flat, slippery rock and rubble; the bottom drops to about 4 m a short way out. The entry is easy in calm water and turns moderate when waves run onto the shore. Swim 145 m west-southwest, straight out from the shore, across the terrace to the dive area on the top of the first reef. The exit is at the same beach.
* **Parking.** The gravel lot beside EEG Boulevard marked by yellow stones, just before the Cargill sign driving south, about 15 m from the water. Free, with room for 8 to 10 cars.
* **Access.** Open roadside access at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit, tying up to the mooring.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The mooring.** A yellow mooring buoy sits north of the entry (to the right, facing the sea), about 40 m from shore, in about 6 m of water over the terrace, short of the reef edge. A descent can start at the mooring or on the sand nearby.
* **The terrace.** Sand with scattered coral heads, shelving from about 4 m at the entry to about 8 m at its edge, about 110 m out. It runs on along the coast in both directions.
* **The first reef.** Begins at the terrace edge around 8 to 10 m and slopes down to about 22 m. It runs north-northwest to south-southeast along the coast, and a dive can follow it either way with the reef on one side and sand on the other.
* **The sand channel.** At the foot of the first reef, a sand channel at 24 to 30 m separates the two reefs, with coral bommies scattered across it. The second reef lies out of sight of the first.
* **The second reef.** Rises out of the channel to a crest at about 20 to 25 m, flatter and easier to swim over than the first, and drops away on its outer side to well past 40 m. The crossing runs straight offshore, west-southwest; the return to the first reef runs east-northeast. Take a compass bearing before leaving the first reef.
* **The return.** Come back up onto the terrace at about 8 to 10 m for the swim home; the entry lies 145 m east-northeast of the dive area.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight or absent, at most a light drift along the reef. Check its direction before entering and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 247° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 247° |

The swim out runs 145 m west-southwest from the entry, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves and surge straight onto the rocky entry and makes getting in and out hard work.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast (NNW-SSE): offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 820 m from the north-northwest and over 290 to 750 m from the southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, around 20 m on an average day, and best on calm mornings before the wind picks up.
* **Wind and swell.** Waves from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) stir the shallow terrace and can leave the water murky near shore, with the second reef at times clearer than the first.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Juvenile reef fish among the coral heads, including juvenile French angelfish, with scorpionfish on the sand. Turtles graze the shallows.
* **First reef.** Mostly soft coral and sea fans, with parrotfish including rainbow parrotfish, blue tang, French and queen angelfish, spotted drum, and green and other morays in the coral. Schools of Caribbean reef squid hover over the reef.
* **Sand channel.** Southern stingrays on the sand and spotted eagle rays passing over it, and the occasional large peacock flounder.
* **Second reef.** Sea fans, plate and leaf coral, and what remains of the star and brain coral, with grunts, butterflyfish and wrasse. Barracuda, triggerfish, horse-eye jacks, snapper and large groupers patrol its outer side.
* **Night.** Tarpon hunt in divers' lights, and ostracods glow in the shallows.
* **Coral.** Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease, and turf and red algae now spread across parts of both reefs and the sand channel.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish are seen on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Depth on the crossing.** The sand channel lies at 24 to 30 m and the second reef's outer side drops past 40 m. Crossing from one reef to the other over open sand pulls a diver deeper than intended; watch the depth gauge throughout, and turn at a planned depth and gas.
* **Navigation.** The second reef is out of sight from the first, and the crossing is a swim over featureless sand. Take a compass bearing out and back, and turn back to the first reef on a reciprocal heading if the second does not appear.
* **Rocky entry.** Flat, slippery rock and rubble at the waterline; wear sturdy booties and take the entry slowly. Surge can build at the waterline on the exit.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind or swell from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) runs straight onto the shore and batters the entry; choose another site when any of these blows fresh or a swell is running.
* **Fire coral.** Grows on the terrace and the shallow reef; keep clear of it on the swim out and back.
* **Boats.** Dive boats tie up to the mooring. Ascend with a surface marker buoy away from it.
