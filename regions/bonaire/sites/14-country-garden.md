# 14. Country Garden (Mushroom City)

A boat dive on Bonaire's northwest coast below a high limestone cliff, where three large pillars have broken away from the cliff and fallen onto the reef near its crest, at about 7 to 13 m. Each pillar is grown with hard coral, gorgonians and sponges, and shelters schoolmasters, grunts and goatfish in its lee; their shape gives the site its other name, Mushroom City. Beyond the pillars the reef drops steeply into deep water. The headline hazards are boat traffic over the site and a wind driven current that runs strong at times.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride from a boat tied to the site mooring, then a descent onto the reef crest and the pillars.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** By boat from Kralendijk, about 15 minutes away. The cliff along the shore offers no way down to the water. A long swim along the reef, about 380 m east-southeast at 8.5 to 10 m, also reaches the site from a marked shore entry on a coral rubble beach to the west-northwest. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Cliff and terrace.** A high limestone cliff runs along the shore, and a narrow terrace at 4 to 6 m runs along its foot.
* **Reef crest.** The bottom steps down from the terrace to a reef crest at about 9 to 10 m, roughly 60 m off the cliff. The dive area coordinate sits just past the crest, at the top of the drop-off.
* **The three pillars.** Large blocks of limestone broken from the cliff, lying near the crest at about 7 to 13 m, the site's defining feature.
* **Drop-off.** Past the crest the reef falls away steeply, below 25 m and on past 40 m.
* **Along the reef.** To the west-northwest the reef runs at 8.5 to 10 m for about 380 m toward a long coral rubble beach.

* **Drivers.** The east trades. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light to moderate, and strong at times.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 217° |

The shore here runs northwest to southeast below the cliff.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 5.6 km to more than 30 km, along the coast from the south-southeast or across open sea from the south and west. Wind from this quarter raises chop and swell at the mooring below the cliff.
* **Mixed.** Southeast (SE), cross-shore over 7.3 km down the coast, running a swell along the shore past the mooring.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the cliff, and cross-shore over 290 m to 2.3 km from the west-northwest, northwest and east-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the site calm at their usual strength; trades veering southeast fall in the Mixed sector.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m, often excellent, best on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Wind and swell from the south-southeast through west stir the shallows below the cliff and cut visibility there; the water clears over the drop-off.
* **Rain.** Rain from October to January washes sediment off the land into the shallows for a short time.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Pillars.** Hard coral, gorgonians and sponges cover each pillar face, with cleaning shrimp in the cracks. Schoolmasters, grunts and goatfish shelter in their lee, with snapper, grouper, French angelfish, parrotfish, blue tang, boxfish and puffers around them. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Along the reef.** Turtles and spotted eagle rays along the reef, with a Caribbean reef shark or a seahorse on rare occasions.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats use the mooring and drop groups over the site; carry a surface marker buoy and ascend along the reef or the mooring line.
* **Current.** The wind driven current runs strong at times; start the dive into it and keep enough gas to return to the mooring.
* **Depth.** The slope drops steeply past 40 m directly below the reef crest, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Swell.** Wind from the south-southeast through west puts chop and swell on the mooring under the cliff; choose another site when it is running.
