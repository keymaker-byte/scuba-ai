# 20. Oil Slick Leap

One of the best known shore dives on Bonaire's west coast, about 7 km north of Kralendijk, named for the oil storage terminal once proposed for this stretch of shore and never built. Divers enter with a giant stride off a low limestone cliff into about 5 m of water and climb back out on a steep steel ladder. A sand and rubble terrace slopes gently from the foot of the cliff to a drop-off a short swim out, where schools of barracuda hold over the reef. The headline hazard is the ladder exit, steep and at times missing its lower rungs, and harder in any swell.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride off the rock ledge just northwest of the ladder, to the right of it facing the sea, drops about 2 to 3 m into roughly 5 m of water. Divers who prefer not to jump climb down the ladder. Exit is up the ladder. Thick-soled boots give grip on the rocky ledge and the ladder rungs.
* **Parking.** A sand strip beside the road at the yellow marker stone, room for about eight to ten cars, a few steps from the ladder. Keep clear of the gate to the neighbouring dive facility. Leave nothing of value in the vehicle.
* **Access.** Open to diving, by shore or by boat from the site mooring, and a popular night dive. Its shallows also make a good snorkel. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** The platform and steel ladder at the cliff edge.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The ladder.** The steel ladder at the platform marks the entry and exit, with about 5 m of water at its foot.
* **Shallow terrace.** Sand, rubble and scattered coral heads slope gently south-southwest from the ladder, reaching about 10 m some 30 m out and about 11 to 12 m some 70 m out.
* **Mooring buoy.** The site mooring sits about 50 m off the ladder, over the terrace, and shows the day's set on its line.
* **Drop-off.** Beyond the terrace the reef steepens into a slope of overhangs and crevices, with the densest coral and most of the fish between about 12 and 25 m, and runs on past 40 m.
* **Coral nursery.** Reef Renewal Foundation Bonaire keeps a coral nursery at the site, rope lines strung with coral fragments.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current is usually slight here, and can sit still for days, then run when the trades pick up or shift.
* **On the day.** The mooring buoy line shows any set right after entry, and the return to the ladder is planned against it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southwest, 211° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 211° |

The dive area lies about 30 m south-southwest of the ladder, straight out from the entry shore.

* **Bad.** South-southeast (SSE), onshore over 7.9 km down the coast, and south-southwest through west (SSW-W), onshore over 5.3 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises a sea against the cliff and the ladder.
* **Short fetch.** South (S), onshore over 3.5 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the ladder.
* **Mixed.** West-northwest (WNW), cross-shore over 5.9 km, running a swell along the shore past the ladder.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the land, and cross-shore over 180 m to 1.9 km from the northwest, east-southeast and southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, at its best on calm early mornings.
* **After storms.** Swell stirs the sand and rubble terrace and can cut the visibility for a day or two afterwards.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The temperature holds steady through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef slope.** Schools of barracuda, at times 20 or more fish, hold over the reef at about 10 to 15 m and tolerate divers well. Tarpon, French and queen angelfish, schools of blue tang, parrotfish, trumpetfish and snapper work the slope.
* **Turtles.** Turtles visit the reef, hawksbills among them.
* **Small life.** Spotted drum, scrawled filefish, flamingo tongues and nudibranchs on the reef, frogfish and seahorses for a patient eye, and green morays in the rubble and holes.
* **Around the ladder.** Octopus in the shallows near the foot of the ladder, and glassy sweepers sheltering under the ledges.
* **Coral.** Hurricane swells in 2008 and 2010 stripped much of the coral from the shallows near the ladder, leaving rubble and sand, and the reef grows healthier from about 12 m down. Over 750 elkhorn corals raised in a nursery here were planted back onto the reef in 2018. Elkhorn coral across the island has declined sharply since 2023.
* **Night.** Octopus hunting in the open, spiny lobster and crabs on the move, and parrotfish asleep in the coral.

## Hazards

* **The ladder.** Steep and slippery, and storms have repeatedly torn off its lowest rungs. Inspect it before kitting up, look up for divers on the ladder before surfacing beneath it, and keep strength in reserve for the climb.
* **The jump.** Check that the water below the ledge is clear of divers and snorkelers before each stride, and hold the mask.
* **Swell and surge.** Northern swell in winter, and swell from passing tropical systems from June to November, surges at the foot of the ladder and in heavy swell can wash over the platform. Judge it from the cliff before entering.
* **Boats.** Dive boats use the site mooring; a surface marker buoy shows a diver's position away from the ladder.
* **Vehicle theft.** Vehicles parked here are a target for break-ins; leave them empty.
