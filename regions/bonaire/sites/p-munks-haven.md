# P. Munk's Haven

A boat dive on the west side of Klein Bonaire, marked site P of Bonaire National Marine Park, facing west into the open Caribbean. A broad, shallow terrace of sand and scattered coral heads runs about 180 m out from the shore at 4 to 11 m, home to yellowhead jawfish and sand tilefish, before a wall drops from about 12 m past recreational depth, with cleaning stations visited by large groupers in its deeper reaches. The headline hazards are the wall below the gentle terrace, where depth builds quickly, and the site's full exposure to any wind from the south-southwest through the northwest.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a slow descent onto the sand of the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from the Kralendijk marinas. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required. Confirm the site is open with STINAPA or the dive operator before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring on the terrace, in about 8 m.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Fine coral sand at 4 to 11 m, crossed by burrows and dotted with coral heads and gorgonians, running about 180 m out from the shore. The mooring sits on it, and it serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive and as a snorkel site.
* **The crest.** High coral heads line the outer edge of the terrace, about 40 m west of the coordinate, where the bottom tips over at about 12 m.
* **The wall.** Drops from the crest past 40 m, with hard corals in its upper part and gorgonians, sea fans and large barrel sponges taking over below 20 m. The usual route follows it down from the crest, with a different coral community at each depth.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight, at times strong.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 270° |

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over 11.3 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast through south (SSE-S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and north-northwest through north (NNW-N), cross-shore over 6.8 to 8.3 km, each running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through southeast: offshore, blowing off the islet itself, over 140 m to 5.9 km.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m and often more, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the south-southwest through the northwest stirs the sand of the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Sand dwellers.** Yellowhead jawfish hover over their burrows across the terrace, and sand tilefish build dens by piling rubble over their burrow mouths, diving into them when approached too closely.
* **Coral heads.** Spotted drums shelter among the coral heads on the terrace, with chromis and sergeant majors around them, and flamingo tongues on the gorgonians.
* **The wall.** Hard corals near the crest give way to soft corals, sea fans and barrel sponges deeper, with morays in the reef, anemones with their cleaner shrimp, and parrotfish and French angelfish along the face.
* **Cleaning stations.** In the deeper part of the wall, where large groupers come in to be cleaned.
* **Blue water.** Barracuda patrol off the wall.

## Hazards

* **The wall.** The terrace slopes gently to its edge at about 12 m, the wall then drops steeply, and the cleaning stations draw divers deep. Watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Wind and swell.** The site faces the open sea; a wind from the south-southwest through the northwest runs straight onto it, putting chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Current.** Wind driven current can build when the trades strengthen or shift, making the return to the mooring hard work.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
