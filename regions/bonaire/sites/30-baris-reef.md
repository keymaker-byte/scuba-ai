# 30. Bari's Reef

The house reef of Sand Dollar and Den Laman on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, entered from shore down the public ramp at Isidel Beach Park. A shallow sand and rubble terrace runs about 77 m out to its edge at about 9.5 m, where the reef slopes away past barrel and tube sponges toward sand near 40 m. Over 300 fish species have been recorded here, the highest count of any surveyed site in the Caribbean, and the reef is a favourite of macro photographers and night divers. The headline hazards are boat traffic around the resort pier and the slope below the terrace edge, which runs past the 34 m maximum operating depth of EAN32.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down the sloping, railed ramp at the seaward edge of Isidel Beach Park into about 4 m of water. The lower, grated metal section of the ramp is slippery, and the footing at its foot is rocky, so wear boots. The dive area lies 77 m west across the terrace. Exit by the same ramp.
* **Parking.** In the parking at the Isidel Beach Park entrance by the Hato roundabout on Kaya Gobernador N. Debrot, free, about 120 m from the ramp along the park's footpath.
* **Access.** Isidel Beach Park is a public park and open to divers. The ramp can back up with snorkelers and swimmers on busy days. Guests of Den Laman and Sand Dollar can also enter and exit from the resort pier 50 m north of the ramp, which is closed to everyone else. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** Isidel Beach Park's restrooms, shaded palapas, barbecues, food stands and a separate accessibility ramp into 1.2 m of water. The Den Laman and Sand Dollar pier, with its stairs, for resort guests.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The ramp.** Runs down from the park to the water at the shoreline. A boat ramp lies on the park's north side.
* **The old pier.** Concrete remains of a collapsed pier lie in 1 to 2 m of water about 40 m south-southwest of the ramp, overgrown and busy with fish.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble with scattered coral heads and sponges, holding at 4 to 5 m for the first 60 m out from the ramp, then dropping to 8 to 10 m along its outer edge. The dive area sits on that edge.
* **The resort pier.** The Den Laman and Sand Dollar pier runs about 45 m out from the shore 50 m north of the ramp, its end in about 5 m of water some 60 m north-northeast of the dive area.
* **Reef balls.** Concrete reef balls, placed in front of Sand Dollar after Hurricane Lenny in 1999 destroyed much of the shallow coral, add artificial structure to the terrace.
* **The reef slope.** Beyond the terrace edge the reef slopes away through barrel, tube and elephant ear sponges and hard coral to sand near 40 m. The reef runs on north and south along the coast, so a dive can follow it either way and turn back.
* **The elkhorn point.** A stand of elkhorn coral grows in under 3 m of water close to shore off a rocky point about 90 m north of the resort pier. Elkhorn along the leeward coast has taken heavy losses since 2023.
* **The return.** The ramp lies 77 m east of the dive area, straight back across the terrace.

## Current

| **Current type** | Wind driven |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What sets its direction on a given day is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight or absent. At times a current runs along the reef, north or south, and it can run strong enough to stop a diver swimming against it. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and let it carry you back toward the ramp.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 259° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 256° |

The swim out runs 77 m west from the ramp, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** West-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), onshore over 7.6 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises waves on the ramp and stirs up the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over 1 to 1.5 km, and west (W), onshore over 1.4 km, each from Klein Bonaire across the channel, enough to build a chop at the ramp.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south (NNW-S), and west-southwest (WSW): offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, cross-shore over 210 m to 4.7 km from the north-northwest, north, south-southeast and south, and onshore over 990 m from the west-southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the ramp calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on a typical day, best in the calm early morning.
* **Wind.** Wind from west-northwest through northwest stirs up the sand and rubble of the terrace and cuts visibility in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Fish diversity.** Over 300 fish species recorded, the highest count of any surveyed site in the Caribbean. Schools of blue chromis, grunts, blue tang and yellowtail snapper fill the reef, with parrotfish, angelfish, butterflyfish, goatfish, trumpetfish and tiger grouper common.
* **Terrace and sand.** Yellowhead jawfish hover over their burrows in the rubble, peacock flounder lie on the sand, and hawksbill turtles forage among the coral heads.
* **The old pier.** French grunts and blue chromis school among the concrete.
* **Reef slope.** Barrel, tube and elephant ear sponges shelter frogfish, often found by looking twice at a yellow or orange sponge. Seahorses live on the reef and take searching to find. Green morays shelter in the coral, with barracuda off the slope and the odd spotted eagle ray passing along it. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Tarpon.** Tarpon patrol the terrace and slope, often approaching divers in the sandy shallows, most of all early and late in the day.
* **Night.** Morays swim free over the reef, octopus and spiny lobster come out to forage, and tarpon hunt in divers' lights.
* **Coral spawning.** Corals spawn here on a few nights after the full moon in late summer.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats pick up and moor at the resort pier, divers jump in from it, and boats launch from the ramp on the park's north side. Keep clear of the pier, stay down until back at the ramp, look up before surfacing, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Depth.** The slope below the terrace edge runs on toward 40 m, past the 34 m maximum operating depth of EAN32. Turn at a planned depth.
* **The ramp.** Its lower, grated metal section is slippery and its foot rocky; enter and exit slowly, in boots.
* **Current.** Usually slight, at times strong in either direction; keep enough gas to return to the ramp against it.
* **Debris and fire coral.** Old debris lies on the bottom in places, and fire coral grows in the shallows near the resort pier; keep hands and fins clear.
* **Reef balls.** The reef balls and the coral growing on them are fragile; keep fins and gauges clear of them.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW) puts waves on the ramp, and from south-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW) and west (W) a chop; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
