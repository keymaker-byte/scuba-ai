# T. Mi Dushi

Mi Dushi means my sweetheart in Papiamentu, a double reef site on Klein Bonaire's southwest side well suited to beginners and checkout dives. Staghorn and yellow pencil coral fill the shallows, home to smaller reef fish including yellow headed jawfish and sailfin blennies; the staghorn here has thinned along with the rest of Bonaire's since the 2023 SCTLD outbreak and the bleaching events that followed. This is currently one of the more reliable seahorse sites on the islet, with several regularly seen around 15 m, especially in the transition zone between the lagoon and the sand channel; a guide familiar with the site's reef zones is worth having on a first visit.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat, ideal for a checkout dive given the site's gentle double reef structure.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; a guide is recommended for a first visit to find the seahorses reliably. Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The transition zone.** Between the lagoon and the sand channel, the most reliable spot for seahorses.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The shallows hold staghorn and yellow pencil coral, with seahorses typically found around 15 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north (WSW-N), onshore over 5.7 km to more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises chop at the mooring.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest: offshore from east-northeast through south, off the islet, and cross-shore over 350 m to 4.8 km from the north-northeast, northeast and south-southwest.

## Visibility

Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m or more.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Staghorn and yellow pencil coral, thinned since 2023, home to yellow headed jawfish and sailfin blennies.
* **Transition zone.** Several seahorses regularly seen around 15 m.
