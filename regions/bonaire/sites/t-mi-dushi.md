# T. Mi Dushi

A boat dive on the west side of Klein Bonaire, also listed as Johanna's Revenge; Mi Dushi means my sweetheart in Papiamentu. A shallow terrace of yellow pencil coral, restored with outplanted staghorn in 2019, holds yellowhead jawfish and sailfin blennies and runs out at about 10 m past the mooring to a steep slope of plate, wire and black coral. Yellow frogfish are often found in the sponges under the mooring, and seahorses have been found around 15 m on the upper slope. Gentle conditions make it a common choice for checkout and training dives. The headline hazard is a wind driven current, usually mild in the lee of the islet, that can run moderate when the trades strengthen or shift.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by a dive operator's boat, about 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring on the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Starts at about 5 m along the shore, deepens to about 10 m within 60 m of it, and runs nearly flat at 10 m out past the mooring. Staghorn, yellow pencil coral and gorgonians cover it, and its shallow inner part serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The mooring.** Sits on the terrace at about 8 to 10 m. Frogfish are often found in the sponges directly beneath it, so many dives finish here.
* **The slope.** Falls steeply from the outer edge of the terrace in coral ridges and canyons, past 30 m. Seahorses have been found on its upper part around 15 m, and plate, wire and black coral take over deeper.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild in the lee of the islet, enough for a gentle drift along the reef, and can run moderate on a day of strong or shifting trades.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north (WSW-N), onshore over 5.7 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south: offshore from east-northeast through south, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 410 m to 4.9 km from the north-northeast and northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m and at times up to 40 m, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-southwest through north stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Staghorn and yellow pencil coral, with gorgonians and some elkhorn. Over 2,700 staghorn colonies from 17 genotypes were outplanted here in 2019 to restore the site's once thriving Acropora; Bonaire's staghorn has thinned since the bleaching and coral disease of 2023 and 2024.
* **Small fish.** Yellowhead jawfish and sailfin blennies on the terrace, with harlequin bass, slender filefish, scrawled filefish, black durgon and large trumpetfish. Reef squid hover over the shallows, and flamingo tongues and barnacle blennies sit on the coral.
* **Frogfish.** Yellow frogfish are often found in the sponges directly under the mooring.
* **Seahorses.** Found around 15 m on the upper slope; sightings come and go.
* **Reef.** Queen angelfish, schools of blue tang, blue parrotfish, goldentail and spotted morays, scorpionfish and coneys, with lobsters in the crevices.
* **Turtles.** Hawksbill turtles visit the reef.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Wind driven current is usually mild here, but can run moderate when the trades strengthen or shift and make the return to the mooring hard work; check the surface before entering.
* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the terrace past 30 m; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-southwest through north runs onto this shore over open water and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the south-southwest through southwest runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Several operators use the mooring, and boats making a live drop when it is taken work the water around it; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
