# 1. Boka Bartol

Bonaire's northernmost dive site, set in a remote bay inside Washington Slagbaai National Park. The shallow reef terrace carries large, unusual coral formations and a sandy bottom favoured by rays and garden eels, with open water beyond the shelf drawing larger fish and pelagics. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering the whole park.

| **Location** | Washington Slagbaai National Park, northwest coast, Bonaire |

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the south side of the bay, exiting at the cliff on the return. The shoreline is rock and coral rubble rather than sand.
* **Parking.** Beside the park road at the bay; unpaved.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's 50 km of unpaved road; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **South bay wall.** Entry runs along the south side of the bay onto the reef terrace before working out toward open water.
* **Exit point.** The cliff at the north side of the bay serves as the exit, different from the entry.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Boka Bartol carries a reputation for strong current even by the park's standards, part of why it is rated for advanced divers only. Current tends to run stronger the further the dive pushes off the terrace into open water.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The terrace sits around 5 m, dropping to the mid 20s on the upper slope; the surrounding seabed falls away fast beyond the shelf, reaching depths of 50 m and more within a few hundred metres of the terrace edge.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since May 2023; treat as not diveable until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Strong current.** Rated for advanced divers even when open, with current that can run hard off the terrace.
* **Remote entry.** No facilities, difficult road access, and a rock and rubble shore entry; a boat entry is the easier alternative when the site is open.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest, at the exposed northern tip of the leeward coast.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell here faster than at sites further down the sheltered west coast.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against the site's already strong current is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Large, unusual coral formations on the terrace, with a sandy bottom holding southern stingrays and garden eels.
* **Open water.** Larger fish species and deep water pelagics, including reports of manta rays, whale sharks and humpback whales further out over the drop-off.
