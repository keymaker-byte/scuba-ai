# 1. Boka Bartol

Bonaire's northernmost dive site, set in a remote bay inside Washington Slagbaai National Park. The shallow reef terrace carries large, unusual coral formations and a sandy bottom favoured by rays and garden eels, with open water beyond the shelf drawing larger fish and pelagics. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering this stretch of the park's coast.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the south side of the bay, exiting at the cliff on the return. The shoreline is rock and coral rubble.
* **Parking.** Beside the park road at the bay; unpaved.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's 50 km of unpaved road; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **South bay wall.** Entry runs along the south side of the bay onto the reef terrace before working out toward open water.
* **Exit point.** The cliff at the north side of the bay is the exit.
* **Depth profile.** The terrace sits around 5 m, dropping to the mid 20s on the upper slope; the surrounding seabed falls away fast beyond the shelf, reaching depths of 50 m and more within a few hundred metres of the terrace edge.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Current runs strong here, and stronger the further the dive pushes off the terrace into open water.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | northwest, 320° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northwest, 308° |

The bay opens northwest onto open sea at the exposed northern tip of the leeward coast, and the swim out runs 132 m north-northwest from the entry to the reef terrace.

* **Bad.** West through north-northeast (W-NNE), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, straight into the mouth of the bay. Any wind from this quarter builds swell quickly on the rock and rubble entry.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest (WSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through southwest: offshore from east through south-southwest, off the land, and cross-shore over 180 to 780 m from the northeast, east-northeast and southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the bay flat, though at strength they drive a stronger current off the terrace.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Large, unusual coral formations on the terrace, with a sandy bottom holding southern stingrays and garden eels.
* **Open water.** Larger fish species and deep water pelagics, with manta rays, whale sharks and humpback whales seen on occasion further out over the drop-off.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since 1 May 2023, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Strong current.** Rated for advanced divers even when open, with current that can run hard off the terrace.
* **Remote entry.** No facilities, difficult road access, and a rock and rubble shore entry; a boat entry is the easier alternative when the site is open.
