# 1. Boka Bartol

Bonaire's northernmost dive site, a remote rocky bay inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, opening northwest onto open sea a few kilometres from the island's exposed northeast coast. The reef starts right at the shore, with large, unusual coral formations on a shallow terrace, a sandy floor holding rays and garden eels, and a slope where barracuda, eagle rays and large groupers move along the reef. Surf on the rocky entry and a changeable current, from still to very strong, make it a site for experienced divers on a calm day, and diving here is suspended under STINAPA's closure of the park to diving.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the rocks on the south side of the bay, over loose stones, coral gravel and sharp coral, with waves breaking against the rocks even on a fair day. A second entry about 50 m along the shore toward the cliff on the north side of the bay often has calmer water and reaches the reef more directly, and is harder to find. Exit at the cliff on the north side, or back at the entry on the south side, where the way out runs through rocks and coral channels worth scouting from shore first.
* **Parking.** Informal, on the coral gravel beside the hard-packed dirt road along the coast, a short carry over rough, stony ground to the entry. Park on gravel, off the sand. No parking fee.
* **Access.** Closed to diving: STINAPA closed every site on the northern leeward coast on 1 May 2023 over Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease, reopened the coast south of the park in January 2024, and keeps Washington Slagbaai National Park closed to diving while the park stays open for every other activity. Confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive here. When open, the site lies about 24 km from Kralendijk, 30 to 45 minutes by unpaved road from the park entrance north of Rincon on the long (yellow) route. The park opens Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, closed on Mondays and holidays, with last entry at 14:30; it can close fully or in part after heavy rain. A high clearance 4x4 (about 30 cm) is required. The Nature Fee e-tag covers both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai. Fill up before entering the park; the nearest fuel is in Rincon.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Terrace.** The reef joins the shore directly, with coral gardens from about 5 to 8 m spreading across the terrace from the entry.
* **Sand.** The terrace gives onto a wide sandy floor from about 9 m, with garden eel colonies and rays between the coral blocks.
* **Slope.** Heading out from the south side, the bottom slopes down past coral heads below 30 m, continuing to about 40 m; the slope deepens gradually, so a diver following the bottom gets deep without noticing.
* **Exit.** The cliff on the north side of the bay marks the exit, and is the landmark to swim back to from the reef.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds, which run strongest from late morning into the afternoon. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Changeable, from still to very strong, and stronger the further the dive pushes off the terrace into open water. A rising current can carry divers past the planned exit; turn with enough gas to swim back against it.
* **Rip currents.** Water running back out to sea funnels through the channels between coral formations in the shallows, faster than it looks from the surface.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | northwest, 320° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northwest, 308° |

The bay opens northwest onto open sea, and the swim out runs 132 m north-northwest from the entry to the reef terrace.

* **Bad.** West through northeast (W-NE), over 230 m to more than 30 km. From west through north-northeast, onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, straight into the mouth of the bay, building surf on the rock and rubble entry. From the northeast, cross-shore over 230 m of the bay, a wind that drives swell around the island's northern tip and onto the entry, making the site unsafe.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest (WSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** East-northeast clockwise through southwest: offshore from east through south-southwest, off the land, and cross-shore over 180 to 780 m from the east-northeast and southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the bay at its calmest, with a small surf still breaking on the rocks, though at strength they drive a stronger current off the terrace. A day with the wind from the south or southeast suits the site best.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 10 to 30 m, typically around 25 m, best through the dry months from March to September and in the calm of the early morning.
* **Swell and rain.** North winds and swell from November to February stir the shallows, and rain from October to January washes sediment off the land.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm wetsuit suits a long dive; a dive skin or shorty is enough on a short one.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Large, unusual coral formations, sea fans and sponges, rarely dived and among the island's least disturbed. Trumpetfish, French and queen angelfish, parrotfish, and blue tang along the reef, with black durgon.
* **Sand.** Garden eel colonies, southern stingrays, peacock flounder and hermit crabs.
* **Slope.** Schools of barracuda along the reef, large groupers in the overhangs, green morays, eagle rays over the sand between coral blocks deeper down, and green and hawksbill turtles.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Washington Slagbaai National Park is closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD measures, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Entry surf.** Waves break on sharp rock and coral at the entry, and the site turns unsafe in north or northeast winds and winter swell. Wear boots with good grip, and choose another site when the surf is up.
* **Strong current.** Changeable, from still to very strong, with rip currents through the reef channels in the shallows. Carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Depth creep.** The slope deepens gradually past 30 m toward 40 m, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Remote.** Out of mobile phone range, with a long unpaved drive to help; leave a dive plan and an expected return time with someone outside the park, and finish diving with time to clear the park gate by 17:00.
