# 46. Aquarius

Aquarius is a double reef shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 1.7 km north of Salt Pier on Bonaire's southwest coast. A small sandy patch in a low, rocky shore makes an easy entry, onto a broad sand and coral terrace that a 152 m swim crosses to the top of the first reef. The first reef slopes down to a sand channel at about 35 m, and a second reef rises beyond it to a crest around 30 m. The headline hazard is depth on the crossing to the second reef: the sand between the reefs lies at about 35 m, past the maximum operating depth of EAN32, and out of sight of either reef.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk a few metres down the short slope from the road to the small sandy patch, where rocks mark the best way in; the shore either side is elkhorn rubble and rough rock. Enter on the sand, over scattered rocks and some uneven footing. Swim 152 m west, close to straight out from the shore, across the terrace to the top of the first reef. Exit at the same sandy patch.
* **Parking.** The paved shoulder of EEG Boulevard beside the yellow rocks, free. The ground between the road and the shore can flood after heavy rain.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry.** The sandy patch lies just below the parking shoulder. Note the shore around it on the way out: either side lie elkhorn rubble and rough rock.
* **The terrace.** Sand and hard pan with scattered coral heads, shelving from about 4 m deep just off the waterline to about 5 m at 40 m out, 8 m at 80 m, and 10 m at 110 m. It is worth swimming underwater, and it serves as the safety stop on the way back.
* **The first reef.** The dive area, 152 m west of the entry, sits on the reef top at about 10.5 m, uneven ground running from about 7 to 15 m within 25 m. The reef carries on along the coast in both directions and slopes away seaward down to the sand channel. Along the reef, head north or south into the current.
* **The sand channel.** At about 35 m between the two reefs, with garden eels on the sand. Crossing it takes several minutes, with a long stretch out of sight of either reef; hold a compass heading out and back. To the north the two reefs nearly merge; to the south the channel runs deeper and wider, and the swim back from there is longer.
* **The second reef.** Rises beyond the channel to a crest around 30 m, then drops past 40 m on its seaward side. It shows from the first reef only on clear days.
* **The return.** Come back up the first reef onto the terrace and head east, 152 m to the sandy patch.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs north or south along the reef and can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Light to moderate along the reef. Check its direction before entering, and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 253° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 253° |

The swim out runs 152 m west from the entry, 6° off the shore's facing, close to straight out.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the shore, stirs up the shallows and puts surge on the entry and exit at the sandy patch.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast (NNW-SSE): offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 390 m to 1.0 km from the north-northwest, north and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m on the reef, best on calm mornings.
* **The shallows.** The terrace near the shore can turn murky and silty. Waves breaking on the shore from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) cut visibility in the shallows, while the reef further out stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Turtles in the shallows around 6 m, and spotted eagle rays feeding in the sand. Sand tilefish, flounder and juvenile spotted drum on the sand and rubble, flamingo tongues on the sea fans, and at times horse-eye jacks near the entry.
* **First reef.** Many schooling species, among them large schools of blue tang, snapper and doctorfish, with barred hamlets, queen and French angelfish, rock beauties, trumpetfish, coronetfish and large rainbow parrotfish. Barracuda patrol the reef.
* **Macro.** Green morays, often free swimming, spotted morays, octopus, spiny lobster and spotted cleaner shrimp.
* **Sand and second reef.** Rays and garden eels on the sand between the reefs. Groupers, barracuda, turtles and eagle rays along the second reef.
* **Coral.** Mostly hard coral on both reefs, with soft corals and anemones on the terrace. The second reef has taken damage, and large formations there are sparse. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef, down to the sand.

## Hazards

* **Depth.** The first reef runs down to the sand channel at about 35 m, below EAN32's 34 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4. Cross to the second reef only on a planned depth of 30 m or shallower, swimming above the sand, and keep within the no-decompression limit, which is short at that depth.
* **Navigation.** The crossing to the second reef runs out of sight of both reefs. Hold a compass heading out and back, and when unsure of the way home, climb the first reef onto the terrace before surfacing.
* **Gas.** The 152 m swim each way, plus the crossing to the second reef, uses a large share of a cylinder. Turn on gas with the swim home over the terrace still to come.
* **The exit.** Missing the sandy patch lands on elkhorn rubble and rough rock. Take a bearing back to it before leaving the terrace.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind or swell from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) breaks on the shore and makes the entry and exit rough; choose another site when any of these blows fresh or a swell is running.
* **Boats.** Dive boats visit. Ascend with a surface marker buoy.
