# 28. Reef Scientifico

A stretch of fringing reef on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, lying between the Buddy Dive Resort pier and the Captain Don's Habitat pier and reached from either. It is named for a grid built on the reef to study algae growth, and remains of the grid still lie among the coral. A shallow sand and rubble terrace runs out from the pier to the top of the reef slope, where coral restoration trees stand at about 6 m, and the slope continues down to sand at about 40 m. The site is at its best at night, when free swimming morays hunt over the reef. The headline hazard is the slope below the dive area, which leads well past the maximum operating depth of EAN32.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Through Buddy Dive Resort to its pier, down one of the two ladders into about 4.5 m of water. From the pier the dive area lies 134 m west-northwest, out across the terrace and north along the reef slope. Exit by the same ladders. The site can also be reached from the end of the Captain Don's Habitat divers-only pier, about 125 m north-northeast of the dive area, by swimming south along the reef.
* **Parking.** In the Buddy Dive Resort lot beside the coastal road, about 110 m from the pier, free and open to divers who are not hotel guests.
* **Access.** On private resort property, open to divers around the clock. Divers who are not hotel guests check in at the Buddy Dive shop before gearing up. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** Benches on the pier for kitting up, rinse tanks, showers, changing rooms and lockers.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pier.** The resort's pier stands in about 4.5 m of water, with guide ropes running from it out toward the reef.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble with scattered coral heads, holding at 4 to 5 m for the first 45 m out from the pier.
* **The restoration trees.** Coral restoration trees and platforms hung with staghorn fragments stand along the top of the reef slope at about 6 m, north and south of the pier.
* **The research grid.** Remains of the algae research grid the site is named for lie on the reef.
* **The reef slope.** Past the terrace the reef slopes away from about 6 m, reaching 9 m some 80 m out from the pier and about 13 m at the dive area, and runs on down to sand at about 40 m. Follow the slope north at 9 to 13 m from the pier to reach the dive area.
* **The upturned boat.** A small fishing boat wreck lies upside down at about 14 to 15 m, about 80 m north-northwest of the dive area, marking the north end of the site.
* **The return.** The Buddy Dive pier lies 134 m east-southeast of the dive area; follow the reef slope back south and cross the terrace to the ladders.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight, and occasionally strong along the reef. Start the dive into whatever current is running and let it carry you back toward the pier.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 246° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 245° |

The swim out runs 134 m west-northwest from the pier, angled north along the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South (S), onshore over 5.4 km down the coast, and west through west-northwest (W-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises waves at the pier and stirs up the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), onshore over 1.1 to 1.3 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the pier.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over 6.6 km, running a swell along the shore past the pier.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 260 to 660 m from the north-northwest, southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the pier calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on a typical day, best on calm mornings.
* **Wind.** Wind from south through northwest stirs up the sandy terrace and cuts visibility in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Green morays shelter in the reef and parrotfish are common across it. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Restoration.** Staghorn fragments grow on the restoration trees and platforms along the top of the reef slope, part of the island's coral restoration effort.
* **Night.** Free swimming morays hunt over the reef, parrotfish sleep in their mucus cocoons among the coral, and tarpon at times hunt in divers' lights.

## Hazards

* **Depth.** The slope below the dive area leads on to sand at about 40 m, below the maximum operating depth of EAN32 and outside a no-decompression plan. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats run in and out beside both resorts' piers, the Buddy Dive pier at the entry and the Captain Don's Habitat pier at the north end of the site. Stay down until back at the ladders, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Restoration structures.** The trees and platforms at the top of the reef slope are fragile; keep fins and gauges clear of them.
* **Current.** Usually slight, and occasionally strong; keep enough gas to return to the pier against it.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from south (S) and from west through west-northwest (W-WNW) puts waves on the pier, from south-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW) a chop, and from the northwest (NW) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
