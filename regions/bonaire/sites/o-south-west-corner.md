# O. South West Corner

A boat dive off Klein Bonaire's southwest tip, where the islet's south and west shores meet. A shallow terrace of low, compact coral, sea fans, sea rods and sea plumes runs out from the shore past the mooring to a sloping reef wall, with black durgon and yellowtail snapper in numbers and hawksbill and loggerhead turtles passing along the reef. The coral grows low and tidy against the water movement, and the headline hazard is a current that builds toward the corner and can run strong.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace, with the edge of the slope 20 to 50 m farther out.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring on the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Coral, sea fans and sand shelving from about 4 m near the shore to about 9 to 10 m at its outer edge, with the mooring about 100 m out. Coral grows densest in the shallows close to the shore, out of the strongest current, where the shallow end serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive and as a snorkel site.
* **The wall.** A gently sloping reef wall begins at the outer edge of the terrace and falls to about 37 m.
* **The corner.** The reef turns here from the south shore onto the west shore, and the current gathers around the point.

## Current

|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | Not established |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Ranges from mild to strong. Currents meet around the point and run strongest there; the shallows close to the shore sit out of the strongest of it.
* **Drift.** A common plan starts on the reef to the east and drifts west along the south shore onto the corner.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 304° |

The facing averages the curve of the tip; the nearest shore lies about 100 m to the east-northeast of the mooring.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north (WSW-N), onshore over 7 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south: cross-shore over 120 to 190 m from the north-northeast and northeast, and offshore from east-northeast through south, blowing off the islet itself.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 15 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-southwest through north, or a swell from the south-southwest through southwest, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Black durgon and yellowtail snapper in numbers, with groupers, doctorfish, parrotfish, squirrelfish and shrimp among the coral.
* **Terrace.** Sea fans, sea rods and sea plumes host cleaning stations where tangs shelter from the current.
* **Turtles.** Hawksbill and loggerhead turtles pass along the reef.
* **Blue water.** Barracuda and jacks off the wall, with spotted eagle rays at times.

## Hazards

* **Current.** The current builds toward the point and can run strong; plan the dive with it, keep the shallows along the shore as the way out of it, and carry a surface marker buoy for a pickup away from the mooring.
* **The wall.** The slope falls from the terrace edge at about 9 to 10 m to about 37 m, below the 30 m floor of the dived range; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-southwest through north runs onto this shore and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the south-southwest through southwest runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a diver surfacing away from the mooring raises a surface marker buoy.
