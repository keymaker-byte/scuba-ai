# 15. Bon Bini na Kas

A boat dive on Bonaire's northwest coast below a large overhanging cliff, its name Papiamentu for welcome home. The overhang forms a complex of caves along the shore, and from its foot a shallow terrace of sea fans, staghorn and lavender stovepipe sponges slopes gently out to about 10.5 m before the reef runs on down to 30 m and beyond. In the usual east trades the water is calm, and with its gentle profile and slight current it makes a relaxed dive for newer divers. The headline hazards are boat traffic over the site and the clear water drawing divers deeper than planned down the slope.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or backward roll from a boat tied to the site mooring, which sits over the terrace at about 6 m.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** By boat from Kralendijk with a local dive operator. The overhanging cliff along the shore offers no way down to the water. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Cliff and cave complex.** A large overhanging cliff runs along the shore, and the undercut at its foot forms a complex of caves at the waterline, the site's defining feature.
* **Shallow terrace.** From about 4 m at the foot of the cliff the bottom slopes gently out, passing 6 m about 20 m off the cliff and reaching about 10.5 m some 40 to 50 m out.
* **Slope.** Past the terrace the reef continues down in a gradual slope of sand and coral, through 30 m and on past 35 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trades. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight over the gently sloping bottom.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 193° |

The shore here runs west-northwest to east-southeast below the cliff, with the nearest shore 61 m north-northeast of the dive area.

* **Bad.** Southeast through west-southwest (SE-WSW), onshore over 5.1 km to more than 30 km, along the coast from the southeast and south-southeast or across open sea from the south through west-southwest. Wind from this quarter raises chop and swell at the mooring and against the cliff.
* **Mixed.** West (W), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open sea, running a swell along the shore past the mooring.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from northwest through east-northeast, off the cliff, and cross-shore over 630 m to 1.4 km from the west-northwest, east and east-southeast. The prevailing east trades run along the shore over a short fetch and leave the site calm at their usual strength; trades veering southeast fall in the Bad sector.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m and sometimes more, best on calm mornings. The water is clear enough to make depth hard to judge on the slope.
* **Swell.** Wind from the southeast through west-southwest, and swell running along the shore on a west wind, stir the shallows below the cliff and cut visibility there; the water clears over the slope.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Shallow terrace.** Sea fans and soft corals sway over the terrace, with schools of blue tang and of creole and bluehead wrasse common. Staghorn colonies grow on the shelf, thinned like the rest of Bonaire's staghorn by the 2023 and 2024 bleaching events, among lavender stovepipe sponges in abundance.
* **Slope.** Hard corals cover much of the slope, with Christmas tree worms on the coral heads and glass gobies resting on them. Morays, trumpetfish, pufferfish and boxfish shelter in the reef, and turtles pass along it.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats use the mooring and other boats run along this coast; carry a surface marker buoy and ascend along the mooring line or the reef.
* **Depth.** The gradual slope and the clear water make it easy to drift deeper than planned; watch the computer on the way down the slope.
* **Swell.** Wind from the southeast through west-southwest puts chop and swell on the mooring under the cliff and against the caves along the shore; choose another site when it is running.
* **Current.** The wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; start the dive into it and keep enough gas to return to the mooring.
