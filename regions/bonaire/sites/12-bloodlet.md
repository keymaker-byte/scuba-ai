# 12. Bloodlet

A boat dive on Bonaire's northwest coast below the low rock shore of the coastal road, where a shallow sand and coral terrace runs out to a reef that drops steeply into deep water. The site takes its name from its shore entry, rough enough over the rock to draw blood, and is dived from a boat. Dense reef on the slope shelters schools of blue tang among yellow and green tube sponges, and turtles are often seen. The headline hazards are a current that runs strong at times and surge across the shallow terrace in swell, whether raised by a south or west wind or arriving from winter storms and passing tropical systems.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride from a boat tied to a public mooring above the terrace, then a descent onto the terrace or straight over its edge onto the slope.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** Dived by boat, a regular stop on the resort boats' near north itineraries. The shore below the coastal road is jagged rock, and a shore entry over it is tricky, often painful, and best left alone. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Terrace.** A shallow plateau of sand and coral runs out from the rock shore, starting about 4 m deep and sloping gently to about 7 m at its outer edge, about 50 to 60 m off the shore. Near that edge the terrace reads 6.6 m, about 54 m off the nearest shore.
* **Slope.** Past the edge of the terrace the reef runs steeply down, densely grown with hard coral and sponges, past 25 m and on into deep water.
* **Route.** Drop over the edge and work along the slope at depth, then come back shallower over the terrace to find the mooring for the exit.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trades. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Runs along the coast, usually light and at times strong.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 208° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 6.1 km to more than 30 km, down the coast from the south-southeast and across open sea from the south and west. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the rock shore, surges the shallow terrace, sets the boat rocking at its mooring and makes reboarding rough.
* **Mixed.** Southeast (SE), cross-shore over 9.8 km down the coast, running a swell along the shore.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: cross-shore over 760 m from the west-northwest and 310 m from the northwest, offshore from north-northwest through east over 50 to 110 m, off the land, and cross-shore over 3.5 km from the east-southeast. Trades from the east blow off the land and leave the mooring sheltered; as they swing southeast they run a swell along the shore. Swell in winter and from passing tropical systems still surges the terrace under a light wind.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 20 to 30 m or more.
* **Wind and swell.** Best on calm mornings, lower when wind and swell stir the terrace, as with northern swell in winter and swell from passing tropical systems from June to November.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Dense coral growth on the slope, with plate corals, sea fans, and yellow and green tube sponges throughout. Brain corals, hit hard by SCTLD across the island, are now scarce.
* **Reef fish.** Schools of blue tang graze the reef, among angelfish, parrotfish and snapper.
* **Larger animals.** Sea turtles are often seen. Barracuda patrol the slope, nurse sharks rest under the coral, stingrays lie on the sand, and the odd eagle ray passes along the slope.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Usually light, and strong at times. Check the set from the mooring line before descending, start the dive into the current, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Surge on the terrace.** The shallow terrace surges in swell; keep buoyancy tight and keep clear of the coral.
* **Boat at the mooring.** The boat rocks at its mooring in a south or west wind or swell, and reboarding can be rough. Brief boat positioning and the ladder exit before entering.
* **Rock shore.** The shore is jagged rock, a painful scramble out of the water; every dive ends at the boat.
* **Depth.** The slope drops past 25 m right off the terrace, and depth runs up quickly along it. Set a depth limit before descending and stay well inside the NDL.
* **Stony coral tissue loss disease (SCTLD).** Disinfect gear before, between and after every dive per STINAPA's protocol, and keep clear of the coral.
