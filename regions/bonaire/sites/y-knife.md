# Y. Knife

A boat dive on the north side of Klein Bonaire, named for its knife-like coral formations. A shallow terrace of coral rubble and sand runs about 100 m out from the shore, grazed by large midnight and rainbow parrotfish, to a reef edge at about 8 to 10 m. From there a reef slope of coral heads of varied heights, with sea rods and sea plumes near the drop-off, falls on past recreational depth. Green morays shelter in the crevices of the formations, and French and queen angelfish and trumpetfish work the reef. The headline hazards are the drop past the terrace edge, a wind driven current that is usually slight but strong on some days, and wind from the west-northwest round to the northeast running onto this shore.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by boat, usually a dive operator's, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Snorkelers also reach it on foot, a long walk of about 900 m across the islet from No Name Beach, where the water taxi lands. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Coral rubble and sand with algae patches, about 4 m deep near the shore and shelving gently to about 6 to 7 m, running about 100 m out to the reef edge. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The reef edge.** The terrace ends at about 8 to 10 m, where the bottom drops away.
* **The knife formations.** Coral formations rising from the reef in sharp, blade-like shapes, their crevices sheltering morays.
* **The slope.** Coral heads of varied heights, with sea rods and sea plumes near the drop-off, falling on past 40 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds, which blow along this shore from the east and off the islet from the southeast. A north wind brings more water movement to this shore. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight over the terrace and along the slope, and strong on some days.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | north-northwest, 344° |

* **Bad.** West-northwest through north-northwest (WNW-NNW), onshore over 5.4 to more than 30 km of open water, putting chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** North through northeast (N-NE), onshore over 2.6 to 4 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** East-northeast clockwise through southwest: cross-shore over 2.5 to 2.8 km from the east-northeast and east, and offshore from east-southeast through southwest, blowing off the islet itself.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m year round, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the north or northwest stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The terrace.** Large midnight and rainbow parrotfish grazing the algae patches over the rubble and sand, with stoplight and princess parrotfish, cowfish and wrasses.
* **The formations.** Green morays in the crevices of the knife formations.
* **The slope.** Coral heads of varied heights sheltering many small, hidden creatures, orange sponges, and sea rods and sea plumes near the drop-off, with octopus among the coral heads. French and queen angelfish and trumpetfish move between the formations, and brown seahorses are found at times.
* **Off the drop-off.** Horse-eye jacks and reef squid, with spotted eagle rays passing at times.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The bottom falls away from the terrace edge at about 8 to 10 m and runs on past recreational depth; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Current.** Wind driven current is usually slight here but runs strong on some days; check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the north through northeast runs onto this shore across the channel from Bonaire and puts chop at the mooring; a wind from the west-northwest through north-northwest brings open water chop and surge over the terrace.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
