# 53. Pink Beach

Pink Beach is a shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 1.9 km south of Salt Pier on Bonaire's southwest coast, off a long, narrow beach once known for sand tinted pink by the ground shells of foraminifera; Hurricane Lenny in 1999 stripped most of that sand, and the shore is now coral rubble with patches of sand. From the north end of the beach, a wide, shallow sand and coral terrace with a restored staghorn patch runs about 250 m out to a reef slope of soft coral and sponges that reaches sand at about 30 m. The headline hazards are the long surface swim and a current that changes from day to day, from still water to strong.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk about 25 m from the road to the water at the north end of the beach, in line with the northernmost palm trees. The shore is coral rubble and ironshore with sandy channels through it; pick a channel, wade in over sand and rubble to about knee depth, and put fins on there. Swim 159 m west, straight out from the shore, to the dive area on the outer terrace, and on another 100 m to the top of the reef slope. Exit at the same channel.
* **Parking.** Free, beside EEG Boulevard. Keep the car on the coral rubble and off the sand, where turtles nest.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit, from the site's mooring.
* **Facilities.** None at the site. A few palm trees give some shade.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry.** A gate beside EEG Boulevard leads to the north end of the beach. The site's mooring lies south of the entry, across a wide stretch of sand.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral shelve from the waterline to about 4 m, hold there for the first 30 m, then slope gently to about 6 m at 70 m out, 8 m at 90 m and 10 m at 120 m. A large staghorn patch begins at about 6 m. Past the dive area, 159 m out at about 10 m, the bottom rolls between 11 and 15 m over coral heads for about 100 m. The terrace serves as the safety stop on the way back.
* **The reef slope.** Begins at the outer edge of the terrace, about 250 m west of the entry, and slopes down through soft coral, sea rods and large sponges to sand at about 30 m, with deeper water beyond.
* **The return.** Come back up the slope onto the terrace and head east, 159 m from the dive area to the entry. The wide sand of the terrace makes the entry hard to find; take a bearing on the way out.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs north or south along the shore, ranging from still to strong from one day to the next, and strengthens when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Weakest in the shallows of the terrace and strongest along the reef slope and the drop-off. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and drift back to the entry.
* **To the south.** Toward the point about 1.2 km south the current turns swirling and stronger, and the shore there offers a poor exit. Keep the dive north of it and the return toward the beach.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 279° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 279° |

The swim out runs 159 m west from the entry, 1° off the shore's facing, straight out.

* **Bad.** Southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW), onshore over 10.2 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the rubble shore, stirs up the shallows and puts surf on the entry and exit.
* **Mixed.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry. A strong south wind builds breaking surf on the entry and exit.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast (N-SSE): offshore from northeast through south-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 2.5 km from the north and 820 m from the north-northeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on the reef, typically about 20 m, and much the same year round. Best on calm mornings.
* **The shallows.** Waves breaking on the shore from southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW) stir up the terrace near the entry and cut visibility there.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Southern stingrays and bonefish along the sandy bottom, flounder and sand tilefish, turtles grazing, and juvenile reef fish on the swim out. Spotted eagle rays pass over the sand, most often early in the morning and late in the afternoon.
* **Staghorn patch.** A restored staghorn thicket of about 490 m², grown from 1,250 nursery colonies outplanted by Reef Renewal Foundation Bonaire, holds more fish and more live hard coral than the bare rubble beside it. Staghorn and elkhorn along the whole leeward coast have suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Reef slope.** A forest of soft coral, sea fans and sea rods with barrel and tube sponges, home to queen and French angelfish, parrotfish, wrasses, blue tang, trumpetfish, honeycomb cowfish and filefish. Cleaning stations among the coral heads service larger fish and morays. Spotted and green morays, lobster, octopus and squid in the reef, schools of small barracuda along the slope, and tarpon passing.
* **Sand.** Garden eels in the sand at the foot of the slope.
* **Open water.** Dolphins pass offshore on occasion.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **The surface swim.** About 250 m from the entry to the reef slope, and the same back. Swim out on the surface or over the terrace, and turn the dive with enough gas left for the 250 m swim home.
* **Current.** Can run strong along the slope on any day; start into it so the return runs with it, and carry a surface marker buoy for a drift. Toward the point to the south it turns swirling and harder to swim against.
* **Finding the exit.** The wide sand of the terrace and the long rubble shore make the entry hard to find from the water. Take a bearing back to it before leaving the terrace, and at night mark it with a light, since the shore is dark.
* **The entry and exit.** Coral rubble and ironshore lie between the sandy channels. Wear sturdy boots and take the channel back out.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind from southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW), and a strong south wind, drive surf onto the shore and make the entry and exit rough; choose another site when it blows fresh from that quarter or a swell is running.
* **Depth.** The reef slope reaches sand at about 30 m, close to EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar, and the bottom keeps falling beyond it. Watch the computer and keep within the no-decompression limit.
* **Boats.** Dive boats use the mooring and pleasure boats pass the site. Ascend with a surface marker buoy.
