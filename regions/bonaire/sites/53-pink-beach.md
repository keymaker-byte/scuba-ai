# 53. Pink Beach

Just off one of Bonaire's most famous beaches, its pink tinge coming from foraminifera, microscopic shelled organisms ground to powder by the waves, though Hurricane Lenny in 1999 washed away much of the original colour. Southern stingrays and bonefish work the shallow sandy bottom, especially in the early morning and late afternoon, and the current here can run stronger than the more sheltered sites further north.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A shore entry off the beach.
* **Parking.** Roadside at Pink Beach.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach itself.** A landmark in its own right, one of the island's best known.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Current here can run stronger than the sheltered sites further up the coast; a logged dive found it slack, but treat that as one day's reading rather than the norm.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The shallow sandy bottom in front of the beach leads through the recreational range to 30 m; a logged dive reached 24.5 m.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Can run stronger here than at more sheltered sites; check conditions before committing to the full dive.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 279° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 278° |

The swim out runs 247 m southwest from the entry, along the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** Southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW), onshore over 10.4 km to more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the entry.
* **Mixed.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from northeast through south-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 750 m to 2.8 km from the north and north-northeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast; a logged dive rated it 5 out of 5 (excellent).

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October; a logged November dive measured 29 C. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Sandy shallows.** Southern stingrays and bonefish, best early morning and late afternoon.
