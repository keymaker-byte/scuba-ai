# 18. Jeff Davis Memorial

A shore dive on Bonaire's northwest coast road north of Kralendijk, named for Jefferson C. Davis, MD, an Air Force flight surgeon and pioneer of hyperbaric medicine, to whom the island dedicated the reef in 1990. A short scramble over rock and a 1 m drop from the coral edge lead to a small sand beach, and the water opens onto a gorgonian-covered terrace that deepens from about 5 to 10 m before large coral formations form chutes down the slope to sand. The terrace holds one of Reef Renewal Foundation Bonaire's largest staghorn restoration sites, and the slope is known for its many cleaning stations. The headline hazard is the entry, a rough step down over rock that turns difficult in any swell.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A walk of about 30 m over rough coral rock and limestone leads from the road to a coral edge about 1 m above a small sand beach. Stacked stones form a rough step down. Lower the gear to a buddy standing on the beach, climb down without it, then gear up and walk out over the sand.
* **Parking.** Free, roadside beside the yellow marker stone, with room for about four or five cars.
* **Access.** Open, with the STINAPA Nature Fee required in the water. Dive boats also visit the site.
* **Facilities.** None. The only shade is from thorny divi divi trees over rocky ground.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The memorial.** A memorial marker to Dr. Davis stands beside the road, alongside the yellow stone that marks the site.
* **Terrace.** Gorgonians, soft coral and coral heads over sand and rubble, at about 5 m just off the beach and 8 m some 40 m out. The dive area lies 87 m from the entry to the southwest, 216°, at 9.2 m, where the terrace ends.
* **Staghorn restoration.** A thicket of outplanted staghorn coral on the terrace, grown since 2015 over what was a patch of sand and rubble of about 1,000 m², and accessible to snorkelers.
* **Coral chutes.** Beyond the terrace edge the reef drops steeply; large coral formations along the slope form chutes that channel down to a sandy bottom.
* **Cleaning stations.** Spread from the terrace edge down the slope, mostly between 8 and 20 m.
* **The exit.** The beach is a short gap in a rocky shore; note its position before descending and return to it on the same bearing, 36°.

* **Drift.** Usually limited, at most a light to moderate drift along the reef.
* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 214° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 214° |

The swim out runs 87 m southwest from the entry, 2° off the entry shore's facing, straight out from the beach.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through west (SSW-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises swell on the beach and breaks it against the coral edge, which makes the step up and down dangerous.
* **Short fetch.** South-southeast through south (SSE-S), onshore over 4.1 to 4.7 km from the direction of Klein Bonaire and the town shore, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the land, and cross-shore over 350 m to 4.0 km from the west-northwest, northwest, east-southeast and southeast, where the coast to either side cuts the fetch short.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m along this coast, often near 30 m, and clearest on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Waves on the beach stir sand in the shallows and cut visibility near the entry.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds an even temperature through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Dense gorgonians and soft coral, with turtles and seahorses among them, and fish and invertebrates sheltering in the staghorn thicket.
* **Staghorn.** The outplanted staghorn first spawned in 2017 and spawns in August. Staghorn along Bonaire's leeward coast has taken widespread partial mortality from disease and bleaching since 2023.
* **Cleaning stations.** Cleaner shrimp and gobies working larger reef fish from the terrace edge down the slope.
* **Reef fish.** Parrotfish, French and queen angelfish, trumpetfish, spotted drum, filefish and puffers, with reef squid over the reef.
* **Morays and crevices.** Spotted and other morays, spiny lobster and scorpionfish.
* **Slope and sand.** Azure vase and orange elephant ear sponges on the drop-off, and southern stingrays on the sand.

## Hazards

* **Entry.** The 1 m step from the coral edge to the beach and the rough rock above it; wear sturdy boots, and hand gear down to a buddy on the beach. Swell from the south or west breaks against the edge and makes the entry and exit dangerous; skip the site when waves break on the beach.
* **Missing the exit.** The beach is the only easy way out along a stretch of rocky shore; surface and locate the gap in the rocks before swimming in.
* **Depth on the slope.** The reef drops steeply past recreational depth beyond the terrace edge; watch depth and no-decompression time.
* **Vehicle break-ins.** Cars at this roadside parking have been broken into; leave nothing of value in the car.
* **Sun and thorns.** The parking and the rocks sit in near full sun, and the divi divi and scrub carry thorns.
