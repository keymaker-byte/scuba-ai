# K. Capt. Don's Reef

A boat dive on the south coast of Klein Bonaire, named for Don Stewart, Bonaire's diving pioneer, whose plaque lies at the mooring on the edge of the drop-off. A flat shallow terrace runs about 100 m out from a rocky shore to an edge at about 10 m where the reef wall falls in a series of steps, cut by vertical ridges and sand channels, through the recreational range to sand far below. Black durgon school along the edge and turtles graze the shallows. Current is usually mild to moderate and can run strong; the headline hazards are that current and how quickly the wall gains depth.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring at the edge of the drop-off, then a descent along the mooring line to the plaque.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring on the drop-off edge.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The plaque.** At the foot of the mooring, about 10 m, placed in 1987 and dedicated to Don Stewart for his work preserving Bonaire's reefs. Stewart introduced the permanent mooring system that protects the island's reefs and led the campaign that banned spearfishing and tropical fish collecting on Bonaire.
* **The terrace.** A flat area of sand and coral between the shore and the mooring, shoaling from about 10 m at the edge to about 4 m near the rocks. It serves as the safety stop and the last part of the dive, and as a snorkel site.
* **The wall.** Begins at the mooring and drops in a series of steps that grow less steep with depth, cut by vertical ridges and sandy channels that run on down to a sand floor at about 46 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild to moderate, and at times strong enough to push a diver around in the shallows.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 181° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west-southwest (SSE-WSW), onshore over 10.1 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** Southeast (SE), onshore over 4.3 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from northwest through east-northeast, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 220 to 980 m from the west, west-northwest and east, and 4.4 km from the east-southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly about 30 m, at its best on calm mornings. A swell from the south through west stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest from August to October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Drop-off edge.** Schools of black durgon and smaller reef fish hold along the edge around the mooring.
* **Wall.** Hard and soft corals on the steps, with crabs, lobsters and morays sheltering in the ridges and channels.
* **Terrace.** Green turtles graze the shallows.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The wall begins right at the mooring and keeps falling past 30 m to sand at about 46 m; watch the computer on the descent and along the wall.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen or shift and can turn the dive into hard work; check the surface before entering, start the dive into the current, and stay close to a buddy.
* **Swell and surge.** A wind from south-southeast through west-southwest, or a swell from the south through west, runs onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow terrace.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
