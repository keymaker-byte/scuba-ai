# 8. Carel's Vision

A remote site on Bonaire's northwest coast, between the BOPEC oil terminal and Nukove, named for Carel Steensma, a Dutch resistance fighter of the Second World War who helped found Bonaire National Marine Park, and opened as a dive site in 2005. A short footpath from the dirt road leads down to a pebble beach, and from there a shallow sandy shelf runs out to a reef terrace at 9 to 12 m and a slope past 30 m, where black coral grows among plate, star and boulder corals that grew back after Hurricane Lenny wrecked the reef top in 1999. The shore entry is unmarked from the road. The headline hazard is a westward current that can run strong off a coast with few places to get out.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the north side of the pebble beach, where the fewest obstacles lie underwater. The clearest way out to the deeper reef runs directly behind the large rock off the beach. The beach is pebbles and small stones, slippery when wet, so boots with a firm sole are essential. Exit by the same route.
* **Parking.** A small spot for a car at right angles to the dirt road, with the footpath to the beach starting a few metres back along the road. Parking is free.
* **Access.** The site is formally a boat site, and the shore entry is reached by the footpath below. Follow the coastal road north past Karpata to the BOPEC terminal, take the unpaved road around the terminal toward Playa Frans, and continue past the saliña near Tailor Made and Candyland; the parking spot comes before the Nukove turnoff. The road is unpaved and rough, and a high-clearance vehicle suits it. The footpath from the road to the beach is narrow, unpaved, overgrown and unmarked from the road. The site lies outside both of the marine park's reserves and outside Washington Slagbaai National Park; the Nature Fee e-tag is required. STINAPA closed every site north of Karpata on 1 May 2023 over Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD), and reopened this stretch of coast, between BOPEC and Washington Slagbaai National Park, on 27 January 2024. The site is absent from STINAPA's dive map; confirm with STINAPA that it is open before planning a dive.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Pebble beach.** A small beach of pebbles and stones below the road, facing west-southwest, with a large rock just off it.
* **Shallow shelf.** Sand at 4.5 to 6 m for about 60 m from the beach, a good area for the safety stop.
* **Reef edge.** Between 60 and 80 m out the bottom drops to the reef terrace at 9 m, where the dive area coordinate sits 79 m from the entry.
* **Terrace.** Coral at 9 to 12 m for about 70 m further out, before the slope.
* **Slope.** Past the terrace the reef drops toward 30 m and beyond, with the black coral deeper down.
* **Return.** Swim back east-northeast, 63°, across the terrace and shelf to the north side of the beach; the entry sits 79 m from the dive area.

* **Character.** Moderate on most days, and strong on some. A strong westward set carries a diver away from the beach, and there are few places along this coast to get out of the water, so check the current at the start of the dive and plan to return to the entry.
* **Drivers.** Not established for this site.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 242° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 244° |

The swim out runs 79 m west-southwest, 243°, from the entry straight out across the shelf to the reef edge.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter puts surf on the pebble beach and makes the entry and exit hazardous.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast (SSE), and northwest through north-northwest (NW-NNW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast over 70 m to 1.4 km, and cross-shore over 150 m from the southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land or along a short stretch of shore here and leave the beach flat. Swell from the north or west, from winter storms or passing tropical systems, still makes the entry harder under a light wind.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 10 to 30 m, typically around 25 m, best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** Lower when wind and swell stir the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard, and boots guard against the pebbles and stones of the entry.

## Marine life

* **Coral.** Hurricane Lenny badly damaged the reef top in 1999. The coral has grown back since, with a different structure: black coral is the most prominent, along with plate, star and boulder corals. The site lay inside the 2023 Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease outbreak zone, which hit boulder and brain corals hardest across Bonaire.
* **Fish.** Triggerfish, parrotfish and juvenile reef fish, with filefish and lionfish.
* **Invertebrates and rays.** Large spiny lobsters, shrimp and sponges in the reef, with stingrays and snake eels on the sand.

## Hazards

* **Current.** A westward current can run strong, and this coast has few places to exit; carry a surface marker buoy and turn back toward the entry early if the current builds.
* **Finding the entry.** The footpath and beach are unmarked and hard to find from the road on a first visit; use the parking and entry coordinates.
* **Entry and exit.** Pebbles and stones at the waterline are slippery and uneven, and turn hazardous with any surf on the beach. Choose another site when there is surf.
* **Remote.** The site lies outside mobile phone coverage, with help a long, rough drive away. Leave a dive plan and an expected return time with someone, save the parking coordinates for emergency responders, and bring drinking water.
* **Status.** The site is absent from STINAPA's dive map; confirm with STINAPA that it is open before diving.
