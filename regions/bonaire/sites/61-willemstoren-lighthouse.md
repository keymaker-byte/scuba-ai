# 61. Willemstoren Lighthouse

Willemstoren Lighthouse is a shore site at Bonaire's southern tip, below the island's first lighthouse, built in 1837. A broad, flat terrace about 4 m deep runs out from a rocky shore to a reef slope forested in large sea fans, gorgonians and soft coral, rarely dived and among the most intact soft coral growth on the island, with tarpon, barracuda and schooling fish drawn in by the plankton the current carries. The tip lies open to the trade winds and the open sea, and the headline hazards are surf on a rough, rocky entry and a current that can run hard in either direction along the shore and change during the dive.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk 38 m south-southeast from the parking to the water. The shore is rough coral rock, slippery and uneven; a yellow rock marked as the entry and exit shows the way down. Scout the route without gear first, wait for a lull between waves, wade in to about waist depth, put fins on, and get under quickly. Rocks give way to a sandy bottom past the surf line. Swim 130 m south-southeast across the terrace to the dive area, and on about 20 m to where the reef slope begins. Exit at the same rock, timing the waves.
* **Parking.** Free, on the coral gravel beside the lighthouse, with room for six to eight cars.
* **Access.** Open at all hours, by the paved coast road south from Kralendijk past the salt pans. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Bring water and shade for the surface interval.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The lighthouse.** The stone tower of Willemstoren and the old keeper's house beside it stand just behind the shore, the landmark back to the entry from the surface anywhere on the dive.
* **The terrace.** Flat and 4 to 5 m deep from the surf line out to the dive area, 130 m from shore, over sand and rock with sea fans, gorgonians and elkhorn coral close to shore. It runs at the same depth for at least 200 m west-southwest along the coast.
* **The reef slope.** Begins about 20 m south-southeast of the dive area at about 5 m and falls away from there, to 7 m at 30 m from the dive area, 9 m at 50 m and 10 m at 80 m, then on down past 30 m. Large sea fans, gorgonians and soft coral cover it, with hard coral heads, overhangs and cracks along the way.
* **The return.** Come back up the slope onto the terrace and head north-northwest toward the lighthouse, 130 m from the dive area to the entry.

## Current

| **Usual set** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still or slight for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** The current runs along the shore, east-northeast or west-southwest, and can reverse between days, build suddenly during a dive, or meet an opposing flow so the two clash. It is weakest on the terrace and strongest along the reef slope. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and turn back at once when a constant fight against it sets in.
* **Drift.** With any real current the reef slope quickly becomes a drift dive. The coast holds few safe exits, so plan the dive to return to the entry, and carry a surface marker buoy.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southeast, 151° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southeast, 151° |

The swim out runs 130 m south-southeast from the entry, straight out from the shore. The shore lies open to more than 30 km of sea from northeast clockwise through west-southwest, onshore from east through south-southwest, and offshore from west through north-northeast.

* **Bad.** All directions (N-NNW), calm only. At this exposed tip an onshore wind of any strength raises waves on the rocky entry, any wind stacks onto a current that already builds and shifts without warning, and the prevailing east to southeast trades blow straight onshore over open sea. The site is dived only on a calm day with low waves.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Varies widely, about 10 to 25 m. The plankton the current carries lowers it, and on a calm day with the current slack the water off the slope runs clear and deep blue.
* **The shallows.** Swell from the south and southwest stirs the terrace and cuts visibility near the entry.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Sea fans, gorgonians and soft coral swaying in the surge, with elkhorn coral near the shore.
* **Reef slope.** Huge sea fans and gorgonians, with soft coral, sponges and hard coral heads. Lobsters hide under the coral and in the overhangs, and large green morays live in the cracks. Parrotfish, angelfish, groupers, triggerfish and trumpetfish along the slope, and juvenile fish in the soft coral.
* **Hard coral.** Hard corals along the whole coast, elkhorn included, have suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and stony coral tissue loss disease.
* **Open water.** Schooling fish, barracuda, tarpon and horse-eye jacks in the blue off the slope, with green and hawksbill turtles, spotted eagle rays, and on occasion a Caribbean reef shark or a passing pod of dolphins.

## Hazards

* **Surf and surge.** The southern tip faces open sea. An onshore wind, a strong trade or a passing swell puts surf and surge on the rocks, and swell can build during a dive. Watch the waves from the shore before kitting up, and choose another site when they break on the entry.
* **The entry and exit.** Sharp, slippery coral rock and an uneven bottom through the surf line. Wear sturdy boots, secure all gear before entering, and come back to the same rock.
* **Current.** It can run strong, and it reverses or builds mid-dive without warning. Turn with half the gas left, keep close to the buddy, and end the dive early when it runs harder than planned.
* **Depth.** The reef slope keeps falling past recreational depth. Watch the computer, stay above EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar, and keep within the no-decompression limit.
* **Remoteness.** The site is isolated, with the nearest help in Kralendijk. Check all gear before driving south, and carry a surface marker buoy and a whistle.
