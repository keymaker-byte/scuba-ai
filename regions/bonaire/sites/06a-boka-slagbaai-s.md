# 6a. Boka Slagbaai S

The southern of two routes at Boka Slagbaai and the southernmost dive site inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, sharing the same sheltered bay and historic colonial buildings as Boka Slagbaai N. Slagbaai takes its name from the Dutch slachtbaai, slaughter bay, once one of the island's two main ports, where goats were slaughtered and salted before shipping to Curaçao. This route holds the site's real history: two 18th century cannons at about 3 m, preserved by the salt water and now grown over with coral and sponge, along with scattered anchors, anchor chains and ballast stones from ships that once worked the bay. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering the whole park.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** The same sandy beach as Boka Slagbaai N, beside the park's historic yellow building, with parking only metres from the shoreline.
* **Parking.** At the beach, metres from the water.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30; the Slagbaai area must be left by 16:00. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's unpaved roads, though this stretch is more accessible than the park's remoter sites; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** The park's historic buildings sit on the beach here, though they hold no dive facilities.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Yellow building.** Marks the shared entry point on the beach.
* **Cannons at 3 m.** Two genuine 18th century cannons, grown over with coral and sponge, mark the south route's signature feature.
* **Anchors and ballast.** Anchors, anchor chains and ballast stones from historic shipping are scattered through the same area.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. The bay is sheltered relative to the park's more exposed points, though current still builds with wind the same way it does along the rest of the coast.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef terrace sits around 5 m, with the cannons themselves at about 3 m and the south route working out to the mid 20s.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since May 2023; treat as not diveable until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Historic wreckage.** The cannons, anchors, anchor chains and ballast stones are genuine 18th century artifacts; treat them as fixed and don't handle them.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 297° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 302° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north-northwest (WSW-NNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, straight into the mouth of the bay. The bay takes the edge off a swell from this quarter, but any wind with north or west in it still builds one at the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through southwest: offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, cross-shore over 80 to 150 m from the north-northeast, northeast, south-southwest and southwest, and onshore over 630 m from the north, across the bay. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the bay flat.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Large, unusual coral formations over a sandy bottom, with southern stingrays and garden eels.
* **Wreckage.** Coral and sponge growth has taken over the cannons and other ironwork, now part of the reef itself.
