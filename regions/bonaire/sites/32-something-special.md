# 32. Something Special

Something Special, also known as Pali Grandi, is a shore site on the Kralendijk waterfront beside the entrance to the Harbour Village marina, entered from the rocks next to the Between Two Buns restaurant. A sand and rubble terrace runs about 94 m out to a reef edge at about 10 m, and the reef slopes on to sand where garden eels live from about 17 m. Coral is sparse near the marina mouth, but the marina draws an unusually wide range of fish, schools coming in to feed with predators behind them, and the site is one of the island's best for frogfish and other macro life. The headline hazard is boat traffic in and out of the marina entrance.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the restaurant lot, walk the short walkway toward the boulevard; the entry lies to the right of the footbridge, by a wall painted with a mural. Step in over rock and sand into about 4 m of water, in sturdy boots. The dive area lies 94 m southwest, 218°, straight out across the terrace. Exit at the same spot.
* **Parking.** Free in the Between Two Buns restaurant lot, about a minute's walk from the entry. Space is limited and easiest to find outside the restaurant's opening hours; leave the surrounding houses and businesses clear.
* **Access.** Open to shore diving. The site lies off the waterfront road where it becomes a one-way street; the yellow-painted stone stands at the water by the entry, out of sight from the main road. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** The Between Two Buns restaurant beside the parking.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The mural wall.** The painted wall beside the footbridge marks the entry, with the yellow stone at the water.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble with small coral heads, about 4 m at the entry, 5 m at 30 m out, 7 m at 45 m out and 9 m from 60 to 75 m out, reaching the reef edge at the dive area, about 10 m deep and 94 m out. Old rubble patches across it are overgrown with sponges and coral.
* **The reef slope.** From the reef edge the slope runs down through the main reef from 10 to 18 m, then alternates with sand below about 17 m, out toward 30 m. Garden eels fill the sand from about 17 m.
* **North to the marina.** The reef runs north to the entrance of the Harbour Village marina, where the coral thins to rubble and sand. Turn short of the channel itself.
* **South along the waterfront.** The reef continues south along the town waterfront, the direction to take when the current runs north.
* **The return.** The entry lies 94 m northeast, 38°, of the dive area, straight back across the terrace to the mural wall.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What sets its direction on a given day is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight, at times strong, and it can change during a dive and run differently at night than by day. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and let it carry you back toward the entry.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southwest, 210° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 207° |

The swim out runs 94 m southwest, 218°, from the entry, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water south of Klein Bonaire. Wind from this quarter puts waves on the entry rocks and stirs up the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** South-southeast through south (SSE-S), onshore over 2.3 to 3.3 km along the town waterfront, and west-southwest through west (WSW-W), onshore over 1.0 to 1.1 km from Klein Bonaire, each enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** West-northwest (WNW), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open sea, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through southeast (NW-SE): offshore from north-northwest through east, off the land over 90 to 170 m, and cross-shore over 290 to 710 m from the northwest, east-southeast and southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land or along the shore and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on a typical day, best on calm mornings.
* **Shallows and marina.** The sandy shallows run hazier than the slope, and boats stir the water around the marina entrance. Runoff after heavy rain clouds the shallows for a time, most often in the rainy season from October to January.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Fish.** An unusually wide range of species, drawn by the marina. Schools of bigeye scad come into the marina entrance to feed with jacks and other predators close behind. Parrotfish in large numbers, French and queen angelfish, trumpetfish, spotted drum, sergeant majors, creole wrasse and blue chromis along the reef.
* **Macro.** Frogfish in the sponges, with sharptail snake eels, secretary and sailfin blennies, shrimp in the anemones, crabs in the sponges and the odd seahorse on the coral.
* **Sand.** Southern stingrays, peacock flounder and garden eels on the sand, with spotted eagle rays passing over it.
* **Larger animals.** Morays of several species, nurse sharks resting under overhangs in the deeper parts, and hawksbill and green turtles.
* **Night.** Octopus, lobsters and crabs come out to forage, squid hover over the terrace, and tarpon hunt along the reef.
* **Coral.** Many of the larger corals on the patch reefs and the slope have died since 2023 from bleaching and disease, and algae now covers much of the reef.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats and pleasure craft run in and out of the marina entrance, busiest in the late afternoon, when the boats return, and on weekends. Descend early, stay down until back at the entry, turn short of the channel, and carry a surface marker buoy. At night boats are harder to see.
* **Current.** Usually slight, at times strong, and it can change direction during a dive; keep enough gas to return to the entry against it.
* **Depth.** The slope runs on below 30 m toward the maximum operating depth of EAN32, about 34 m at 1.4 bar, where the no-decompression limit runs short. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Entry footing.** Rock and sharp coral remnants at the entry, with sea urchins in the shallows; wear sturdy boots.
* **Restoration structures.** Reef Renewal Foundation Bonaire keeps a staghorn nursery here and, deeper on the reef, a tray nursery of boulder coral species susceptible to stony coral tissue loss disease. Both are fragile; keep fins and gauges clear of them.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from south-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW) puts waves on the entry, from south-southeast through south (SSE-S) and west-southwest through west (WSW-W) a chop, and from west-northwest (WNW) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these blows hard enough to call the entry marginal or no go.
