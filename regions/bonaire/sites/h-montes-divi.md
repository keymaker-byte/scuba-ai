# H. Monte's Divi

A boat dive on the south side of Klein Bonaire, named for the lone divi divi tree that stands on the shore directly opposite the mooring. The tree grows upright, where the trade winds bend most of the island's divi divi trees into a lean, which makes the site easy to pick out from the water. A shallow terrace runs about 100 m out from the shore to the top of a steep reef slope covered in hard and soft corals and a variety of sponges, falling through the recreational range to about 30 m. Staghorn coral and finger sponges grow in the shallows, and the site gives a good chance of a seahorse. Current is usually mild to moderate; the headline hazards are how quickly the slope gains depth and a current that can build strong enough to swim against.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring opposite the divi divi tree, then a descent to the edge of the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring off the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The divi divi tree.** Stands upright on the shore directly opposite the mooring, the landmark that gives the site its name.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral at about 4 to 9 m, running about 100 m out from the shore, with staghorn coral and finger sponges. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive, and as a snorkel site.
* **The slope.** Begins at the outer edge of the terrace and descends steeply, covered in hard and soft corals and sponges, to about 30 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild to moderate, and at times strong enough to make headway along the reef hard work.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 206° |

* **Bad.** South through west (S-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** South-southeast (SSE), onshore over 3.9 km from Bonaire's west coast south of Kralendijk, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 600 m to 1.4 km from the west-northwest and northwest, and 3.2 to 3.3 km from the east-southeast and southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, averaging about 30 m, at its best on calm mornings. A swell from the south stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Stands of staghorn coral and finger sponges, the staghorn thinned by the bleaching and disease losses since 2023, with seahorses among the coral and sponges, and stingrays on the sand.
* **Slope.** Hard and soft corals and a variety of sponges, with surgeonfish, angelfish and grouper along the reef and barracuda off it.
* **Turtles.** Green and hawksbill turtles are seen on the reef.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the edge of the terrace at about 9 m toward 30 m; watch the computer on the descent.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen and can run strong enough to make headway along the reef hard work; check the surface before entering and stay close to a buddy.
* **Swell and surge.** A wind or swell from the south runs straight onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow terrace.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
