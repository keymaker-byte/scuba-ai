# H. Monte's Divi

Named for the lone divi divi tree standing guard on shore directly opposite the mooring, growing upright rather than bent by the trade winds the way most of the island's divi divi trees are shaped. The reef descends steeply from the shallows, covered in hard and soft coral with a variety of sponges; staghorn coral and finger sponges once grew thick in the shallows, though the staghorn has thinned in the bleaching events since 2023. This is one of Klein Bonaire's more reliable seahorse sites.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat only.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The divi divi tree.** Stands upright onshore directly opposite the mooring, an unusual sight since the trade winds normally bend these trees into a lean.
* **Depth profile.** The reef descends steeply from the shallows through the recreational range to about 30 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 206° |

* **Bad.** South through west (S-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** South-southeast (SSE), onshore over 3.9 km from the south end of Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the islet, and cross-shore over 600 m to 3.3 km from the west-northwest, northwest, east-southeast and southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Staghorn coral, thinned since 2023, and finger sponges, with a good chance of a seahorse.
* **Reef.** Stingrays and sea turtles.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
