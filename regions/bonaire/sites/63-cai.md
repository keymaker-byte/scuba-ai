# 63. Cai

The most challenging and celebrated dive on Bonaire's east coast, entered off the mouth of Lac Bay where the bay's mangroves meet the open sea, a transition that creates unusual diversity in habitat and marine life. The channel out runs about 7 m deep with a strong current pushing seaward; a local guide is required, current is strong and unpredictable, and this is a dive for strong, experienced swimmers only. Leave the reef wall with 55 to 70 bar in hand, since running low against the current here can mean a hard surface swim back.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Through the channel at the mouth of Lac Bay, about 7 m deep, with a strong outgoing current; always dived with a local guide.
* **Parking.** At Lac Bay.
* **Access.** Requires a local guide given the current.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The channel.** Where the mangrove waters of Lac Bay meet the open sea, the defining feature of the site.
* **Depth profile.** The channel runs about 7 m deep, with the reef beyond through the recreational range to 30 m.

## Current

| | |
|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | Seaward, out of the Lac Bay channel |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** The channel carries a strong, changeable current pushing seaward; a local guide is mandatory.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south, 175° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 174° |

The site sits on the windward coast at the mouth of Lac Bay, and the swim out runs 94 m south-southeast through the channel to the dive area.

* **Bad.** East-southeast through south (ESE-S), onshore over more than 30 km of open ocean, the quarter the prevailing trades blow from. A wind from here blows straight against the channel's seaward outflow and worsens an already demanding site; that combination rules the dive out.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over 2 to 2.9 km along the coast, enough to build a chop at the channel mouth.
* **Mixed.** East (E), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell across the channel mouth.
* **Fine.** West-southwest clockwise through east-northeast: offshore from west-northwest through northeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 360 m to 2.5 km from the west-southwest, west and east-northeast. Calmer conditions ease the channel, though the current remains a factor regardless.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Varies significantly with current, from about 10 to 30 m and averaging around 20 m, as the current stirs sediment from the channel and mangrove areas.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Channel and reef.** Large schools of tarpon and sea turtles.
* **Anemone field.** The highest concentration of giant anemones anywhere on Bonaire, some over a metre across.

## Hazards

* **Strong outgoing current.** Leave the reef wall with 55 to 70 bar remaining to avoid running low against the current and facing a hard surface swim back.
* **Guide required.** Dived only with a local guide familiar with the channel.
