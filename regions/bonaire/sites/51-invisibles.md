# 51. Invisibles

The southernmost of Bonaire's double reef sites, a shore dive on the coast road about 650 m south of Salt Pier, opposite the salt works. A shallow sand terrace holds one of the island's best garden eel colonies around the mooring at about 6 m, and runs on to a solid first reef that slopes to about 20 m, onto a sand channel at about 25 m. Beyond the channel the second reef rises to about 17 to 20 m and breaks into coral islands separated by sand chutes, where eagle rays circle the peaks. The headline hazard is navigation: the mooring is out of sight from the reef, the second reef's islands are easy to lose, and a wrong heading leads into deep open water.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk down a short rocky beach from the roadside into the water, about 4 m deep a few metres out. The shallows are cut by holes and ridges, mostly running at an angle to the beach, that are hard to see when waves are breaking; booties help, and in a small surf, enter sideways and fin up once past waist depth. Swim about 50 m due west over sand to the mooring, then on across the terrace to the top of the first reef, 147 m west of the entry, close to straight out from the shore. Exit at the same beach.
* **Parking.** Along the roadside at the yellow stone with the site name, with room for several cars, most within 15 m of the entry. Free.
* **Access.** Open. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The mooring.** A yellow mooring buoy about 50 m due west of the entry, in about 6 m over sand, with the garden eel colony around it. It is out of sight from the reef, so the return is navigated by compass.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble shelving from about 4 m at the entry to about 11 m at the top of the first reef, 147 m from shore.
* **The first reef.** A solid band of hard coral that slopes from about 11 m to its foot at about 20 m.
* **The sand chute.** A sand chute runs seaward from the mooring and down the first reef toward the second, and marks the turn back to the entry on the return.
* **The sand channel.** About 25 m deep, running parallel to the shore between the two reefs.
* **The second reef.** Rises from the channel to a top at about 17 to 20 m and slopes away past 35 m. It is broken into coral islands separated by sand chutes, and lies to the west-southwest of the mooring; a heading due west from the sand chute passes the gaps between its islands and runs out into deep open water.
* **North and south.** Heading north, the second reef runs more continuous and more gently sloped. Heading south, it fragments further, and a wide sand channel runs between its sections at 27 m and deeper. Its southernmost section, a large mound locally called The Rock, stands almost separate from the rest.
* **The return.** Follow the first reef back to the sand chute, then head east up the terrace toward the mooring and the shore.

## Current

| **Usual set** | south, 180° |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild, at times moderate. Check its direction before entering and start the dive into it, heading north when it sets south as usual.
* **Eddies.** When the current builds, it turns into eddies and downwellings around the second reef's islands and sand chutes.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 260° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 259° |

The swim out runs 147 m west from the entry, 2° off the shore's facing, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the rocky beach and hides the holes in the shallows, making the entry and exit rough.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 8.6 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south (N-S): offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 500 m from the north and over 410 m to 1.2 km from the south-southeast and south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, around 20 m on an average day, and best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** Surf on the beach stirs the terrace and the shallows. On a low visibility day the second reef is hard to find from the first; keep to the first reef.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Sand around the mooring.** One of Bonaire's best garden eel colonies, at about 6 m, retreating into the sand at the slightest disturbance; approach slowly and keep some distance, or watch them on the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **Terrace.** Spotted eagle rays and palometas over the sand, with peacock flounders, yellowhead jawfish, razorfish, filefish, goldspotted snake eels and juvenile reef fish. Large schools of grunts gather over the shallows.
* **First reef.** Hard coral with occasional sponges and sea fans. Yellowtail snapper, parrotfish schools, spotted drum, trumpetfish, French, queen and gray angelfish and rock beauties, with scorpionfish, flamingo tongues, and anemones hosting shrimp and crabs.
* **Second reef.** Denser hard coral and large sponges on the coral islands. Eagle rays circle the peaks, especially early in the morning, with groupers, barracuda, bigeye jacks and green morays. Green turtles feed across both reefs.
* **Night.** Squid over the terrace, and the bioluminescent ostracod displays seen along the leeward coast in the days after the full moon.
* **Coral.** Bleaching and disease since 2023 have cut hard coral cover sharply along the leeward coast, and algae now covers parts of the coral here.

## Hazards

* **Navigation.** The mooring is out of sight from the reef, the second reef's islands look alike, and a heading due west from the sand chute leads off the reef into open water. Take a heading from the mooring, carry a compass, note the sand chute on the way out, and surface to check position before the return when unsure.
* **Depth.** The second reef slopes away past 35 m, and the sand channels between its southern sections run to 27 m and deeper; following them, a diver reaches 40 m quickly. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Entry and exit.** Rocks at the waterline, and holes and ridges in the shallows hidden under breaking waves. Choose another site when heavy surf is running onto the beach.
* **Current.** Usually mild, but it can build with the trades and eddy around the second reef. Plan the dive into it, and turn early when it builds.
* **Scorpionfish and lionfish.** Both are venomous; scorpionfish lie camouflaged on the reef and rubble, and lionfish shelter in the reef. Watch where hands and fins go near the bottom.
* **Boats.** Dive boats tie up to the mooring. Ascend with a surface marker buoy away from it.
