# 34. Calabas Reef

The house reef of Divi Flamingo Beach Resort on J.A. Abraham Boulevard, just south of central Kralendijk on Bonaire's west coast, entered from the resort's dive pier. A shallow sand terrace runs about 70 m out from the pier end to its edge at about 11 m, where the reef slopes away past sponges and soft corals toward sand beyond 30 m, with a large old anchor in the shallows and a small sailboat wreck on the slope. Calm and sheltered, it is a standard check-out dive and a favourite first night dive, when tarpon hunt in divers' lights. The headline hazards are boat traffic at the resort's piers and the slope below the terrace edge, which continues past 30 m toward the 34 m maximum operating depth of EAN32.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the end of the Divi Flamingo dive pier, down its stairs or ladder into about 4 m of water, or from the resort beach, wading out over a gently sloping sand bottom to knee depth before finning out. The dive area lies 68 m west of the pier end, straight out across the terrace. Exit by the same pier or beach.
* **Parking.** Outside the resort walls, on Kaya C.E.B. Hellmund or along J.A. Abraham Boulevard, about 30 m from the water. Parking inside the resort grounds, where the dive centre allows it, costs about 10 USD and includes use of the pool. Spaces are limited and fill in the December to April high season.
* **Access.** Check in at Divi Dive Bonaire, the resort's dive centre, before unloading or diving. Divi Flamingo guests on a dive package shore dive the house reef at any hour, night included; non-guests arrange access with the dive centre ahead of time. Guests of the neighbouring Carib Inn enter from that inn's own pier. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** The Divi Dive Bonaire dive centre, the dive piers with rinse tanks, benches and lockers, and the resort's restaurants, restrooms and pool.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The piers.** The Divi Flamingo dive pier runs about 30 m out from the shore, its end in about 4 m of water. Further piers lie close by to the north and south, among them a second resort pier about 130 m north and the Carib Inn pier to the south, and dive boats moor and pick up along this stretch.
* **The terrace.** Sand with scattered coral heads, sponges and seagrass, at 4 to 7 m for the first 40 m out from the pier end, then dropping to 9 to 11 m along its outer edge. The dive area sits on that edge.
* **The anchor.** A large old ship's anchor, about 2 m across and grown over with coral and sponge, lies in about 5 m of water on the terrace out from the resort, a landmark for briefings and a turning point on the way back in.
* **The reef slope.** Beyond the terrace edge the reef slopes away, steepening below about 15 m, past tube and barrel sponges, sea fans and soft corals toward sand beyond 30 m. The reef runs on north and south along the coast, so a dive can follow it either way and turn back.
* **The sailboat.** A small sailboat wreck, about 8 to 10 m long, sits upright on the slope at about 20 m, grown over with sponges and coral.
* **The return.** The pier end lies 68 m east of the dive area, straight back across the terrace.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift. What sets its direction on a given day is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight or absent. In very strong trades a slight current can run along the reef, mostly along the deeper slope. Check its direction before entering and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 257° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 257° |

The swim out runs 68 m west from the pier end, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** Southwest through west (SW-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea past the south end of Klein Bonaire. Wind from this quarter puts waves on the pier steps and the beach and stirs up the sand terrace.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest (SSW), onshore over 1.4 km, and west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), onshore over 2.0 to 2.8 km from Klein Bonaire across the channel, each enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south (NNW-S): offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 320 m to 2.1 km from the north-northwest, north, south-southeast and south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength, though a strong afternoon trade wind roughens the surface.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 10 to 30 m, typically about 20 m, best in the calm early morning and on dives off the deeper slope.
* **Wind and rain.** Strong wind and runoff after rain, mostly October to January, cloud the shallow terrace for a time, while the slope stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Green and hawksbill turtles graze the sand and seagrass, and parrotfish, trunkfish, trumpetfish, grunts and snapper work the coral heads. Southern stingrays rest on the sand. Reef fish here are used to divers and approach them closely.
* **Reef slope.** Angelfish, blue tang, groupers and barracuda along the slope, green and spotted morays in the coral, and the odd spotted eagle ray passing along it, most often early in the morning. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Macro.** Seahorses in the soft corals and sponges, frogfish on yellow and orange sponges, mantis shrimp, octopus, sharptail eels, shrimp and nudibranchs, all rewarding a slow search.
* **The sailboat.** Yellowtail snapper and grunts school around the wreck, with octopus in its recesses.
* **Night.** Tarpon follow divers' lights and hunt the fish they pick out, spiny lobster and octopus forage over the reef, and parrotfish sleep in the coral.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats moor, pick up and run along this stretch from the Divi and Carib Inn piers. Stay down until back at the terrace near the pier, look up before surfacing, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Depth.** The slope below the terrace edge steepens below 15 m and continues past 30 m toward the 34 m maximum operating depth of EAN32. Turn at a planned depth.
* **The pier stairs.** Wet and slippery; climb in and out slowly.
* **Night tarpon.** Tarpon hunting in a light dart past and around divers; keep a steady position and shield the beam when they crowd in.
* **Stinging plankton.** Jellyfish near the surface and sea lice in the water bring a stinging rash on some days.
* **Debris.** Old metal, rope and wood lie on the bottom in places close to the piers; keep hands and fins clear.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from southwest through west (SW-W) puts waves on the pier steps and the beach, and from south-southwest (SSW) and west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW) a chop; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
