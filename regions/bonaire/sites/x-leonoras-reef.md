# X. Leonora's Reef

A boat dive toward the west end of Klein Bonaire's north coast. A flat, shallow sand and coral shelf at the mooring gives onto a reef that runs steeply down from about 7 to 9 m on to 40 m, with sandy channels running down through it. The reef is known for its huge coral heads with hollow centres, the oldest estimated at over 75 years, with the largest formations of plate and star coral around 16 m and a small tunnel through the coral that frames a photograph. The shallow shelf, crowded with fish, suits novice divers and snorkelers. The headline hazards are the slope, which runs on past recreational depth, and a wind driven current that is usually slight, at times moderate.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A backroll or giant stride from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, onto the flat shelf before the reef drops away.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk, usually as one of two dives on a Klein Bonaire boat trip. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shelf.** Flat sand and coral at about 4 to 9 m between the shore and the reef edge, covered in smaller coral heads and crowded with fish.
* **The slope.** Drops steeply from the shelf edge at about 7 to 9 m, running on to about 40 m. Sandy channels run down through it and make easy routes out and back over sand.
* **The coral heads.** The largest formations sit around 15 to 20 m, centred on about 16 m: huge plate coral and mountainous star coral, many hollow at the centre, the oldest estimated at over 75 years.
* **The tunnel.** A small tunnel runs through the coral formations, a favourite frame for photographers.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight, at times moderate.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | north-northwest, 332° |

* **Bad.** West through north-northwest (W-NNW), onshore over 5.7 to more than 30 km of open water, putting chop at the mooring and surge over the shelf.
* **Short fetch.** North through north-northeast (N-NNE), onshore over 3.6 to 4.3 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through west-southwest: cross-shore over 2.8 to 3 km from the northeast and east-northeast, offshore from east through south-southwest, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 260 to 780 m from the southwest and west-southwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m and at times to 40 m, at its best on calm mornings before the wind picks up. A wind from the west through north-northwest stirs the shelf and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The shelf.** Parrotfish, yellowtail snapper, foureye butterflyfish, damselfish, honeycomb trunkfish and juvenile drums among the smaller coral heads, with turtles in the shallows.
* **The coral.** Plate and star coral, hard corals, purple sea fans with flamingo tongue snails, wire coral, black coral, sea plumes, yellow finger coral and sponges. Star coral is among the species hit hardest by the coral disease and bleaching of 2023 and 2024.
* **The slope.** Groupers, queen and other angelfish, barracuda along the reef edge, and turtles. Green morays shelter in the hollow coral heads, with spotted and chain morays also seen.
* **Macro.** Seahorses in the coral, fireworms and octopus, the seahorses best found with a guide.

## Hazards

* **The slope.** The reef drops steeply from the shelf edge and runs on to about 40 m, past recreational depth; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Current.** Wind driven current is usually slight here, at times moderate; check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west through north-northwest runs onto this shore over open water and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the shelf; a wind from the north or north-northeast builds a shorter chop across the channel.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
