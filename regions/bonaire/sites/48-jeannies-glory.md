# 48. Jeannie's Glory

Jeannie's Glory is a shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 400 m north of Salt Pier on Bonaire's southwest coast. A rocky step-in below the road opens onto a shallow sand and coral terrace with soft corals, which a 165 m swim crosses to the reef edge at about 7 m. Below the edge, a shelf at 12 to 14 m gives onto a reef slope that drops steeply past 30 m. The headline hazard is the entry and exit over rough coral rock and a low shoreline ledge, which turns dangerous whenever waves are running onto the shore.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk about 30 m straight down from the road over coral gravel and loose rock to the waterline. The shore is solid coral rock with large boulders and a rutted ledge; look for a rock standing about 60 cm proud of the shore with a small pool to its left, hold the rock and step in. Sturdy boots help on the rock. Swim 165 m west, close to straight out from the shore, across the terrace to the reef edge. Exit at the same rock.
* **Parking.** The roadside shoulder of EEG Boulevard beside the yellow rocks, free.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shoreline ledge.** A low undercut ledge runs along the shore in about 1 to 2 m just off the entry.
* **The terrace.** Sand with scattered coral and soft corals, shelving gently from about 3 m deep just past the shoreline ledge to 4 m at 70 m out and 5 to 6 m at 125 m. It serves as the safety stop on the way back.
* **The reef edge.** The dive area, 165 m west of the entry, sits where the terrace ends at about 7 m on uneven ground running from about 5 to 11 m within 25 m. North of the entry the bottom at the same distance out runs shallower, about 5 to 6 m; to the south it reaches 12 m.
* **The shelf.** About 30 m beyond the edge the bottom steps down to a shelf at 12 to 14 m, about 80 m wide.
* **The reef slope.** Beyond the shelf, about 110 m past the edge, the reef slopes away steeply past 30 m and on past 45 m.
* **Along the reef.** Heading north the reef grows richer. Heading south toward Salt Pier it thins out, with the pier's first pilings marking the turn.
* **The return.** Come back up onto the terrace and head east, 165 m to the entry. Triangular piles of rock stand on the shore near the exit rock; stay underwater at 1.5 m or deeper until the rock is in sight.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs north or south along the reef and can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Light to moderate along the reef, at times strong enough to make the swim back hard. It can run differently on the terrace and on the slope below. Check its direction at depth before entering the reef, and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 258° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 258° |

The swim out runs 165 m west from the entry, 3° off the shore's facing, close to straight out.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the shore, stirs up the terrace and puts surge on the rocky entry and exit.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and north-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 7.4 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast (N-SSE): offshore from north-northeast through southeast over 160 to 310 m of water, off the land, and cross-shore over 1.0 to 1.2 km from the north and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, averaging about 20 m, best on calm mornings.
* **The shallows.** Waves breaking on the shore from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) stir up the terrace, while the reef further out stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Soft corals and gorgonians over the sand, with southern stingrays buried in it. Turtles feed in the shallows.
* **Reef.** Surgeonfish, parrotfish, snapper and French and queen angelfish, with barracuda patrolling along the edge. Cleaner shrimp and cleaner wrasse work cleaning stations along the reef.
* **Macro.** Moray eels, crabs and scorpionfish among the coral.
* **In the blue.** Spotted eagle rays pass along the reef at times.
* **Coral.** Hard and soft coral on the reef. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **The entry and exit.** Solid coral rock, large boulders and a rutted ledge make a rough step-in and climb out. Wear sturdy boots and take time to pick a clear path.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind or swell from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) breaks on the shore and makes the entry and exit dangerous; choose another site when any of these blows fresh or a swell is running.
* **Depth.** The reef slope runs on past 45 m, below EAN32's 34 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar. Plan a maximum depth and hold it, and keep within the no-decompression limit.
* **Gas.** The 165 m swim each way takes a large share of the gas, more against a current. Turn on gas with the swim home over the terrace still to come.
* **The exit.** Missing the exit rock lands on boulders and rough coral. Take a bearing back to it before leaving the terrace.
* **Ships at Salt Pier.** Salt freighters berth at the pier 400 m south, worked by tugs. Turn back before the pier when a ship is in, and ascend with a surface marker buoy.
