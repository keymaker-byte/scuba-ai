# R. Sharon's Serenity

A boat dive at Klein Bonaire's southwest corner, named for Sharon, who once worked for Captain Don and counted this her favourite site. A shallow terrace of soft corals and sand runs out past the mooring to a steep slope of star corals, sponges and soft corals, with large groupers on the reef and basket stars that unfurl to feed after dark. The mooring lies close enough to shore to make it a snorkel site as well. The headline hazard is a wind driven current, usually mild in the lee of the islet, that can run moderate when the trades strengthen or shift.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring on the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand, seagrass and soft corals shelving from about 4 m at the shore to 9 to 10 m around the mooring, and running on past it toward the drop-off. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive, and as a snorkel site.
* **The slope.** Falls steeply from the outer edge of the terrace, covered in star corals, sponges and soft corals, to about 30 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild in the lee of the islet, and can run moderate along the slope on a day of strong or shifting trades.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 296° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north (WSW-N), onshore over 6.4 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** North-northeast (NNE), cross-shore over 5.5 km, and south-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, each running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through south: offshore from east-northeast through south, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 540 m from the northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 15 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-southwest through north, or an overcast, windy day, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Soft corals of many kinds, with elkhorn and staghorn, both thinned since the bleaching and coral disease of 2023 and 2024. Southern stingrays cross the sand and seagrass.
* **Slope.** Star corals and sponges, with large groupers common, green morays in the holes, French angelfish, often in pairs, and queen angelfish.
* **Turtles.** Hawksbill turtles are seen regularly on the reef.
* **Blue water.** Bar jacks and schools of horse-eye jacks off the slope.
* **Basket stars.** Unfurl their arms after dark to feed on plankton; by day they sit curled up under the coral.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Wind driven current is usually mild here, but can run moderate when the trades strengthen or shift and make the return to the mooring hard work; check the surface before entering.
* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the terrace toward 30 m; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-southwest through north runs onto this shore over open water and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the south-southwest through southwest, or the north-northeast, runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
