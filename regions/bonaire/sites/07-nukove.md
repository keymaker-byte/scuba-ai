# 7. Nukove

A small sandy cove reached by turning off just before the Bonaire Petroleum Corporation (BOPEC) storage tanks in the Karpata area, at the southern edge of Washington Slagbaai National Park's chain of remote sites. The detour around the oil terminal has historically been rewarded with some of the most pristine coral on the island, elkhorn stands in the shallows and large midnight blue parrotfish working the reef; STINAPA has found no SCTLD in this closed stretch, though the island-wide bleaching events of 2023 and 2024 affected shallow coral here regardless of disease status. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering the area from Karpata north.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A sandy beach entry into a small, sheltered cove.
* **Parking.** Plenty of parking right at the cove.
* **Access.** Reached by turning off the coast road just before the BOPEC storage tanks. All diving in the area from Karpata north, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **BOPEC tanks.** The turn to the cove comes just before the oil terminal's storage tanks on the coast road.
* **Sandy cove.** Entry and the shallow elkhorn stands sit inside the cove itself, with the reef proper beyond it.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Current at Nukove can run from mild to strong depending on wind, consistent with the rest of this stretch of coast.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The cove and shallow elkhorn stands sit around 4 m, with the reef working out through the recreational range to about 30 m.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since May 2023; treat as not diveable until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Variable current.** Can run from mild to strong with wind; check conditions before committing to the full reef beyond the cove.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell here.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against this site's current is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Elkhorn coral in the cove, though the 2023 and 2024 bleaching events left their mark here as everywhere on the island.
* **Reef.** Schools of algae-eating reef fish and large midnight blue parrotfish, along with larger fish species typical of this less dived stretch of coast.
