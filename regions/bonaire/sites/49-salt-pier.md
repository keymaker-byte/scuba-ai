# 49. Salt Pier

An active industrial pier where Cargill loads salt from the adjacent salt works, its pilings now fully encrusted with sponge and coral and shading large schools of fish beneath the structure overhead. Easy for a novice and a favourite for photography, but only diveable when no ship is moored and no maintenance is underway; check at the yellow guard shack under the pier before entering.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** An easy shore entry beside the pier.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Only diveable with no ship moored and no maintenance underway; check at the guard shack (yellow, under the pier) for permission before entering, and never dive if a ship is present or expected.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pilings.** Fully encrusted with sponge and coral, the core of the dive, running the length of the pier.
* **Depth profile.** The site stays shallow throughout, from about 4 to 15 m among the pilings.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the entry.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and north-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 7.9 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 950 m to 1.1 km from the north and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, with the shade under the pier itself adding good contrast for photography.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at these shallow depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The pilings.** Large schools of fish congregate in the shade beneath the pier, on pilings dense with sponge and coral.

## Hazards

* **Active shipping.** The pier is a working industrial structure; confirm no ship is moored or expected before diving, and never enter if a vessel is present.
