# 49. Salt Pier

The Cargill salt works loading pier on EEG Boulevard south of Kralendijk, where a conveyor carries salt from the salt pans out to cargo ships along a T-shaped pier about 185 m long. Its round concrete and steel pilings stand in rows from a shallow sand and seagrass terrace out to the reef edge, encrusted with sponges, orange cup corals and hard coral, and large schools of fish, tarpon and barracuda shelter in the shade beneath the deck. It is an easy shore dive and a favourite for photography and night diving. The headline hazard is the working pier itself: diving is allowed only when no ship is moored and no maintenance is underway.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk down from the parking to the shore on the north side of the pier and in through a break in the shoreline rock, over sand, coral rubble and hard rock to about 1 to 2 m where you can float and put on fins; booties make it easier. A sandy entry on the south side of the pier is the alternative. The first pilings stand about 20 m out. Swim 131 m west-southwest, close to straight out from the shore, under the pier to the dive area. Exit at the same break.
* **Parking.** The free coral rubble lot beside EEG Boulevard on the north side of the pier, about 30 m from the waterline, with room for about ten to fifteen cars. Park on the rubble, clear of the sand, and only where the signs allow.
* **Access.** Diving is allowed only when no ship is moored at the pier and no maintenance is underway. Check at the yellow security hut under the pier that no ship is due before entering. A freighter can arrive with little warning, and loading closes the site for many hours, at times for days. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pier.** Runs about 185 m west-southwest from the shore beside the entry, with the conveyor overhead. Its pilings are round columns about 1 m across, standing in rows that carry the deck out to the head of the pier.
* **The terrace.** Sand and seagrass with scattered coral rubble and sponges, shelving from about 4 m near shore to about 7 m under the pier at the dive area, 131 m out.
* **The outer pilings.** The head of the pier forms the bar of the T, running north to south along the reef edge about 185 m from shore, where the bottom drops past 10 m and the outer pilings stand in about 10 to 15 m.
* **The reef slope.** Beyond the head of the pier the natural reef slopes on to sand with coral blocks at about 30 m.
* **The return.** Follow the pilings back toward the shore on a bearing of about 76°, and finish over the shallow terrace, where turtles often graze in the seagrass.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually little or none under the pier, at times strong at the outer pilings and over the reef slope. The pilings give shelter from it; check its direction before entering and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |

The swim out runs 131 m west-southwest from the entry, 1° off the shore's facing, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves straight onto the rock and rubble at the entry and makes the exit hard.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and north-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 7.9 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast (N-SSE): offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 660 m from the north and over 890 m from the south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, around 20 m on an average day, lower in the shallows near shore and clearer out at the pilings. The shade under the deck and the light falling between the columns give strong contrast for photography, best with the sun low, in the morning and late afternoon.
* **Wind and swell.** Surge from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) stirs the sand terrace and cuts visibility there.
* **Seasons.** Steady through the year. Northern swell in winter, and swell from passing tropical systems from June to November, clouds the shallows while it runs onto the shore.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **The pilings.** Orange cup corals and orange, yellow and purple tube sponges, with gorgonians, anemones and coral growing over the columns. Banded coral shrimp and spotted cleaner shrimp, blennies and yellownose gobies, and green, spotted, chain and goldentail morays in the cracks.
* **Schools.** Grunts, snapper including yellowtail, bogas, creole wrasse, glassy sweepers, sergeant majors guarding their nests, and bar and horse-eye jacks in the shade beneath the deck. Schools of barracuda circle the pilings at mid depth, and tarpon over 1 m long hold under the deeper pilings at the head of the pier.
* **Reef fish.** French and queen angelfish, trumpetfish, trunkfish, spotted drum, parrotfish including queen parrotfish, and lionfish around the coral blocks.
* **Macro.** Longlure frogfish and longsnout seahorses on the pilings, with octopus in the dark corners of the structure.
* **Terrace and slope.** Green and hawksbill turtles grazing the seagrass on the swim out and back, bonefish in the shallows, peacock flounder and southern stingrays on the sand, and spotted eagle rays along the reef edge.
* **Night.** Morays and octopus hunt among the pilings, squid hover around the columns, slipper lobsters, shrimp and crabs come out, basket stars unfurl, and tarpon hunt in divers' lights.
* **Above the water.** Brown pelicans fish around the pier and dive into the water beside it.

## Hazards

* **Active shipping.** The pier is a working loading terminal. Never enter while a ship is moored or approaching, keep well clear of loading operations, and leave the water at once when a ship approaches or security staff signal.
* **Overhead work.** Maintenance on the conveyor and the deck closes the area beneath the pier, with a risk of falling steel. Stay out of the water under the pier while work is underway.
* **Pilings and debris.** The column edges are sharp with encrusting growth, and old construction debris, tires and trash lie on the bottom under the pier, some of it protruding. Keep about 1 m off the pilings and the bottom.
* **Entry and exit.** Hard rock and coral rubble at the waterline, rough in any surf from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW); time the exit between waves, and choose another site when a swell is running onto the shore.
* **Current.** At times strong at the outer pilings and over the reef slope. Plan the dive into it and use the pilings as shelter.
* **Depth.** The reef slope beyond the head of the pier runs on to about 30 m; turn at a planned depth.
* **Boats.** Dive boats visit the pier. Ascend with a surface marker buoy.
