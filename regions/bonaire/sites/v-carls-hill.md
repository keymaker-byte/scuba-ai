# V. Carl's Hill

Named after underwater photographer Carl Roessler, on the northwest tip of Klein Bonaire. The main feature is a sheer wall beginning about 18 m offshore and dropping to a sandy bottom at 21 m. The wall face is covered in sponges and gorgonians, along with the seldom seen featherbush hydroid, which sits on the upper wall filter feeding on passing plankton. Hawksbill turtles cruise the wall and seahorses anchor to gorgonians in the shallower sections.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat, close to the wall which begins just 18 m offshore.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The wall.** Begins about 18 m offshore, dropping to a sandy bottom at 21 m.
* **Featherbush hydroid.** On the upper wall face, a seldom seen filter feeder worth looking for.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Can run strong at times.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 251° |

* **Bad.** Southwest through northwest (SW-NW), onshore over 8.9 km to more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises chop and current along the wall.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest through north (NNW-N), cross-shore over 5.2 to 6.5 km, running a swell along the wall.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the islet, cross-shore over 160 to 260 m from the south-southeast and south, and onshore over 990 m from the south-southwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m or more.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Wall.** Sponges, gorgonians and the seldom seen featherbush hydroid; barracuda, bar jacks and large schools of blue tang.
* **Shallower sections.** Hawksbill turtles cruising the wall, and seahorses anchored to gorgonians.

## Hazards

* **Possible strong current.** Advanced divers only, particularly along the exposed wall face.
