# V. Carl's Hill

A boat dive on the northwest tip of Klein Bonaire, also known as Punt P'abou, named after the underwater photographer Carl Roessler. A narrow terrace of sand, soft corals and elkhorn at about 5 to 10 m runs 30 to 40 m out from the shore before a sheer wall drops away, steeper than most of Bonaire's reef, its face covered in sponges, orange cup corals and gorgonians, with the seldom seen featherbush hydroid on its upper face. Schools of blue tang work the reef, with barracuda and bar jacks along the wall, and seahorses and frogfish hide in the coral and sponges. The headline hazards are the wall itself, which falls past recreational depth straight off the terrace edge, and a wind driven current that is usually slight but runs strong on some days.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a prompt descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by boat, usually a dive operator's, about 10 to 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring, fixed to a helix anchor bolt.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand, soft corals and elkhorn at about 5 to 10 m, running 30 to 40 m out from the shore along the islet's northwest tip. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The wall.** Drops sheer from the terrace edge at about 9 to 10 m and falls on steeply past 30 m into deep water. Its upper face holds the featherbush hydroid.
* **The point.** The wall follows the shore around the islet's northwest tip.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight along the wall, and strong on some days, enough to sweep a diver who lingers at the surface away from the mooring.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | northwest, 310° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north (WSW-N), onshore over 5.2 to more than 30 km of open water, putting chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, running a swell along the wall.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest: cross-shore over 4 to 4.2 km from the north-northeast and northeast, offshore from east-northeast through south, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 660 m from the south-southwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 15 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-southwest through north, or a swell from the southwest, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Soft corals and elkhorn over sand, the elkhorn thinned since the bleaching and coral disease of 2023 and 2024, with princess parrotfish, spotted drums, trunkfish and butterflyfish among the coral.
* **The wall.** Tube and encrusting sponges, orange cup corals and gorgonians, with the featherbush hydroid filter feeding on the upper face. Trumpetfish, scorpionfish, purple cleaner shrimp and arrow crabs on the face.
* **Seahorses and frogfish.** Seahorses in the gorgonians and soft corals, and frogfish in the sponges, both hard to spot without a guide.
* **Turtles.** Hawksbill turtles cruise along the wall.
* **Schooling fish.** Large schools of blue tang over the reef, with black durgon, dog snapper, barracuda and bar jacks along the wall.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The wall falls sheer from the terrace edge at about 9 to 10 m and runs on past recreational depth; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Current.** Wind driven current is usually slight here but runs strong on some days; descend promptly after entering, since a diver who lingers at the surface can be carried off the site, and check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-southwest through north runs onto this shore over open water and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the southwest runs a swell along the wall.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
