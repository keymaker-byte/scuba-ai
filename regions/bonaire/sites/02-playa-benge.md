# 2. Playa Benge

A small rubble beach between low limestone cliffs on the northwest coast of Washington Slagbaai National Park, about 22 km northwest of Kralendijk, fronting some of the most pristine coral on Bonaire and many of its larger fish. A shallow shelf of finger coral and loose rock runs out to a step that drops from about 5 m to 11 m, an uneven reef terrace beyond it, and a steep drop into deep water. The entry over loose, swell-moved rock and a moderate to strong current that often changes direction mid-dive make it a site for experienced divers, and diving here is suspended under STINAPA's closure of the park to diving.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the parking area, a well-worn path leads down to a field of large, loose rocks, and on over the rock deposits to the beach; boots go on at the top. The entry is a strip 20 to 30 m wide of loose coral pieces, stones and dead finger coral that shift with every wave, and the beach itself changes from sand to rock with the season. Waves of 1 m or more make entry and exit dangerous; wear thick-soled boots, go in during a lull between sets, and expect the exit to be the harder of the two. The site is also dived from a boat, which avoids the rubble entry entirely.
* **Parking.** A small, free parking area at the barrier at the end of the track, about 50 m from the water.
* **Access.** Closed to diving: STINAPA closed every site north of Karpata on 1 May 2023 over Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD), reopened the coast between BOPEC and the park, Nukove and Carel's Vision included, on 27 January 2024, and keeps Washington Slagbaai National Park closed to diving while the park stays open for every other activity. Confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive here. When open, the site is reached only on the park's long (yellow) route, past Boka Bartol and Boka Katuna, a long drive on rough, unpaved tracks. The park opens Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, closed on Mondays and holidays, with last entry at 14:30; it can close fully or in part after heavy rain. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended, and the park speed limit is 25 km/h. The Nature Fee e-tag covers both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai.
* **Facilities.** A bench in the shade at the parking area; bring fresh water for rinsing and drinking.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shelf.** From the beach the bottom runs out almost flat at about 4 m for the first 100 m, over finger coral, rubble and scattered coral blocks.
* **Step.** About 110 to 140 m out from the entry, west-northwest, the shelf drops steeply from about 5 m to 11 m.
* **Reef terrace.** Below the step, an uneven reef terrace runs a further 120 m out at 11 to 14 m, with the dive area at 14 m about 186 m from the entry, west-northwest, 294°.
* **Drop-off.** Beyond the terrace the reef falls away steeply into deep water. Take a compass bearing back to the entry before descending, since the beach is narrow and easy to miss from the water.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds, with north winds driving a markedly stronger current. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Moderate to strong, and changeable: it commonly reverses direction during a single dive, so a dive started into the current can end with the current against the swim home. Turn with enough gas to swim back against it.
* **Rip currents.** Water running back out to sea pulls seaward through the shallows, strongest with surf on the beach. Swim across a rip, parallel to the shore, to get out of it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 289° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 297° |

The beach faces west-northwest onto open sea, and the swim out runs 186 m west-northwest, 294°, nearly straight off the beach.

* **Bad.** Southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, building surf on the loose rubble entry and rip currents through the shallows.
* **Mixed.** North through north-northeast (N-NNE), and south-southwest (SSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry. North winds also strengthen the current and stir the shallows.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through south: offshore from northeast through south-southeast over 150 to 190 m, off the land, and cross-shore over 210 m from the south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry at its calmest, usually in the early morning.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m, typically around 25 m, best on calm mornings.
* **Surf and storms.** Surf on the beach clouds the shallows near shore, and after strong onshore wind visibility drops to about 15 m; after a tropical storm the water can stay murky for several days.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm wetsuit suits a long dive; a dive skin or shorty is enough on a short one.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Some of the most pristine coral on Bonaire, remote and lightly dived, with large sponges, groupers, and schools of glassfish around the coral formations.
* **Sand and shallows.** Southern stingrays, peacock flounder and gobies among the rubble and sand channels, and nurse sharks resting in the sand channels.
* **Open water.** Spotted eagle rays along the reef, and on occasion manta rays and hammerhead sharks off the drop-off.
* **Beach.** A sea turtle nesting beach; keep clear of any marked nests.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Washington Slagbaai National Park is closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD measures, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Entry and exit.** Loose coral rubble and stones that roll underfoot in the surf, a sprain and cut risk on every entry and exit. Postpone the dive when waves reach 1 m.
* **Changing current.** Moderate to strong, often reversing during a dive, with rip currents through the shallows. Carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Remote.** Outside mobile phone coverage, and a long unpaved drive from help; leave a dive plan and an expected return time with someone outside the park, and finish diving with time to clear the park gate by 17:00.
