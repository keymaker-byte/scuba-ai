# 2. Playa Benge

A remote beach in Washington Slagbaai National Park fronting some of the most pristine coral on Bonaire, about 22 km northwest of Kralendijk. The shore entry crosses a steep field of finger coral and loose rock that shifts in the swell, difficult enough that a boat entry is the easier way in when the site is open. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering this stretch of the park's coast.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A steep finger-coral shore with about a metre of swell typical; the coral gives way to a field of loose rock the swell keeps moving, making entry and exit the hardest part of the dive. A boat entry avoids this stretch entirely.
* **Parking.** Beside the park road at the beach; unpaved.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's unpaved roads; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Finger coral shelf.** The entry crosses this before reaching the main reef.
* **Depth profile.** The reef here runs from about 4 m in the shallows down to 30 m, with the pristine coral for which the site is known found through that whole range.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** The current can run hard enough to shape the dive plan.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 289° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 297° |

* **Bad.** Southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Any wind from this quarter adds swell to an entry that is already difficult on a calm day.
* **Mixed.** North through north-northeast (N-NNE), and south-southwest (SSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through south: offshore from northeast through south-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 210 m from the south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Some of the most pristine coral on Bonaire, and larger fish species than the busier sites further south.
* **Open water.** Manta rays and hammerhead sharks pass off the site on occasion.
* **Beach.** A sea turtle nesting site.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since 1 May 2023, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Difficult entry and exit.** Steep finger coral into a field of loose, swell-moved rock; a fall here is the main injury risk at this site.
* **Strong current.** Rated for advanced divers even when open.
