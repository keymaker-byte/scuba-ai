# 11. Rappel

One of Bonaire's most popular boat dives, below a sheer cliff on the northwest coast that stands about 20 m above the water. The site takes its name from the early days, when divers rappelled down the cliff on ropes in full scuba gear to reach the reef. A shallow plateau of sea fans and gorgonians, arranged neatly enough to look tended by a gardener, runs out from an undercut at the foot of the cliff to a steep wall that drops far past recreational depth. The site was renamed Bruce's Rappel on 18 October 2021 in honour of Bonaire dive pioneer Bruce Bowker, and is still widely called Rappel. The headline hazards are surge in the undercut and the shallows whenever swell runs onto the cliff, and a boat that rocks hard at its mooring close under the cliff.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride from a boat tied to the permanent mooring close under the cliff, renewed in 2021, then a descent onto the plateau or straight down the wall.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** Boat only. The cliff offers no way down to the water or back up without ropes, so the reef is reached by boat from Kralendijk, about 20 minutes away. The mooring sits close under the cliff and takes careful boat handling. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Cliff and undercut.** The cliff drops sheer into the water. Along its foot, an undercut, carved by the sea when sea level stood lower, runs just below the waterline, like a cave open along one side, with waves breaking overhead and air pockets in its crevices that vanish and return with each wave. In calm water a diver can see well under the overhang.
* **Plateau.** From about 3.5 m at the foot of the cliff the bottom slopes gently out across a coral plateau to about 8 m, roughly 70 m off the cliff. The dive area coordinate sits on the plateau at 7.6 m, about 40 m out from the cliff to the south-southwest.
* **Drop-off.** At the outer edge of the plateau, from about 8 to 10 m, the reef falls away as a steep wall with several small caverns, past 30 m and on past 60 m.
* **Route.** Head out along the wall at depth, then turn and come back shallower over the plateau to find the mooring for the exit.

* **Drivers.** The east trades. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light to still, and strong at times.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 206° |

The dive area lies hard under the cliff, about 40 m off the shore.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 6.3 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves against the cliff, sets the boat rocking at its mooring, makes reboarding rough, and puts surge through the undercut and the shallows.
* **Mixed.** Southeast (SE), cross-shore over 10.1 km down the coast, running a swell along the cliff.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: cross-shore over 240 m from the west-northwest and 120 m from the northwest, offshore from north-northwest through east over 40 to 80 m, off the cliff, and cross-shore over 200 m from the east-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow off the land here and leave the mooring sheltered. Swell in winter and from passing tropical systems still surges the undercut and the shallows under a light wind.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m or more, typically around 25 m.
* **Wind and swell.** Best on calm mornings, lower when wind and swell stir the shallows, as with northern swell in winter and swell from passing tropical systems from June to November.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Plateau.** Large sea fans and gorgonians in every shape and colour, among hard corals and sponges. French angelfish, often in pairs, and queen parrotfish are abundant.
* **Undercut.** Orange cup corals line the shaded overhang and feed with their tentacles open in the low light. Large snappers and the odd nurse shark rest under the overhangs by day.
* **Macro.** Frogfish, seahorses, slender filefish, lettuce sea slugs and juvenile spotted drums, found by moving slowly or hovering over one patch of reef.
* **Wall.** Tiger groupers are seen along the plateau edge and the wall. Barracuda patrol along the wall, at times in small schools, with black durgon and schools of creole wrasse.

## Hazards

* **Surge in the undercut.** Waves breaking against the cliff push a diver in the undercut against the rock. Enter it only in calm water, and stay out of it whenever swell runs onto the cliff.
* **Surge on the plateau.** The shallow plateau surges strongly in swell; keep buoyancy tight and keep clear of the coral.
* **Boat at the mooring.** The mooring sits close under the cliff, and the boat rocks there, hardest in a south or west wind or swell; reboarding can be rough. Brief boat positioning and the ladder exit before entering.
* **No shore exit.** The sheer cliff offers no way out of the water anywhere on the site; every dive ends at the boat.
* **Depth.** The wall drops past 30 m right off the plateau and continues past 60 m, and depth runs up quickly along it. Set a depth limit before descending and stay well inside the NDL.
* **Stony coral tissue loss disease (SCTLD).** Disinfect gear before, between and after every dive per STINAPA's protocol, and keep clear of the coral.
