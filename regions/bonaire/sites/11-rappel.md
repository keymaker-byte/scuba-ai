# 11. Rappel

One of Bonaire's most popular boat dives, named for the era before boat diving when divers used ropes to rappel down the rugged shoreline cliff to reach the water. A shallow plateau extends from the cliff before the reef proper begins, its coral formations and sea fans arranged well enough to look tended by a gardener. STINAPA's own map still lists the site as Rappel; the local dive community renamed it Bruce's Rappel in October 2021 in honour of the late Bruce Bowker.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Northwest coast, Bonaire, south of Karpata |
| **Coordinates** | 12.21745, -68.3438 (about 2 m on the shallow plateau) |
| **Type** | Boat |
| **Depth range** | 9 to 30 m |

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From a boat onto the shallow plateau extending from the cliff.
* **Parking.** Not applicable, boat access only.
* **Access.** Open. The mooring line sits close to the cliff ledge on shore, and boats tend to rock at this mooring; brief divers on boat positioning before entry.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallow plateau.** Extends from the cliff before the wall drops away.
* **Mooring line.** Close to the ledge on shore; the boat's position here is worth noting before descending.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. A logged dive here found slack to negligible current at excellent visibility, consistent with the site's reputation as an easy, popular dive.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The shallow plateau extends from the cliff before the reef drops through the working range to 30 m; a logged dive reached 25.1 m.

## Hazards

* **Boat rocking at the mooring.** The line's proximity to the shore ledge means boats can rock more than usual here; a minor surface hazard during entry and exit rather than an underwater one.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell and worsens the boat's rocking at the mooring.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against any current on the wall is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast; a logged dive rated it 5 out of 5 (excellent).

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October; a logged November dive measured 29 C. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Large sea fans and gorgonians of varied shape and colour, arranged densely enough to look deliberately planted.
