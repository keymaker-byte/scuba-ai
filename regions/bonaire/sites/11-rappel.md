# 11. Rappel

One of Bonaire's most popular boat dives, named for the era before boat diving when divers used ropes to rappel down the rugged shoreline cliff to reach the water. A shallow plateau extends from the cliff before the reef proper begins, its coral formations and sea fans arranged well enough to look tended by a gardener. STINAPA's own map still lists the site as Rappel; the local dive community renamed it Bruce's Rappel in October 2021 in honour of the late Bruce Bowker.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From a boat onto the shallow plateau extending from the cliff.
* **Parking.** Not applicable, boat access only.
* **Access.** Open. The mooring line sits close to the cliff ledge on shore, and boats tend to rock at this mooring; brief divers on boat positioning before entry.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallow plateau.** Extends from the cliff before the wall drops away.
* **Mooring line.** Close to the ledge on shore; the boat's position here is worth noting before descending.
* **Depth profile.** The shallow plateau extends from the cliff before the reef drops through the working range to 30 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Current here usually runs light.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 206° |

The dive area lies hard against the foot of the cliff.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 6.3 km to more than 30 km, along the coast from the south-southeast or across open sea from the south and west. Any wind from this quarter raises swell against the cliff and worsens the boat's rocking at the mooring.
* **Mixed.** Southeast (SE), cross-shore over 10.1 km down the coast, running a swell along the cliff.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the cliff, and cross-shore from the west-northwest, northwest and east-southeast, over 140 m at most.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Large sea fans and gorgonians of varied shape and colour, arranged densely enough to look deliberately planted.

## Hazards

* **Boat rocking at the mooring.** The line's proximity to the shore ledge means boats can rock more than usual here; a minor surface hazard during entry and exit.
