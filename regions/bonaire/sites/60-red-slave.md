# 60. Red Slave

Red Slave is a shore site on EEG Boulevard at the southern tip of Bonaire, the southernmost marked site on the leeward coast, entered beside two rows of small stone slave huts built in 1850 for the workers of the salt pans. A broad sand shelf where spotted eagle rays and stingrays feed runs about 140 m out from the shore to a reef edge at about 12 m, and below it a steep slope of soft coral, sea fans and sand chutes falls past recreational depth. The entry sits about 300 m from the island's southern point, and the headline hazard is a current that is still on some days and strong on others, changes within a dive, and sets a diver who drifts south past the point toward open sea.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk 36 m southwest from the parking, past the southernmost huts, and down a berm of coral rubble to the water. The edge is a low step of ironshore and rubble, slippery with algae, and the bottom just inside it is irregular rubble before it turns to sand. Walk the path once without gear, time the entry between waves, and put fins on as soon as the water is deep enough. Swim 104 m west-southwest (240°) to the dive area on the outer shelf, and on about 40 m to the reef edge. Exit at the same place. A second path reaches the water near a ruined house about 200 m north-northwest along the shore, with a longer swim to the reef edge and often smaller waves than at the huts.
* **Parking.** Free, on the coral gravel beside the huts, with room for about 8 to 10 cars. Keep vehicles on the gravel and off the sand, where turtles nest.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. The huts are protected monuments; keep off the structures.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The slave huts.** Two rows of small coral stone huts, painted ochre and barely tall enough to crawl into, stand between EEG Boulevard and the shore. A painted obelisk nearby, one of four markers that guided the salt ships to their loading points, also marks the site. The huts stand right above the entry and mark it from the water.
* **The shelf.** About 4 m deep for the first 70 m from the entry, then sloping to 7 m at 85 m and 10 m at the dive area, 104 m out. Wide sand channels separate patches of soft coral, sea fans and gorgonians, with low ledges where fish hide. The shelf holds at 8 to 10 m along the shore to the northwest and shallows to 4 to 5 m toward the southern point.
* **The reef edge.** About 140 m west-southwest of the entry, at about 12 m.
* **The reef slope.** Falls steeply from the edge through a forest of soft coral and sea fans, with large sponges, and runs well past recreational depth. Sand chutes run down it at intervals; count them on the way out and count them back on the return.
* **The anchors.** Historic anchors from the salt trade lie on the deep slope from about 40 to 60 m and deeper, beyond recreational range.
* **The southern point.** The shore turns east about 300 m southeast of the entry, and Willemstoren Lighthouse stands about 1.6 km east. Every easy exit lies at or north of the entry.
* **The return.** Come back up the slope onto the shelf and head east-northeast, 104 m from the dive area to the entry.

## Current

| | |
|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | southeast, 145°, along the shore toward the southern point |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. A strong trade drives the set southeast past the point. On many days the water is still or slight, and on some the set runs northwest along the shore.
* **Character.** The current changes direction and strength within minutes, and runs in layers: the surface and the water at depth can differ in both strength and direction. It is often strongest along the reef slope and through the sand chutes, and a calm surface says little about the water below.
* **Past the point.** A southeast set carries a diver along the reef, past the southern point and away from the shore toward open sea, where the coast turns onto a rough windward shore with no easy exit.
* **Planning.** Check the set before descending. On a southeast set, start northwest into it and turn back early so the return runs with it. On a northwest set, keep the dive north of the entry and drift with the set only as far as the gas for the swim back allows. Exits lie along the shore to the north.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 235° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 235° |

The swim out runs 104 m west-southwest from the entry, 5° off the shore's facing, straight out. The shore turns east at the island's southern point, about 300 m southeast of the entry.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the rubble edge, puts surf and surge on the entry and exit, and stirs sand off the shelf.
* **Short fetch.** East through east-southeast (E-ESE), offshore over 120 to 250 m. The trade wind swell wraps the southern point onto the entry, so a strong trade puts breaking waves on the edge, and it drives the southeast set past the point.
* **Mixed.** Southeast through south-southeast (SE-SSE), cross-shore over more than 30 km past the southern point, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through east-northeast (NW-ENE): offshore from north through east-northeast, off the land over 100 to 190 m, and cross-shore over 400 m to 1.1 km from the northwest and north-northwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m, typically about 20 m, best on calm mornings.
* **The shelf.** Surge and competing currents lift sand off the broad shallows and cut visibility there, and waves from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) do the same near the entry.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Sand shelf.** Spotted eagle rays feed in the sand, leaving deep, wide holes, and are a regular sight here, best in the early morning. Southern stingrays rest on the sand, with flounders and schools of palometa. Green and hawksbill turtles are seen on most dives.
* **Shallows.** Seahorses cling to soft coral and small sea fans from about 6 m.
* **Reef slope.** A dense forest of soft coral, sea fans and large sponges. Groupers, large snapper, schools of yellow snapper and midnight parrotfish, black durgon, French grunts, goatfish, creole wrasse, brown chromis, queen and French angelfish, filefish and pufferfish along the slope, with green morays and octopus in the coral.
* **Open water.** Schools of bigeye scad (masbango in Papiamentu) hunted by larger predators, horse-eye jacks, tuna, large barracuda and schools of small barracuda. Dolphins pass offshore at times.
* **Hard coral.** Hard corals along the whole leeward coast have suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and stony coral tissue loss disease.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Still on some days and strong on others, changing in direction and strength within a dive, and setting southeast past the southern point toward open sea. On a southeast set start northwest into it, on a northwest set stay north of the entry, turn with enough gas for the return, and end the dive at once when it runs harder than planned. Carry a surface marker buoy and a signalling device, and leave the dive plan with someone ashore.
* **Surf and surge.** Wind from south through west-northwest (S-WNW), a passing swell, or a strong trade wrapping the point puts surf and surge on the rubble edge. Watch the waves from shore before kitting up, and choose another site when they break on the entry.
* **The entry and exit.** Slippery ironshore and rubble, a step down into the water, and an uneven bottom inside it. Wear sturdy boots, take the entry slowly, and come back to the same place.
* **Depth.** The reef slope keeps falling well past recreational depth. Watch the computer, stay above EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar, and keep within the no-decompression limit.
* **Remote site.** The southern tip has no facilities and few passers-by, so a diver in trouble at the surface may wait a long time for help.
* **Turtle nesting.** Stay off the sand with vehicles, and keep clear of any marked nest.
