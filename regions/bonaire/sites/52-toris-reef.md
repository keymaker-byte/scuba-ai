# 52. Tori's Reef

Directly opposite the outflow from the Cargill salt works, where fresh outlet water meets the sea and draws an exceptional concentration of fish, second in the entire Caribbean for fish diversity behind only Bari's Reef. The shore entry runs through a channel under the highway onto the reef; check the aggregations of fire coral on the way out, home to many rare fish and eels. Beyond it, strands of elkhorn coral, thinned along with the rest of Bonaire's since the 2023 SCTLD outbreak and the bleaching events that followed, cross a sandy bottom, with soft coral abundant in the shallows just past the mooring.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Through a channel that passes under the highway before opening onto the reef, a fairly easy shore entry.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The channel.** Runs under the highway from the road to the reef, the entry route.
* **Fire coral aggregations.** On the way out to the reef; worth checking closely for the rare fish and eels hiding there, but not for touching.
* **Salt works outflow.** Directly opposite the site, the source of its unusually rich fish life.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. Elkhorn coral strands cross the sandy bottom through the recreational range to 30 m.

## Hazards

* **Fire coral.** Aggregations sit along the route out to the reef; avoid contact.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces southwest.
* **Bad.** Any wind with south or west in it raises swell.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east trade winds are favourable here.
* **Wind against current.** A building southerly against any current here is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

Typically 15 to 30 m, in line with the rest of the leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Overall.** Ranked #2 in the Caribbean for fish diversity; schools of brown chromis and Creole wrasse, with abundant eel, octopus, barracuda and seahorse.
* **Fire coral zone.** Rare fish and eels hiding among the aggregations.
* **Shallows near the mooring.** Abundant soft coral.
