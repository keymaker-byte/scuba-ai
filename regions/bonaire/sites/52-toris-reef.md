# 52. Tori's Reef

A shore dive on EEG Boulevard below the Cargill salt works, entered through the drainage channel that runs under the road at the second of two red and white bridges and links the salt pans to the sea. The channel opens onto a wide sand terrace, foraged by spotted eagle rays and stingrays, that slopes to the edge of the reef at about 10 m, 162 m out; the reef top runs on at 10 to 15 m before dropping away into deep water. Its fish diversity ranks second in the Caribbean. The headline hazard is the gate in the channel, which can draw water through it strongly enough to pull a diver to the far side.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk from the roadside to the seaward side of the bridge and step down off the flat rocks into the channel, onto a sandy bottom; booties help on the rocks. Keep well clear of the gate in the channel. Swim out the shallow channel onto the sand terrace and on 162 m west-northwest to the reef, close to straight out from the shore. Exit back through the channel mouth.
* **Parking.** Along the roadside on either side of the second bridge, usually with plenty of room, about 20 m from the entry. Free.
* **Access.** Open. Yellow painted stones mark the site at the bridge. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The bridge and the channel.** The second, southern red and white bridge on the coast road south of Salt Pier carries the road over a drainage channel from the salt pans, closed off by a gate. The channel runs shallow over sand from the bridge to the sea.
* **The channel mouth.** A low, squat wall runs along the shore either side of the mouth and is hard to climb. On the return, the mouth is hard to hit squarely coming in over sand; aim a little to one side, meet the wall, and follow it to the opening.
* **The sand terrace.** A broad sand patch shelving from about 4 m at the channel mouth to about 10 m at the reef, with strands of elkhorn, aggregations of fire coral, scattered coral heads and outplanted staghorn along the way.
* **The mooring.** A mooring buoy about 70 m out over the sand, in about 8 m, with abundant soft coral in the shallows just beyond it.
* **The reef.** Begins at about 10 m, 162 m west-northwest of the entry, and runs on as a reef top at 10 to 15 m for about 120 m before dropping away steeply. The second reef beyond lies past 40 m.
* **North.** The reef extends both ways from the entry. Heading north, the shallow reef shelf runs about 600 m up the coast toward a large coral mound, and the drop-off steepens.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current is usually light or absent along the reef, and runs along the shore when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **The channel.** When the salt works open the gate, water flows through the channel and across the shallows, from limited to strong, and at irregular times. Check the flow in the channel before entering.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 280° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 280° |

The swim out runs 162 m west-northwest from the entry, 5° off the shore's facing, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** Southwest through north-northwest (SW-NNW), onshore over 9.6 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises surf and surge at the channel mouth, makes the step in off the rocks awkward and stirs the sand terrace.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest (SSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south (N-S): offshore from northeast through south-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 530 m to 1.3 km from the north and north-northeast and over 810 m from the south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, around 20 m on an average day, and best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** Surf at the channel mouth stirs up the sand terrace and can leave the shallows murky.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Channel and shallows.** Crowds of juvenile fish shelter in the shallows close to shore, with octopus, banded butterflyfish, sergeant majors and schools of juvenile grunts and snapper. Snorkellers find the most fish heading right, north, close to shore.
* **Sand terrace.** Spotted eagle rays dig for food in the sand, with southern stingrays, flounders, spotted trunkfish, sand divers and palometas around the mooring. Eels and less common reef fish hide in the fire coral.
* **Reef.** Soft corals and sponges with brain, lettuce and staghorn coral, among parrotfish, French and queen angelfish, trumpetfish, goatfish, boxfish, pufferfish and porcupinefish, spotted drums and scorpionfish. Large schools of brown chromis and juvenile creole wrasse hang over the reef.
* **Eels.** Green, spotted, chain and goldentail morays, with large green morays sometimes crossing the reef in the open.
* **Larger animals.** Green turtles are common, with the occasional hawksbill, barracuda, bar jacks, black margates and large black groupers.
* **Coral.** Staghorn outplanted by Reef Renewal Bonaire grows in patches on the terrace. Bleaching and disease since 2023 have cut hard coral cover along the leeward coast, and the elkhorn strands on the terrace share in the island wide elkhorn loss.

## Hazards

* **The channel gate.** Water flows through the gate when the salt works open it, and can draw a diver who enters too close to it through to the other side. Enter well seaward of the gate, check the flow first, and choose another site when water is running hard through the channel.
* **Entry and exit.** Slippery flat rocks at the step in, and surge in the channel mouth when a swell is running onto the shore. Time the entry between the larger sets, and choose another site in heavy surf.
* **The channel mouth wall.** Low but hard to climb out over; return to the opening itself.
* **Depth.** Past the reef top the reef drops away steeply into deep water. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Fire coral.** Aggregations sit along the swim out across the terrace; look closely, but keep hands and fins clear.
* **Scorpionfish and lionfish.** Both are venomous; scorpionfish lie camouflaged on the reef and rubble, and lionfish shelter in the reef. Watch where hands and fins go near the bottom.
* **Boats.** Dive boats tie up to the mooring. Ascend with a surface marker buoy away from it.
