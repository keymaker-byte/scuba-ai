# 23. Andrea I

A shore dive on Bonaire's west coast about 5 km north-northwest of Kralendijk, at the end of a concrete road through a residential development and entered through a sand channel beside a yellow-painted rock. A shallow terrace of rubble, fire coral and patch reef runs out about 55 m before stepping down to a coral ledge at 10 to 12 m and a slope of hard and soft coral beyond. Seahorses hide in the soft corals, and large blue, midnight and rainbow parrotfish feed in the shallows. The headline hazards are the slick, rocky entry, with sea urchins and fire coral in the first metres, and a wind driven current along the shore.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the parking area, walk about 35 m north along the beach to the yellow-painted rock beside the thatched shelter. A sand channel beside the rock leads into the water, and the bottom there is sand and coral gravel at about 4 m within a few metres of the shore. Loose stones and very slick rock at the water's edge make the first steps the hardest part of the dive, and thick-soled boots are needed. Many divers put fins on once afloat.
* **Parking.** From Kaya Gobernador N. Debrot, turn into the residential development at the yellow stone marked "Andrea 1 + 2", take the left fork and follow the yellow stones down the concrete road to the small parking area at its foot beside the beach. Space is limited; when it is full, cars park higher up the road. Leave nothing of value in the vehicle.
* **Access.** Open to diving from shore, and by boat from the site mooring. A popular snorkel site. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** A thatched shade shelter on the beach, with shade under the trees along the shore.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Yellow rock.** The yellow-painted rock marks the entry and the only practical exit; the rocky shore either side is lined with shallow coral and fire coral. Fix the rock and the thatched shelter by eye from the water before descending.
* **Mooring buoy.** The site mooring sits off the entry and gives a bearing for the descent.
* **Shallow terrace.** Sand, coral gravel and the skeletons of old elkhorn run out from the entry at 4 to 5 m for about 55 m, with fire coral and patch reef. Close inshore to the north the terrace shallows to about 3 m.
* **Terrace edge.** About 55 m off the entry the terrace drops to 9 m within a few metres, onto a ledge at 10 to 12 m, the main dive area, 83 m west of the entry.
* **Slope.** Past the ledge, about 125 m out, the reef slopes on through 30 m and past 35 m to sand, a mix of hard and soft coral with sea fans, sea plumes and sponges, cut by sand channels.
* **Along the shore.** About 60 m out the depth holds at 8 to 10 m for 200 m either side of the entry, and the shelf grows broader to the north. The slope can be followed either way; start into the current so the return runs with it.

## Current

| | |
|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | south-southeast, 167°, along the shore |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs stronger when the trades strengthen and sits near still on calm days. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light, and strong at times. It runs along the shore, most often toward the south-southeast, and can carry a diver well along the coast from the entry.
* **On the day.** The mooring buoy and the surface show any set right after entry. Start the dive into the current so the return to the yellow rock runs with it, and surface within sight of the beach.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 257° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 257° |

The dive area lies 83 m west of the entry, straight out from the entry shore.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW), onshore over 9.3 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises chop and swell on the rocky entry.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over 2.4 to 3.7 km across to Klein Bonaire, and northwest (NW), onshore over 2.7 km along the coast, each enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 270 m to 2.9 km from the north-northwest, north, south-southeast and south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 20 to 30 m, at times past 30 m, and down to about 10 m when the water carries suspended particles. Best on calm early mornings.
* **Swell.** Wind and swell onto the shore stir the sand and rubble terrace and cut the visibility there; the water clears over the slope.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C through the year, coolest from February to March and warmest from August to October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Seahorses.** Seahorses hide among the soft corals, gorgonians and sponges, most often on the ledge and upper slope, from about 10 m down.
* **Shallows.** All three of Bonaire's large parrotfish, the blue, the midnight and the rainbow, each about a metre long, feed across the terrace, alongside schools of blue tang and Bermuda chub, French angelfish, trumpetfish, scrawled filefish, honeycomb cowfish, four-eye butterflyfish and territorial damselfish. Reef squid hover over the terrace, most often seen in the shallows at the end of a dive. Green turtles graze the shallows.
* **Reef.** Anemones with cleaner shrimp, blennies, spotted drums and juvenile smooth trunkfish among the coral, with green, spotted, chain and goldentail morays, Spanish lobster, octopus, tiger tail sea cucumbers and sea slugs, the lettuce sea slug among them.
* **Larger animals.** Large barracuda and the occasional tarpon over the slope, with smaller groupers, and the odd spotted eagle ray off the deeper reef.
* **Coral.** Boulder, brain, pillar, starlet and orange cup coral on the terrace and ledge, with sea fans and sea plumes. Much of the shallow elkhorn is dead, leaving skeletons on the terrace, and the staghorn survives in scattered patches at 3 to 4 m. Elkhorn and staghorn across the island have declined sharply since 2023.

## Hazards

* **The entry.** Loose stones and very slick rock at the water's edge, with sea urchins hidden between the rocks and fire coral in the first metres. Keep to the sand channel by the yellow rock, and turn back when waves are breaking on the shore.
* **Exit.** The yellow rock is the only practical exit; elsewhere the shore is lined with shallow coral and fire coral. Navigate back to it, and surface to take a bearing when unsure.
* **Current.** The along-shore current can build during a dive and set a diver well down the coast from the entry. Start the dive into it so the return to the yellow rock runs with it, and surface within sight of the beach.
* **Boats.** Dive boats use the mooring, and boat traffic over the site is common. A surface marker buoy shows a diver's position away from the entry.
* **Vehicle theft.** Vehicles parked at this site have been broken into; leave them empty.
