# 57. Atlantis

Atlantis is a shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 12 km south of Kralendijk on Bonaire's southwest coast, at Atlantis Beach, also called Kite Beach, the one place inside the marine park where kitesurfing is allowed. A short sand beach in front of the kite schools gives onto a broad sand and coral terrace that runs about 160 m out to a reef edge at about 11 m, where the reef drops away down a slope of soft corals, sponges and hard coral to a deep shelf, with turtles, rays and larger fish among its regulars. There is no mooring and no buoy line. The headline hazard is the kitesurfers, foilers and rescue boats that work the water over the site whenever the wind blows, which makes it a site for windless days, followed by a current that runs stronger here than at most of the leeward coast.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** The approach from the road to the beach is firm and dry, and the beach itself is short and sandy. Just inside the waterline the shore drops over a low rock ledge and a band of eroded ridges, edged with fire coral, before the bottom turns to smooth sand; pick a line through it, help a buddy in and put fins on once on the sand. The rock ramps down most gently a little north or south of a line straight out from the access road. The swim runs 124 m west-southwest to the dive area on the terrace and about 160 m to the reef edge. Exit at the same stretch of beach.
* **Parking.** Roadside on the hard-packed coral and the parking strips beside EEG Boulevard, free. The strips fill on weekends and holidays.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Kitesurfing is allowed inside the marine park only in the entry and exit zone at Atlantis Beach. The kite schools are at the beach daily from 09:30 to 17:00, and kiters and foilers fill the water through the middle of the day on windy days, busiest from about 10:00 to 16:00. Dive on a windless day, best before 09:30 or after 17:00, outside the kite schools' hours.
* **Facilities.** The two kite schools' buses at the beach, which serve their own customers only.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry.** The kite schools' buses and the end of the short access road off EEG Boulevard. With no mooring or buoy line, take a compass bearing on the way out and note the buses and the parked cars for the return.
* **The terrace.** Sand with scattered coral heads, shelving from about 4 m just past the ledge to 7 m at 60 m out, 10 m at 90 m out and about 9.5 m at the dive area 124 m out, then holding at 10 to 11 m to the reef edge about 160 m west-southwest of the entry. The sand area is wide here. Descend once on the sand and swim the terrace along the bottom, clear of the surface in the kite zone.
* **The drop-off.** The reef edge at about 11 m, where the hard and soft corals are at their best. The slope below carries a forest of soft corals and sponges from the edge to about 18 m, and patchier coral and fish beneath it.
* **The deep shelf.** The slope reaches a shelf at about 30 to 38 m, reaching past EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth, which slopes on to about 45 to 50 m before a second drop.
* **Which way.** The usual set runs north-northwest along the shore, so a dive started against it runs south along the edge first; check the current at the reef edge before choosing, since it can reverse and often runs differently on the slope than on the terrace.
* **The return.** Come back up the slope onto the terrace and head east-northeast, about 160 m from the reef edge to the beach, and come up close to shore only after checking the surface overhead.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The site sits toward the south end of the leeward coast and runs more current than most of it, though it is usually moderate, and the set can reverse to run south along the shore.
* **Depth.** The current often runs in a different direction on the deeper slope than on the terrace, and stronger there at times. Check it at the reef edge before descending the slope.
* **The return.** With the usual set, a dive that starts south against the current comes back with it, and the return along the edge can turn into a drift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 245° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 245° |

The swim out runs 124 m west-southwest from the entry, 6° off the shore's facing, straight out. The shore lies open to more than 30 km of sea from south-southeast through northwest, cross-shore over 300 m from the southeast and 590 m from the north-northwest, and offshore from north through east-southeast.

* **Bad.** All directions (N-NNW), calm only. The east trades blow offshore here, the reason the beach is the island's kitesurfing site: any wind strong enough to kite fills the water over the site with kites, foilers and rescue boats, and onshore wind from the south through west-northwest (S-WNW) drives waves onto the beach and the ledge at the entry. The site is dived only when there is no wind.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m, typically about 25 m, best on calm early mornings.
* **The shallows.** Wind and waves stir the sand on the terrace and cut visibility near shore.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Green and hawksbill turtles, commonly seen, especially where the sand meets the reef. Stingrays half buried in the sand, smooth trunkfish and other boxfish over the bottom, and chromis and juvenile fish around the coral heads and ridges in the shallows.
* **Reef edge and slope.** Parrotfish, queen and French angelfish with their juveniles, wrasses and grunts, schools of blue tang and other surgeonfish, and black durgon. Green morays in the coral. Tiger groupers and barracuda on the slope, jacks and spotted eagle rays passing along it, and larger fish in the blue off the edge.
* **Coral.** Hard and soft corals arranged along the reef edge, and a forest of soft corals and sponges down the slope.

## Hazards

* **Kitesurfers, foilers and boats.** The kite zone sits right over the entry, and on windy days kiters, foilers and the kite schools' rescue boats run across the terrace and the reef edge. Dive only on a windless day, best before 09:30 or after 17:00, swim out along the bottom, deploy a surface marker buoy from depth before ascending, and hold at the safety stop until the surface overhead is clear.
* **The entry and exit.** The rock ledge and eroded ridges just inside the waterline, with fire coral on them. Wear sturdy boots, help a buddy in and out, and pick the line through to the sand.
* **Current.** Stronger here than along most of the leeward coast, at times reversed, and often in a different direction on the slope than on the terrace. Check it at the reef edge, start the dive against it so the return runs with it, and turn on gas with the 160 m swim home over the terrace still to come.
* **Depth.** The slope reaches a shelf at about 30 to 38 m, reaching past EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar. Watch the computer on the slope and keep within the no-decompression limit, which is short at that depth.
* **Navigation.** The site is unmarked underwater. Navigate by compass, out and back.
* **Surf and swell.** Wind from the south through west-northwest (S-WNW) drives waves onto the beach and surge over the ledge at the entry; choose another site when it blows from that quarter or a swell is running.
