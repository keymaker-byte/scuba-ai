# 35. 18th Palm

The house reef of Plaza Resort on Bonaire's west coast south of Kralendijk, named for the palms in front of the Lt. Governor's house beside it. A sandy beach at the resort's northwest end opens onto a shallow terrace and a reef edge at about 9 m, and here the reef splits into a double reef: an inner reef close to shore, separated by a sandy channel from an outer reef running along the bottom at about 27 m. Tarpon rest in the channel by day and hunt in divers' lights at night. The headline hazards are the depth of the outer reef and boat traffic around the resort's marina.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the sandy beach at the resort's northwest end, which slopes gently into the water. The bottom at the water's edge is uneven, so step in with care. The dive area lies 84 m west-northwest across the terrace. Exit at the same beach.
* **Parking.** At Plaza Resort, included in a resort stay or the day pass. The carry from the parking area and the dive shop to the beach is long; Toucan Diving stages filled tanks near the beach for its customers, and wheelbarrows help with the rest.
* **Access.** The site lies inside the resort grounds, behind a guarded gate. Resort guests dive it freely. Other divers need Plaza's day pass, $125 to $163 a person by season and valid 10:00 to 18:00; confirm access for divers with Toucan Diving before going. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** Plaza Resort, with a dive shop, freshwater showers and rinse tanks near the beach, restaurants and bars.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The palms.** The Lt. Governor's house beside the resort has at least eighteen palms in its front yard, the landmark that gives the site its name.
* **The terrace.** Sand and rubble deepening from about 3 m off the beach to about 7 m at the dive area, 84 m out, then to the reef edge at about 9 m some 20 m farther out.
* **The double reef.** Beyond the edge the reef splits in two: an inner reef close to shore, separated by a sandy channel from an outer reef running along the bottom at about 27 m.
* **The reef line.** The reef runs north and south along the shore and reaches farther out to the south.
* **The return.** The beach lies 84 m east-southeast of the dive area; come back across the terrace to it.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild or barely felt, which makes the site a common choice for training and night dives.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 301° |

The swim out runs 84 m west-northwest from the beach, close to straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, and north-northwest (NNW), onshore over 6.9 km down the coast. Wind from these quarters raises waves on the beach and stirs up the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** West-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), onshore over 2.5 to 2.6 km from Klein Bonaire, and north (N), onshore over 1.1 km across the bay off town, each enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open sea, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest (NNE-SSW): offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, and cross-shore over 120 to 350 m from the north-northeast, northeast and south-southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the beach calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on a typical day, best on calm mornings.
* **Wind.** Strong wind, onshore wind above all, stirs up the sand of the terrace and cuts the visibility in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Sand channel and sand.** Tarpon rest in the sand channel during the day, often several together. Southern stingrays lie in the sand and spotted eagle rays pass over it.
* **Reef.** Green and spotted morays among outcrops and in crevices, groupers, barracuda, French angelfish, parrotfish and honeycomb cowfish, with large schools of chromis toward the south end of the reef. Fire coral grows in the shallows. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Turtles.** Green turtles are regular on the reef.
* **Macro.** Frogfish among the sponges, seahorses on the reef, and octopus in holes in the reef.
* **Night.** Tarpon hunt in divers' lights, octopus forage in the open, and spiny lobsters leave their holes.

## Hazards

* **Depth.** The outer reef lies at about 27 m and the slope runs on past 30 m. Turn at a planned depth inside the no-decompression limit and the maximum operating depth of the gas.
* **Boat traffic.** The resort's marina and lagoon open at its south end, and dive boats moor over the reef. Stay down until back over the terrace, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Fire coral.** Grows in the shallows on the way out and back; keep hands and knees clear of it.
* **The entry.** The bottom at the water's edge is uneven; step in and out with care, fins in hand.
* **Current.** Usually mild, and stronger when the trades pick up; check it from the surface before entering.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from west-southwest through west (WSW-W) and north-northwest (NNW) puts waves on the beach, from west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW) and north (N) a chop, and from the southwest (SW) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
