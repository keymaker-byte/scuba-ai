# 56. Red Beryl

Red Beryl is a shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 11.5 km south of Kralendijk on Bonaire's southwest coast, at the north end of Atlantis Beach, the island's kitesurfing beach. A short walk and an entry over sand and loose rock give onto a sand and coral terrace that shelves gently for about 150 m before the reef drops away down a coral wall of soft corals and gorgonians, with turtles and rays among its regulars. The wall runs on to sand at about 33 to 35 m and a second drop below recreational range. The headline hazard is the kitesurfers and foilers who work the water beside the site through the middle of the day, followed by the depth of the wall.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A sandy path leads about 25 m from the roadside to a narrow beach of sand and loose rock, where a large grey rock at the waterline marks the entry. Just past it a shallow rock shelf edged with fire coral lies close to the surface; wade in carefully with a buddy's shoulder to hold, put fins on once deep enough, and swim out. The swim runs 86 m west-southwest to the dive area on the terrace and about 150 m to the reef edge. Exit at the same rock.
* **Parking.** Roadside on the coral rubble beside EEG Boulevard, on the sea side of the road, by the yellow-painted stone that marks the site, free. Room for several cars; keep off the sand.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Kitesurfing is allowed inside the marine park only in the entry and exit zone at Atlantis Beach, which begins at the site and runs south; kiters and foilers are active from about 10:00 to 16:00 on windy days, so dive before 10:00 or after 16:00.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry.** The yellow-painted stone by the road and the large grey rock at the waterline. Note the shore around the rock on the way out; the beach either side is rockier.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral rubble with scattered coral heads, gorgonians and soft corals, and patches of staghorn and elkhorn, shelving from about 4 m just off the rock shelf to 8 m at 70 m out, 10 m at the dive area 86 m out, and 11 m at the reef edge, about 150 m west-southwest of the entry. The first stretch to about 8 m is fairly bare. A forest of soft coral begins at about 8 m, about 70 m out, and runs on to the edge.
* **The drop-off.** The reef edge at about 11 m, where a cut in the coral at the shoulder serves as a landmark for the return. The wall below carries dense coral from about 12 to 30 m, cut by sand channels, with soft corals, gorgonians and sponges.
* **The deep sand.** The wall reaches sand at about 33 to 35 m, at and past EAN32's maximum operating depth, with a second drop to about 50 m below it.
* **Which way.** The kite zone lies to the south. Turn north along the wall to stay clear of it. The usual set runs the same way, so the outbound leg is down-current: turn early on gas and keep the swim back in hand. When the current runs south, the northward leg starts into it.
* **The return.** Come back up the wall onto the terrace and head east-northeast, about 150 m from the reef edge to the grey rock.

| **Usual set** | northwest, 323°, along the shore |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current is usually limited, and runs fairly strong at times when the trades strengthen.
* **Depth.** The current can run stronger along the deeper wall than on the terrace, at times in a different direction. Check it at the reef edge before descending the wall.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 235° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 233° |

The swim out runs 86 m west-southwest from the entry, 3° off the shore's facing, straight out.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the beach, puts surge on the rock shelf at the entry and stirs up the shallows.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast (SSE), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through southeast (NW-SE): offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 1.4 km from the northwest, 270 m from the north-northwest and 320 m from the southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry flat at their usual strength, but the same wind brings kitesurfers onto the water beside the site.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m on the wall, typically about 25 m, best on calm mornings.
* **The shallows.** Stronger trades and waves from the south through west-northwest stir the sand on the terrace and cut visibility near shore, while the wall stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Green turtles grazing in the shallows, often more than one. Stingrays and peacock flounder on the sand, garden eels, barracuda over the sand, and many juvenile fish among the fire coral and gorgonians. Lettuce sea slugs, arrow crabs and corkscrew anemones in the coral heads, and octopus.
* **Wall.** French and other angelfish, butterflyfish, parrotfish, grunts, blue tang, palometa, filefish, dog snapper and occasional groupers. Green morays, with spotted and chain morays in the shallower coral. Scorpionfish and frogfish among the sponges. Barracuda along the wall and schooling fish feeding near the surface off it. Hawksbill turtles on occasion.
* **Night.** Green morays hunting in the open from twilight, octopus and arrow crabs. A few nights after the full moon, ostracods put on their light displays above the soft coral at about 8 m just after dark.
* **Coral.** Soft corals and gorgonians cover the terrace edge and the wall, with hard coral among them. Elkhorn and staghorn in the shallows have suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and disease, as along the whole leeward coast.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Kitesurfers and foilers.** The kite zone at Atlantis Beach begins at the site, and kiters, foilers and the kite schools' boats run over it from about 10:00 to 16:00 on windy days. Dive before 10:00 or after 16:00, turn north rather than south along the wall, deploy a surface marker buoy from depth before ascending, and hold at the safety stop until the surface overhead is clear.
* **The entry and exit.** Loose rock on the beach and the shallow rock shelf edged with fire coral just past the waterline. Wear sturdy boots, help a buddy in and out, and pick the line in past the grey rock.
* **Depth.** The wall runs on past 30 m to sand at about 33 to 35 m, at and past EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar. Watch the computer on the wall, and keep within the no-decompression limit, which is short at that depth.
* **Current.** Usually limited, and fairly strong at times, stronger and sometimes in a different direction on the deeper wall. Check it at the reef edge, plan the return against it, and turn on gas with the 150 m swim home over the terrace still to come.
* **Surf and swell.** Wind from the south through west-northwest (S-WNW) drives waves onto the beach and surge over the rock shelf at the entry; choose another site when it blows fresh from that quarter or a swell is running.
