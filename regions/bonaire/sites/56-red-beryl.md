# 56. Red Beryl

A versatile shore dive about 3 km south of Kralendijk, marked by a row of yellow buoys next to Atlantis Beach, the island's main kitesurfing spot. The profile runs in three clear stages: a shallow sandy plateau to about 8 m good for a buoyancy check, a middle zone from 8 to 20 m where hard and soft coral thicken with cleaning stations, and a steep wall beyond 20 m running well past recreational range. Dive before 10:00 or after 16:00, and use extra caution on windy days, to stay clear of the kitesurfers who work the beach beside the site.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A shore entry marked by a row of yellow buoys.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open, but dive before 10:00 or after 16:00 and use extra caution on windy days to avoid the kitesurfers at neighbouring Atlantis Beach.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Sandy plateau.** The shallow zone to about 8 m, good for a buoyancy check before heading out.
* **The wall.** Begins around 20 m and runs well past recreational limits.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The sandy plateau sits to about 8 m, a coral transition zone runs from 8 to 20 m, and the wall begins beyond that.

## Hazards

* **Kitesurfers.** Active at the neighbouring beach; dive before 10:00 or after 16:00 and watch for kite lines on the surface, especially on a windy day.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces south.
* **Bad.** Wind is also what brings the kitesurfers out; treat a windy day as a reason for extra caution even where the swell itself is manageable.
* **Fine.** Calm or light wind conditions, when kite traffic is minimal.
* **Wind against current.** A building southerly against any current here is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

Typically 15 to 30 m, in line with the rest of the leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Overall.** Barracuda, rays, sea turtles, scorpionfish, boxfish, pufferfish, surgeonfish and moray eels.
* **Middle zone.** Cleaning stations at the transition between coral communities.
