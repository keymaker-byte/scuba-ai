# 22. Andrea II

A shore dive on Bonaire's west coast about 5 km northwest of Kralendijk, reached through a residential development on the coast road and entered over a narrow rocky beach. A shallow terrace of sand, rubble and old coral runs out to a reef slope where plate coral, sea fans and soft corals grow, and the site is one of the island's better places to find a seahorse. Large blue, midnight and rainbow parrotfish feed in the shallows. The headline hazards are the rocky entry, with sea urchins and fire coral in the first metres, and a wind driven current along the shore.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A short walk from the parking area to the beach, entering between the two large yellow stones, where a rock cairn topped with a yellow stone marks the way. The beach is narrow and rocky, with holes between the rocks; a sandy gap between the rocks makes the easiest line into the water. Enter one at a time, the first diver in the water steadying the second. Thick-soled boots are needed.
* **Parking.** From Kaya Gobernador N. Debrot, turn into the residential development at the yellow wall and the yellow stone marked "Andrea 1 + 2", then follow the yellow stones, a right turn and then a left, down the paved road to the parking area at its end beside the beach. Leave nothing of value in the vehicle.
* **Access.** Open to diving from shore, and by boat from the site mooring. The paved access road ends abruptly at the beach, where erosion has at times opened a drop between the road and the sand that low vehicles struggle to cross; STINAPA rebuilt the crossing in July 2026. A popular snorkel site, and dived at night. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** Shade shelters on the beach.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Yellow stones.** The two large yellow stones at the water's edge mark the entry and exit, and are worth fixing by eye from the water before descending, since the low rocky shore looks much the same along its length.
* **Mooring buoy.** A yellow mooring buoy sits straight out from the entry, over the edge of the terrace.
* **Shallow terrace.** Sand, rubble and the skeletons of old elkhorn coral run out from the entry at 4 to 5 m for about 40 m, with scattered fire coral and coral heads. Living hard and soft coral begins at about 6 m, some 50 m out.
* **Reef break.** About 70 m off the entry the terrace reaches 8 m and the reef begins to slope, through 11 m at 90 m out to 14 m about 100 m out, where the dive area coordinate sits on the upper slope.
* **Slope.** The reef slopes on down through 30 m and past 35 m, with the healthiest coral in the middle depths, where large plate coral formations stand around 20 m among sea fans, whip coral and brain coral, with sand patches between them.
* **Along the shore.** The terrace is broad here: 50 m out it holds at 6 to 8 m for 100 m either side of the entry, and shallows to about 5 m some 200 m along the shore in either direction. The slope can be followed either way along the shore; start into the current so the return runs with it.

## Current

|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | Not established |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light, and strong at times. It runs along the shore, toward the north-northwest, 329°, or the south-southeast, 149°, and can carry a diver well along the coast from the entry.
* **On the day.** The mooring buoy line shows any set right after entry. Start the dive into the current so the return to the yellow stones runs with it, and surface within sight of the beach.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 239° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 239° |

The dive area lies 100 m west-southwest of the entry, straight out from the entry shore.

* **Bad.** Southwest through west-northwest (SW-WNW), onshore over 8.5 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises chop and swell on the rocky entry.
* **Short fetch.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), onshore over 2.7 to 2.8 km across to Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 270 m to 3.3 km from the northwest, north-northwest, southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically about 20 m, ranging from 10 to 30 m, at its best on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Wind and swell onto the shore stir the sand and rubble terrace and cut the visibility there; the water clears over the slope.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 25 to 29 °C through the year, coolest from January to March and warmest from August to October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Seahorses.** Longsnout seahorses hide among the soft corals, sea fans and sponges, mostly between about 8 and 20 m.
* **Shallows.** All three of Bonaire's large parrotfish, the blue, the midnight and the rainbow, each about a metre long, feed across the terrace, alongside schools of blue tang and ocean surgeonfish, French angelfish, trumpetfish, scrawled filefish, cowfish, trunkfish and Bermuda chub. Southern stingrays rest in the sand patches.
* **Reef.** Anemones and soft corals give cover to predator and prey alike, with Pederson cleaner shrimp in the anemones and spotted drum, green and spotted morays, octopus, lionfish, flamingo tongues and sea slugs among the coral. Schools of reef squid hover over the terrace and slope.
* **Larger animals.** Green and hawksbill turtles, large barracuda over the slope, groupers and snapper, with the occasional spotted eagle ray off the deeper reef.
* **Coral.** Much of the shallow elkhorn is dead, leaving skeletons and rubble on the terrace, and living coral begins at about 6 m. Elkhorn coral across the island has declined sharply since 2023.
* **Night.** Tarpon hunt in divers' lights, octopus and squid move in the open, lobsters leave their holes, and bioluminescent ostracods show in the shallows in full darkness.

## Hazards

* **The entry.** Uneven rocks with holes between them, slippery underfoot, with sea urchins and fire coral in the first metres. Scout the line before kitting up, and turn back when waves are breaking on the rocks.
* **Current.** The along-shore current can build during a dive and set a diver well down the coast from the entry. Start the dive into it so the return to the yellow stones runs with it, and surface within sight of the beach.
* **Boats.** Dive and tour boats use the mooring, and recreational boats pass close inshore. A surface marker buoy shows a diver's position away from the entry.
* **Vehicle theft.** Vehicles parked at dive sites along this coast are a target for break-ins; leave them empty.
