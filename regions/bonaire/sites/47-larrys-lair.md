# 47. Larry's Lair

A site about 800 m north of Salt Pier with no mooring buoy, so navigation here runs on compass and natural landmarks. A shallow sandy plateau leads to a double reef, well overgrown and full of fish, running from about 10 to 30 m before giving way to sand. The shore entry crosses numerous sharp rocks, more demanding than the easier beaches further north.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Over a shallow sandy plateau; the shoreline itself has numerous sharp rocks, so watch footing on the way in and out.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open. No mooring buoy marks this site; navigate by compass and natural reference points.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **No mooring buoy.** Unlike most Bonaire sites, there is nothing marking the reef from the surface; note a compass heading before descending.
* **The double reef.** Runs from about 10 to 30 m before the bottom turns to sand.
* **Depth profile.** The sandy plateau leads to the double reef at about 10 m, running through the recreational range to 30 m before the sand.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 260° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 259° |

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the entry.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 600 m to 2.3 km from the north-northwest, north and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Stingrays, turtles and squid, with reef fish including puffers and trumpetfish.

## Hazards

* **Sharp rocks at entry.** More demanding underfoot than the easier beaches further north.
* **No mooring buoy.** Navigation relies on compass and landmarks.
