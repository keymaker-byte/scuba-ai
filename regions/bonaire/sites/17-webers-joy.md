# 17. Weber's Joy (Witch's Hut)

A shore dive on Bonaire's northwest coast road south of the Radio Nederland towers, known locally as Witch's Hut after a small house that once stood on the beach; an old abandoned house beside the parking marks the site today. A short paved path and a few steps lead down to a small beach of coral rubble and rock, and the water opens onto a sandy terrace at about 5 m that steps down to 9 m and then slopes away along the coast. The reef is rich in fish and macro life, with a large lavender stovepipe sponge beside the mooring, and the short, gradual entry suits every level in calm conditions. The headline hazards are fire coral and sharp rubble in the shallows, which a southerly or westerly swell makes difficult to cross.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A paved path and a short flight of concrete steps lead to a small beach of coral rubble and white rock, which slopes gradually into the water. The rubble is uneven and sharp; wear sturdy boots, and put fins on in chest-deep water.
* **Parking.** Free, beside the old house at the top of the path, with room for about five or six cars and more along the roadside. It fills early on busy days.
* **Access.** Open, with the STINAPA Nature Fee required in the water. Dive boats also tie up to the site's mooring.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The old house.** An abandoned concrete house, overgrown and covered in graffiti, stands beside the parking and marks the site from the road.
* **Shallows.** Sand and coral rubble with scattered coral heads and fire coral, at 3 to 5 m a few metres off the beach.
* **Terrace.** Sand and coral outcrops at about 5 m. The dive area lies 43 m from the entry to the southwest, 220°, at 5.0 m.
* **North of the entry.** Coral outcrops scattered over the sand north of the entry hold a good share of the site's macro life.
* **The step.** About 55 m out on the same bearing, the terrace drops to a second level at about 9 m, which runs out roughly another 50 m before the reef slopes away past 10 m.
* **Slope.** Soft and hard coral, sponges and coral outcrops, with overhangs and swim-throughs deeper down, continuing past recreational depth.
* **The mooring.** A large lavender stovepipe sponge sits just south of it.

* **Drift.** Usually slack or a light drift along the shore, building at times to a moderate current along the slope.
* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 215° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 215° |

The swim out runs 43 m southwest from the entry, 5° off the entry shore's facing, straight out from the beach.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through west (SSW-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises swell and surge on the rubble entry and stirs the shallows.
* **Short fetch.** South-southeast through south (SSE-S), onshore over 4.5 to 4.8 km from the direction of Klein Bonaire and the town shore, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the land, and cross-shore over 250 m to 3.2 km from the west-northwest, northwest, east-southeast and southeast, where the coast to either side cuts the fetch short.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, averaging about 25 m, and clearest on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Wave action from the south or west stirs sand in the shallows and cuts visibility near the beach.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds an even temperature through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef fish.** Rock beauties, French and queen angelfish, butterflyfish, parrotfish including midnight parrotfish, soapfish, trumpetfish, black durgon, spotted drum, puffers and boxfish, with schools of blue tang and squid.
* **Macro life.** Giant and corkscrew anemones with Pederson cleaner shrimp, arrow crabs, octopus, lobster in the crevices, nudibranchs and flamingo tongues on the sea whips.
* **Sand.** Southern stingrays on the terrace and the sand patches.
* **Slope.** Moray eels, barracuda and hawksbill turtles, with spotted eagle rays passing in the blue.
* **Coral.** Soft corals in the shallows and on the slope, with elkhorn and staghorn below the shallows, and star, brain and plate corals and tube and stovepipe sponges deeper. The elkhorn and staghorn have taken heavy losses from disease and bleaching since 2023.
* **At night.** Ostracod bioluminescence from as shallow as 3 to 5 m, with octopus and lobster out on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Entry.** Sharp, uneven coral rubble and fire coral in the shallows; wear boots, and keep hands and knees clear of the bottom when crossing it. A swell from the south or west makes the entry and exit difficult; skip the site when waves break on the beach.
* **Slippery steps.** The steps down to the beach are slick when wet.
* **Depth on the slope.** The reef slopes well past recreational depth beyond the 9 m step; watch depth and no-decompression time.
* **Night exit.** The shore is dark and unlit; leave a light on the beach to find the exit.
* **Vehicle break-ins.** Cars parked here have been broken into; leave nothing of value in the car.
* **Sun.** The beach and the parking sit in full sun; bring water.
