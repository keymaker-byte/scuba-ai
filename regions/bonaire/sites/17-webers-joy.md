# 17. Weber's Joy (Witch's Hut)

A double reef system on the protected coast south of 1000 Steps, officially named Weber's Joy on the marine park map and known locally as Witch's Hut after a small house that once stood on the beach. A short walk down a paved path to a rocky beach leads into an easy, gently sloping entry suited to any level, with rich fish life that makes it a favourite for wide-angle photography.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Northwest coast, Bonaire, south of 1000 Steps |
| **Coordinates** | 12.2069, -68.3178 (dive area, about 3 m); entry at 12.206655, -68.316507, the top of the paved path down to the rocky beach |
| **Type** | Shore |
| **Depth range** | 6 to 30 m, some routes to 40 m |

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A paved path leads from the road to a rocky beach that slopes gradually into the water.
* **Parking.** Directly across the street from the site.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The mooring.** A large lavender stovepipe sponge sits just south of it.
* **Double reef.** The site's structure runs as two parallel reef systems rather than a single wall.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Weber's Joy typically runs minimal current, part of why it suits a relaxed dive.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef begins around 3 m and slopes through the recreational range, with some routes reaching 40 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest, on the protected stretch of coast.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against any current here is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

Averages around 25 m, excellent even by the standards of Bonaire's leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Rock beauties, angelfish and butterflyfish abound, along with schools of squid, parrotfish and soapfish among the sponges and corals.
* **Macro life.** Giant anemones, Pederson cleaner shrimp, corkscrew anemones, arrow crabs, octopus and nudibranchs.
