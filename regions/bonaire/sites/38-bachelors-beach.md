# 38. Bachelor's Beach

A small sand beach in Belnem, on EEG Boulevard about 3 km south of Kralendijk on Bonaire's west coast, tucked below a 3 m limestone cliff and reached by a seven step stairway from the roadside. The beach is also called Playa Machete, and in Papiamentu Fòrn'i Kalki. A sand and rubble terrace runs out past a yellow mooring buoy to a sloping reef of hard and soft coral, with a coral restoration nursery in the shallows, an easy dive and a favourite for night diving. The headline hazard is the entry, a rocky last step and a beach that shrinks to a sliver at high water.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk down the seven step limestone stairway in the cliff to the beach. The last step comes down over rock and is slippery when wet, so wear boots with grip and take it slowly. Wade in from the sand to about knee depth before putting on fins, then swim 114 m west-northwest, straight out from the beach, across the terrace to the dive area. Exit at the same beach and climb the stairway back up.
* **Parking.** On the coral gravel at the roadside on EEG Boulevard, 15 m from the top of the stairway. Free. The space holds a handful of cars and fills quickly when a dive group arrives.
* **Access.** A public beach, open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee.
* **Facilities.** Picnic tables beside the parking. No toilets, showers or shade. A burger food truck often parks beside the stairs.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach.** A narrow strip of white sand at the foot of the cliff, hidden from the road, its yellow marker stone at the roadside above the stairway. Low rock walls flank it on either side.
* **The terrace.** Sand and rubble near the beach, with coral growing in from around the mooring, shelving from about 5 m off the beach to 7 to 10 m across the first 110 m out, and on to 10 to 12 m at its outer edge about 130 m from the beach.
* **The mooring.** A yellow mooring buoy about 70 m out from the beach, tied down on the terrace at about 6 m where the coral begins. A dive can head north or south along the reef from it.
* **The coral nursery.** South of the mooring at 5 to 9 m: five tree nurseries and five rope nurseries hung with staghorn and elkhorn fragments.
* **The reef slope.** From the terrace edge the reef slopes away past 30 m, a mix of hard and soft coral with large sponges and gorgonians deeper down.
* **The return.** Come back up onto the terrace for the swim home; the beach lies 114 m east-southeast of the dive area.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight or absent at the site, at times a light current along the reef. Check its direction before entering and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 299° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 299° |

The swim out runs 114 m west-northwest from the entry, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, and north (N), onshore over 5 km across the bay off Kralendijk. Wind from these quarters puts waves on the small beach and the rocks at the foot of the stairway.
* **Short fetch.** Northwest through north-northwest (NW-NNW), onshore over 2.7 to 3.7 km across the bay off Kralendijk, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest (NNE-SSW): offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, and cross-shore over 290 m to 3 km from the north-northeast and northeast and over 420 m from the south-southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, best on a calm early morning and off the reef slope.
* **The shallows.** Sand stirred by swimmers and by waves at the beach clouds the terrace near shore, and heavy rain lowers visibility for a day or two.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Sergeant majors, blue tang, parrotfish and surgeonfish over the sand and coral heads, with southern stingrays resting on the sand and octopus in the shallows. Turtles are seen regularly, and spotted eagle rays pass along the terrace.
* **Reef slope.** Angelfish, trumpetfish, filefish, cowfish, wrasse and grouper, with barracuda over the reef. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease, and the restoration nursery in the shallows grows staghorn and elkhorn to replant it.
* **Macro.** Seahorses, frogfish on the sponges, scorpionfish, morays, blennies, shrimp and crabs.
* **Night.** Tarpon and barracuda at times hunt in divers' lights, and morays, octopus and lobster forage over the reef.

## Hazards

* **The entry.** The last step of the stairway comes down over rock, slippery when wet, and loose stones lie around the waterline. Wear boots with grip and keep a hand free for balance.
* **High water.** At high water the sea can reach the rocks and leaves little or no sand at the foot of the stairway, which makes the exit harder.
* **The coral nursery.** The nursery trees and ropes are off limits to touch, and only permitted restoration divers handle them. Keep fins and gauges clear when passing it.
* **Depth.** The reef slope continues past 30 m. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from west-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW) or north (N) puts waves on the beach, and from northwest through north-northwest (NW-NNW) a chop; choose another site when any of these blows fresh.
