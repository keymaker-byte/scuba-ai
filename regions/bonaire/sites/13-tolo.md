# 13. Tolo (Ol' Blue)

A shore site on Bonaire's northwest coast off a long stretch of coral beach, below a low fossil coral cliff on the one-way section of the coastal road, marked by a yellow stone reading Tolo. A short swim crosses a sandy, rubble-strewn shallows to a reef that tops out around 8 to 10 m and drops steeply into deep blue water, with large sponges, friendly French angelfish, schooling fish, tarpon and turtles, and the odd whale shark passing offshore. The headline hazard is the entry and exit over loose coral rubble, which turns rough in surf from the southeast through southwest.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down rough rock steps in a low fossil coral cliff, about 3 m high, to a beach of coral rubble and small rounded coral stones that roll underfoot, then a short sand channel just left of the steps into the water. Beach erosion has left the foot of the cliff a loose rubble step; a rubble ramp at the east end of the parking area avoids the drop. Carry the gear down and kit up at the water's edge, put fins on once floating, and time the entry and exit between waves. Wear boots with good grip.
* **Parking.** A large, open lot of hard-packed coral and sand off the coastal road, with little shade. Parking is free.
* **Access.** Open. Drive north from Kralendijk on the coastal road to where it becomes one way, and turn in at the yellow stone marked Tolo on the right. The return continues north and inland through Rincon, a longer drive back to town. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallows.** The sandy and rubble shallows run about 60 m out from the entry at 4 to 6 m, with gorgonians and scattered coral heads.
* **Reef crest.** The reef tops out at about 8 to 10 m, roughly 70 m south of the entry, and drops steeply below it onto the slope.
* **Right of the entry.** Facing the sea, the reef to the right runs west along the shore toward a rocky point, with more hard coral and a wall-like drop-off, and soft corals clustered along the sand line at about 5 m. The site mooring sits on top of the reef at about 8 m on this side, the usual turnaround before returning along the shallower reef.
* **Left of the entry.** To the left, east along the shore, a shallow shelf leads to large coral outcroppings at about 9 m, crowded with grunts, sergeant majors, damselfish and parrotfish.
* **Return.** The beach sits below a continuous low cliff with few places to climb out; take a bearing on the entry before descending, and note it on the reef on the way back.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current is usually mild or still on the reef, and can run harder when the trades pick up or shift.
* **The point.** West of the entry, past the rocky point, the current often reverses and speeds up, running with the outbound swim. A dive that suddenly feels easy on the way out has passed it, and the swim back runs against it.
* **Surface.** The surface can run rough with a stronger set than the reef below; start the dive into the current and keep enough gas to swim back to the entry.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south, 187° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 185° |

The shore here runs west to east, and the swim out runs 101 m south, 180°, from the entry to the dive area.

* **Bad.** Southeast through west-southwest (SE-WSW), onshore over 5.8 km to more than 30 km, along the coast from the southeast or across open sea from the south and southwest. Wind from this quarter puts waves on the rubble beach and makes the entry and exit difficult.
* **Mixed.** West (W), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from northwest through east-northeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 170 m to 2.8 km from the west-northwest, east and east-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow along the shore over a short fetch and leave the entry calm at their usual strength; trades veering southeast fall in the Bad sector and put surf on the beach.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m, often excellent, best on calm mornings.
* **Surf.** Waves on the beach stir the rubble shallows; the water clears beyond the reef crest.
* **Rain.** Rain from October to January washes sediment off the land into the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Green turtles graze the shallows, with parrotfish, trumpetfish, squid, porcupinefish, peacock flounder and octopus among the rubble and coral heads. Elkhorn and staghorn coral grow in the shallows and, along the whole leeward coast, have suffered heavy partial mortality from bleaching and disease.
* **Reef.** Friendly French and queen angelfish, schooling fish with bar jacks hunting through them, snapper, grouper, morays, lobsters and spotted drum, with large purple finger and tube sponges sheltering crabs and shrimp. Coral banded shrimp, arrow crabs, nudibranchs and flamingo tongues reward a slow look.
* **Open water.** Tarpon, barracuda and spotted eagle rays along the drop-off, hawksbill turtles at the sponges, and whale sharks on rare occasions.

## Hazards

* **Entry and exit.** Loose rubble and rounded stones roll underfoot, and waves break on the beach in wind from the southeast through southwest. Choose another site when surf is running.
* **Current at the point.** Past the rocky point west of the entry the current often reverses and speeds up, running with the outbound swim; turn before it or plan the swim back against it.
* **Depth.** The slope drops steeply past 40 m directly below the reef crest, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Boats.** Dive and pleasure boats use the mooring; carry a surface marker buoy and ascend along the reef.
* **Night exit.** The cliff leaves few places to get out, and the entry is easy to miss in the dark; mark it with a light on shore.
