# 13. Tolo (Ol' Blue)

A beautiful reef off a long stretch of coral beach just north of 1000 Steps, named for its clear blue water and identified on the road from Kralendijk by a yellow stone reading Tolo. The reef suits divers of any level, with a mix of hard and soft coral and reef fish from the shallows to the working depth, and an occasional whale shark passing through the blue beyond it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | Northwest coast, Bonaire, just north of 1000 Steps |
| **Coordinates** | 12.2145, -68.339 (near shore, under 1 m); entry at 12.215411, -68.337364 |
| **Type** | Shore |
| **Depth range** | 6 to 27 m |

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the coral beach; the coral rocks make a musical sound as the surf washes them, audible during a shore entry.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the beach.
* **Access.** Open. Look for the yellow stone marked Tolo on the right when driving north from Kralendijk.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Yellow stone.** Marks the site from the coast road.
* **Coral beach.** The long stretch fronting the site, distinct from a sand beach.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. A logged dive here found slack to negligible current at excellent visibility.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef is enjoyable through its whole range from the shallows to the working depth; a logged dive reached 27.4 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against any current here is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast; a logged dive rated it 5 out of 5 (excellent).

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October; a logged November dive measured 29 C. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Friendly French angelfish, schooling fish, tarpon, parrotfish, snapper and turtles throughout the reef.
* **Open water.** Whale sharks have been seen here on occasion.
