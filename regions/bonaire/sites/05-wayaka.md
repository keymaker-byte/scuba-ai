# 5. Wayaka

A remote site in Washington Slagbaai National Park, on the park's northwest coast, at the middle of three small bays known together as Wayaka, after the Papiamentu name for the lignum vitae tree. A stone stairway leads down to a small sandy beach, and a shallow plateau of dead coral runs out to a reef terrace with a steep drop into deep water at its outer edge, where larger animals turn up in the blue. The current is usually limited but can run strong, and any swell from the west makes the entry rough. Diving here is suspended under STINAPA's closure of the park to diving.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A stone stairway, slippery when wet, leads from the parking area down to a small sandy beach between rocky outcrops. The beach is narrow and much of it can be awash, so keep gear well up the sand or on the rocks. In calm water the entry is easy over soft sand; any swell from the west makes it rough.
* **Parking.** Free, in a small sandy area above the stairway with room for only a few cars, about 50 m from the park road. The space fills quickly, since the beach is also a popular snorkel spot.
* **Access.** Closed to diving: STINAPA closed every site north of Karpata on 1 May 2023 over Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD), reopened the coast between BOPEC and the park, Nukove and Carel's Vision included, on 27 January 2024, and keeps Washington Slagbaai National Park closed to diving while the park stays open for every other activity, snorkeling included. Confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive here. When open, the site lies on the stretch of road shared by the short (green) and long (yellow) routes from the park entrance north of Rincon, each a one-way dirt road with a 25 km/h limit. STINAPA recommends at least 30 cm of ground clearance, and rangers at the gate may turn away a lower vehicle depending on road conditions. The park opens Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, closed on Mondays and holidays, with last entry at 14:30, and the Wayaka beaches must be cleared by 15:30. The park can close fully or in part after heavy rain, and rangers sometimes close the Wayaka beaches to swimmers in rough weather; ask at the gate. The Nature Fee e-tag covers both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai. The site is also dived from a boat.
* **Facilities.** A stone stairway down to the beach. No toilets, showers or shade.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach.** A small sandy bay between rocky outcrops, with two more small bays a short way north and south along the same shore.
* **Inner plateau.** A shallow plateau of dead coral and rubble at about 4 m runs west-southwest from the beach for its first 110 m, cut by sandy channels, with parrotfish and surgeonfish grazing over it.
* **Reef terrace.** At about 110 m out the plateau steps down to a terrace at 11 to 13 m, reaching 11.5 m at the dive area 129 m from the entry, 248°, and holding at 11 to 13 m to its outer lip about 250 m from the beach.
* **Drop-off.** Past the lip at about 12 m the reef falls away steeply, into water past 100 m deep within about 60 m and nearly 200 m deep some 400 m from the beach.
* **Route.** Visit the drop-off first, then work back up onto the terrace and the plateau for the rest of the dive. Start into whatever current runs on the day, so the return to the beach runs with it, and turn back while the beach is still close enough to find.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Usually limited, picking up in strong wind, and strongest along the drop-off.
* **Exits.** The shore either side of the bay is rock; a diver carried past the beach faces a swim back to it or a climb out over rock.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 261° |

The beach opens west-southwest onto open sea, and the swim out runs 129 m west-southwest, 248°, from the entry across the plateau to the dive area, nearly straight off the beach.

* **Bad.** Southwest through northwest (SW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, onto the beach, building surf on the sand and the rocks either side of it.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southwest: offshore from north-northeast through southeast over 120 to 200 m, off the land, cross-shore over 240 m from the north, 270 m from the south-southeast and 590 m from the south, and onshore over 730 m from the south-southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the beach flat, though strong trades drive the current on the reef.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 20 to 30 m.
* **Swell and rain.** Swell from the north or west stirs the shallows, and heavy rain washes sediment off the land, lowering it for a time.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm shorty or full 3 mm wetsuit is standard.

## Marine life

* **Plateau.** Mostly dead coral, with new growth starting to return, grazed by large schools of blue tang, stoplight, midnight and rainbow parrotfish, and doctorfish. French angelfish, sergeant majors and Bermuda chubs move over it, with peacock flounder on the sand. Green turtles feed in the shallows, and sea turtles nest on the beach.
* **Terrace and drop-off.** Morays and blennies in the reef, with barracuda along the edge.
* **Open water.** Larger animals turn up off the drop-off, the reason to keep an eye on the blue through the dive.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Washington Slagbaai National Park is closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD measures, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Entry.** A slippery stone stairway to a small beach, rough in any swell from the west; rangers sometimes close the beach in rough weather.
* **Current.** Usually limited, strong at times, and strongest on the drop-off. Overshooting the beach leaves only rock to exit on. Carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Depth.** The terrace lip at about 12 m falls steeply into water past 100 m deep.
* **Remote.** The park lies outside mobile phone coverage and a long dirt road from help; leave a dive plan and an expected return time with someone outside the park, and clear the beach by 15:30.
