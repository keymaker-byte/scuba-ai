# 5. Wayaka

A little dived site inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, named for the local Guaiacum (lignum vitae) tree that once grew along this stretch of coast. STINAPA registers it as a boat site, though the beach is also reached over land by 4x4. Little dived, it turns up larger animals in the blue. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering this stretch of the park's coast.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the beach when reached by land, or by boat; either way expect a more challenging entry than the park's easier sites, especially when waves are up.
* **Parking.** At the beach, reached only by 4x4 over the park's unpaved road.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30; the Wayaka beaches close at 15:30, earlier than the rest of the park. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's unpaved roads; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Little charted ground.** The site is little dived, with no set route; each dive here is exploratory.
* **Depth profile.** The reef here runs from about 5 m down through the 10 to 30 m range where most of the diving happens, with the seabed beyond falling toward 75 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 261° |

* **Bad.** Southwest through northwest (SW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Any wind from this quarter raises the wave state that already governs whether this site is diveable at all.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southwest: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, cross-shore over 240 to 590 m from the north, south-southeast and south, and onshore over 730 m from the south-southwest. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Open water.** Larger animals turn up here in the blue.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since 1 May 2023, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Sea state dependent.** A more challenging entry when waves are up; dive it only in calm seas.
* **Remote and little dived.** No facilities and no well established route underwater.
