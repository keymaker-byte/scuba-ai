# 27. La Machaca

The house reef at Captain Don's Habitat, and a chance for a novice diver to do a wreck dive in shallow water: a 14 m, locally built boat lies at about 15 m, roughly 30 m from shore. A large green moray eel, well accustomed to divers, makes its home in the wreck and is worth approaching with some caution given its size and confidence around people.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From Captain Don's Habitat's pier, by its ladder.
* **Parking.** At Captain Don's Habitat.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** Captain Don's Habitat resort sits onshore.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The wreck.** A 14 m, locally built boat at about 15 m, roughly 30 m from shore, the site's centrepiece.
* **Depth profile.** The reef runs from about 7 m to the wreck at 15 m and on through the recreational range to 23 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 249° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 249° |

* **Bad.** West through northwest (W-NW), onshore over 6.5 km to more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the entry.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), onshore over 1.2 to 1.4 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over 5.5 km down the coast, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 190 to 760 m from the north-northwest, north and south-southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The wreck.** A large, confident green moray eel makes its home there.
* **Reef.** Octopus, eels and a wide range of reef fish; tarpon follow divers on a night dive.

## Hazards

* **The resident moray.** Large and accustomed to divers, but still worth approaching with caution given its size, particularly on a night dive.
