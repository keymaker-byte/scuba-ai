# 27. La Machaca

The house reef of Captain Don's Habitat on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, entered from the resort's divers-only pier. A sandy terrace runs about 50 m out to a drop-off at 9 to 10 m, and a guide line runs from the pier down the reef past La Machaca, a small fishing boat lying upside down at about 14 to 15 m, which gives a novice diver a shallow first wreck dive and shelters a large green moray. The wall and slope below reach sand at about 40 m, and the line carries on down to a second, almost fully broken-down wreck, the Hesper, at 34 to 40 m. The headline hazard is depth: the line and the slope both lead below the maximum operating depth of EAN32.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the end of the resort's divers-only pier, known as the baby dock, by a giant stride or down its stair ladders. The water is about 5 m deep at the end of the pier and shallows quickly toward shore, so take a giant stride only from the end. The resort's other dock serves its dive boats. Exit by the ladders.
* **Parking.** In the Captain Don's Habitat lot, a short walk from the pier, with room to drive closer in to unload gear, for guests and for divers with the resort's permission.
* **Access.** On private resort property. Hotel guests dive from the pier 24 hours a day; non-guests check in at the resort's dive shop for permission and any pier fee before diving. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** The Captain Don's Habitat dive shop and a second dive shop, Technical Diving Services, beside the pier, with rinse tanks, showers, guest lockers, and the resort's restaurant and bar.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pier.** The divers-only pier reaches out over sand to about 5 m at its end, and the bottom slopes gently away from there.
* **The guide line.** A rope runs along the bottom from the pier down past the wreck to the edge of the reef and on into deep water. It keeps divers clear of the resort's boat traffic and leads straight back to the pier.
* **The terrace.** Sand with clusters of starlet coral slopes gently from the pier to the drop-off at 9 to 10 m, about 50 m out.
* **The wreck.** La Machaca, a small fishing boat about 11 to 14 m long, lies upside down at about 14 to 15 m, a short swim out from the pier along the line and just south of it, overgrown with algae, sponges and coral. It was sunk deliberately as a dive attraction and later moved to its present spot as a set for the 1979 Dutch television series Duel in de Diepte.
* **The wall and slope.** Below the drop-off the reef runs on down a steep wall and slope to sand at about 40 m.
* **The Hesper.** At the end of the line a second, small wreck lies on the sand at 34 to 40 m, almost completely broken down.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually slight to none, and occasionally strong along the reef. Start the dive into whatever current is running and let it carry you back.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 249° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 249° |

The swim out runs 85 m southwest from the pier, slightly south of straight off the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** West through northwest (W-NW), onshore over 6.5 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises waves at the pier and stirs up the shallows.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), onshore over 1.1 to 1.4 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the pier.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over 5.5 km down the coast, running a swell along the shore past the pier.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 190 to 750 m from the north-northwest, north and south-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the pier calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m on a typical day, sometimes more.
* **Wind.** Wind from south through northwest stirs the sandy terrace and cuts visibility in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **The wreck.** A large green moray shelters in the wreck and is well used to divers. Snappers, cubera snapper among them, tiger and Nassau groupers shelter under the hull, and porcupinefish hide in its cavities.
* **Terrace and reef.** Spotted morays, sharptail eels, octopus, frogfish and scorpionfish, with an occasional seahorse among the sponges and corals. Angelfish, parrotfish, butterflyfish, grunts and blue chromis are common. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Open water.** Tarpon hold over the deeper reef by day, with turtles and an occasional spotted eagle ray passing.
* **Night.** Tarpon come up to 7 to 10 m and hunt in divers' lights, following them around the site. Octopus, lobsters, shrimp and crabs come out on the reef and around the wreck.

## Hazards

* **Depth.** The line and the slope both lead past the wreck into deep water, the Hesper at 34 to 40 m and sand beyond the wall at about 40 m, below the maximum operating depth of EAN32 and outside a no-decompression plan. Turn at a planned depth on the line.
* **Boat traffic.** The resort's dive boats run in and out beside the pier; follow the line out to depth and back, and surface along it.
* **The resident moray.** Large and used to divers, but approach it with caution, particularly on a night dive.
* **Current.** Usually slight, and occasionally strong; keep enough gas to return to the pier against it.
* **The pier.** The water shallows quickly toward shore under it, so a giant stride goes in only from its end, and the ladders can be slippery; climb out with care.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from west through northwest (W-NW) puts waves on the pier, from south-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW) a chop, and from the south (S) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
