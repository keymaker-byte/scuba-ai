# 47. Larry's Lair

A site about 1.4 km north of Salt Pier with no mooring buoy, so navigation here runs on compass and natural landmarks rather than a fixed line. A shallow sandy plateau leads to a double reef, well overgrown and full of fish, running from about 10 to 30 m before giving way to sand. The shore entry crosses numerous sharp rocks, more demanding than the easier beaches further north.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Over a shallow sandy plateau; the shoreline itself has numerous sharp rocks, so watch footing on the way in and out.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open. No mooring buoy marks this site; navigate by compass and natural reference points.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **No mooring buoy.** Unlike most Bonaire sites, there is nothing marking the reef from the surface; note a compass heading before descending.
* **The double reef.** Runs from about 10 to 30 m before the bottom turns to sand.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The sandy plateau leads to the double reef at about 10 m, running through the recreational range to 30 m before the sand.

## Hazards

* **Sharp rocks at entry.** More demanding underfoot than the easier beaches further north.
* **No mooring buoy.** Navigation relies on compass and landmarks rather than a fixed line.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces south.
* **Bad.** Any wind with south or west in it raises swell.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east trade winds are favourable here.
* **Wind against current.** A building southerly against any current here is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

Typically 15 to 30 m, in line with the rest of the leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Stingrays, turtles and squid, with reef fish including puffers and trumpetfish.
