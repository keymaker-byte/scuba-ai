# 40. Lighthouse Point

A southern site of sand flats and fringing reef where current runs from south to north and can build suddenly, keeping the water in near constant circulation and the visibility clear. This is not a site for a newly certified diver; local operators expect drift diving experience and comfort with changing conditions, and only run trips in good weather. A boat lets the captain read the current and choose the best entry rather than committing a shore diver to a fixed point.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Most dive operators use a boat here so the captain can position for the current; a shore entry exists but needs real current experience.
* **Parking.** Roadside for the shore option.
* **Access.** Open; local operators run trips here only in good weather.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Sand flats.** Extensive, characteristic of this stretch of the south coast.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Current runs from south to north and can increase suddenly in strength, part of what keeps the water clear and draws the site's baitfish.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. Sand flats and fringing reef run through the recreational range to about 30 m.

## Hazards

* **Sudden current increase.** Not a beginner site; drift diving experience and comfort with changing conditions are expected.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces south, at the exposed southern tip of the island.
* **Bad.** Any wind with south or west in it adds to an already current-driven site.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east trade winds are more favourable here than further up the sheltered west coast.
* **Wind against current.** Given the current's own tendency to build suddenly, any wind opposing it is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

Averages around 25 m, kept clear by the site's constant water circulation.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Open water.** Massive schools of baitfish swirl overhead, drawn by the nutrients the current brings.
* **Shallows.** Rays are a regular sighting on the sand flats.
