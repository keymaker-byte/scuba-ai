# 40. Lighthouse Point

Lighthouse Point is the reef off Punt Vierkant, the bulge in the coast south of Flamingo Airport, named for the small light on the point and serving as the house reef of Delfins Beach Resort. A wide terrace of sand, rubble and soft coral runs about 140 m out from the entry to an edge at about 10 m, where the reef slopes away to sand at about 30 m. Sitting on a point, the site takes more current than the reefs either side of it, and that current can build suddenly or switch direction during a dive.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A short climb over a low wall and a few steps down onto a rocky shore of dead coral and rubble, then a surface swim of about 140 m west-northwest across the terrace to the edge. Guests of Delfins Beach Resort enter from the resort's own beach or its pier about 120 m north.
* **Parking.** Free, above the shore at the end of a one-lane coral road off Punt Vierkant, with room for several cars.
* **Access.** Open, with a STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag; the site is marked for both shore and boat diving. The resort beaches either side of the entry are for their guests.
* **Facilities.** None at the public entry.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallows.** From the entry the bottom runs over sand and coral rubble at 3.5 to 5 m for the first 40 m.
* **Terrace.** A flat sandy terrace at 7 to 8 m, scattered with soft corals and sponges, carries the rest of the swim out.
* **Terrace edge.** The dive area, at about 10 m, 140 m west-northwest of the entry. The terrace holds 9 to 11 m along the edge for several hundred metres north and south.
* **Reef slope.** Beyond the edge, hard and soft corals and large sponges slope down to sand at about 30 m.
* **Mooring.** A dive mooring sits on the terrace just inside the edge south of the resort, at about 8.5 m.
* **Route.** Start into the current along the slope and return with it, ending on the terrace for the safety stop and the swim back to the entry.

## Current

The point concentrates the current running along this stretch of coast, and it sets along the slope and the terrace edge.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | north, 0° |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Reversal.** On some days it sets south, 180°, running strongly along the slope from the north.
* **Character.** The current can strengthen suddenly close to shore and switch direction during a dive. A dive planned against it on the way out can return into it once it turns.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 263° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 263° |

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Wind from this quarter drives waves straight onto the rocky entry and builds surge on the terrace.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast. North-northeast through southeast blows offshore across 130 m to 4.9 km, off the land; north-northwest, north and south-southeast blow cross-shore over 3.9 to 4.5 km. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry sheltered from wind waves.

## Visibility

* **On the reef.** Usually 20 to 30 m on the slope, kept clear by the moving water, and best on calm mornings.
* **At the entry.** Swell and afternoon surf stir the sandy shallows down to a few metres.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water column stays well mixed through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Sand.** Southern stingrays and other rays rest in the sandy shallows and on the terrace, with eagle rays passing over it.
* **Terrace.** Soft corals and sponges grow thickly across the terrace, with trumpetfish, slender filefish, octopus and spiny lobster.
* **Reef slope.** Green morays, queen angelfish, schoolmaster snapper and schools of goatfish and grunts, with creole wrasse in the open water above.
* **Larger visitors.** Hawksbill turtles and tarpon.

## Hazards

* **Sudden current.** The current can strengthen or reverse without warning, carrying a diver along or off the point. Carry a surface marker buoy and plan the exit for either direction.
* **Surge and surf.** The site takes more surge than most on the leeward coast, and swell and afternoon waves can break on the entry. Dive in the morning, and judge the surf from the shore before kitting up.
* **Rocky entry.** A low wall, steps and a shore of dead coral and rubble make the entry and exit awkward with a full rig, especially in surf.
* **Long surface swim.** About 140 m out to the terrace edge and the same back, which tires a diver before and after a current dive.
