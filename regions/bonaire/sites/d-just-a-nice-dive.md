# D. Just a Nice Dive

A relaxed boat dive at the east end of Klein Bonaire's south side, where a narrow sand and coral terrace at 4 to 6 m ends at a lip around 8 m and the reef drops steeply away, well past recreational depth. Yellow pencil coral and leaf and sheet corals cover the reef top, schooling fish work the lip, and large sponges, ocean triggerfish and turtles turn up along the wall. Current is usually slight, and the headline hazard is the drop itself: the wall falls fast enough that depth builds quickly for a diver who descends straight down it.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat from a STINAPA mooring off the reef, a giant stride or back roll depending on the boat, then a descent onto the terrace and out to the lip.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Best dived in the morning, when the trade wind is at its lightest. STINAPA closes the site during Bonaire's Regatta week in October; confirm status with STINAPA or the operator before a trip in that month.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral shelving to 4 to 6 m, running about 60 m out from the islet's shore to the lip.
* **The lip.** Around 8 m, where yellow pencil coral concentrates and schooling fish hold over the reef top.
* **The wall.** Drops steeply from the lip, past 50 m within about 100 m of it. Following the reef along its face and working deeper gradually keeps the profile under control; the same face leads back toward the mooring at the end of the dive.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Current is usually slight.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | southeast, 138° |

* **Bad.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water, raising swell at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** East through south-southeast (E-SSE), onshore over 2 to 2.6 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring. The prevailing trades blow from this quarter, which is why the site dives best in the morning.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** West-southwest clockwise through east-northeast: offshore from west through north-northeast, off the islet, and cross-shore over 220 m to 2 km from the west-southwest, northeast and east-northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, at its best on calm mornings. A strong trade or a swell from the south stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef top.** Abundant yellow pencil coral with leaf and sheet corals and gorgonians, and schooling fish over the lip, with grunts and snappers.
* **The wall.** Large sponges, the elephant ear and giant barrel sponges among them, with angelfish, parrotfish, surgeonfish and butterflyfish.
* **Passing.** Ocean triggerfish, turtles and mackerel.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The wall falls steeply from 8 m and keeps going well past recreational depth; watch the computer on the descent and move along the face to gain depth gradually.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
* **Surface chop.** The east to southeast trade blows onshore across the channel and builds a chop at the mooring as it strengthens through the day.
* **Boat traffic.** The channel between Kralendijk and Klein Bonaire carries dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
