# D. Just a Nice Dive

A relaxed boat dive off Klein Bonaire's east point, facing Kralendijk across the channel, where a shallow sand and coral terrace at 4 to 5 m ends at an edge around 6 m and the reef drops steeply away past recreational depth. Yellow pencil coral and leaf and sheet corals cover the reef top, schooling fish work the edge, large elephant ear sponges grow along the slope, and turtles and ocean triggerfish pass along it. Current is usually slight, and the headline hazard is the drop itself: depth builds quickly for a diver who descends straight down the face.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat from the STINAPA mooring marked D, a giant stride or back roll depending on the boat, then a descent onto the terrace and out to the edge.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive or snorkel operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Best dived in the morning, when the trade wind is at its lightest. STINAPA closes the site during Bonaire's Regatta week in October; confirm status with STINAPA or the operator before a trip in that month.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral at 4 to 5 m, running about 60 m east from the islet's shore to the edge.
* **The edge.** Around 6 m, where the terrace breaks, yellow pencil coral concentrates and schooling fish hold over the reef top. The reef passes 10 m within about 25 m of it.
* **The slope.** Runs north and south along the islet's east face and drops steeply past recreational depth. Swimming along the face and working deeper gradually keeps the profile under control; on a moored dive, the same face leads back to the mooring at the end.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds, which push water against Klein Bonaire's north and east sides. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Current is usually slight, and boats often run the site as a gentle drift along the reef.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | east, 87° |

* **Short fetch.** North-northeast through southeast (NNE-SE), onshore over 1.1 to 2.5 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring. The prevailing trades blow from this quarter, which is why the site dives best in the morning.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 5.6 km, running a chop along the reef.
* **Fine.** North, and south-southeast clockwise through northwest: offshore from south-southwest through northwest, off the islet, and cross-shore over 2.2 to 4.1 km from the north, south-southeast and south.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, at its best on calm mornings before the trade wind builds. A strong trade stirs up the terrace and lowers visibility in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef top.** Abundant yellow pencil coral with leaf and sheet corals, and schooling fish over the edge, with grunts and snappers.
* **The slope.** Large elephant ear sponges and barrel sponges, with angelfish and parrotfish.
* **Passing.** Turtles, regular here, with ocean triggerfish, mackerel, squid and large pufferfish.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The reef falls steeply from the edge and keeps going past recreational depth; watch the computer on the descent and move along the face to gain depth gradually.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
* **Surface chop.** The east to southeast trade blows onshore across the channel and builds a chop at the mooring as it strengthens through the day.
* **Boat traffic.** The channel between Kralendijk and Klein Bonaire carries dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
