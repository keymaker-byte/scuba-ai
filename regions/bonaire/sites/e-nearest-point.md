# E. Nearest Point

A boat dive off the southeast point of Klein Bonaire, also called Piedra Kolet, where the islet's south side begins. A sand terrace at 4 to 8 m runs about 70 m out from the shore to a reef lip around 8 m, and beyond it a steep slope of coral ridges and sand channels falls to sand around 40 m. Purple sea fans and gorgonians grow between coral heads on the terrace, mountain star coral heads line the drop-off, and black coral and large tube sponges grow on the slope below. Current is usually slight; the headline hazards are how quickly the slope gains depth and the chop the trade wind raises across the channel as the day goes on.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the deep mooring off the reef, then a short swim to the reef and a descent onto the lip.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Best dived in the morning, when the trade wind is at its lightest. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA deep mooring off the drop-off.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** White sand and coral at 4 to 8 m, running about 70 m out from the islet's shore, with sea fans and gorgonians growing between the coral heads. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The lip.** Around 8 m, where the terrace ends and large mountain star coral heads mark the top of the drop-off.
* **The slope.** Steep, with ridges of coral separated by sand channels, falling to sand around 40 m. Swimming out along the face of the reef before working deeper makes for a gradual descent; the same face leads back toward the mooring.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight, and it can change direction during a dive. It builds when a strong easterly blows.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | southeast, 138° |

* **Bad.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water, raising swell at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** East through south-southeast (E-SSE), onshore over 2 to 2.6 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring. An easterly trade blows into this sector and builds through the day, which is why the site dives best in the morning.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** West-southwest clockwise through east-northeast: offshore from west through north-northeast, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 340 m from the west-southwest and 1.9 to 2 km from the northeast and east-northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, at its best on calm mornings. A strong trade or a swell from the south stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Purple sea fans, gorgonians, soft corals and sea rods between coral heads on the sand, with schools of goatfish, the occasional ray on the sand, and squid over the shallows.
* **Drop-off and slope.** Large mountain star coral heads at the lip, then hard and soft corals, elephant ear sponges and yellow, orange and purple tube sponges, with black coral deeper on the slope.
* **Fish.** Angelfish, snappers, triggerfish and trumpetfish along the reef, barracuda off the slope, and large spotted morays and octopus in the coral.
* **Turtles.** Seen regularly on the reef.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from 8 m to sand around 40 m; watch the computer on the descent and move along the face to gain depth gradually.
* **Surface chop.** The east to southeast trade blows onshore across the channel and builds a chop at the mooring as it strengthens through the day.
* **Swell and surge.** A swell from the south runs straight onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow terrace.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
* **Boat traffic.** The channel between Bonaire and Klein Bonaire carries dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
