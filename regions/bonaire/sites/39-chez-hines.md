# 39. Chez Hines

Also known as South Belnem, one of the first sites heading south where shore access starts to get more challenging than the easy stretch around town. A shallow plateau with good coral and abundant marine life gives way to a second reef at about 37 m, a genuine deep dive for experienced divers only. Turtles are seen here more reliably than almost anywhere else on the island, alongside rays making their way along the coastline and nurse sharks resting in the shallows.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Possible from shore but tricky enough that a boat is generally used.
* **Parking.** Roadside for the shore option.
* **Access.** Open. Current can run strong at times; check conditions before committing to a shore entry.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallow plateau.** The main dive area, with good coral cover.
* **Second reef.** Down at about 37 m, a distinct deep dive rather than part of the standard recreational profile.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Current can run strong at times, more so than the sites immediately north.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The shallow plateau runs through the recreational range to about 30 m, with a second reef further out at about 37 m.

## Hazards

* **Tricky shore entry and exit.** A boat is the more straightforward way in.
* **Current.** Can run strong at times; not a site to underestimate.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell on the plateau.
* **Short fetch.** Northwest through north (NW-N), onshore over 3.2 to 4.9 km across the bay off town, enough to build a chop on the surface.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south: offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, and cross-shore over 570 m to 4.2 km from the north-northeast and northeast.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallow plateau.** Parrotfish, nurse sharks and trumpetfish, with turtles spotted here more than almost anywhere else on the island.
* **Along the coastline.** Rays making their way along the shallows.
