# 39. Chez Hines

A boat dive off the villas of South Belnem, the name the site also goes by, about 4 km south of Kralendijk on Bonaire's west coast. A broad, shallow plateau of coral heads scattered over sand shelves from about 5 m near the shore to about 12 m at its outer edge, where the reef slopes away past 30 m to a second reef at about 37 m. Turtles are common here, with nurse sharks at times resting on the plateau and rays crossing the sand. The headline hazard is the current, usually slight but strong at times.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the boat, by giant stride over the plateau, where the bottom sits at 8 to 12 m. Gather on the plateau before heading out to the reef edge, and come back up onto it to end the dive under the boat.
* **Parking.** Not applicable for a boat dive.
* **Access.** By dive boat from Kralendijk. The shore behind the site is private villa frontage; a shore entry is possible only from a villa's own grounds, and the entry and exit there are tricky. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shore.** A line of residential villas along the shore, clear from the surface and a useful reference for position before descending and after surfacing.
* **The plateau.** Sand with scattered coral heads, shelving gently from about 5 m near the shore to about 8 m at the dive area and about 12 m at its outer edge, some 250 m out. The coral grows in separate formations with clear sand between them, easy to navigate and to hold buoyancy over.
* **The reef slope.** From the plateau's edge the reef drops away seaward through the recreational range, with the richest mix of life between about 15 and 30 m.
* **The second reef.** At about 37 m, beyond the 34 m maximum operating depth of EAN32. The dive stays on the plateau and the slope above 30 m.
* **The return.** Work back up the slope onto the plateau for the end of the dive and the safety stop, then ascend to the boat.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight on the plateau and stronger on the deeper reef, at times strong. The boat briefing sets the start of the dive by the direction it runs on the day; start into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 299° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises a swell on the shallow plateau and makes the boat entry and pickup rough.
* **Short fetch.** Northwest through north (NW-N), onshore over 3.2 to 4.9 km across the channel to Klein Bonaire and the bay off Kralendijk, enough to build a chop on the surface.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south (NNE-S): offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, and cross-shore over 630 m to 4.2 km from the north-northeast and northeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the surface calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** A westerly swell stirs the sand on the shallow plateau and clouds it for a time, while the slope stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Plateau.** Turtles graze and rest across the plateau. Nurse sharks are at times found resting under the coral heads, and parrotfish, trumpetfish, filefish and angelfish work the scattered formations.
* **Sand.** Rays rest buried in the sand between the coral heads and cruise the shallows along the shore. Small bottom dwellers and morays hide in the sand and the bases of the coral heads.
* **Reef slope.** Brain and star coral with soft corals and sponges between them. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease, and the brain and star corals are among the species hit hardest.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Usually slight, but strong at times, especially on the deeper reef. Check its direction before entering and keep the dive close enough to the boat's pickup point to swim back against it.
* **Depth.** The slope runs on past 30 m to the second reef at about 37 m and on into deep water. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Boat traffic.** Other dive boats may be working the area. Carry a surface marker buoy and deploy it before surfacing.
* **Swell on the plateau.** Wind from west-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW) puts a swell over the shallow plateau, and from northwest through north (NW-N) a chop; choose a sheltered site when any of these blows fresh.
