# B. Ebo's Reef

A boat dive on Klein Bonaire's north shore, facing Bonaire across the channel, named for Ebo Domacassé, one of Bonaire's early dive pioneers. A shallow terrace runs out to a reef crest at about 8 m, and below it a steep slope carries giant orange elephant ear sponges, many topped with black crinoids, and big colonies of lavender stovepipe sponge. The mooring is anchored in deep water off the slope at about 43 m, so every dive starts and ends with a short swim over open blue water, and the current along this shore can run strongly and change direction.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or backward roll from the boat at the mooring, then a short swim over open water toward Klein Bonaire to reach the reef.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by a dive operator's boat from Kralendijk, a short crossing of the channel. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A mooring buoy.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The mooring.** Anchored on the slope at about 43 m, below the dived reef in deep water. The reef lies a short swim toward the island; take a bearing on the island before descending, and swim back out over blue water to the line at the end of the dive.
* **The terrace.** A flat, shallow terrace at about 4 m runs out from the shore, steps down to about 8 m, and ends at the reef crest about 130 m offshore.
* **The slope.** From the crest the reef drops steeply into deep water, with the elephant ear sponges and stovepipe colonies spread over the upper slope. Swimming along the reef and working down at an angle makes a gentler descent than dropping straight down the face.

* **Drivers.** The east to northeast trade winds. The north and east sides of Klein Bonaire take the strongest current around the islet, where the trades push water against it, and the current runs stronger when the trades blow hard.
* **Variable set.** The current here is often light, but it can run strongly along the reef, and its direction varies and is hard to predict. Read it at the surface before descending, swim into it first, and let it carry you back toward the mooring at the end of the dive.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | northeast, 42° |

The nearest shore lies 130 m to the southwest, 223°, and the reef looks out across the channel toward Bonaire.

* **Short fetch.** North-northwest through east (NNW-E), onshore over 1.0 to 4.7 km across the channel from Bonaire. The trades blow from the east and east-northeast end of this sector most of the year over about 1 km of water, enough to put a chop on the surface over the mooring and the swim to the reef.
* **Mixed.** West-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), cross-shore over 8.4 km to more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** East-southeast clockwise through west: cross-shore over 1.8 to 3.1 km from the east-southeast and southeast, and offshore from south-southeast through west, off the islet over 130 to 330 m.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m, reaching 40 m on the clearest days. Calm mornings run clearest, and strong trades stirring the channel bring it down.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water stays evenly mixed through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Sponges.** Giant orange elephant ear sponges, some more than a metre across, standing out from the slope like umbrellas, many with black crinoids perched on top. Huge colonies of lavender stovepipe sponge and other tube sponges between them make the site a favourite for wide-angle photography.
* **Coral.** A varied mix of hard and soft coral along the crest and the slope.
* **Reef fish.** Reef fish in abundance, with parrotfish and boxfish around the sponges.
* **Larger animals.** Green turtles, southern stingrays and spotted eagle rays are seen along this shore, with bigger fish off the slope in the blue.

## Hazards

* **Blue water swim.** The mooring sits well off the reef in deep water, so the swims out and back cross open blue water out of sight of the bottom. Hold a bearing, keep the buddy close, and keep enough gas for the swim back to the line.
* **Depth.** The slope drops steeply past the recreational range below the reef, and the mooring line itself runs to about 43 m. Watch the computer on the descent and along the slope.
* **Current.** The set can be strong and its direction hard to predict. Turn the dive early if the swim back to the mooring would be against the current, and carry a surface marker buoy for a pickup away from the line.
