# 55. Margate Bay

A shore dive on Bonaire's southwest coast, reached across a small salina beside a line of mangrove bushes off the coast road. Shallows of coral heads, staghorn and elkhorn run out onto a broad reef top at about 9 to 10.5 m, and beyond it a reef slope lush with gorgonians, sea fans and soft corals falls away to sand at about 40 m. The site takes its name from the black margates that sleep among the gorgonians along the drop-off with several kinds of snapper, and turtles are commonly seen. The headline hazards are the rugged ironshore entry, hard going when any surf is running, and a current that can build and change during a dive.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk about 40 m from the roadside, through or beside the salina and the mangrove bushes, to the yellow-painted stones that mark the entry at the water's edge. The salina is dry in dry spells and holds a few centimetres of water after rain. The entry is a step down of about 30 cm off ironshore onto rocks, with sea urchins in the holes, into about 4 m within a few metres of the edge. Swim out 107 m southwest to the reef top, then exit at the same stones.
* **Parking.** An unpaved strip along the roadside at the yellow stone with the site name, with room for several cars. Park fully off the narrow road. Free.
* **Access.** Open. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. All access is on foot from the roadside. Heading south on the coast road, the site is the first marked stone after the road narrows from two lanes to one.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The salina.** A flat salt pan between the road and the shore, edged by mangrove bushes, that marks the site from the road.
* **The shallows.** Coral heads, staghorn and elkhorn over sand and rubble, sloping from about 4 m at the entry to about 6 m some 60 m out, with patches of fire coral near the entry.
* **The mooring.** A yellow mooring buoy off the shallows, a reference point for the descent and the return.
* **The reef top.** The bottom steps down from about 6 m to about 10 m about 70 to 90 m out, onto a broad, level reef top at about 9 to 10.5 m that runs alongshore and reaches about 190 m from the entry.
* **The reef slope.** Beyond the reef top the reef slope drops away to sand at about 40 m, with gorgonians, sea fans, sea whips and soft corals above about 15 m and more hard coral and barrel sponges below.
* **The return.** Follow the reef back with the current, then head northeast up the shallows to the yellow stones. The shallows are wide enough for a long safety stop.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can stay slack for days, then run hard when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild, at times strong, and it can change direction during a dive. It runs north or south along the reef; check its direction before entering and start the dive into it, returning with it in the shallows.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 226° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 226° |

The swim out runs 107 m southwest from the entry, 7° off the shore's facing, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the ironshore, making the step down and the exit rough.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast (SSE), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through southeast (NW-SE): cross-shore over 230 to 610 m from the northwest and north-northwest, offshore off the land from north through east-southeast, and cross-shore over 820 m from the southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, around 20 to 25 m on an average day, and best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** A southerly or westerly wind, and surf on the shore, stir the shallows and cut visibility close in.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Staghorn and elkhorn coral with soft corals, sheltering schools of juvenile reef fish, crabs, snails and shrimp, with spotted eagle rays at times over the shallows.
* **Reef top and slope.** Gorgonians, large sea fans and soft corals on the upper slope, more hard coral and large sponges deeper. Black margate and snapper sleep among the gorgonians along the drop-off, with parrotfish, blue tang and surgeonfish, grunts, trumpetfish, spotted drum, French angelfish, rock beauties, longsnout butterflyfish and honeycomb cowfish.
* **Morays.** Green, spotted and goldentail morays in the reef.
* **Larger animals.** Turtles commonly seen, with tarpon, barracuda, groupers and jacks off the reef and spotted eagle rays along it. Seahorses turn up occasionally in the gorgonians.
* **Coral.** Bleaching and disease since 2023 have thinned the staghorn and elkhorn in the shallows.

## Hazards

* **Entry and exit.** Ironshore with a step down onto uneven rock, sea urchins in the holes, and fire coral in the shallows. Wear hard-soled boots, help a buddy across the step, and choose another site when surf is running onto the shore.
* **Current.** Usually mild, but it can build with the trades and change during a dive. Start into it and turn early when it builds.
* **Kitesurfers.** Kitesurfers from the kite beach to the south range along the coast here. Ascend with a surface marker buoy and keep a lookout before surfacing.
* **Depth.** The reef slope falls to sand at about 40 m. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Mosquitoes.** Mosquitoes can be thick around the mangroves early in the day.
