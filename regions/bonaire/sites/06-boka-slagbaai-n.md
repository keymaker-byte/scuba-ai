# 6. Boka Slagbaai N

The northern of two routes at Boka Slagbaai, a sheltered bay in Washington Slagbaai National Park fronted by the restored yellow buildings of the old Slagbaai plantation. Slagbaai takes its name from the Dutch slachtbaai, slaughter bay, once one of the island's two main ports, where goats were slaughtered and salted before shipping to Curaçao. A swim from the beach crosses a broad terrace of large, unusual coral formations over sand, home to rays and garden eels, out to a drop-off where larger fish patrol, and the route holds a set of replica cannons left from a film shoot. Diving here is suspended under STINAPA's closure of the park to diving, and, when open, the site turns rough in winter north swell and strong northeast trades.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the sandy beach on the north side of the largest of the historic buildings, an easy walk-in from a sheltered bay. Coral rubble lies right at the waterline and the sand is soft; boots help.
* **Parking.** About eight spaces on coral gravel beside the historic buildings, metres from the water. Parking is free.
* **Access.** Closed to diving: STINAPA closed every site north of Karpata on 1 May 2023 over Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD), reopened the coast between BOPEC and the park, Nukove and Carel's Vision included, on 27 January 2024, and keeps Washington Slagbaai National Park closed to diving while the park stays open for swimming, snorkelling and every other activity. Confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive here. When open, the site lies about 45 minutes from Kralendijk, the last stretch about 20 minutes over unpaved road from the park entrance north of Rincon on the long (yellow) route. The park opens Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, closed on Mondays and holidays, with last entry at 14:30; it can close fully or in part after heavy rain. STINAPA sets the last dive at Slagbaai to start by 14:30, with divers out of the water by 16:00 to reach the park exit before it closes. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is required. The Nature Fee e-tag covers both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai; the park takes card payment only. Fill up before entering the park; the nearest fuel is in Rincon.
* **Facilities.** The historic plantation buildings, picnic tables and palapas on the beach, and a toilet, open intermittently. No restaurant, shop or drinking water.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Swim out.** From the beach the bottom runs west-northwest, 301°, over sand and scattered coral, dropping past 7 m within 60 m of the entry and reaching 10 m at about 140 m.
* **Terrace.** A broad, gently deepening terrace of large coral formations separated by sand, 10 to 13 m deep, running out to about 260 m from the beach and north along the bay's outer edge at the same depth.
* **Drop-off.** The terrace edge, about 260 m west-northwest of the entry at 13 m, where the reef falls steeply into deep water. A long surface swim reaches it; a mooring buoy marks the reef.
* **Film cannons.** Replica cannons left from a film shoot lie on this route; leave them and any other debris in place.

* **Drivers.** The trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Usually light inside the bay, which is sheltered from the east, and at times strong enough to slow the swim back. Turn with enough gas to swim back to the beach against it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 297° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 294° |

The bay opens west-northwest onto open sea, and the swim out runs 153 m west-northwest, 301°, from the entry to the dive area, straight off the beach.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north-northwest (WSW-NNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, straight into the mouth of the bay, building a swell at the entry. Winter north swell reaches the beach under a light breeze too.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through southwest: offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, cross-shore over 130 to 350 m from the north-northeast, northeast, south-southwest and southwest, and onshore over 630 m from the north, across the bay's own northern arm. The east to southeast trades at their usual strength blow off the land here and leave the bay flat; strong northeast trades from December to February come with rough conditions at the entry, covered under Hazards.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 10 to 30 m, typically around 25 m, best through the dry months from January to August and in the calm of the early morning.
* **Swell and rain.** North winds and swell stir the shallows, and rain from September to December washes sediment off the land, dropping visibility to 10 to 15 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water stays evenly mixed through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Large, unusual coral formations over sand, with barrel and tube sponges, brain coral and lettuce coral. The shallows near the beach run to rubble, and the reef carries a good deal of dead coral after SCTLD and coral bleaching. Schools of reef fish gather around 10 to 12 m, with large parrotfish including midnight parrotfish, blue tang, French and queen angelfish, and creole wrasse.
* **Sand.** Southern stingrays, garden eel colonies and peacock flounder.
* **Reef and drop-off.** Larger fish patrol the reef and drop-off: barracuda over the reef, large green morays in the crevices, octopus, lobster, and the occasional eagle ray, hawksbill or green turtle. Lionfish are present.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Washington Slagbaai National Park is closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD measures, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Long swim and return current.** The drop-off lies about 260 m from the beach, and a current that builds during the dive can run against the swim back. Carry a surface marker buoy, and turn with gas to spare.
* **Winter swell.** North swell and strong northeast trades from December to February make the entry unsafe; choose another day when the surf is up.
* **Remote.** No mobile phone coverage in the park, and a long unpaved drive to help; leave a dive plan and an expected return time with someone outside the park, and finish diving with time to clear the park gate by 17:00.
