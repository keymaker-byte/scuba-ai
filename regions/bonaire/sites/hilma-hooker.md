# 43. Hilma Hooker

Bonaire's most famous wreck and one of the most iconic wreck dives in the Caribbean, near the start of the double reef system. The Hilma Hooker was launched in the Netherlands in 1951 and traded under six names and seven owners before her final owner's crew was caught with over 11,000 kg of marijuana hidden behind false bulkheads while tied up at the Bonaire pier in June 1984. Impounded as evidence, she was moved to an anchorage next to Angel City that September and sank there on 12 September 1984 after struggling to stay afloat for five days. The site is now also a working example of how coral colonizes an artificial structure, over four decades on the seabed.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A shore entry finds the shallows full of fish before reaching the wreck; a boat entry drops divers closer to it but leaves less bottom time given the depth.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The stern mooring.** Marks the site and sits over the top of the wreck, at about 18 to 19 m.
* **The mast.** The wreck's deepest point, at about 30 m.
* **Angel City.** Sits immediately adjacent, where the ship was originally anchored before she sank.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Logged dives here have found slack to negligible current.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The top of the wreck sits around 18 m and the mast at about 30 m; logged dives here have reached 30.4 m. Boat divers in particular should track bottom time closely given the depth.

## Hazards

* **Depth and limited bottom time.** The wreck sits deep enough on a recreational profile that a boat dive here allows only a short working time; plan gas and NDL margins accordingly.
* **Wreck penetration.** Structural entry into a wreck this age carries its own risks; stay outside unless trained and equipped for penetration.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces west.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against any current here is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast; logged dives here have rated it 5 out of 5 (excellent).

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October; logged dives here measured 29 C. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **The wreck.** Over four decades of coral growth now covers the hull, a living example of colonization on an artificial site.
* **Shallows.** Full of fish on the shore approach to the wreck.
