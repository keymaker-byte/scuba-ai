# N. Forest

A boat dive off a small rocky peninsula on the south side of Klein Bonaire, toward the islet's west end, named for the fern-like black coral that grows in dense colonies down its slope. A shallow terrace of gorgonians and sand runs out from the shore past the mooring to the edge of a steep drop-off, where black coral grows more abundant with depth among sponges and hard corals. A large cave at about 23 m gives occasional shelter to a green moray, a loggerhead turtle or a nurse shark, and a large overhang sits at about 27 to 30 m. The headline hazards are how quickly the slope gains depth toward the overhang and a current that builds when the trades strengthen or shift.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace, with the drop-off about 30 m farther out.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring on the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and gorgonians at about 5 to 10 m, running about 90 m out from the shore, with the mooring about 60 m out. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive, and as a snorkel site.
* **The drop-off.** Begins at the outer edge of the terrace at about 10 m and falls steeply, with black coral growing denser the deeper it goes.
* **The cave.** A large cave in the slope at about 23 m.
* **The overhang.** A large overhang at about 27 to 30 m, the deepest feature of the dive.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild to moderate along the slope.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | southeast, 135° |

* **Bad.** East-southeast through south-southwest (ESE-SSW), onshore over 5 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** East (E), onshore over 1.6 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Mixed.** Southwest through west-southwest (SW-WSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east-northeast: offshore from west through north-northeast, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 180 to 470 m from the northeast and east-northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 15 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the east through south-southwest, or a swell from the southwest, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Black coral.** Fern-like black coral grows in large colonies on the slope, sparse near the edge and plentiful from about 20 m down, alongside many sponges and hard corals.
* **The cave.** A green moray, a loggerhead turtle or a nurse shark shelters here on occasion.
* **The overhang.** Nurse sharks, groupers, grunts and chromis gather in its shelter, with jacks off its face.
* **Terrace.** Gorgonians in the shallows, where seahorses hide and cleaning stations work, and sandy patches crossed by turtles and spotted eagle rays. French angelfish approach divers closely, a habit left from boats feeding them in the past, which the park prohibits.
* **Blue water.** Schools of barracuda and black durgon off the drop-off.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the edge of the terrace at about 10 m, and the cave and overhang draw divers to 23 to 30 m; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen or shift and can make the return to the mooring hard work; check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the east through south-southwest runs onto this shore and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the southwest through west-southwest runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
