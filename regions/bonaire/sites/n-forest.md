# N. Forest

Named for its forest of black coral, on a small southwestern peninsula of Klein Bonaire. The mooring sits at about 8 m next to a steep drop-off; black coral becomes more abundant with depth, alongside many sponges and hard corals, and a large overhang looms around 27 m. At about 23 m a large cave occasionally shelters a green moray, a female loggerhead turtle, or a nurse shark. A loggerhead known locally as Forest returns to the site's beach every two to three years to nest and rest along the overhang.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat, at a mooring about 8 m deep next to the drop-off.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The overhang.** Around 27 m, a major feature of the drop-off.
* **The cave.** At about 23 m, occasional shelter for a green moray, a resident female loggerhead, or a nurse shark.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The mooring sits at about 8 m, with the drop-off, cave and overhang running through the recreational range to about 30 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces the southwest peninsula of Klein Bonaire.
* **Bad.** A strong wind from the exposed quadrant raises chop.
* **Fine.** Calmer conditions, or wind blocked by the bulk of the islet.
* **Wind against current.** Worth checking before entry, as at any boat-only Klein Bonaire site.

## Visibility

Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m or more.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Deeper reef.** A forest of black coral, increasingly dense with depth, alongside sponges and hard coral.
* **The cave.** A green moray, a resident female loggerhead turtle, or a nurse shark, on occasion.
