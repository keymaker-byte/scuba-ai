# L. South Bay

A protected site on Klein Bonaire with mild current, beautiful coral formations and Klein Bonaire's typically excellent visibility. The reef runs from about 9 to 30 m and works well for both a standard dive and a night dive.

|---|---|

| **Coordinates** | 12.14965, -68.320133 (about 0 m at the shore-side edge, deepening offshore) |

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat only.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The reef.** Runs from about 9 to 30 m, without a single standout landmark beyond its overall coral cover.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Current here typically runs mild, part of why the site is regarded as protected.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef runs from about 9 to 30 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 181° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west-southwest (SSE-WSW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises chop at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** Southeast (SE), onshore over 4.5 km from the south end of Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from northwest through east-northeast, off the islet, and cross-shore over 290 m to 4.6 km from the west, west-northwest, east and east-southeast.

## Visibility

Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Groupers and schools of bar and horse-eye jacks, often seen feeding on schools of boga just off the reef.
