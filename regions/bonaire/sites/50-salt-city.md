# 50. Salt City

Salt City is a double reef shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 250 m south of Salt Pier on Bonaire's southwest coast, named for the Cargill salt works behind it. A slick ironshore entry gives onto a long, gently shelving sand and coral terrace that runs about 300 m out before the first reef slopes away to a sand channel at about 21 to 25 m, where stingrays live, with a second reef beyond it. The first reef suits any level. The headline hazards are the entry and exit over slick ironshore, rough at times, and the depth and navigation of the crossing to the second reef.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk 46 m south from the parking area to a gap between two sets of bushes along the road, where the ironshore has worn to an easy slope into the water, slick underfoot. Wade in with fins in hand to about knee depth, put them on, and swim out. Swim 181 m west-southwest, straight out from the shore, to the dive area on the terrace, and on across it to the first reef. Exit at the same slope, where small steps lead back up near the parking area.
* **Parking.** The small parking area beside EEG Boulevard, free.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit, from the site's mooring.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry.** The worn slope lies between the site's mooring to the south and Salt Pier to the north. Note the shore around it on the way out; the ironshore either side is rougher.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral shelving from the waterline to about 4 m within a few metres of shore, holding there for the first 50 m, then gently to about 6 m at 70 m out, 8 m at 140 m and 9 m at the dive area, 181 m out. Beyond the dive area it levels at 10 to 12 m for another 120 m. Elkhorn grows in the shallows near the entry. The terrace serves as the safety stop on the way back.
* **The first reef.** Begins at the edge of the terrace, about 300 m west-southwest of the entry and roughly even with the end of Salt Pier, and slopes gently down to the sand channel. To the south it runs on some way before breaking up; to the north it runs deeper.
* **The sand channel.** At about 21 to 25 m between the two reefs, where stingrays live and garden eels hold in the sand. Crossing it needs a compass heading out and back.
* **The second reef.** Rises beyond the channel, older hard coral and orange sponges, with the deepest diving at about 30 m.
* **The return.** Come back up the first reef onto the terrace and head east-northeast, 181 m from the dive area to the worn slope.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs along the reef and can stay slack for long spells, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually light to moderate along the reef, and strong at times. Check its direction before entering, and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 253° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 254° |

The swim out runs 181 m west-southwest from the entry, 2° off the shore's facing, straight out.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the ironshore, stirs up the shallows and puts surge on the entry and exit.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and north-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 8.2 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast (N-SSE): offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 790 m from the north and 860 m from the south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on the reef, typically about 20 m, and much the same year round. Best on calm mornings.
* **The shallows.** Waves breaking on the shore from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) stir up the terrace near the entry and cut visibility there, while the reef further out stays clearer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Green turtles grazing in the shallows, often several at once. Rays and flounder on the sand on the swim out, with rays in the shallows late in the afternoon, and flamingo tongues on the sea fans.
* **First reef.** Blue tang, stoplight parrotfish, palometa, angelfish, trumpetfish, spotted drum and squid, with barracuda patrolling, groupers, and tarpon passing. Blennies, gobies and cleaner shrimp in the coral.
* **Sand and second reef.** Resident stingrays and garden eels in the sand between the reefs. Spotted eagle rays and large green morays on the transition to the second reef. Hawksbill turtles on occasion.
* **Night.** Morays hunting in the open, with lobster, crabs and tarpon on the reef.
* **Coral.** Hard and soft coral on both reefs, softer coral more abundant on the first. Elkhorn and other hard coral along the whole leeward coast have suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **The entry and exit.** The ironshore is slick and can be rough to cross, more so at night. Wear sturdy boots, help a buddy in and out, and exit at the worn slope; take a bearing back to it before leaving the terrace.
* **Depth.** The sand channel lies at about 21 to 25 m and the second reef runs to about 30 m, close to EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar. The gentle slope makes it easy to go deeper than planned; watch the computer, and keep within the no-decompression limit, which is short at that depth.
* **Navigation.** The second reef is found by compass. Hold a compass heading across the channel and back, and when unsure of the way home, climb the first reef onto the terrace before surfacing.
* **Gas.** The 300 m swim to the first reef and back, plus the crossing to the second reef, uses a large share of a cylinder. Turn on gas with the swim home over the terrace still to come.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind from south-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW) drives waves and surge onto the ironshore and makes the entry and exit rough; choose another site when it blows fresh from that quarter or a swell is running.
* **Boats.** Dive boats visit the mooring. Ascend with a surface marker buoy.
