# 50. Salt City

The first double reef site south of Salt Pier, named for its proximity to the salt works, with landscapes alternating between sandbar and coral reef. The first reef suits any level; the second reef, at about 21 m and needing some navigation to reach, is best left to divers with more experience. A sandbar between the two reefs is a reliable spot for stingrays, and eagle rays and large morays turn up on the transition between them.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A shore entry typical of this stretch of coast.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The sandbar.** Between the first and second reef, a resident stingray spot and the point to navigate carefully to find the second reef.
* **Second reef.** Begins around 21 m and needs some navigation; not a route for a beginner.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The first reef sits in the shallows, with the sandbar and second reef beginning around 21 m and the site running to about 30 m.

## Hazards

* **Second reef navigation.** Reaching it needs real navigation skill; not recommended for a beginner working alone.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 251° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 253° |

The swim out runs 278 m south-southwest from the entry, along the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the entry.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over more than 30 km, and north-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 8.4 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 530 to 600 m from the north and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

Typically reaches about 20 m to the entry point, in line with the rest of the leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Sandbar.** Resident stingrays.
* **Transition to the second reef.** Eagle rays and large morays, along with a cleaning station.
* **Reef.** Green turtles and barracuda.
