# W. Ebo's Special

A boat dive on the north shore of Klein Bonaire toward its west end, also known as Jerry's Jam, named for Ebo Domacassé, Bonaire's first certified diver. A sand terrace runs about 90 m out from the shore, deepening from about 4 m to an edge at about 8 to 10 m, and the reef falls away beyond it down a steep slope. Small caves and caverns in the shallows shelter lobsters and large groupers, and at times a resting nurse shark. The mooring is fixed to a helix anchor bolt, a design that held through the 1999 storm surge on this side of Klein Bonaire and became the pattern for later replacement moorings. The headline hazard is the drop off the terrace edge, which runs on past 30 m.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a short descent onto the sand terrace at about 7 m.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by boat, usually a dive operator's, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring, fixed to a helix anchor bolt.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand with scattered coral, about 4 m deep along the shore and deepening gently to about 7 m at the mooring, then on to an edge at about 8 to 10 m some 90 m out from the shore. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The shallow caves.** Small caves and caverns in the shallows close to the shore, the site's signature feature, holding lobsters, large groupers and at times a resting nurse shark.
* **The slope.** Falls steeply from the terrace edge, its top the most colourful part of the reef, and runs on past 30 m into deep water. A small cave along the slope at about 18 m can hold a sleeping nurse shark.
* **The way back.** The reef runs parallel to the shore, so a dive out along the slope in one direction and back along the terrace returns to the mooring.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild to none on the terrace and the upper slope.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | north, 350° |

* **Bad.** West-northwest through north-northwest (WNW-NNW), onshore over 5.9 to more than 30 km of open water, putting chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** North through northeast (N-NE), onshore over 3.5 to 4.8 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Mixed.** West (W), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** East-northeast clockwise through west-southwest: cross-shore over 3.2 km from the east-northeast and 260 m from the east, offshore from east-southeast through southwest, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 320 m from the west-southwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-northwest through northeast, or a swell from the west, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Caves.** Caribbean spiny lobsters, often several to a cave, with large groupers resting inside and at the entrances. A nurse shark sleeps at times in a shallow cavern or among the coral heads, or in a small cave along the slope at about 18 m.
* **Terrace.** Sand with scattered hard coral heads.
* **The slope.** Hard corals, sponges and gorgonians, with cleaning stations where chromis and parrotfish gather. A large school of creole wrasse often hangs over the reef, with snapper, angelfish and chubs along it, octopus in the crevices, and barracuda off the slope. Lionfish, an invasive species, turn up on the reef.
* **Turtles.** Seen frequently along the reef.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the terrace edge at about 8 to 10 m and runs on past 30 m; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Caves.** The caves and caverns are small; look in with a light, keep clear of the walls and the coral, and leave the animals inside undisturbed.
* **Current.** Wind driven current is usually mild here but can build when the trades strengthen or shift; check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-northwest through north-northwest runs onto this shore over open water and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the north through northeast builds a shorter chop across the channel; a wind from the west runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire's north shore; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
