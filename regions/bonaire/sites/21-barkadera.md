# 21. Barkadera

A boat dive on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, directly opposite the headquarters of STINAPA and the Bonaire National Marine Park at Barcadera. A shallow terrace about 100 m wide steps from 4 m at the shore to 11 m before the reef slopes away, its midzone carrying elkhorn coral and fire coral among the rubble of the staghorn and elkhorn thickets that once covered it, with schools of blue tang grazing across it and large star coral heads along the reef break. The headline hazards are boat traffic over the site and a wind driven current that runs strong at times.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a boat tied to a park mooring, since anchoring is banned throughout the marine park, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** By boat from Kralendijk, about 10 to 15 minutes away. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **STINAPA headquarters.** The park headquarters at Barcadera stands across the coast road from the site, the clearest reference from the water.
* **Terrace.** The bottom runs at about 4 m along the foot of the shore and deepens to 7 m about 40 m out, where the dive area coordinate sits.
* **Reef break.** About 60 m off the shore the terrace reaches 11 m and holds there to roughly 100 m out, where the reef breaks and the slope begins.
* **Slope.** Past the break the reef drops away through 30 m toward sand at about 40 m.
* **Along the terrace.** Along the 40 m line off the shore, the terrace shallows to about 5 m within 300 m to the east-southeast and deepens to 8 to 9 m within 300 m to the west-northwest.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light, and strong at times.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 206° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast (SSE), onshore over 7.5 km down the coast, and southwest through west (SW-W), onshore over 30 km or more of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises chop and swell at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), onshore over 3.2 to 3.8 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** West-northwest (WNW), cross-shore over 6.6 km, running a swell along the shore past the mooring.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the land, and cross-shore over 100 m to 1.2 km from the northwest, east-southeast and southeast. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the site calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m and often more, best on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Wind and swell from the south-southeast through west stir the shallow terrace and cut visibility there; the water clears over the slope.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Elkhorn coral and fire coral grow among wide fields of dead elkhorn and staghorn rubble, the remains of the thickets that covered the whole terrace before white band disease in the early 1980s. The surviving elkhorn took further losses in the bleaching events of 2023 and 2024, part of a leeward coast pattern of about 19% of colonies dead outright and 55% more with partial mortality.
* **Reef break and slope.** Large star coral heads with sea whips line the reef break, with plate corals and cleaning stations down the slope. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Fish.** Schools of blue tang graze across the terrace, with filefish, rock beauties and drums on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats and pleasure boats pass over the site; carry a surface marker buoy and ascend along the reef or the mooring line.
* **Current.** The wind driven current runs strong at times; start the dive into it and keep enough gas to return to the mooring.
* **Depth.** The slope continues to 40 m directly below the reef break, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Elkhorn.** The surviving elkhorn colonies on the terrace sit at 5 to 10 m; hold buoyancy well clear of them on the descent and the safety stop.
* **Swell.** Wind from the south-southeast through west puts chop and swell on the mooring; choose another site when it is running.
