# Z. Sampler

A boat dive on the north coast of Klein Bonaire, the islet's northernmost site and one of the most visited in the marine park. A shallow sand and coral terrace at about 4 to 9 m runs about 100 m out from the shore before a steep slope drops away from about 9 to 12 m, with large coral growth and overhangs in its deeper reaches. French and queen angelfish, used to divers, swim up close, and the sand holds yellowhead jawfish, sand divers and sailfin blennies, while morays, octopus, frogfish and orange seahorses hide in the reef. The headline hazards are the slope, which runs on past recreational depth, and the open water to the northwest, which puts chop on the site whenever the wind comes from that quarter.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat, then a descent onto the terrace. Snorkelers reach the shallows by swimming west along the shore from No Name Beach, where the water taxi lands, about 530 m to the east.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by boat, usually a dive operator's, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00, and the last water taxi back to Bonaire leaves at about 16:45. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral from the shore at about 4 m, levelling out at about 7 to 9 m and running about 100 m out from the shore along the islet's north coast. It holds the sand species and serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The drop.** The terrace edge falls away steeply from about 9 to 12 m and runs on past 30 m into deep water.
* **The deeper reef.** A rocky reef profile with ample overhangs and large, healthy coral growth along the slope, sheltering morays deeper down.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually slight, and the site is commonly dived with little or no current. When the trades strengthen, the water they push onto the islet can set harder along the north side.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | north, 355° |

* **Bad.** West-northwest through north-northwest (WNW-NNW), onshore over 5.1 to more than 30 km of open water, putting chop at the site and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** North through northeast (N-NE), onshore over 2.3 to 3.6 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop over the site.
* **Mixed.** West (W), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** East-northeast clockwise through west-southwest: cross-shore over 2.1 to 2.5 km from the east-northeast and east, offshore from east-southeast through southwest, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 240 m from the west-southwest. The islet shelters the site from the prevailing trade winds.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-northwest through northeast, or a swell from the west, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows, and rain washing sediment off the land lowers it for a time.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Angelfish.** French and queen angelfish approach divers closely, used to being fed in the past; feeding any marine life is prohibited in the marine park.
* **Sand.** Yellowhead jawfish, sand divers and sailfin blennies on the terrace. Green turtles rest and feed on the sand and the reef, and hawksbill turtles pass along the slope.
* **Reef.** Morays of several kinds, more of them deeper on the slope, with octopus, frogfish, lobster and crabs in the overhangs. Parrotfish, trumpetfish, grunts, snapper, groupers and lionfish work the reef.
* **Seahorses.** Orange seahorses in the coral and sponges, hard to spot without a guide.
* **Open water.** Barracuda and tarpon off the slope, with the odd spotted eagle ray.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the terrace edge at about 9 to 12 m and runs on past recreational depth; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Current.** Usually slight, but it can set harder along the north side when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-northwest through north-northwest runs onto this shore over open water, and one from the north through northeast builds a chop across the channel from Bonaire; a wind from the west runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water along the north coast; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the boat.
