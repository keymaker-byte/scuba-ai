# Z. Sampler

Klein Bonaire's northernmost dive site, well visited and one of the most photographed on the islet. Particularly friendly angelfish approach divers looking for a handout, along with many of the reef's other residents. A rocky reef profile with ample overhangs makes a haven for moray eels, octopus and frogfish, and the common sandy bottom species, sand divers, yellow headed jawfish and sailfin blennies, are easy to find. Large, healthy coral growth appears in the deeper areas, and many snorkelers reach this site from the beach after the water taxi drops them on the island.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat for divers; snorkelers can reach the shallows from the beach after arriving on the water taxi.
* **Parking.** Not applicable for the boat dive.
* **Access.** Reached by a dive operator's boat, or as a snorkel from the beach; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Overhangs.** Scattered through the rocky reef profile, sheltering moray eels, octopus and frogfish.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The rocky reef runs through the recreational range, with the largest, healthiest coral growth in the deeper areas.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces the north coast of Klein Bonaire.
* **Bad.** A strong wind from the exposed quadrant raises chop.
* **Fine.** Calmer conditions, or wind blocked by the bulk of the islet.
* **Wind against current.** Worth checking before entry, as at any boat-only Klein Bonaire site.

## Visibility

Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m or more.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Friendly angelfish that approach divers, moray eels, octopus and frogfish in the overhangs.
* **Sandy bottom.** Sand divers, yellow headed jawfish and sailfin blennies.
