# 24. Petries Pillar

A boat dive on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, named by Captain Don Stewart as a wedding gift for a friend. A sand and coral terrace runs from about 4 m at the shore out to 9 to 11 m roughly 100 m off, wider than the terrace along most of the coast, before the reef slopes away to 30 m and beyond. It is an easy dive suited to beginners, where a large school of blue tang often works the reef. The headline hazard is a wind driven current that is usually light and moderate at times.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a boat tied to the park mooring, a yellow buoy carrying the site's name, since anchoring is banned throughout the marine park, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** By boat from Kralendijk, a short run north along the coast. The site has no yellow stone and no marked shore entry; from land it is reached only by a surface swim of about 240 m south from the nearest marked entry to the north, or down an unmarked rock trail through scrub from the end of the residential road above it. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Inner terrace.** The bottom sits at about 4 m along the foot of the shore and holds at 4 to 6 m for the first 40 to 60 m out, mostly sand with patches of shallow coral.
* **Outer terrace.** Beyond that the terrace deepens to 9 m about 75 m off the shore, where the dive area coordinate sits, and to 9 to 11 m by about 100 m off. Coral humps along its outer edge rise to about 7 m.
* **Slope.** Past the terrace edge the reef slopes away through 30 m.
* **Along the shore.** North along the coast the terrace stays wide, holding 5 to 6 m water well out from the shore before it deepens.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light, and moderate at times.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 255° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west-northwest (WSW-WNW), onshore over 10 to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises chop and swell at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over 2.2 to 3.2 km from Klein Bonaire, and northwest (NW), onshore over 3.9 km from the coast to the north, each enough to build a chop over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** South-southeast (SSE), cross-shore over 5.8 km down the coast, running a swell along the shore past the mooring.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through southeast, and south: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 390 m to 2.4 km from the north-northwest, north and south. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the site calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m and often more, best on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Onshore wind and swell stir the shallow terrace and cut visibility there; the water clears over the slope.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Patches of shallow coral among the sand, busy with small reef fish. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Reef.** A large school of blue tang often works the reef, with yellowtail snapper, butter hamlet, rock hind, sleeping soapfish and lettuce sea slugs. Schools of chub pass along the reef, and ocean triggerfish turn up off the slope.
* **Also seen.** Turtles and frogfish.
* **Night.** Small reef fish are plentiful on the reef after dark.

## Hazards

* **Current.** The wind driven current runs moderately at times; start the dive into it and keep enough gas to return to the mooring.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats and pleasure boats pass along the coast; carry a surface marker buoy and ascend along the reef or the mooring line.
* **Depth.** The slope continues below 30 m past the terrace edge, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Swell.** Wind from the south-southwest through northwest puts chop and swell on the mooring; choose another site when it is running.
