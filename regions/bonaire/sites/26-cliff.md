# 26. Cliff

A short vertical wall on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, the house reef in front of the Hamlet Oasis Villas and the Dive Friends shop there. A shallow terrace of sand, rubble and coral runs about 64 m out from the entry to a lip at 9 to 10 m, where a wall dense with whip coral drops to its base around 22 m and the reef keeps sloping into deep water beyond. The site holds Captain Don's underwater Stone Memorial to the divers who have gone before, marked with a plaque and a dive flag, and a large pipe running down the reef from the entry makes it an easy site to navigate by day or night. The headline hazard is depth: the slope below the wall continues well past recreational limits.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down the short flight of stairs behind the Dive Friends shop to the yellow stone, then a few steps over loose dead coral and rubble into the water. Other points along this stretch of shore have sharp rock and surf and are hazardous to enter. Boats from Kralendijk and the resorts to the south also dive the site from its mooring.
* **Parking.** In the lot behind the Dive Friends shop at Hamlet Oasis, about 40 m from the water, free for Hamlet Oasis guests and Dive Friends customers. Park away from the villas.
* **Access.** On private resort property, open to shore divers through Dive Friends; ask at the shop before diving. The shop is open daily 08:00 to 17:00 and the property's gates are locked in the evening, so arrange a night dive with the shop beforehand. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** The Dive Friends Bonaire shop at Hamlet Oasis, with gear rinse and disinfection tanks and seating near the entry.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pipe.** A large pipe sheathing a cable runs from beside the entry straight down the reef to about 18 m. It is the main navigation line: follow it out to the reef, and return along the reef top to meet it for the exit.
* **Terrace.** The bottom sits at about 3.5 m at the entry and slopes gently over sand, rubble and scattered coral to the lip, about 64 m out.
* **Lip of the wall.** At 9 to 10 m the terrace ends and the wall begins, running south from in front of the entry, on the left when swimming out.
* **The wall.** A near-vertical face from the lip to its base around 22 m, covered in whip coral, plate corals and sponges, with large elephant ear sponges around 18 m.
* **The small cliff.** A small partial cliff lies directly under the mooring buoy.
* **Captain Don's Stone Memorial.** An underwater stone dedicated to the divers who have gone before, marked with a plaque and a dive flag.
* **Below the wall.** Past the base the reef keeps sloping into deep water, well beyond 40 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually limited along the wall, and occasionally strong enough to make the return swim hard work.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 236° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 236° |

The swim out runs 64 m west-southwest from the entry, straight off the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South (S), onshore over 5.8 km down the coast, and west through west-northwest (W-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from these quarters raises waves at the entry and stirs up the shallows.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), onshore over 1.3 to 1.6 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over 6.1 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 270 m to 1 km from the north-northwest, southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength, and a north wind leaves the water calm and clear.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m, often more, holding through the year.
* **Light.** The wall faces southwest and lies in shadow in the early morning; it is fully lit from about midday through the afternoon.
* **Wind.** Wind from south through west-northwest stirs the shallow terrace and cuts visibility there.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **The wall.** Whip coral, plate corals, sea fans and sponges, with large elephant ear sponges around 18 m. Seahorses are found among the whip coral and gorgonians on the wall face. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Open water.** Large tarpon patrol the wall, with barracuda, jacks and an occasional spotted eagle ray in the blue.
* **Terrace.** Frogfish on the terrace, green and spotted morays, sharptail eels, peacock flounder, octopus, and schools of reef squid in the shallows. Turtles feed on the terrace, and juvenile fish crowd the small corals.
* **Fish.** French and queen angelfish, parrotfish, trumpetfish, groupers, and occasionally a juvenile spotted drum.
* **Night.** Tarpon hunt in divers' lights, and lobsters, octopus, banded coral shrimp and arrow crabs come out on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Depth.** The wall gives way to a slope that continues well past 40 m, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats tie to the mooring over the wall, and pleasure boats pass along the coast; carry a surface marker buoy and surface along the reef or the pipe.
* **Current.** The wind driven current is usually limited and occasionally runs strong; start the dive into it and keep enough gas for the return to the pipe.
* **Entry footing and fire coral.** Loose dead coral makes the footing uneven at the entry, and fire coral grows in the shallows beside it.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from the south (S) and from west through west-northwest (W-WNW) puts waves on the entry, from south-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW) a chop, and from the northwest (NW) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
* **Exit.** The shore beside the entry is rocky; exit where you entered, at the pipe and the yellow stone.
