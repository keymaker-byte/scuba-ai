# 4. Bise Morto

A remote site in Washington Slagbaai National Park whose name means dead calf, a short walk south of Playa Funchi along the same exposed stretch of coast. Elkhorn and staghorn coral crowd the shallows, and the open water beyond the reef edge is one of the park's better spots for a sighting of a deep water pelagic. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering the whole park; STINAPA has found no SCTLD in this closed stretch, though the island-wide bleaching events of 2023 and 2024 affected shallow coral here regardless of disease status.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Shore entry off the park road, similar rocky footing to the rest of this stretch of coast.
* **Parking.** Beside the park road at the site; unpaved.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's unpaved roads; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallows.** Elkhorn and staghorn coral dominate close to shore.
* **Reef edge.** The bottom drops away quickly beyond the shallows, the same fast wall profile as neighbouring Playa Funchi.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. Current at Bise Morto can run moderate to strong, enough that the site suits an intermediate to advanced diver rather than a beginner.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The shallows with the elkhorn and staghorn coral sit around 6 m, dropping quickly beyond the reef edge to well past 100 m within a short distance, the same steep profile as the coast either side of it.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since May 2023; treat as not diveable until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Moderate to strong current.** Suits an intermediate to advanced diver.
* **Remote entry.** No facilities and a rocky shore.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it raises swell at this exposed stretch of the park.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against this site's current is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Elkhorn and staghorn coral, though the 2023 and 2024 bleaching events left their mark here as everywhere on the island.
* **Open water.** One of the park's better spots for deep water pelagics, with reports of whale sharks, manta rays and spouting humpback whales off the reef edge.
