# 10. La Dania's Leap

One of Bonaire's few true vertical walls, on the northwest coast east of Karpata, entered from shore with a giant stride off the low limestone ledge that gives the site its name. The rock offers no way back out, so the dive is a one-way drift of about 500 m west-northwest along the reef and onto a slope of ridges and sand chutes, ending at the Karpata platform. The headline hazards are the commitment of the leap, a wall that drops past 30 m within a few metres of its lip, and the King Willem-Alexander Reserve just beyond the Karpata exit.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A path of about 60 m from the road crosses sharp, jagged limestone, a stretch of dirt through low scrub, then more rock to the ledge, which stands about 1 to 1.5 m above about 4.5 m of water. Its edge rolls off rounded, like the leading edge of a wing, so lean forward into the giant stride. Gear up at the road, cross the rock in sturdy hard-soled boots, and enter with fins on, regulator in and the BCD partly inflated, timing the stride between swells. Once in, swim straight out across the terrace to the wall. The exit is the Karpata platform (12.219349, -68.352014), about 450 m west-northwest of the ledge in a straight line.
* **Parking.** The pull-off at the trailhead holds two or three cars. The usual plan is to drop divers and gear here, drive on to Karpata and park there, and walk back about 500 m along the road, 5 to 10 minutes, so the car waits at the exit. Parking is free.
* **Access.** The coastal road runs one way past the site toward Karpata, so a missed turn-off cannot be driven back to; drive slowly and watch for the pile of stacked rocks on the seaward side of the road. STINAPA closed Karpata and La Dania's Leap from July to September 2022 after a fast-spreading coral disease was found on a pillar coral at Karpata; both reopened in September 2022 and stayed open through the 2023 closure of the coast to the north. Confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required. The site is also dived by boat, from the mooring off the ledge.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Terrace.** From the ledge the bottom runs out flat at about 4.5 m for 35 m, then steps down to 8 m by 45 m out.
* **Lip of the wall.** The dive area coordinate sits on the lip at 10.0 m, 70 m south, 190°, from the ledge. The mooring buoy, when in place, marks the area just off the ledge.
* **Wall.** A vertical wall of overhangs and crevices drops from the lip past 30 m, with a second, steeper wall below from about 40 m to past 60 m. Keep it on the right, the shore side, heading west-northwest.
* **Ridges and sand chutes.** After about a third to half of the distance the wall gives way to a slope of steep coral ridges and sand chutes running down from the shore, the start of the Karpata reef.
* **Karpata anchors.** Old ship anchors embedded in the coral around 11 m mark the approach to Karpata, a short swim east-southeast of the Karpata mooring.
* **Exit.** From the anchors, climb onto the shallow terrace and follow the line of large concrete blocks toward shore to the platform. The King Willem-Alexander Reserve begins about 90 m past the platform.

## Current

| | |
|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | west-northwest, 290°, along the coast toward Karpata |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east trades. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually light, from still to a gentle push toward Karpata, and at times strong. It can run stronger where the wall gives way to the Karpata ridges, where swimming higher on the reef top takes the edge off, and over the ridge crests, with the valleys between them sheltered.
* **Reversal.** On some days the set reverses and runs east-southeast, away from the only exit. Check the set before entering, from the mooring line or the water off the ledge, or by diving a neighbouring site first.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south, 181° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 178° |

The coast here runs east to west, and the swim out runs 70 m south, 190°, straight out from the ledge.

* **Bad.** Southeast through west-southwest (SE-WSW), onshore over 6.8 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves against the ledge, making the stride off it hazardous, and onto the Karpata platform at the exit.
* **Mixed.** West (W), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the ledge.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east-southeast: cross-shore over 120 m from the west-northwest, offshore from northwest through east-northeast over 60 to 150 m, off the land, and cross-shore over 450 m from the east and 4.4 km from the east-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow along the shore here over a short fetch and leave the ledge flat. Swell in winter and from passing tropical systems still puts surf on the ledge and the Karpata platform under a light wind; skip the dive when waves break against the rock.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m, typically around 25 m, clear along the wall.
* **Wind and swell.** Best on calm mornings, lower when wind and waves stir the shallows later in the day.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm wetsuit or shorty is standard, with long sleeves worth having for a drift close to the wall, and hard-soled boots for the rock.

## Marine life

* **Wall.** Gorgonian sea fans in yellow, orange and purple, wire coral, sponges and soft corals cover the wall, with plate corals deeper. Schools of barracuda patrol along it, with French and queen angelfish, stoplight and queen parrotfish, and trumpetfish.
* **Mid to deep wall.** Tiny juvenile sunshine fish, yellow on top and bright blue below.
* **Crevices.** Green and spotted morays, with green morays sometimes swimming free over the reef.
* **Turtles.** Green turtles are regular, and hawksbills turn up less often.
* **Terrace and ridges.** Large parrotfish among the coral heads of the Karpata shallows, and lionfish.

## Hazards

* **No exit at the entry.** The rock offers no way back out of the water, and there is no exit between the ledge and the Karpata platform. Once in, the dive ends at Karpata, whatever the current does.
* **Reversed current.** When the set runs east-southeast, the swim to Karpata runs against it the whole way. Confirm the set before entering, keep the dive shallow on the reef top where the current is weaker, and keep gas in hand to swim the full distance.
* **Overshooting Karpata.** The exit is easy to miss, and a diver who drifts past it enters the King Willem-Alexander Reserve, where diving is prohibited and the shore offers no exit. Learn the Karpata exit beforehand by diving Karpata from its platform, watch for the anchors, and head up onto the terrace early.
* **Depth.** The wall drops past 30 m right off its lip and continues past 60 m, and depth runs up quickly while drifting along it. Set a depth limit before the leap and stay well inside the NDL to leave time for the long shallow run to Karpata.
* **Gas.** The drift runs about 500 m and 45 to 60 minutes at a relaxed pace; turn shallow with enough gas to reach the platform and make a safety stop.
* **Entry rock and surf.** The path and ledge are sharp, jagged limestone, and surf can push a diver back against the rock on entry. Wear sturdy boots, and stay out when waves break against the ledge.
* **Car and gear.** Gear and divers wait unattended at the road while the driver shuttles the car, and break-ins happen at the isolated Karpata parking; leave nothing of value in the vehicle, and nothing behind at the trailhead.
* **Stony coral tissue loss disease (SCTLD).** Disinfect gear before, between and after every dive per STINAPA's protocol, and keep clear of the coral.
