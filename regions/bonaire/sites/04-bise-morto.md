# 4. Bise Morto

A remote site in Washington Slagbaai National Park, on the park's northwest coast, whose name means dead calf. A narrow pebble beach below a low limestone cliff gives onto a broad reef terrace once known for wide fields of staghorn and elkhorn coral, with a steep drop into deep water at its outer edge where deep water pelagics turn up on occasion. The current runs from slight to strong, and the climb down to the beach makes the entry the hardest part of the dive. Diving here is suspended under STINAPA's closure of the park to diving.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A steep path leads from the road down a limestone cliff about 4 m high to a narrow pebble beach, the last stretch a climb over and between rocks with gear in hand. The beach is small and can be awash, and the rocks take any swell, so the entry is rough in all but calm water.
* **Parking.** Free, on unpaved ground beside the park road, where a yellow stone painted with the site's name marks the spot.
* **Access.** Closed to diving: STINAPA closed every site north of Karpata on 1 May 2023 over Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease (SCTLD), reopened the coast between BOPEC and the park, Nukove and Carel's Vision included, on 27 January 2024, and keeps Washington Slagbaai National Park closed to diving while the park stays open for every other activity, snorkeling included. Confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive here. When open, the site lies on the stretch of road shared by the short (green) and long (yellow) routes from the park entrance north of Rincon, each a one-way dirt road with a 25 km/h limit. STINAPA recommends at least 30 cm of ground clearance, and rangers at the gate may turn away a lower vehicle depending on road conditions. The park opens Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, closed on Mondays and holidays, with last entry at 14:30, and can close fully or in part after heavy rain. The Nature Fee e-tag covers both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai. When the park reopens to diving, licensed operators also reach the site by boat, a run of 20 to 30 minutes from Kralendijk.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach.** A narrow strip of pebbles at the foot of the cliff, with rock either side, the only practical exit along this stretch of shore.
* **Inner shallows.** Shallows at 4 to 6 m for the first 20 m west of the entry, deepening to about 10 m some 40 m out.
* **Reef terrace.** A broad terrace at 8 to 13 m runs west from there, reaching 12 m at the dive area 109 m west of the entry, 281°, and holding at 10 to 11 m to its outer lip about 235 m from the beach. The terrace holds the site's coral gardens.
* **Drop-off.** Past the lip at about 11 m the slope falls away steeply, into water past 100 m deep a short way beyond.
* **Route.** Visit the drop-off first, then work back up onto the terrace for the rest of the dive. Start into whatever current runs on the day, so the return to the beach runs with it, and turn back while the beach is still close enough to find.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift; early morning usually brings the calmest water.
* **Character.** The current runs from slight to strong along this stretch of the park's coast.
* **Exits.** The shore either side of the beach is rock and cliff; a diver carried past the beach faces a long swim back.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 267° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 260° |

The beach opens west onto open sea, and the swim out runs 109 m west, 281°, from the entry across the shallows to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea, straight onto the beach, building surf on the rocks and pebbles of the entry.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest through north (NNW-N), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south: offshore from north-northeast through southeast over 90 to 150 m, off the land, and cross-shore over 200 m from the south-southeast and 420 m from the south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the beach flat, though strong trades drive the current on the reef.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 10 to 30 m, typically around 25 m.
* **Swell and rain.** Swell from the north or west stirs the shallows, and heavy rain washes sediment off the land, lowering it for a time.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A 3 mm shorty or full 3 mm wetsuit is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef terrace.** Staghorn and elkhorn coral among brain and finger coral, the staghorn and elkhorn much reduced and damaged since the bleaching of 2023 and 2024, with groupers, triggerfish, queen angelfish, grunts and tarpon over the reef, and scorpionfish and lettuce sea slugs for a closer look.
* **Drop-off and open water.** Barracuda and eagle rays along the edge. Deep water pelagics turn up off the reef on occasion: whale sharks, manta rays, and humpback whales passing along the coast.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Washington Slagbaai National Park is closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD measures, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Entry.** A steep path and a climb down the cliff over rocks to a narrow beach, rough in any swell.
* **Current.** From slight to strong, and variable. Overshooting the beach leaves only rock and cliff to exit on. Carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Depth.** The terrace lip at about 11 m falls steeply into water past 100 m deep.
* **Remote.** The park lies outside mobile phone coverage and a long dirt road from help; leave a dive plan and an expected return time with someone outside the park, and finish diving with time to clear the park gate by 17:00.
