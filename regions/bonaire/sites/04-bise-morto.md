# 4. Bise Morto

A remote site in Washington Slagbaai National Park whose name means dead calf, a short walk south of Playa Funchi along the same exposed stretch of coast. Elkhorn and staghorn coral crowd the shallows, and the open water beyond the reef edge is one of the park's better spots for a sighting of a deep water pelagic. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering this stretch of the park's coast; STINAPA has found no SCTLD in this closed stretch, though the island-wide bleaching events of 2023 and 2024 affected shallow coral here regardless of disease status.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Shore entry off the park road over rocky footing.
* **Parking.** Beside the park road at the site; unpaved.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's unpaved roads; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Shallows.** Elkhorn and staghorn coral dominate close to shore.
* **Reef edge.** The bottom drops away quickly beyond the shallows in a fast wall.
* **Depth profile.** The shallows with the elkhorn and staghorn coral sit around 6 m, dropping quickly beyond the reef edge to well past 100 m within a short distance.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run hard when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Character.** Current at Bise Morto can run moderate to strong, enough that the site suits an intermediate to advanced diver.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 268° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 269° |

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at this exposed stretch of the park.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 120 to 360 m from the north, south-southeast and south. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Elkhorn and staghorn coral, though the 2023 and 2024 bleaching events left their mark here as everywhere on the island.
* **Open water.** One of the park's better spots for deep water pelagics, with whale sharks, manta rays and spouting humpback whales seen on occasion off the reef edge.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since 1 May 2023, until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Moderate to strong current.** Suits an intermediate to advanced diver.
* **Remote entry.** No facilities and a rocky shore.
