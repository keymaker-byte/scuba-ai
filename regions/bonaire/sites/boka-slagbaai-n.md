# 6. Boka Slagbaai N

The northern of two routes at Boka Slagbaai, a sheltered bay in Washington Slagbaai National Park fronted by the park's historic colonial buildings. Slagbaai takes its name from the Dutch slachtbaai, slaughter bay, once one of the island's two main ports, where goats were slaughtered and salted before shipping to Curaçao. The shallows carry large, unusual coral formations over a sandy bottom that favours rays and garden eels, and the north route also holds a set of cannons left over from a 1980s film shoot rather than the real colonial ones found on the south route. Diving here is currently suspended under STINAPA's coral disease closure covering the whole park.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A sandy beach beside the park's historic yellow building, one of the easier entries in the park, with parking only metres from the shoreline.
* **Parking.** At the beach, metres from the water.
* **Access.** Inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, open Tuesday to Sunday 08:00 to 17:00, last entry 14:30; the Slagbaai area must be left by 16:00. A high clearance vehicle (about 30 cm) is recommended for the park's unpaved roads, though this stretch is more accessible than the park's remoter sites; the park can close after heavy rain. The Bonaire Nature Fee covers entry to both the marine park and Washington Slagbaai on one tag. All diving inside the park, including this site, has been suspended since 1 May 2023 under STINAPA's Stony Coral Tissue Loss Disease closure of every site from Karpata north to Malmok; STINAPA has continued to find no SCTLD in the closed northern stretch and the closure remains in force, so confirm current status with STINAPA before planning a dive here.
* **Facilities.** The park's historic buildings sit on the beach here, though they hold no dive facilities.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Yellow building.** Marks the entry point on the beach.
* **Film cannons.** The north route's cannons are props left from a 1980s film shoot, not the real 18th century pieces found on the south route at Boka Slagbaai S.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. The bay is sheltered relative to the park's more exposed points, though current still builds with wind the same way it does along the rest of the coast.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef terrace sits around 5 m, with diving on the north route working out to the mid 20s.

## Hazards

* **Diving suspended.** Closed to diving under STINAPA's SCTLD order since May 2023; treat as not diveable until STINAPA lifts the closure.
* **Historic structures underwater.** The film-prop cannons and any other debris on this route are not the load-bearing colonial ironwork found on the south route; treat both as fixed and don't handle them.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces northwest, in a bay that is more sheltered than the open points either side of it.
* **Bad.** Any wind with north or west in it still raises swell here, just less readily than at the exposed sites further up the coast.
* **Fine.** The prevailing east to southeast trade winds blow offshore to cross-shore.
* **Wind against current.** A building northerly against the bay's current is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast, occasionally reduced by swell stirring the shallows when wind runs out of the north or west.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Large, unusual coral formations over a sandy bottom, with southern stingrays and garden eels.
* **Reef.** Larger fish species than the busier sites further south on the island.
