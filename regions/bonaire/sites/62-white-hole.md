# 62. White Hole

A sand-floored hole in the shallow reef plateau off Bonaire's east coast, just southeast of the mouth of Lac Bay, about 140 m long and dropping from a 4 m plateau to 14 m. Its white sand floor shows from the surface and from the air, giving the site its name. Schools of tarpon rest in the hole by day, with turtles, stingrays and spotted eagle rays around it. The site lies on the windward coast, dived by boat with Bonaire East Coast Diving as a guided drift, and wind, waves and surge across the shallow plateau decide on the day whether it can be dived.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A backward roll from the boat, with re-entry by ladder. Boats leave from the fishermen's pier at Sorobon and cross Lac Bay out to the site in a few minutes.
* **Parking.** At the fishermen's pier at Sorobon, at the end of the road past the Jibe City windsurf shops.
* **Access.** Dived with [Bonaire East Coast Diving](https://www.bonaireeastcoastdiving.com/), the boat operator that specializes in the east coast, on a guided trip from the fishermen's pier at Sorobon. The crew sets the day's sites from the weather and the group, so White Hole is dived when conditions allow. Divers bring all their own gear, weights included, with tanks supplied on the boat, and are expected to hold steady buoyancy and carry enough gas for an hour at an average depth of 14 m. The approach from shore at Sorobon is a long wade and surface swim across shallow reef in breaking surf, unsafe with dive gear, so the site is reached by boat. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The plateau.** A broad, flat reef plateau at about 4 m surrounds the hole, spreading for hundreds of metres to the north, south and west and 120 to 200 m east to the terrace edge.
* **The hole.** About 140 m long, running roughly north to south, and 30 to 40 m across, falling from the plateau to a white sand floor at 11 to 14 m, deepest near its middle. One side drops as a near-vertical wall and the other as a steep reef slope.
* **The terrace edge.** About 120 to 200 m east of the hole the plateau tips over onto the outer reef slope, which drops away beyond the depths dived into the open ocean.
* **The sea fan plateau.** South of the hole the plateau, at the same depth of about 4 m, is covered in sea fans and soft corals, the usual route out of the hole on the drift.
* **Dive pattern.** Descend beside the hole and work through it from north to south with the tarpon, then leave over the sea fan plateau and drift south with the guide, surfacing to a boat pickup.

* **Drivers.** The trade winds, blowing from the east-northeast through east-southeast. The current can sit still for days, then run as a steady drift when the trades pick up or shift.
* **Drift.** The site is dived as a guided drift with the boat following, and the current ranges from nearly still water in the hole to a drift across the plateau. It can change in strength and direction during a dive.
* **Surge.** Waves breaking over the shallow plateau put surge on the plateau around the hole, strongest on the crossing between the hole and the sea fans.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | east-northeast, 65° |

The nearest shore lies 490 m to the southwest, 222°, and the plateau around the hole opens onto more than 30 km of ocean from east-northeast through south-southeast.

* **Bad.** East-northeast through east-southeast (ENE-ESE), onshore over more than 30 km of open ocean. The trades blow from this quarter most of the year, building waves that break across the shallow plateau and make the boat entry and exit and the reef crossing rough.
* **Short fetch.** North through northeast (N-NE), onshore over 1.4 to 2.7 km, enough to build a chop over the plateau.
* **Mixed.** Southeast through south-southeast (SE-SSE), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the plateau past the hole.
* **Fine.** South clockwise through north-northwest: offshore from south through west-northwest, off the land over 610 m to 3 km, and cross-shore over 1.9 to 2 km from the northwest and north-northwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 10 to 20 m, reaching about 30 m on calm days. Waves and surge stirring the shallow plateau bring it down, and calm early mornings run clearest.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water stays evenly mixed through these shallow depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The hole.** Schools of tarpon resting in the hole by day, often dozens and sometimes around a hundred, though numbers vary widely and some days few are present. Barracuda, green morays and lobsters live around its walls, schools of grunts and snapper gather along the wall edge, and stingrays rest on the sand floor.
* **The plateau and sea fans.** Green and hawksbill turtles throughout, resting, feeding and visiting cleaning stations among the sea fans, with spotted eagle rays and stingrays over the sand.
* **Occasional.** Nurse sharks, with larger pelagic fish passing along the terrace edge. Seahorses among the sea fans and around the hole.

## Hazards

* **East coast exposure.** The site faces the open ocean on the windward coast. Waves, swell and surge over the shallow plateau make the boat entry and exit and the reef crossing demanding, and conditions can build quickly through the day as the trades strengthen.
* **Drift.** The current can change in strength and direction during the dive. Stay with the guide, carry a surface marker buoy and whistle, and surface to the boat as briefed.
* **Navigation.** The flat, uniform plateau gives few landmarks, and a diver separated from the group can be carried away from the hole and the boat.
* **Remote site.** Help is a boat ride and a drive from Kralendijk, so rescue and evacuation take longer than on the west coast.
