# 31. Front Porch

A stretch of fringing reef on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, entered from the beach in front of Eden Beach Resort, where a steel pipe standing above the water marks the way in. A short sand terrace gives onto a reef slope of sponges, hard coral and scattered metal left from the former Sunset Beach Hotel, and the slope runs down to sand where the upturned tug New York lies at about 28 m, its dark recesses sheltering big tooth cardinalfish. The headline hazards are the scrap metal across the bottom and the slope below the wreck, which runs on toward 40 m.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the beach in front of the steel pipe, over a short mix of sand and rock where water shoes help, into water that deepens gradually from about 3 m. The dive area lies 72 m west-southwest across the terrace. Exit at the same beach.
* **Parking.** In the small gravel lot across from La Balandra on Kaya Gobernador N. Debrot, free, with room for about 8 to 10 cars and about 60 m from the entry. It fills early in high season.
* **Access.** Open to shore diving across the resort beach. The reef continues north to the public Isidel Beach Park, whose ramp is a second way onto the water. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** Eden Beach Resort and its dock, and La Balandra beach bar and restaurant.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The steel pipe.** Stands above the water in front of the beach, fixed underwater to a large concrete block at about 6 m. It marks the entry and makes a reference point for the dive: the reef runs north and south from it along the slope.
* **The terrace.** Sand with scattered coral heads at 4 to 6 m for the first 30 m out from the beach, then 9 to 11 m across the outer terrace to the reef edge, 72 m out.
* **The coral nursery.** Coral restoration trees hung with staghorn and elkhorn fragments stand just north of the resort dock at 5 to 9 m.
* **The reef slope.** From the reef edge at about 9 m the slope runs down through sponges and hard coral, patchy and strewn with old pipes, cables, tyres and wreck parts, to sand at about 24 to 30 m, where sand flats run on down toward 40 m.
* **The tug New York.** A tug about 10 m long lies upside down on the sand at about 28 m, bow to the north, off the remains of the old Sunset Beach pier north of the entry. A rope runs above it. The hull has partly broken apart.
* **The garden eel fields.** Garden eels fill the sand at the base of the slope from about 24 m, with a second field behind the wreck.
* **The old pier.** Concrete remains of the Sunset Beach Hotel pier, destroyed by Hurricane Lenny in 1999, lie in shallow water at the north end of the site.
* **The return.** The steel pipe lies 72 m east-northeast of the dive area; come back up the slope to the concrete block and follow the pipe to the beach.

## Current

|---|---|
| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | Not established; it runs north on some days and south on others |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What sets its direction on a given day is not established.
* **Character.** Usually mild, and at times strong, more so in the deeper water. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and let it carry you back toward the pipe.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 299° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |

The swim out runs 72 m west-southwest from the beach, angled south along the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** West-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), onshore over 8 km to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises waves on the beach and stirs up the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** North-northwest (NNW), onshore over 3.8 km down the coast, enough to build a chop at the entry.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest (SSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km through the open water south of Klein Bonaire, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south (N-S), and southwest through west (SW-W): offshore from east-northeast through south, off the land, cross-shore over 90 to 840 m from the north-northeast, northeast and southwest, and onshore over 600 to 990 m from the north, west-southwest and west. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the beach calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m on a typical day, best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and rain.** Wind from west-northwest through northwest stirs up the fine sand of the terrace, and runoff after heavy rain clouds the shallows for a time.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **The wreck.** Big tooth cardinalfish school in the darkened recesses of the upturned hull. Yellow and spotted morays live in and around it, and scorpionfish lie among the metal.
* **Reef slope.** Scorpionfish, soapfish, trumpetfish, trunkfish, French and queen angelfish, midnight parrotfish, groupers and green morays among barrel and tube sponges, with horse-eye jacks and the odd barracuda off the slope. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Sand.** Garden eels at the base of the slope and yellowhead jawfish in the rubble.
* **Macro.** Frogfish and seahorses turn up on the slope and the concrete blocks, and octopus shelter among the debris.
* **Night.** Tarpon hunt in divers' lights, orange cup corals open on the overhangs, and spiny lobsters and octopus come out to forage.

## Hazards

* **Scrap metal.** Old pipes, cables, tyres and wreck parts lie across the bottom with sharp, rusted edges; keep hands, hoses and fins clear of them.
* **Depth.** The wreck lies at about 28 m and the slope below the dive area runs on toward 40 m, below the maximum operating depth of EAN32 and outside a no-decompression plan. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Current.** Usually mild, at times strong and more so in the deeper water, in either direction; keep enough gas to return to the pipe against it.
* **Boat traffic.** Boats use the resort dock beside the entry and the boat ramp at the north end of the reef. Stay down until back at the pipe, and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Restoration structures.** The nursery trees north of the dock are fragile; keep fins and gauges clear of them.
* **Waves at the entry.** Wind from west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW) puts waves on the beach, from the north-northwest (NNW) a chop, and from the south-southwest (SSW) a swell along the shore; choose another site when any of these is blowing.
