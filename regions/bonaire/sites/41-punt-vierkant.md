# 41. Punt Vierkant

Punt Vierkant, Dutch for square point, is a shore site on EEG Boulevard about 5 km south of Kralendijk, where Bonaire's double reef system begins. A short swim crosses a shallow sand and coral terrace to an inner reef that slopes to a sand channel at about 25 m, with an outer reef beyond it, and schooling fish over both. Villas and resorts line the shore either side of a small public pull-off. The headline hazard is current along the reef, which is usually mild and at times strong.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk 60 m southwest from the pull-off to the water and enter beside the large rock, over a flat bottom that reaches about 4 m within a few metres of the shore. Swim 112 m southwest, straight out from the shore, across the terrace to the dive area. Exit at the same rock. The shore is low and uneven, and the exit turns rough when any swell runs onto it.
* **Parking.** The small pull-off loop beside EEG Boulevard, 60 m northeast of the entry. Free, with room for a handful of cars.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. Villas, apartments and resorts line the shore on both sides, and building work along this stretch has narrowed the public way to the water at times; enter only from the pull-off. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit, tying up to moorings over the reef.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral heads shelving from about 4 m a few metres off the waterline to about 6 m at the dive area, 112 m out, uneven with scattered heads, brain coral and fire coral, and on to about 12 m at its edge. The same terrace runs on along the coast in both directions.
* **The inner reef.** The terrace edge lies about 60 m beyond the dive area. From there the inner reef slopes gradually down to about 25 m.
* **The sand channel.** At the foot of the inner reef, a sand channel separates it from the outer reef, the defining structure of the site. The outer reef rises beyond the channel and drops away to deeper water.
* **The south side.** Toward the south end of the site, the coral on the two reefs differs most clearly, with the outer reef carrying different species from the inner.
* **The return.** Come back up onto the terrace at about 6 m for the swim home; the entry lies 112 m northeast of the dive area.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild on the terrace and stronger along the reefs, at times strong. Check its direction before entering and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 228° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 228° |

The swim out runs 112 m southwest from the entry, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves and surge straight onto the shore and makes the entry and exit at the rock hard work.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast (NNW-SSE): offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 460 m from the north-northwest and over 970 m to 3.4 km from the southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 25 m, up to 30 m on calm days, and best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** Surge from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) stirs the shallow terrace and cuts visibility near the shore.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Rainbow and other parrotfish, at times in schools, with trunkfish, burrfish, filefish and pufferfish among the coral heads, spotted drum, and sea urchins on the sand and rubble. Turtles graze the shallows.
* **Reef.** Many kinds of schooling fish, schools of doctorfish, barracuda, groupers, trumpetfish and longnose butterflyfish. Sea fans carry flamingo tongues, and anemones shelter anemone shrimp.
* **Macro.** Green and spotted morays, octopus, spiny lobster, spider crabs, shrimp, nudibranchs, fireworms, tiny feather duster worms and the occasional frogfish. Caribbean reef squid hover over the reef.
* **Coral.** Brain and fire coral on the terrace, and hard and soft coral on the reefs. Reef Renewal Bonaire set up a staghorn coral tree nursery here in 2020 to restock the shallow reef. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish are seen on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Usually mild, at times strong along the reefs. Plan the dive into it, and turn early when it builds.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind or swell from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) runs straight onto the shore, batters the shallows and makes the exit at the rock difficult; choose another site when any of these blows fresh or a swell is running.
* **Depth.** The inner reef reaches the sand channel at about 25 m and the outer reef drops past 30 m. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Fire coral.** Grows on the terrace and the shallow reef; keep clear of it on the swim out and back.
* **The nursery.** Keep at least 2 m from any coral nursery tree.
* **Boats.** Dive boats tie up to the moorings over the reef. Ascend with a surface marker buoy away from the moorings.
