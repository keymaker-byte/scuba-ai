# H. Monte's Divi

Named for the lone divi divi tree standing guard on shore directly opposite the mooring, growing upright rather than bent by the trade winds the way most of the island's divi divi trees are shaped. The reef descends steeply from the shallows, covered in hard and soft coral with a variety of sponges; staghorn coral and finger sponges once grew thick in the shallows, though the staghorn has thinned since the 2023 SCTLD outbreak and the bleaching events that followed. This is one of Klein Bonaire's more reliable seahorse sites.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat only.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The divi divi tree.** Stands upright onshore directly opposite the mooring, an unusual sight since the trade winds normally bend these trees into a lean.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef descends steeply from the shallows through the recreational range to about 30 m.

## Hazards

None notable beyond the general reef and current awareness that applies along this coast.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces the south side of Klein Bonaire.
* **Bad.** A strong wind from the exposed quadrant raises chop.
* **Fine.** Calmer conditions, or wind blocked by the bulk of the islet.
* **Wind against current.** Worth checking before entry, as at any boat-only Klein Bonaire site.

## Visibility

Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Staghorn coral, thinned since 2023, and finger sponges, with a good chance of a seahorse.
* **Reef.** Stingrays and sea turtles.
