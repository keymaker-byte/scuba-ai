# I. Rock Pile

A boat dive on the southwest shore of Klein Bonaire, named for the pile of rocks on the beach directly opposite the mooring. A shallow terrace of sand and coral runs about 100 m out from the shore to the edge of a steep, sponge-covered reef slope that falls through the recreational range to about 30 m. The site holds one of the island's few large staghorn colonies, thinned since 2023, where schooling grunts and snappers shelter by day, and green morays shelter among the boulder corals. Current is usually mild to moderate; the headline hazards are how quickly the slope gains depth and a current that can build when the trades strengthen.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring opposite the rock pile, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring over the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The rock pile.** A heap of stones on the beach directly opposite the mooring, the landmark that gives the site its name.
* **The terrace.** Sand and coral at about 4 to 10 m, running about 100 m out from the shore, with the staghorn colony, finger coral and golf ball coral. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive, and as a snorkel site.
* **The slope.** Begins at the outer edge of the terrace at about 10 m and drops steeply, adorned with sponges, to about 30 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild to moderate, and at times strong enough to make headway along the reef hard work.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 214° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 8.8 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 290 to 780 m from the west-northwest, northwest and east-southeast, and 3.6 km from the southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, averaging about 30 m, at its best on calm mornings. A swell from the south through west stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Staghorn colony.** A large stand of staghorn coral on the terrace, thinned by the bleaching and disease losses since 2023, gives daytime cover to schooling grunts and snappers. Reef Renewal Foundation Bonaire has grown staghorn fragments here on PVC nursery trees for outplanting onto the reef.
* **Terrace.** Finger coral, golf ball coral, yellow pencil coral and sea plumes, with parrotfish, goatfish and snapper, banded coral shrimp and crabs; seahorses and frogfish are seen at times among the coral.
* **Boulder corals.** Green morays shelter among them, and spotted drum are seen around them.
* **Slope.** Sponges, leaf and sheet corals, with turtles along the reef. A light brings out the life in the crevices.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the edge of the terrace at about 10 m to about 30 m; watch the computer on the descent.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen and can make headway along the reef hard work; check the surface before entering and stay close to a buddy.
* **Swell and surge.** A wind or swell from the south through west runs straight onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow terrace.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
