# 25. Small Wall

A short, near-vertical wall on Bonaire's west coast north of Kralendijk, directly in front of the Black Durgon Inn. A shallow sand and coral terrace runs out about 70 m from the shore to a lip at 10 to 11 m, where the wall drops to a small cavern at its base around 18 m that at times shelters a sleeping nurse shark or a large green moray, and the reef slopes onto sand at about 33 m. It is generally dived by boat, has a calm surface in the prevailing trades, and is a popular night dive. The headline hazard is boat traffic over a busy mooring.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a boat tied to the park mooring, since anchoring is banned throughout the marine park, then a descent to the lip of the wall.
* **Parking.** None; the site is dived from a boat.
* **Access.** By boat from Kralendijk, a short run north along the coast. The shore in front of the site is private property, and shore divers need the owner's permission to cross it. The Black Durgon Inn's private pier is open to the inn's guests day and night; anyone else needs the inn's permission, and a fee may apply. From public shore the wall is reached only by a surface swim of 20 to 25 minutes, south from the coast at the north end of Hato or north from the Hamlet Oasis resort. Open to diving; confirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive. The Nature Fee e-tag is required.
* **Facilities.** None for the public. The Black Durgon Inn's pier, gear storage and rinse tank serve its own guests.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Terrace.** The bottom sits at 3.5 to 5.5 m across the terrace inshore, sand and rubble with scattered coral, with large lobsters on the sand between the pier and the wall at night.
* **Lip of the wall.** About 70 m off the shore the terrace edge falls from about 5.5 m to a lip at 10 to 11 m, where the dive area coordinate sits and the wall begins. An old anchor lies on the reef top.
* **The wall.** Below the lip the reef drops nearly vertically, its face covered in hard and soft corals and sponges.
* **The cavern.** A small cavern at the base of the wall around 18 m, reaching 3 to 4 m back into the reef.
* **Below the wall.** Past the cavern the reef slopes onto sand at about 33 m.
* **North of the wall.** The reef north of the mooring slopes gently, with less boat traffic than the wall itself.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades strengthen or shift. What reverses it is not established.
* **Character.** Usually limited along the wall.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 248° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through northwest (WSW-NW), onshore over 5 to more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter raises chop and swell at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over 1.6 to 1.8 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop on the surface.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 170 m to 2.2 km from the north-northwest, north, south-southeast and south. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the surface calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 20 to 30 m and often more, best on calm mornings.
* **Swell.** Onshore wind and swell stir the shallow terrace and cut visibility there; the water clears along the wall.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **The cavern.** A sleeping nurse shark or a large green moray at times, with black brotulas in its recesses. It is often empty.
* **The wall.** Hard and soft corals and sponges, with groupers, shrimp, morays and parrotfish. Seahorses and frogfish turn up near the top of the wall. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease.
* **Open water.** Barracuda, large tarpon and spotted eagle rays pass along the wall and over the sand.
* **Shallows.** Turtles feed on the terrace.
* **Night.** Lobsters, scorpionfish, juvenile spotted drum and free-swimming morays come out on the wall, and tarpon hunt in divers' lights over the sand near the pier.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** A popular boat site, busiest from about 08:00 to noon, and pleasure boats pass along the coast; carry a surface marker buoy and ascend along the wall or the mooring line.
* **Depth.** The wall gives way to a slope that continues to sand at about 33 m, easy to follow deeper than planned.
* **Current.** The wind driven current is usually limited and can run harder when the trades strengthen or shift; start the dive into it and keep enough gas to return to the mooring.
* **Swell.** Wind from the west-southwest through northwest puts chop and swell on the mooring, and from the south-southwest through southwest a chop; choose another site when it is running.
