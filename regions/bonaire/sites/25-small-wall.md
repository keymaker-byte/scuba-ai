# 25. Small Wall

A vertical wall in front of the Black Durgon Inn, beginning around 6 m and leading down to a small cave at about 18 m that sometimes shelters a sleeping nurse shark or a resident green moray, with black brotulas nearby. Shore access needs permission to cross the Black Durgon Inn's private property, so this is generally a boat dive; guests of the inn can dive from its pier, about 15 m from the wall. The site is regarded as having probably the calmest surface anywhere on Bonaire.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat for most divers; Black Durgon Inn guests can enter from the inn's pier, close to the wall.
* **Parking.** Not applicable for the boat entry; the inn's own lot for guests using the pier.
* **Access.** Shore access crosses private property and needs the inn's permission.
* **Facilities.** None at the site itself; the Black Durgon Inn sits directly onshore.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The wall.** Begins around 6 m.
* **The cave.** A small feature, about 3 to 4.5 m across, at the wall's base around 18 m.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven. The site is known for having about the calmest surface conditions anywhere on the island.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The wall runs from about 6 m to the cave at its base around 18 m.

## Hazards

* **Private property.** Shore access crosses the Black Durgon Inn's land and needs permission; don't assume open access.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 248° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through northwest (WSW-NW), onshore over 5 km to more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell, though the site is sheltered enough to stay calm on most days regardless.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), onshore over 1.6 to 1.8 km from Klein Bonaire, enough to build a chop on the surface.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south: offshore from north-northeast through southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 170 m to 2.2 km from the north-northwest, north, south-southeast and south.

## Visibility

West coast visibility here is typically excellent, in the same 15 to 30 m range as the rest of the leeward coast.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **The cave.** A sleeping nurse shark or resident green moray turns up here, with black brotulas nearby.
* **Sandy bottom.** Large tarpon, eels and eagle rays.
* **Reef.** Resident groupers and prawns.
