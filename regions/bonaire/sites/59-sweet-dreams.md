# 59. Sweet Dreams

Sweet Dreams is a shore site on EEG Boulevard on Bonaire's south coast, off a small beach where sea turtles come ashore to nest and where vehicles are kept off the sand. A shallow terrace of hard coral and gorgonians runs about 160 m out from the shore to a reef edge at about 10 m, and below it a slope dense with soft coral, sea rods and sea fans, with large sponges and coral heads deeper down, falls away well past recreational depth. The south coast faces open sea, and the headline hazards are surf on the rocky entry and a current that is still on most days and strong on others.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk 26 m southwest from the parking to the water. The shore is a low shelf of coral rock with a few gaps where the bottom slopes gently into the water. The entry point is the gap straight out from the parking; a second lies a short walk southeast along the beach, beside a large coral outcrop. The rock is slippery, and the bottom inside the shelf is uneven, with ridges running out from the shore; wade in carefully, time the entry between waves, and put fins on once clear of the rock. Swim 109 m southwest to the dive area on the outer terrace, and on about 50 m to the reef edge. Exit at the same gap.
* **Parking.** Free, on the firm coral gravel area beside EEG Boulevard, about 26 m from the water. Keep the car on the gravel and off the sand, where turtles nest.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Driving on the beach is prohibited. Turtles nest here from May to December; Sea Turtle Conservation Bonaire marks nests with posts and ribbons, and divers keep at least 3 m from a marked nest and show no lights on the beach after sunset.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The entry shelf.** A low ledge of coral rock lines the water's edge, broken by only a few gaps. The exits are limited, so note the chosen gap before swimming out.
* **The terrace.** About 4 m deep for the first 40 m from the entry, sloping to 5 to 6 m at 50 to 80 m out, 7.5 m at 90 m and 9 m at the dive area, 109 m out. Hard coral, gorgonians and soft coral cover the terrace, with sand patches between the coral blocks. A pair of barrels left from an old mooring lies in the shallows near the entry and serves as a reference back to it. No mooring buoy marks the site.
* **The reef edge.** About 160 m southwest of the entry, at about 10 m.
* **The reef slope.** Falls steeply from the reef edge through dense soft coral, sea rods and sea fans, with large sponges and coral heads over a fine white sand bottom deeper down, and keeps falling well past 40 m.
* **The return.** Come back up the slope onto the terrace and head northeast, 109 m from the dive area to the entry. Park close enough to the shore that the car serves as a landmark from the water.

## Current

| **Current type** | Wind driven |
| **Usual set** | Not established |
| **Typical speed** | Not established |

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs along the shore, northwest or southeast, and is still or slight on most days and strong on others.
* **Character.** Weakest on the terrace and strongest along the reef slope. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and drift back to the entry.
* **Drift.** On a day with a northwest set, the reef slope makes a drift dive northwest along the coast, exiting at the next entry up the shore. Plan the exit before descending and carry a surface marker buoy.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 219° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 220° |

The swim out runs 109 m southwest from the entry, 8° off the shore's facing, straight out.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the coral shelf, puts surf and surge on the entry and exit and stirs up the terrace.
* **Mixed.** Southeast (SE), and west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through east-southeast (NNW-ESE): offshore from north-northwest through east, off the land, and cross-shore over 740 m from the east-southeast. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength, though a strong trade wind still raises a cross chop and small rollers on the shore.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** 15 to 30 m, typically 20 to 25 m, best in the dry season from January to April and on calm mornings. Plankton in the rainy season can bring it down toward 15 m.
* **The shallows.** Waves from south-southeast through west (SSE-W) stir up the terrace near the entry and cut visibility there.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Gorgonians of many species, soft coral, and hard coral heads with sergeant majors, parrotfish, surgeonfish and schools of doctorfish. Green and hawksbill turtles graze between about 5 and 10 m. Southern stingrays rest on the sand patches, and flamingo tongue snails sit on the sea fans.
* **Reef slope.** A dense forest of soft coral, with wide fields of golden sea rods, sea fans and large sponges. Creole wrasse in large numbers, grunts, French angelfish, filefish, black durgon and trumpetfish along the slope, and green morays in the coral. Barracuda and tarpon pass in the blue.
* **Hard coral.** Hard corals along the whole leeward coast have suffered heavy losses since 2023 from bleaching and stony coral tissue loss disease.
* **Open water.** Dolphins pass offshore on occasion.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Surf and surge.** The south coast faces open sea. Wind from south-southeast through west (SSE-W), a strong trade wind, or a passing swell puts surf and surge on the coral shelf. Watch the waves from the beach before kitting up, and choose another site when they break on the rock.
* **The entry and exit.** Slippery coral rock, few gaps through the shelf, and an uneven bottom of ridges and holes inside it. Wear sturdy boots, take the entry slowly, and come back to the same gap.
* **Current.** Still or slight on most days and strong on others. Start into it so the return runs with it, turn with enough gas for the swim home, and end the dive early when it runs harder than planned.
* **Depth.** The reef slope keeps falling well past recreational depth. Watch the computer, stay above EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar, and keep within the no-decompression limit.
* **Kiteboarders and boats.** Kiteboarders and their support boats from the kite beach to the north range this far south and run into the shallows at speed, and fishing boats work the coast. Ascend with a surface marker buoy, and surface on the terrace before the final swim to check the water above.
* **Turtle nesting beach.** Stay off marked nests, keep vehicles on the gravel, and show no lights on the beach after dark in the nesting season.
