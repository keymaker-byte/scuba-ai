# 42. The Lake

The Lake is a shore site on the southern coast road about 5 km south of Kralendijk, just past the residential beach villas, near the northern start of Bonaire's double reef. It takes its name from the broad patch of sand lying between its two reefs at about 20 to 24 m, and was first called Lake Bowker, after the Bonaire dive pioneer Bruce Bowker. A long swim crosses a shallow sand and coral terrace to the inner reef, with the sand lake and the outer reef beyond it. The headline hazard is current along the reefs, usually light and at times strong enough to turn a dive back.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk about 20 m southwest from the road to the small sandy beach and step down over a short band of uneven rock and rubble onto sand, about 4 m deep within a few metres of the shore. Swim 98 m southwest, straight out from the shore, to the dive area on the terrace, then on across the terrace to the inner reef. The mooring buoy over the reef, south of the entry, gives a bearing before descending. Exit at the same beach.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site, beside the fence of the neighbouring villa. Free. The driveway down to the beach is rough; leave the car at the road.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit, tying up to the mooring over the reef.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand, rubble and scattered coral heads shelving from about 4 m at the shore to about 6 m at the dive area, 98 m out, and on to about 12 m. Staghorn coral and gorgonians grow across it, with fire coral in the shallows near the entry.
* **The inner reef.** Lies a long swim beyond the dive area and slopes down to the sand lake.
* **The sand lake.** A broad strip of sand at about 20 to 24 m between the two reefs, running along the shore, with garden eels and large coral heads near the foot of the inner reef. It runs parallel to the shore and makes an easy line to follow between the two reefs.
* **The outer reef.** Rises beyond the sand lake and drops away past 30 m, with more varied coral and sponge and more fish than the inner reef.
* **The coast south.** The double reef runs on along the shore toward the southeast.
* **The return.** Recross the sand lake, come back up onto the terrace and swim northeast to the entry. Note a landmark on the shore before entering.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually light or slack on the terrace, and stronger along the reefs past the inner reef, where it runs along the shore in either direction. At times it runs strong enough to turn a dive back. Check its direction before entering and start the dive into it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 230° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 230° |

The swim out runs 98 m southwest from the entry, straight out from the shore, to the dive area.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves and surge straight onto the beach and makes the step over the rocks at the entry and exit hard work.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast (NNW-SSE): offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 290 m from the north-northwest and over 630 m to 2.2 km from the southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land or along the shore over a short fetch, and leave the entry calm at their usual strength.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m, often better than 20 m, and best on calm mornings.
* **Wind and swell.** Surge from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) stirs the shallow terrace and can cut visibility near the shore to a few metres.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Terrace.** Southern stingrays and peacock flounder on the sand on the swim out, with conch, sea urchins and spotted drum. Turtles graze the shallows, and juvenile reef fish, trumpetfish and rainbow and blue parrotfish shelter among the staghorn and gorgonians.
* **Reefs.** Rock beauty, French, queen and gray angelfish, honeycomb cowfish, smooth trunkfish, porcupinefish, blue tang, snapper and grunts, with schools of reef fish circling over the reef and permit and barracuda off it. Spotted eagle rays pass over the outer reef, and giant vase sponges and orange sponges grow on it.
* **The sand lake.** A colony of garden eels at about 20 to 24 m, with large coral heads near the inner reef sheltering bigger snapper and grunts.
* **Macro.** Spotted and large green morays, sharptail eels, seahorses in the gorgonians, octopus, spiny lobster, arrow and spider crabs, anemones with Pederson cleaner shrimp, flamingo tongues on the sea fans and Christmas tree worms. Caribbean reef squid hover over the reef. Ostracods put on light displays shortly after dark on the nights following a full moon.
* **Coral.** Staghorn and gorgonians in the shallows, hard and soft coral on the reefs. Hard coral cover along the whole leeward coast has fallen sharply since 2023 from bleaching and disease, and the shallows here carry turf algae and cyanobacteria mats in patches.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish are seen on the deeper reef.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Usually light, at times strong along the reefs. Plan the dive into it, and turn early when it builds.
* **Long swim.** The inner reef lies a long swim from the beach and the outer reef beyond the sand lake. Plan gas for the return across the sand lake and the terrace, and keep the turn conservative on a dive to the outer reef.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind or swell from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) runs straight onto the beach and makes the step over the rocks difficult; choose another site when any of these blows fresh or a swell is running.
* **Depth.** The sand lake sits at about 20 to 24 m and the outer reef drops past 30 m. Turn at a planned depth.
* **Urchins and fire coral.** Sea urchins cover the rocks and rubble at the entry, and fire coral grows near the entry and in the shallows; wear boots and keep clear of both on the way in and out.
* **Finding the exit.** The shoreline looks much alike for some distance; mark the beach before entering, and surface on the terrace to check it before the final swim in.
* **Boats.** Dive boats tie up to the mooring over the reef. Ascend with a surface marker buoy away from the mooring.
