# F. Keepsake

A boat dive on Klein Bonaire's south side, toward its east end, where a sand and coral terrace shelves from about 4 m near shore to a lip at 8 to 9 m and the reef then drops steeply past 40 m. Sponges, angelfish, grunts and jacks work the reef, and seahorses and goldenspotted eels turn up among the coral. The headline hazard is a 2017 reef collapse just southwest of the mooring, an unstable scar of rubble running from about 12 m to past 40 m, which STINAPA asks divers to stay out of.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat from a STINAPA mooring off the reef, a giant stride or back roll depending on the boat, then a descent onto the terrace and out to the lip.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. Best dived in the morning, when the trade wind is at its lightest. STINAPA asks divers and snorkelers to keep clear of the 2017 collapse area; confirm the site's status with STINAPA or the operator before a trip.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral from about 4 m near shore, shelving gently to 8 to 9 m about 85 m out from the islet.
* **The lip.** At 8 to 9 m, running east-northeast to west-southwest along the shore, level for at least 180 m.
* **The slope.** Drops steeply from the lip, well past 40 m. Following the reef along its face and working deeper gradually keeps the profile under control; the same face leads back toward the mooring at the end of the dive.
* **The collapse.** About 20 m southwest of the mooring, a landslide scar at least 30 m long runs from about 12 m to past 40 m. It is about 16 m wide at its top and about 28 m wide at 23 m, split near its head by a coral ledge about 4 m wide, and holds toppled coral and rubble with very little live coral.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southeast, 152° |

* **Bad.** South through south-southwest (S-SSW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water, raising swell at the mooring.
* **Short fetch.** East through south-southeast (E-SSE), onshore over 2.4 to 2.8 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring. The prevailing trades blow from this quarter.
* **Mixed.** Southwest through west-southwest (SW-WSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east-northeast: offshore from west through north-northeast, off the islet over 80 to 170 m, and cross-shore over 2.3 km from the northeast and east-northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A strong trade or a swell from the south stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Hard corals and sponges, with crabs and shrimps among them.
* **Fish.** French angelfish, porcupinefish, sergeant majors, parrotfish, snappers, butterflyfish, grunts and jacks.
* **Small life.** Seahorses and goldenspotted eels.

## Hazards

* **Reef collapse.** The 2017 landslide scar southwest of the mooring is unstable, and further collapse is possible; keep clear of it and of the slope directly above its head at about 12 m.
* **The drop.** The reef falls steeply from 8 to 9 m and keeps going well past recreational depth; watch the computer on the descent and move along the face to gain depth gradually.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
* **Surface chop.** The east to southeast trade blows onshore across the channel and builds a chop at the mooring as it strengthens through the day.
* **Boat traffic.** The channel between Kralendijk and Klein Bonaire carries dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
