# S. Valerie's Hill

A boat dive on the west side of Klein Bonaire, south of the islet's middle, facing west-northwest onto the open Caribbean. A broad sand and coral terrace runs about 150 m out from the shore, stepping down from about 4 m to 11 m, before the reef falls steeply away past recreational depth. Numerous sponges and plentiful black coral cover the slope, with scrawled filefish and parrotfish often seen. Named after Captain Don Stewart's wife, the site suits divers of any level, and its headline hazards are the depth the slope gains below the terrace edge and a current, usually mild, that builds when the trades strengthen or shift.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace and a swim out to its edge.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by boat from Kralendijk, and dived from a dive operator's boat on the mooring. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Inner terrace.** Sand and coral at about 4 to 7 m, running about 75 m out from the shore. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **Outer terrace.** A step at about 75 m out drops the terrace to about 9 m, where it levels off before sloping to about 11 m at its edge, about 150 m off the shore.
* **The slope.** Falls steeply from the edge of the terrace, covered in sponges and black coral, and runs on well past 30 m.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild along the slope, building when the trades strengthen or shift.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 301° |

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north (WSW-N), onshore over 6 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), cross-shore over more than 30 km of open water, and north-northeast (NNE), cross-shore over 5.2 km, each running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through south: offshore from east-northeast through south, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 420 m from the northeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 15 to 30 m or better, at its best on calm mornings. A wind from the west-southwest through north, or a swell from the south-southwest or southwest, stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The slope.** Numerous sponges and plentiful black coral.
* **Fish.** Scrawled filefish and parrotfish are often seen.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the edge of the terrace at about 11 m and runs on past recreational depth; watch the computer and the no-decompression limit on the way down.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen or shift and can make the return to the mooring hard work; check the surface before entering.
* **Wind and swell.** A wind from the west-southwest through north runs onto this shore and puts chop at the mooring and surge over the terrace; a wind from the south-southwest or southwest, or from the north-northeast, runs a swell along the reef.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
