# G. Bonaventure

A boat dive on the south shore of Klein Bonaire, facing the open Caribbean Sea. A sand and coral terrace at 5 to 9 m runs about 120 m out from the shore to a reef lip, and beyond it the reef drops steeply before breaking into a series of terraces, each with its own growth. Pagodas of star coral share the shallows with a large number of gorgonians that usually hold a seahorse or two, and angelfish and black durgon accompany divers up and down the reef. The headline hazards are how quickly the slope gains depth past the lip and the chop an east-southeast trade raises across the channel.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent to the reef.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring buoy marking the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral at about 5 m near the shore, deepening to about 9 m at the lip about 120 m out. It holds the least life on the site and serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **The lip.** Around 9 m, where the terrace ends and the reef begins to drop.
* **The slope.** Steep at first, then stepping down through several terraces, each with different growth, past 40 m within about 100 m of the lip. Life grows richer with depth.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually mild, and at times strong. Strong surface winds put a current on the surface.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 176° |

* **Bad.** South through southwest (S-SW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water, raising swell at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** East-southeast through south-southeast (ESE-SSE), onshore over 2.7 to 3.2 km across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring. An east-southeast trade blows into this sector.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east: offshore from west-northwest through northeast, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 3 to 3.1 km from the east-northeast and east.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, around 30 m on a typical day.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Shallows.** Pagodas of star coral among a large number of gorgonians, which usually hold a seahorse or two.
* **Slope.** Hard and soft corals, sponges and gorgonians on the terraces.
* **Fish.** Angelfish, French angelfish among them, and black durgon accompany divers up and down the reef.
* **Dolphins.** Occasionally pass the site.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** Past the lip the slope falls steeply to beyond 40 m; watch the computer on the descent and use the terraces to gain depth gradually.
* **Surface chop.** An east-southeast trade blows onshore across the channel and builds a chop at the mooring.
* **Swell and surge.** A swell from the south runs straight onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow terrace.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering.
* **Boat traffic.** The channel between Bonaire and Klein Bonaire carries dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
