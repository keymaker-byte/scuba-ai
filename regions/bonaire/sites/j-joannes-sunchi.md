# J. Joanne's Sunchi

A boat dive on the south-southwest shore of Klein Bonaire whose name means Joanne's kiss in Papiamentu. A shallow sand plateau at about 4 m steps down at about 5 m onto a second terrace of sand and coral at 9 to 11 m, where piles of coral rubble under the boat hold nesting sand tilefish, before the reef slopes steeply away through fields of plate, cobble and leaf coral cut by sand chutes. Current is usually light; the headline hazards are how quickly the slope gains depth past the terrace and a current that can build when the trades strengthen or shift.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the second terrace at 9 to 11 m.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring over the second terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shallow plateau.** Sand at about 4 m running about 50 m out from the beach, dotted with small coral heads and sponges. It serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive, and as a snorkel site.
* **The step.** At about 5 m the plateau's edge drops to the second terrace.
* **The second terrace.** Sand and coral at 9 to 11 m, with the piles of coral rubble directly under the boat where the sand tilefish nest.
* **The slope.** Begins at the outer edge of the second terrace and falls steeply to about 30 m, cut by sand chutes running down the reef between coral ridges.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually light, and at times moderate.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 194° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west-southwest (SSE-WSW), onshore over 9.7 to more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the shallow plateau.
* **Short fetch.** Southeast (SE), onshore over 4 km from the south end of Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east-southeast: offshore from northwest through east-northeast, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 290 m to 1.3 km from the west, west-northwest, east and east-southeast.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, averaging about 30 m, at its best on calm mornings. A swell from the south through west stirs the sand plateau and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Second terrace.** Sand tilefish nest in the piles of coral rubble under the boat.
* **Slope.** Fields of plate coral in the shallower reaches, with cobble and leaf coral deeper, and large tube sponges along the sand chutes.
* **Reef visitors.** Turtles forage along the reef, and seahorses are seen at times.

## Hazards

* **The drop.** The slope falls steeply from the edge of the second terrace to about 30 m; watch the computer on the descent.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen or shift; check the surface before entering and stay close to a buddy.
* **Swell and surge.** A wind from south-southeast through west-southwest, or a swell from the south through west, runs onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow plateau.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
