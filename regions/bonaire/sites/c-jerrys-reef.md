# C. Jerry's Reef

A sponge garden on Klein Bonaire's north side, also known as Jerry's Sponges and named for the underwater photographer Jerry Schnabel, who photographed its sponges. A shallow terrace at 4 to 5 m runs out about 160 m from the shore to a reef crest at about 10 m, where a steep slope of elephant ear and tube sponges, hard and soft coral and fern-like black coral drops past 30 m. Turtles, stingrays and spotted eagle rays are all regular. The steep slope below the crest draws a diver deeper than planned.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the dive boat at the site's mooring, then a swim to the reef crest.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached by a dive operator's boat, about 15 minutes from Kralendijk. STINAPA closes the site temporarily for some sailing events, among them Bonaire Regatta week in October; check its notices before planning. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The mooring.** In deep water beyond the crest; the swim to the reef and back to the boat crosses open water.
* **The reef crest.** At about 10 m, about 160 m off the island's northeast-facing shore, where the terrace ends and the slope begins.
* **The slope.** Steep, running down from the crest past 30 m, with elephant ear and tube sponges set among fields of hard and soft coral on a sand and coral bottom. Fern-like black coral grows deeper on the slope.
* **The terrace.** Shoreward of the crest a flat terrace lies at 4 to 5 m, with ledges where turtles rest. It makes a good place to finish the dive and hold the safety stop.
* **Dive pattern.** Swim from the mooring to the crest and descend there, working down the slope gradually, then return up the slope to the crest and the terrace before swimming back out to the boat.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | northeast, 48° |

The nearest shore lies 164 m to the southwest, 221°, and the reef faces Bonaire across a channel 850 m to 1 km wide.

* **Short fetch.** North through northeast (N-NE), onshore over 1 to 2.1 km, and east-southeast (ESE), onshore over 1.7 km, across the channel from Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring and over the swim to the reef.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over 8.4 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** East-northeast through east, southeast clockwise through west-northwest, and north-northwest: onshore over 850 to 910 m from the east-northeast and east, too short a reach for chop to build; offshore from south through west-northwest, off the islet; and cross-shore over 2.8 to 4.6 km from the southeast, south-southeast and north-northwest.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 30 m, at times up to 40 m. Calm mornings run clearest; wind chop over the channel and the stronger winter trades and swell stir the water and bring it down.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water stays evenly warm through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Sponges and coral.** Elephant ear and tube sponges, with crinoids on the reef, fields of hard and soft coral, and fern-like black coral deeper on the slope.
* **Turtles.** Green and hawksbill turtles, accustomed to divers and often swimming close; they rest under the ledges on the shallow terrace and visit the reef's cleaning stations.
* **Rays.** Southern stingrays on the sand and spotted eagle rays passing along the reef.
* **Reef fish.** Barracuda, drums, sand divers and spotted morays.

## Hazards

* **Steep slope.** Below the crest the reef drops steeply, and a diver following it down runs deep quickly. Descend at the crest and work down gradually, watching depth and no-decompression time.
* **Open water swim.** The mooring sits in deep water off the crest; the swim to the reef and back crosses open water, and a diver who surfaces away from the boat should carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Channel exposure.** The reef faces the channel, and wind from the north through northeast, or from the east-southeast, builds a chop at the mooring and over the swim.
