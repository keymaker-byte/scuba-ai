# C. Jerry's Reef

Also known as Jerry's Sponges, an extensive sponge garden about 170 m from Ebo's Reef on Klein Bonaire's north side, with the same deep water mooring requiring a swim to reach the reef. Tube and vase sponges of various sizes dominate, alongside crinoids, elephant ear sponges and fern-like black coral, and the site is a reliable spot for turtles, stingrays and spotted eagle rays.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** By boat only; the mooring sits in deep water, with a swim to reach the shallower reef.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat; Klein Bonaire itself has no facilities.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The deep mooring.** In deep water, as at neighbouring Ebo's Reef.
* **Sponge garden.** Tube and vase sponges of varied size, the site's central feature.

## Current

No tidal current here; Bonaire's currents are wind driven.

## Depth

Depth is a plain observed figure, Bonaire has no tidal range worth normalizing against. The reef runs through the recreational range to about 30 m, with the mooring itself in much deeper water.

## Hazards

* **Open water swim.** The mooring sits well offshore in deep water; plan the swim to and from the reef accordingly.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** Faces the north side of Klein Bonaire.
* **Bad.** A strong wind from the exposed quadrant raises chop over the open swim to the mooring.
* **Fine.** Calmer conditions ease the surface swim considerably.
* **Wind against current.** Worth checking before committing to the open water crossing to the mooring.

## Visibility

Klein Bonaire's visibility runs excellent, commonly reaching up to 40 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
* **Surface layer.** No sharp difference from depth here.

## Marine life

* **Reef.** Turtles, stingrays and spotted eagle rays are all reliably seen, alongside crinoids, elephant ear sponges and fern-like black coral.
