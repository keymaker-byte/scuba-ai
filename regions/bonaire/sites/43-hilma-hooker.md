# 43. Hilma Hooker

Bonaire's most famous wreck and one of the most iconic wreck dives in the Caribbean, on the southern double reef stretch. The Hilma Hooker was launched in the Netherlands in 1951 and traded under six names and seven owners before her final owner's crew was caught with over 11,000 kg of marijuana hidden behind false bulkheads while tied up at the Bonaire pier in June 1984. Impounded as evidence, she was moved to an anchorage next to Angel City that September and sank there on 12 September 1984 after struggling to stay afloat for five days. The site is now also a working example of how coral colonizes an artificial structure, over four decades on the seabed.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A shore entry finds the shallows full of fish before reaching the wreck; a boat entry drops divers straight onto the wreck at the stern mooring.
* **Parking.** Roadside at the site.
* **Access.** Open.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The stern mooring.** Marks the site and sits over the top of the wreck, at about 18 to 19 m.
* **The mast.** The wreck's deepest point, at about 30 m.
* **Angel City.** Sits immediately adjacent, where the ship was originally anchored before she sank.
* **Depth profile.** The top of the wreck sits around 18 m and the mast at about 30 m.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 234° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 234° |

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water. Any wind from this quarter raises swell at the entry.
* **Mixed.** Northwest (NW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south-southeast: offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 520 m to 1.9 km from the north-northwest, southeast and south-southeast. The prevailing east to southeast trades blow off the land here and leave the entry flat.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically excellent, 15 to 30 m.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **The wreck.** Over four decades of coral growth now covers the hull, a living example of colonization on an artificial site.
* **Shallows.** Full of fish on the shore approach to the wreck.

## Hazards

* **Depth and limited bottom time.** The wreck lies at 18 to 30 m, which leaves a short working time on a recreational profile; plan gas and NDL margins accordingly.
* **Wreck penetration.** Structural entry into a wreck this age carries its own risks; stay outside unless trained and equipped for penetration.
