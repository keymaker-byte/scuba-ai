# M. Hands Off

A boat dive on the south shore of Klein Bonaire toward the islet's west end, set aside early in the marine park's history as a reference reef for measuring how much damage divers do, and closed at the time to photographers and careless divers. A shallow terrace runs out from the shore across a seabed of small hills and valleys, the tongue and groove formation that defines the site, to a reef slope that falls gradually through the recreational range to about 37 m. The slope holds numerous cleaning stations. Current is usually limited to moderate; the headline hazards are the fragile coral the site was named to protect and a slope that keeps going past recreational depth.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A giant stride or back roll from a dive operator's boat on the mooring, then a descent onto the terrace.
* **Parking.** Not applicable.
* **Access.** Reached only by a dive operator's boat, about 15 to 20 minutes from Kralendijk. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00. A valid STINAPA nature fee is required.
* **Facilities.** A STINAPA mooring over the terrace.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and coral from about 4 m near the shore, deepening to about 10 m at its outer edge. Its shallow inshore part serves as the safety stop at the end of the dive.
* **Tongue and groove.** Small hills and valleys run across the terrace and the top of the slope, ridges of coral separated by sandy grooves a metre or two deeper.
* **The slope.** Begins at about 10 to 11 m and falls gradually, with the cleaning stations along it, to sand at about 37 m.

## Current

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current can sit still for days, then run when the trades strengthen or shift.
* **Character.** Usually limited to moderate. When it runs, it runs along the shore, at times west toward the islet's southwest point.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 175° |

* **Bad.** South-southeast through southwest (SSE-SW), onshore over more than 30 km of open water, raising waves at the mooring and surge over the terrace.
* **Short fetch.** East-southeast through southeast (ESE-SE), onshore over 4.8 to 4.9 km from the south end of Bonaire, enough to build a chop at the mooring.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest (WSW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, running a swell along the reef.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east: offshore from west-northwest through northeast, blowing off the islet itself, and cross-shore over 270 m from the west, 210 m from the east-northeast and 1.1 km from the east.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Commonly 20 to 40 m, averaging about 30 m, at its best on calm mornings. A swell from the south through west stirs the terrace and lowers it in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds one temperature from the surface to recreational depth. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Cleaning stations.** Numerous along the slope, where larger reef fish hold still to be cleaned.
* **Reef.** Dense hard and soft coral across the hills and valleys, with sponges on the slope.
* **Night.** A favoured night dive for its night life, with the coral polyps extended to feed across the hills and valleys.

## Hazards

* **Fragile coral.** The hills and valleys put coral close beneath a diver at every depth on the terrace; hold buoyancy well clear and keep fins up when crossing a ridge.
* **The drop.** The slope keeps going past 30 m to about 37 m; watch the computer on the descent.
* **Current.** Wind driven current builds when the trades strengthen; check the surface before entering and stay close to a buddy.
* **Swell and surge.** A wind from south-southeast through southwest, or a swell from the south through west, runs onto this shore and puts surge over the shallow terrace.
* **Boat traffic.** Dive boats, water taxis and pleasure craft work the water off Klein Bonaire; a surface marker buoy marks a diver surfacing away from the mooring.
