# 58. Vista Blue

Vista Blue is a shore site on EEG Boulevard, about 13 km south of Kralendijk on Bonaire's southwest coast and about 1 km south-southeast of a kite beach. A short swim from a small sandy beach crosses a shallow terrace to a reef edge at about 10 m, and the reef slope below is carpeted along its whole length in dense soft coral and gorgonians, home to nesting sergeant majors and visited by turtles, eagle rays and schools of bait fish. The headline hazard is the current, which can change direction or build suddenly during a dive, with kite traffic on the surface close behind.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk about 25 m from the roadside down to the small sandy beach between the coral rubble and ironshore. Wade in over the sand, watching for holes and gullies in the bottom until the water is about 2 m deep, and put fins on there. Swim 122 m southwest to the dive area on the outer terrace, and about 30 m further to the reef edge. Exit at the same beach.
* **Parking.** Free, beside EEG Boulevard at the yellow-painted stone numbered 58. The space is small and fills on weekends.
* **Access.** Open at all hours. The STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag is the only fee. Dive boats also visit, from the site's mooring.
* **Facilities.** None at the site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The terrace.** Sand and rubble sit at 4 to 5 m for the first 70 m from the beach, then slope gently down to about 9 m at 120 m out and 10 m at the reef edge. Soft coral and gorgonians grow among the sand and rubble from the shallows outward, thickening toward the reef edge, with sand patches and channels between them. The site's mooring lies on the terrace.
* **The dive area.** On the outer terrace 122 m southwest of the entry, at about 9 m.
* **The reef edge and slope.** The terrace ends at the reef edge about 150 m out, at about 10 m. The slope below drops steeply through soft coral, gorgonians and sponges, with sand at its foot at about 40 m.
* **To the south.** The shore from this beach south to the next site is unbroken ironshore. Reaching it means the dive has gone too far south; turn north.
* **The return.** Come back up onto the terrace and head northeast, 51°, to the entry.

* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. The current runs along the reef in either direction, from still water on some days to a strong drift on others, and runs hard when the trades pick up or shift. It often runs strongest in the afternoon.
* **Character.** The current can change direction or build suddenly during a dive. It runs strongest along the reef slope and weakest on the terrace. Check its direction before entering, start the dive into it, and drift back to the entry. On a day it runs too strong to swim against, drift north with it, exit at the next beach and walk back along the road.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 236° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 235° |

The swim out runs 122 m southwest from the entry, 5° off the shore's facing, straight out.

* **Bad.** South through west-northwest (S-WNW), onshore over more than 30 km of open sea. Wind from this quarter drives waves onto the beach, puts surge on the entry and exit and stirs sand into the shallows.
* **Mixed.** Southeast through south-southeast (SE-SSE), and northwest (NW), cross-shore over more than 30 km, each running a swell along the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through east-southeast (NNW-ESE): offshore from north through east-southeast, off the land, and cross-shore over 1.5 km from the north-northwest. The prevailing east trades blow off the land and leave the entry calm at their usual strength; when they back to the southeast they run cross-shore, as under Mixed. A wind strong enough for kitesurfing brings kite traffic from the kite beach to the north onto the water above the site.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 20 to 30 m on the reef, reaching 40 m on the best days, and much the same year round. Best on calm mornings.
* **The shallows.** Surf from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) stirs up the sand on the terrace and cuts visibility near the entry. Fins lift the fine sand at the beach and cut visibility to near zero in the last metre of the exit.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water is evenly mixed through recreational depths. A dive skin, shorty or 3 mm wetsuit suits.

## Marine life

* **Soft coral.** Dense soft coral, gorgonians and sea rods run along the entire reef, from the terrace down the slope, the site's defining feature. Bleaching has thinned the hard coral on the slope.
* **Sergeant majors.** Large numbers nest on the reef; their eggs show as purple patches on the coral, guarded fiercely by the males.
* **Reef fish.** French and queen angelfish, blue tang, parrotfish, black durgon, trunkfish, pufferfish, trumpetfish, slender filefish, black margate and palometa, with many juvenile fish among the soft coral.
* **Bait balls.** Schools of mackerel scad gather in dense bait balls around the mooring and along the terrace in the shallows, with barracuda hunting them.
* **Morays and invertebrates.** Green and spotted morays in the reef, with reef squid over the terrace and decorator and arrow crabs.
* **Rays and turtles.** Southern stingrays on the sand patches and at the foot of the slope, and spotted eagle rays passing along the reef. Hawksbill and green turtles forage along the reef.
* **Open water.** Dolphins pass at the surface on occasion.
* **Lionfish.** Invasive lionfish turn up on the reef.

## Hazards

* **Changeable current.** Can change direction or build suddenly during a dive, and run strong along the slope. Start into it so the return runs with it, and carry a surface marker buoy for a drift.
* **Kitesurfers.** Kites from the kite beach about 1 km to the north range over the site on a windy day, and riders may not see a diver's head. Look before entering, ascend with a surface marker buoy deployed, and dive elsewhere when the water is busy with kites.
* **Fishing lines.** Local fishermen work the water off the site, most on weekends. Pause at about 5 m on the way up to look for lines, and ascend clear of them.
* **No exit to the south.** The shore south of the beach is unbroken ironshore. Keep the dive north of it, and turn north on reaching it.
* **The entry and exit.** Holes and gullies in the bottom to about 2 m, coral rubble beside the sand, and surge on a swell day. Wear sturdy boots, and come back out over the sand of the beach.
* **Surge and swell.** Wind from south through west-northwest (S-WNW) drives surf onto the beach and makes the entry and exit rough; choose another site when it blows fresh from that quarter or a swell is running.
* **Depth.** The reef slope runs past 30 m to sand at about 40 m, beyond EAN32's 33 m maximum operating depth at a ppO2 of 1.4 bar. Watch the computer and keep within the no-decompression limit.
