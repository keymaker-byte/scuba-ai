# 16. 1000 Steps

A shore dive beside the Radio Nederland transmission towers on Bonaire's northwest coast, reached down a limestone stairway of about 70 steps cut into the cliff to a small coral rubble beach. A shallow terrace of sand, staghorn and coral heads runs out about 130 m to an edge at 9 to 10 m, where the reef drops steeply in grooved buttresses to sand beyond recreational depth. Under the boat mooring, star coral has grown into pagoda-like towers whose hollow cores shelter reef creatures, and green turtles are common on the terrace. The headline hazard is the climb back up the stairs in full gear after the dive, the site's namesake.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down about 70 limestone steps from the road to a small beach of white coral rubble and pebbles. The shore is mostly rock, with gaps of sand and rubble cut through it to the water, which make an easy entry and exit; wear boots or water shoes on the beach.
* **Parking.** Free, along the road at the two yellow stones on the sea side, just before the towers when driving north, with room for about 15 cars; it fills early on busy days. Past the parking the road runs one-way north to Karpata, so a missed entrance means driving the whole one-way section and returning by way of Rincon.
* **Access.** Open, with the STINAPA Nature Fee required in the water. Carry everything needed down in one go where possible: each forgotten item is another round trip on the stairs.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The stairs.** Visible from the water as a white limestone line down the cliff, and the reference point for finding the exit.
* **Shallows.** About a dozen metres of sand at 0.5 to 1.5 m off the beach, then coral from 1.5 to 3 m, thickening across the terrace.
* **Terrace.** Coral heads, sand channels and staghorn patches at 5 to 10 m. Facing the sea, the larger staghorn patches lie to the left of the entry.
* **Swim out.** The dive area lies 120 m from the entry on a bearing of 229°, southwest, at 7.4 m on the outer terrace; the edge falls away 10 to 20 m beyond it on the same line.
* **Drop-off and mooring.** The terrace ends abruptly at 9 to 10 m in a steep slope cut by vertical grooves, with large star and brain coral, and plate-form star coral deeper down. The boat mooring sits right on the drop-off, over the star coral towers.

* **Drift.** Usually little or none, at most a light drift parallel to the shore along the drop-off.
* **Drivers.** The east to southeast trade winds. Strong winds put a surface current on the swim out and back, and the current can sit still for days, then run harder when the trades pick up or shift.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southwest, 209° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 192° |

The beach sits in a shallow bay where the coast runs west-northwest to east-southeast, with the coast running out to the northwest and southeast on either side.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 5.0 to 5.1 km from the south-southeast and south, across to Klein Bonaire, to more than 30 km of open sea from the south-southwest through west. Wind from this quarter puts chop and surf on the beach entry.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through southeast: offshore from north-northwest through east, off the cliff and the island, and cross-shore over 180 m to 4.3 km from the west-northwest, northwest, east-southeast and southeast, where the coast to either side cuts the fetch short.

## Visibility

* **Overall.** Typically 15 to 30 m year round, often better, and clearest on calm mornings before the trade winds build.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. The water holds an even temperature through recreational depths. A wetsuit or dive skin is standard.

## Marine life

* **Star coral towers.** Under the mooring, star coral pagodas with hollow cores, where slate-coloured male sergeant majors guard bluish patches of eggs.
* **Turtles.** Green turtles common on the terrace and near the drop-off, with hawksbills less often, mostly deeper on the slope.
* **Terrace.** Staghorn patches, brain coral, sea fans and soft corals, with blue tang schools, brown chromis, trumpetfish, parrotfish, French and queen angelfish, spotted trunkfish and grunts. Bonaire's leeward staghorn has taken heavy partial mortality from disease and bleaching since 2023, and fins easily break what remains.
* **Drop-off and slope.** Cleaning stations, green morays in the grooves and crevices, bar jacks, groupers, barracuda and tarpon along the wall, with spotted eagle rays passing in the blue.
* **At night.** Octopus, lobster and hunting morays and tarpon.
* **Open water.** Whale sharks pass on rare occasions.

## Hazards

* **The climb out.** About 70 steps in full gear after the dive, in tropical heat; pace the exit and rest as needed, and avoid pushing the dive to the edge of the no-decompression limit before the climb.
* **Slippery steps.** The limestone is slick when wet, and with morning dew; wear shoes with grip on the stairs.
* **Depth on the wall.** The slope runs well past recreational depth straight off the terrace edge; watch depth and no-decompression time on the wall.
* **Sun.** No shade at the beach or the parking; bring water.
* **Vehicle break-ins.** Cars parked here have been broken into; leave nothing of value in the car.
