# Bonaire Dive Planning

## The region

Bonaire is a special municipality of the Netherlands in the southern Caribbean, one of the ABC islands (Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao) about 80 km off the Venezuelan coast, outside the Atlantic hurricane belt. The island runs about 39 km north to south and 5 to 11 km wide, with Klein Bonaire, a small uninhabited islet, lying about 800 m off Kralendijk on the west side. A nearly continuous fringing reef wraps both Bonaire and Klein Bonaire, dropping from a shallow reef terrace at 5 to 12 m over a sloping wall or drop-off to sand at 30 to 40 m and beyond. Bonaire National Marine Park covers the entire coastline of both islands from the high water mark out to 60 m depth.

Dive sites fall into four areas, each with its own character:

| Area | Where |
|---|---|
| **North** | Along the leeward coast inside and above Washington Slagbaai National Park; remote, 4x4 access, exposed to open water and current |
| **Town** | The stretch either side of Kralendijk; sheltered, calm, the bulk of the easy shore diving and dive shops |
| **South** | From town down past the salt pans to the southern tip near Willemstoren lighthouse, including the Hilma Hooker wreck and Salt Pier |
| **Klein Bonaire** | The islet's reef, boat access only, generally regarded as the island's best diving |

### Boundaries

This folder covers Bonaire and Klein Bonaire only.

## Conditions

- **Water temperature.** 26 to 29 C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
- **Visibility.** Reliably 15 to 30 m or better along the sheltered west coast, sometimes exceeding 30 m; best on calm mornings. North and south exposure sites can run hazier when wind and swell stir the shallows.
- **Character.** Warm, calm, wall-and-slope diving off a near-continuous fringing reef, almost entirely shore accessible along the west coast at marked yellow-painted rocks, with Klein Bonaire and a handful of remote points reached by boat. Current here is wind driven, not tidal, so the same site can sit still for days and then run hard when the trade winds pick up or shift. A wetsuit or dive skin is the standard exposure protocol.

## Conventions

Depth is a plain observed figure. Bonaire's tidal range runs about 30 cm, and current here is wind driven rather than tidal, so there is no fixed datum to normalize against and no slack window to plan around the way a tidal region requires.

Every diver in the water needs a current STINAPA Nature Fee e-tag (Bonaire National Marine Park and Washington Slagbaai National Park), paid online and carried on the day, not bought as a physical tag at a dive shop. Marine park rules ban gloves and touching or collecting coral or marine life, and require boats to use a marked mooring buoy or anchor outside 60 m; a shore entry is made at one of the numbered, yellow-painted rocks marking each site.

## How planning works here

Ordered by what actually decides whether a Bonaire dive goes well:

1. **Coast and wind exposure** (primary). The west coast around town sits in the lee of the island itself and is calm on all but the roughest days; the north (inside and above Washington Slagbaai) and the far southern tip face open water and the prevailing trade winds directly, and can run real current and swell that call for real experience, not just a stronger kick. Pull the forecast from `open_meteo_wind.md` for both the entry and exit time before planning a dive, and confirm from the shore, current sock, or surface chop before entering.
2. **Marine park compliance** (secondary). No gloves, no touching, buoyancy clear of the reef, entry at a marked rock or a maintained mooring, current e-tag in hand. This is a legal requirement here, not just good practice, and citations are enforced.
3. **Gas and NDL discipline** (secondary). Warm water and an easy, comfortable reef slope make it easy to run long; plan a turn on gas or NDL reserve the same as anywhere else and don't let comfort extend the profile past it.
4. **Viz** (informational). Reliably good on the west coast; won't stop a dive, but check a recent report before a north or south site where wind can stir the shallows.

## Dive shops and air fills

Air and nitrox fills concentrate in and around Kralendijk, serving the whole west coast by truck or drive-up tank exchange; the north and Klein Bonaire have no fill stations of their own.

- **Buddy Dive Watersports, Kralendijk.** 6 Kaya Libertador Simon Bolivar, Kralendijk. +599 717 5085. buddydive.com. Air and nitrox, including a drive-through fill station at the main resort; satellite locations at Belmar Oceanfront Apartments and Your Scuba Store.
- **Wannadive Eden (north of town).** Kaya Gobernador N. Debrot 73. +599 701 0882. wannadive.com. Air and nitrox, daily 08:00 to 17:00.
- **Wannadive Windsock (south of town).** EEG Boulevard 3. +599 717 8884. wannadive.com. Air and nitrox, daily 08:00 to 17:00.
