# Bonaire Dive Planning

## The region

Bonaire is a special municipality of the Netherlands in the southern Caribbean, one of the ABC islands (Aruba, Bonaire, Curaçao) about 80 km off the Venezuelan coast, outside the Atlantic hurricane belt. The island runs about 39 km north to south and 5 to 11 km wide, with Klein Bonaire, a small uninhabited islet, lying about 800 m off Kralendijk on the west side. A nearly continuous fringing reef wraps both Bonaire and Klein Bonaire: a shallow, narrow terrace slopes gently from shore to about 10 m before dropping, typically around 45 degrees, to sand at about 40 m, with some sites varying into a vertical wall or a double reef formation. Bonaire National Marine Park covers the entire coastline of both islands from the high water mark out to 60 m depth, an area of about 27 km² (2,700 hectares), and holds 90 officially registered dive sites, over 57 species of soft and stony coral and more than 350 recorded fish species. Washington Slagbaai National Park, the island's terrestrial sanctuary in the northwest, covers a further 5,643 hectares. Lac Bay and Klein Bonaire are both RAMSAR listed wetlands.

Dive sites fall into four areas, each with its own character:

| Area | Where |
|---|---|
| **North** | Along the leeward coast inside and above Washington Slagbaai National Park; remote, 4x4 access, exposed to open water and current |
| **Town** | The stretch either side of Kralendijk; sheltered, calm, the bulk of the easy shore diving and dive shops |
| **South** | From town down past the salt pans to the southern tip near Willemstoren lighthouse, including the Hilma Hooker wreck and Salt Pier |
| **Klein Bonaire** | The islet's reef, boat access only, generally regarded as the island's best diving |

### Boundaries

This folder covers Bonaire and Klein Bonaire only.

## Conditions

- **Water temperature.** 26 to 29 °C year round, coolest around February and March, warmest around August through October. No thermocline worth planning around at recreational depths.
- **Visibility.** Reliably 15 to 30 m or better along the sheltered west coast, sometimes exceeding 30 m; best on calm mornings. North and south exposure sites can run hazier when wind and swell stir the shallows.
- **Character.** Warm, calm, wall-and-slope diving off a near-continuous fringing reef, almost entirely shore accessible along the west coast at marked yellow-painted rocks, with Klein Bonaire and a handful of remote points reached by boat. Current here is wind driven, not tidal, so the same site can sit still for days and then run hard when the trade winds pick up or shift. A wetsuit or dive skin is the standard exposure protocol.
- **Reef decline.** SCTLD, arriving in 2023, and back to back bleaching events in 2023 and 2024 have driven a sharp, measured decline: average live coral cover across STINAPA's monitored sites dropped from 24.3% in 2019 to 9.1% in 2025, while turf algae rose from 31.9% to 46.8% and macroalgae from 11.6% to 17.9%. Disease-susceptible species, boulder brain coral, flower coral, maze coral, great star coral and the smaller brain corals, have been hit hardest and are now rare to absent at monitored sites. Elkhorn and staghorn coral, both far more visible to a diver than the encrusting species the reef now leans on, took heavy losses too: on Bonaire's leeward coast, about 19% of elkhorn colonies died outright and another 55% show partial mortality, while staghorn fared a little better, about 55% partial mortality and close to none outright dead. Site files that describe elkhorn or staghorn stands note this where the loss applies to that site.

## Sites currently covered

Site numbering runs 1 to 63 plus a lettered set A to Z at Klein Bonaire, 90 sites in total. STINAPA counts 88 of these as marked sites along the West Coast and Klein Bonaire; White Hole and Cai sit on the East Coast instead, recommended only for advanced divers with an experienced guide. STINAPA removed Cai from its official dive sites in May 2018 after repeated serious incidents and reclassified White Hole as a boat dive at the same time; its dive map still lists both as advanced-only shore dives. Turtle City, an East Coast boat dive inside the marine park, sits outside this numbering and is listed after Cai.

Every site inside Washington Slagbaai National Park, Boka Bartol through Boka Slagbaai S, has been closed to diving since 1 May 2023 over an SCTLD outbreak, with the park open for every other activity; each affected site file carries the same note. The coast between BOPEC and the park, Carel's Vision and Nukove included, closed on the same date and reopened on 27 January 2024. Karpata and La Dania's Leap, to the south, stayed open through both; STINAPA had closed the pair from July to September 2022 after a fast-spreading coral disease was found at Karpata. STINAPA's current dive map omits Carel's Vision. On Klein Bonaire, Carl's Hill Annex (Yellowman's Reef) is closed per STINAPA's own map. Reconfirm status with STINAPA before planning a dive near any of these boundaries. Klein Bonaire is open to visitors from 07:00 to 19:00, seven days a week. STINAPA held the islet and every one of its moorings to 07:00 to 14:00 from May to September 2023 against SCTLD, and restored the full day on 1 October 2023. The water taxis make their last run back to Bonaire at about 16:45, and overnight stays on the islet are prohibited.

SCTLD precaution applies uniformly across the park: disinfect before, between and after every dive, at every site.

## Emergency

- **Local emergency.** 911, covering ambulance, police and fire with multilingual (Dutch, English, Spanish, Papiamentu) operators; 114 also reaches ambulance dispatch directly. Call EMS first, then DAN once the diver is stabilized and transport is underway.
- **[DAN (Divers Alert Network)](https://dan.org).** +1-919-684-9111, 24/7, collect calls accepted worldwide. Its Annual Diving Report and Incident Insights are the standing source for dive medicine, gear, procedure and debrief questions.
- **Recompression Chamber Bonaire, Kralendijk.** Adjacent to Hospital San Francisco, Kaya Soeur Bartola 2. Admission is through the hospital's emergency room; the chamber's own line, +599 717 8187, is staffed only during an active treatment. o2bonaire.com. The island's only chamber, and it depends on a single on-call dive physician; it has closed for months at a time when that position sat vacant, most recently in early 2025.
