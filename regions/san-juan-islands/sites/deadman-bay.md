# Deadman Bay

Shore dive in a small pebble cove on the west side of San Juan Island, just south of Lime Kiln Point State Park, facing Haro Strait. An open grassy hillside runs down from the road to the beach, and dark rock shorelines lined with kelp frame the bay on either side: a vertical wall and ledge on the east side, and a low rock formation leading to submerged walls on the west. The inner bay is shallow silty sand and eelgrass; the life grows richer toward the mouth, where the bottom drops steeply into Haro Strait. Circular currents that change direction along both shores are the headline hazard, and every dive past the inner bay is timed to slack.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk down the open grassy hillside from the road to the pebble beach at the head of the bay. The beach is medium-sized cobble that gives underfoot, so step carefully on the way in and out. Swim 168 m west-southwest across the bay to the dive coordinate, across eelgrass, with kelp lining either shore in summer and fall.
* **Parking.** Small unpaved pullouts along Westside Road above the bay, room for only a handful of cars at each; arrive early or come on a weekday. The pullout at the parking coordinate sits 87 m from the beach. Lime Kiln Point State Park, about 0.4 km farther along the road, has a larger lot and requires a Discover Pass or a day parking fee.
* **Access.** The beach lies inside Deadman Bay Preserve, a San Juan County Land Bank property open for daytime, pedestrian use only. Stay on the designated trails and leash dogs; commercial kayak launching is prohibited. Trails connect the preserve to Lime Kiln Point State Park and the Land Bank's Limekiln Preserve. The hillside is steep, and the walk down and back up in full gear suits a single tank. Reach the island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes to Friday Harbor, up to about two hours. From the ferry landing, follow Spring Street out of town, turn left onto Douglas Road at about 2.7 km, and stay on it as it becomes Bailer Hill Road and then Westside Road; the preserve and its pullouts come at about 13 km from the ferry.
* **Facilities.** An outhouse part way down the trail above the head of the bay, open year round.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The inner bay.** A shallow cove opening south-southwest, its floor silty sand and eelgrass. From the beach the bottom slopes gently to about 3 m below MLLW, then falls to 7.5 m at the dive coordinate and 19 m across the mouth.
* **East side (left, looking out).** A long rocky shoreline rises vertically above the water and runs south. Below it a rock ledge rounds outward to the south-southwest for about 370 m, with rock formations, ledges and boulders stepping down past its base, about 11 m below MLLW 111 m southeast of the dive coordinate and 15 m at 187 m south-southeast. Thick kelp grows along it in summer and fall.
* **West side (right, looking out).** A low rock formation juts into the bay at the west end of the beach. Past it the rocky shoreline extends outward for about 370 m, rounding to the west-southwest, a series of submerged rock formations and walls covered in sea urchins. The slope here is the steepest at the site: about 7 m below MLLW off the rock formation, 21 m at 147 m west of the dive coordinate, and past 30 m farther out.
* **Boulder field.** Beyond the kelp and eelgrass along the shores, the bottom opens onto a field of large boulders crowded with urchins.
* **Choosing a side.** Both sides make a full dive. The richest life lies on the rock outside the bay, and the current grows stronger and less predictable toward the mouth.

## Current

Haro Strait's tidal stream runs past the mouth of the bay, and inside it the flow breaks into eddies that set in different directions along each shore.

* **Flood.** Sets northwest across the mouth of the bay, toward Lime Kiln Point. Daily peaks at the station run 0.49 to 1.12 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets south-southeast across the mouth of the bay, down the island. Daily peaks at the station run 0.37 to 1.55 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve; slack before a small exchange gives the most forgiving dive. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 66 to 168 minutes and the slack before ebb 66 to 108 minutes, the shortest about 50 minutes between two strong exchanges. Where a weak exchange falls between two slacks, the current can stay under the threshold for 3 hours or more.
* **Circular currents.** Three or more circular current patterns form in the bay on both the flood and the ebb. A diver swimming out along either shore meets the current changing direction, and growing stronger, as the swim goes on.
* **On the flood, east side.** Swimming out along the east side, the current first runs against the diver, then eases through a slack stretch, and then runs with the diver, outward.
* **On the flood, west side.** Swimming out along the west side, the current first runs with the diver, reverses a short distance from the beach to run inbound against the swim, and reverses again farther out along the rock to run outward.
* **On the ebb, east side.** Swimming out along the east side, the current first runs outward with the diver, then turns inbound, and near the mouth turns outward again.
* **On the ebb, west side.** Swimming out along the west side, the current first runs against the diver, eases through a quiet stretch, turns outward, and near the mouth runs against the diver again.
* **Wind and slack.** Wind, offshore wind included, can shift the turn of the current at the site, so treat any predicted slack as approximate.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 224° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 197° |

The bay opens south-southwest between its two rock shores.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through west (SSE-W), onshore over 11 to more than 30 km of open water across Haro Strait, driving wind waves and surge straight into the bay and onto the beach.
* **Mixed.** West-northwest (WNW), along the shore over 17.5 km, running a swell past the mouth of the bay.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through southeast (NW-SE), blocked by the island itself and the bay's two shores, with 240 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** West-northwest (WNW), over 17.5 km, blows against the northwest flood set. South-southeast through south (SSE-S), over more than 30 km, blows against the south-southeast ebb set.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** About 4.5 to 9 m or more, often murkier over the silty floor of the inner bay.
* **Season.** Winter brings the clearest water; spring and summer plankton blooms cut it, worst in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Kelp.** Extensive bull kelp beds along both rock shores through summer and fall, with smaller kelp blanketing the boulders and eelgrass beds in the inner bay. A light brightens the holes and crevices under the canopy.
* **Rock and boulders.** Red, purple and green sea urchins in great numbers on the walls and boulder field, with Christmas anemones, sea squirts, sea cucumbers, sea stars, chitons, limpets, nudibranchs, sharp-nosed crabs, hermit crabs and shrimp.
* **Octopus and crabs.** Giant Pacific octopus den in the rock, and Puget Sound king crabs turn up among the boulders.
* **Fish.** Grunt sculpins are commonly seen, with perch, tubesnouts, blackeye gobies and young rockfish around the kelp and rock.
* **Mammals.** Sea lions pass through, and orcas travel this shore of Haro Strait in summer.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Circular currents change direction along both shores and strengthen toward the mouth, and outside the bay the stream runs at up to 1.55 m/s on the largest ebbs at the station. The inner bay suits any diver; the mouth and the outer walls ask for experience in current. Dive at slack, ideally before a small exchange, and turn back early if the current builds.
* **Bull kelp.** Thick bull kelp along both shores in summer and fall can entangle a diver at the surface. Ascend clear of the canopy and carry a cutting tool.
* **Wind surge.** Wind from south-southeast through west crosses open water straight into the bay, building surge on the rock and chop on the cobble beach.
* **The hillside.** The walk down the steep, grassy hillside and the climb back up in full gear are tiring, and the cobble beach shifts underfoot at the water's edge.
