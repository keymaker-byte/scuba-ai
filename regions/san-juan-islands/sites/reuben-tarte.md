# Reuben Tarte County Park

Shore dive at Reuben Tarte County Park, also known as Reuben Tarte Picnic Area, from a small pebble cove on the northeast corner of San Juan Island where San Juan, Spieden and President channels meet. Rock walls, ledges and cascading boulders run along the shore on both sides of the cove and around the small rocky knoll that forms its east side, with a soft silty slope further northwest. The site is current-swept and full of invertebrates and fish. Current is the headline hazard, and every dive here is timed to slack.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** An easy walk into the water off the dark gray pebble beach in the cove at the foot of the road, about 130 m due south of the dive coordinate.
* **Parking.** The gravel upper lot at the top of the hill, about 200 m in a straight line from the entry, with very few spaces. The lower lot at the beach holds one ADA-accessible space. Drive down the steep paved road to unload gear, then return the car to the upper lot and walk back down.
* **Access.** A San Juan County day park, open dawn to dusk, with overnight use prohibited. The access road to the water is occasionally closed to vehicles, which makes for a long walk in gear. Reach the island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes to Friday Harbor. From Friday Harbor take Roche Harbor Road for about 11.4 km, turn right on Rouleau Road for 1.6 km, right on Limestone Point Road for 1.3 km, then right on San Juan Drive for 0.5 km to the lot on the left.
* **Facilities.** A toilet in the lower beach area.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The cove.** A narrow bay opening north-northeast between the western shore and the knoll, with a silty sand bottom about 3 m below MLLW just inside the mouth. Two distinct dives leave from its beach.
* **Western cliff.** A rock ledge runs out along the west side of the cove to 7.9 m below MLLW just off the mouth. Swim to the cliff, submerge, and continue past its base to the boulders and ledges below, where the bottom reaches about 16 m below MLLW some 50 m north of the dive coordinate.
* **Northwest along the shore.** From the boulders, the left turn follows the rocky shoreline north and northwest along a wall cut with small canyons. Further along, the rock gives way to a quiet, soft silty slope.
* **Mouth of the cove.** The right turn crosses large rocks over the mouth in an arc back toward the knoll, past a dark boulder shaped like a resting lion on the silty sand beneath the knoll's northwest tip.
* **The knoll.** The rocky headland on the right as you look out from the beach, joined to shore behind the beach. A short swim out to it reaches a wall with cascading boulders below.
* **Southeast of the knoll.** A second dive follows the rocky ledges and boulders off the knoll and around it to the south, past a second pebble beach south of the knoll. The bottom sits about 12 m below MLLW some 70 m off the knoll's east side.
* **Open slope.** The bottom keeps falling northeast of the cove mouth, reaching about 28 m below MLLW at 100 m from the dive coordinate and about 33 m at 150 m.

## Current

Three channels converge at this corner of the island and drive the flow around the cove.

* **Inside the cove.** Water inside the cove turns counter-clockwise on both the flood and the ebb.
* **Set along the shore.** Open water at the dive coordinate sets northeast on the flood and southwest on the ebb. Close along the shore the flow runs northwest on the flood and southeast on the ebb, and the routes below ride that along-shore flow.
* **Flood.** Runs west-northwest up San Juan Channel along this shore toward Limestone Point, then turns north-northeast into President Channel at the east end of Spieden Channel. Daily peaks at the station run 0.63 to 1.90 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Comes south-southwest down President Channel and strikes the shore between Limestone Point and the cove. There it divides at a quiet silted area northwest of the cove: to the northwest the water runs away from the cove around Limestone Point into Spieden Channel, and to the southeast it runs toward the cove, past it and through Rocky Bay into San Juan Channel. Daily peaks at the station run 0.67 to 2.34 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 48 to 78 minutes and the slack before ebb 42 to 66 minutes; slacks between strong exchanges shrink to about 30 minutes.
* **Route on slack before flood.** Dive the shoreline southeast of the cove first, so the rising flood carries the return leg northwest toward the beach.
* **Route on slack before ebb.** Dive the shoreline north-northwest of the cove first, staying southeast of the silted area, so the rising ebb carries the return leg southeast toward the beach.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | north-northeast, 24° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northeast, 35° |

* **Bad.** North through east-northeast (N-ENE), onshore over 5.9 to 16.8 km of open water, building wind swell on the beach and the swim out.
* **Short fetch.** East (E), onshore over 3.4 km, enough to raise chop in the cove.
* **Mixed.** East-southeast through southeast (ESE-SE), cross-shore over 6.7 to 8.2 km, running swell past the entry.
* **Fine.** South-southeast clockwise through north-northwest (SSE-NNW), blocked by the island itself, with 680 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** North-northeast through east (NNE-E), over 3.4 to 15.4 km, blows against the northeast flood set in open water. Along the shore, north (N), over 16.8 km, blows against the northwest flood, and east through southeast (E-SE), over 3.4 to 8.2 km, against the southeast ebb. No wind with 1 km of fetch opposes the southwest ebb set.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** About 3 to 9 m. The water is haziest when the current is running and clearest before the spring bloom.
* **Season.** Spring before the bloom reaches about 9 m; summer and early autumn plankton bring it down.
* **Tide.** Strong current lowers it toward the bottom of the range.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Rock walls and boulders.** Dense invertebrate cover: sponges including large aggregated nipple sponges, red and green Christmas anemones, stalked and red sea squirts, giant barnacles, chitons, big red urchins, orange and California cucumbers, sunflower stars and other colorful sea stars. Shrimp swarm over the substrate. Puget Sound king crab turns up here.
* **Fish.** Copper rockfish and schools of juvenile rockfish, plus lingcod, kelp and painted greenling, perch, schools of shiners, black-eyed gobies, sculpins, red Irish lords and gunnels.
* **Dens.** Giant Pacific octopus holes up under the boulders.
* **Silty slope to the northwest.** A strong nudibranch dive: Janolus on the kelp along the bottom, golden dironas and alabasters, with their egg ribbons in season, among helmet crabs, decorated crabs, shrimp and black-eyed gobies, and fewer fish than the rock.
* **Kelp.** Understory kelp over the rock slopes and bull kelp around the knoll. Seals frequent the kelp beds, and orcas pass offshore.

## Hazards

* **Current.** This is a slack dive. A dive outside the window runs short and covers little ground, and peaks at the station exceed 2 m/s on the largest ebbs.
* **Depth off the mouth.** The slope steepens sharply northeast of the cove and passes 30 m within 150 m of the dive coordinate. A diver swept or drifting offshore loses the wall to open water, so stay on the rock and keep the wall on one side.
* **Bull kelp.** Bull kelp around the knoll can entangle a diver at the surface. Ascend clear of it, or swim under it on the way back.
* **Wind swell.** Wind from north through southeast raises swell on the exposed shore and can spoil the entry and exit.
* **Steep access road.** The paved road from the upper lot to the beach is very steep, and a closed road means walking it in full gear both ways.
