# Agate Beach County Park

Shore dive into Outer Bay from Agate Beach County Park, a cobble beach at the south end of Lopez Island looking out past Iceberg Island toward the eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca. The bay is shallow, a floor of eelgrass and sand at about 6 to 9 m below MLLW, with rock formations breaking the surface: a near rock just off the beach, Shark Fin Rock in the middle of the bay, and Outer Rock to its north, whose outer face is covered in urchins. The rocks lie a long surface swim out, and a scooter shortens the swim. The headline hazards are that swim and the access itself: the beach stairs are gone and the shore is armored with rock.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Cross the road from the lot to the bank where the beach stairs stood, and climb down to the beach. The beach is large round cobbles that roll and give way underfoot, and swell from the strait can surge onto it, which makes standing up to don or doff fins difficult. The near rock lies about 200 m west-southwest of the entry, Shark Fin Rock about 460 m west-northwest, and Outer Rock about 580 m out, north of Shark Fin Rock. The dive coordinate at the mouth of the bay lies about 785 m west-northwest.
* **Parking.** A small free lot beside MacKaye Harbor Road, across the road from the beach, shared with hikers walking to Iceberg Point. Space is limited.
* **Access.** San Juan County day park. The beach stairs were undermined by shoreline erosion and removed, and the bank between the road and the beach is armored with rip-rap, so the only way down is over the rock. San Juan County plans to rebuild MacKaye Harbor Road and the park lot from about April through September 2027, with no parking for Agate Beach or Iceberg Point during the work, and then to anchor seasonal aluminum stairs on the new road grade, installed in spring and removed in autumn. Check the current state with San Juan County Parks, (360) 378-8420, before planning a dive. Reach Lopez Island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes, about 40 minutes. From the ferry landing, drive south 3.2 km on Ferry Road, turn left onto Center Road for 9.0 km, turn left at the tee onto Mud Bay Road for 4.5 km, then turn right onto MacKaye Harbor Road and follow it 2.9 km past MacKaye Harbor and Barlow Bay to the park.
* **Facilities.** A small grassy picnic area with picnic tables, a toilet, and a short loop trail through the woods.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach and the bay floor.** Cobbles give way to sand and a large eelgrass bed. The floor reaches about 3.5 m below MLLW some 150 m out and holds at about 6 to 7 m across the middle of the bay.
* **The near rock.** A rock formation about 200 m west-southwest of the entry, standing above the water, with about 3.5 m below MLLW off its outer side. It is the shortest swim of the dive and carries little growth.
* **Shark Fin Rock.** The larger formation in the middle of the bay, shaped like a fin above the water, about 460 m west-northwest of the entry. The floor around it lies at about 6 to 9 m below MLLW.
* **Outer Rock.** A smaller formation north of Shark Fin Rock, near the bluff on the bay's north side. Its outer face is broad and covered in urchins, and the water just outside the gap between the two rocks runs about 9 m below MLLW.
* **North shore.** A rocky bluff with kelp and boulders running from Johns Point back toward the beach, the route home after the rocks.
* **South shore.** Rock and kelp along the bay's south side, running out toward Iceberg Point.
* **Mouth of the bay.** West of the rocks the floor slopes down to 12 m and then 18 m below MLLW at the dive coordinate, opening onto the deep water off Iceberg Point.

## Current

Outer Bay sits off the tidal stream of the eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca, which runs fast outside the mouth while the inner bay stays slow.

* **Flood.** Enters the bay from the west and drives onto the northern rocky face. Where the bluff rounds to the north just outside Outer Rock it splits in two: the outer branch runs northwest along the bluff toward Johns Point, and the inshore branch runs along the bluff toward the beach, turns clockwise along the beach, and flows back out along the southern shore. A diver who returns from Outer Rock along the north shore on a flood rides it back to the entry. Daily peaks at the station run 0.40 to 1.22 m/s over 30 days; at the dive coordinate the flow stays far weaker, about 0.05 m/s at most near the bottom, setting weakly northwest along the bay's axis.
* **Ebb.** Almost nonexistent inside Outer Bay on a small or moderate exchange, about 2 m or less. On a larger exchange a weak ebb current moves inshore. Daily peaks at the station run 0.90 to 2.39 m/s over 30 days; inside the bay the ebb stays slack or near it.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve, and the inner bay stays diveable through much of the tide. The long swim to Shark Fin Rock and Outer Rock suits the slack before flood best, with the flood then carrying the return along the north shore. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, a single slack before flood runs about 30 to 115 minutes, typically about 70, and a single slack before ebb about 35 to 215 minutes, typically about 65. The strong ebbs, up to 2.39 m/s, narrow the slacks either side of them. Around a weak exchange the station stays under the threshold across two slacks in one window, for 210 to 280 minutes.
* **Eddies in the bay.** The flow inside the bay turns and splits around the rocks and the bluff, so the set at any one spot can run across or against the tide outside.
* **Outside the mouth.** Beyond the mouth of the bay the water opens onto the stream off Iceberg Point, which picks up quickly with the tide.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 280° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 214° |

The beach faces west out of the bay's mouth, past Iceberg Island to the open strait. The dive area's nearest shore is the bay's north side, which faces southwest across the mouth.

* **Bad.** Southwest through west (SW-W), onshore over 27 to more than 30 km of open water across the Strait of Juan de Fuca, driving wind waves and swell into the bay and surge onto the cobble beach.
* **Short fetch.** West-northwest through north-northwest (WNW-NNW), onshore over 1.9 to 2.6 km, building chop across the mouth of the bay onto the beach.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southwest (N-SSW), blocked by the island itself and by the headland running out to Iceberg Point, with 790 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** The current at the dive coordinate peaks at about 0.05 m/s near the bottom, too weak to stack against the wind.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** Often milky and cloudy, down to about 1.5 m.
* **Season.** Winter and early spring bring the clearest water; late spring and summer plankton blooms cut it.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Eelgrass and sand.** A large eelgrass bed covers much of the bay floor, with small crabs throughout; shrimp larvae can fill the water in early summer.
* **Rocks.** Sea urchins in great numbers, covering the broad outer face of Outer Rock. Gumboot chitons, small rock scallops and nudibranchs live on the rock.
* **Octopus.** Giant Pacific octopus are seen around the near rock.
* **Kelp and boulders.** Kelp grows over the boulders along the north and south shores.
* **Mammals and birds.** Killer whales pass outside the bay, and gulls work the rocks.

## Hazards

* **The swim.** Shark Fin Rock lies about 460 m out and Outer Rock about 580 m, a long surface swim each way in a drysuit. Save air and energy for the return, plan the swim out at slack before flood, or use a scooter.
* **Access.** The beach stairs are gone, and the bank down to the beach is rock armor. Climbing it in gear is a fall hazard.
* **The beach.** The round cobbles roll underfoot, and surge off the strait makes standing, donning and doffing fins hard at the waterline.
* **Wind waves and swell.** Wind from southwest through west crosses the open strait straight into the bay and can make the entry and exit on the beach unsafe.
* **Current outside the bay.** Beyond the mouth of the bay the stream off Iceberg Point runs fast, up to 2.39 m/s at the station. Stay inside the bay, and expect the flood to drive onto the north shore.
* **Kelp.** Kelp grows along both rocky shores. Ascend clear of it, and carry a cutting tool.
