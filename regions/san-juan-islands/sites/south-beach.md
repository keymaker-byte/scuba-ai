# South Beach (American Camp)

Shore dive off South Beach, the longest public beach in the San Juan Islands, in the American Camp unit of San Juan Island National Historical Park, facing south across the eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca. A gently sloping bottom of cobble, coarse sand and then fine sand runs out through wide bands of eelgrass to a nearly flat shelf, a dive for small animals in a sandy habitat: sand lances, gunnels, hermit crabs, nudibranchs and flatfish. Divers can drive to within a few metres of the water and enter anywhere along the beach below the lot. The beach takes wind waves straight off the strait, and surf on the entry and exit is the headline hazard.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk from the lot across the driftwood line onto the sand and cobble beach and wade in, then swim out over the shallow, gently sloping bottom. Any point along the beach serves as an entry or an exit, so a dive can drift with the current along the shore and finish wherever the walk back suits. Check the surf before kitting up: with any wind from east-southeast through southwest, waves break on the beach.
* **Parking.** The South Beach day use parking lot, a few metres above the water at the end of Pickett's Lane. Parking is free, and the park charges no entrance fee. Space is limited, and the lot is busy on summer days.
* **Access.** Reach the island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes to Friday Harbor. From Friday Harbor, follow Spring Street out of town past the airport, turn left on Douglas Road for about 2.4 km, left on Little Road for about 800 m, then right on Cattle Point Road. Pass the American Camp visitor center, and turn right on Pickett's Lane, a short gravel road down to the lot. The park grounds are open 24 hours a day year round. The beach runs about 3 km east toward Cattle Point and is reached from land only at its two ends. Launching and landing watercraft is permitted at the South Beach day use area. The park prohibits collecting natural materials, seashells included, so leave every animal and shell where it lies.
* **Facilities.** The South Beach picnic area, with accessible restrooms and picnic tables along the beach, a few reached across short stretches of sand.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shore.** A straight sand and cobble beach running east and west, backed by a driftwood line and grassy bluffs. Cattle Point lighthouse stands on its bluff at the southeast tip of the island, visible to the east.
* **The flat.** On a low tide, the cobble and sand flat dries for about 50 m out from the entry before the water deepens.
* **Eelgrass.** Swim out south, 183°, from the entry. The bottom passes from cobble to coarse and then fine sand and reaches two species of eelgrass growing in wide bands from about 2 to 5 m below MLLW, with patches of kelp.
* **The sand.** Past the eelgrass, at the dive coordinate, open sand slopes very gently offshore: about 9 m below MLLW at 250 m from the entry and 11 m at 400 m. A few isolated rocks lie scattered on the bottom from about 8 m down.
* **Shelf edge.** About 450 m out, at roughly 12 m below MLLW, the shelf ends and the bottom drops steeply into the strait: about 23 m at 550 m from the entry and 30.5 m at 600 m. The shore dive stays on the shelf.

## Current

The flow along the beach is weak on the shelf, and the strong tidal stream runs offshore of it, through the strait and into San Juan Channel around Cattle Point.

* **Flood.** Sets west-northwest along the beach, away from Cattle Point. Daily peaks at the station run 0.46 to 1.22 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets east-southeast along the beach, toward Cattle Point. Daily peaks at the station run 1.22 to 2.47 m/s over 30 days, the stronger of the two exchanges.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 40 to 80 minutes and the slack before ebb 50 to 65 minutes, the shortest under 15 minutes between two strong exchanges. Where a weak exchange falls between two slacks, the current can stay under the threshold for over 4 hours.
* **Along the beach.** Close inshore the current runs parallel to the shore, often to the east, and the long open beach makes a drift dive simple: let it carry you along the shelf and exit wherever it leaves you, then walk back along the sand.
* **Off the shelf.** Past the shelf edge the bottom drops into the open strait, where the tidal stream runs far stronger than on the shelf.

* **Mixed.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W), along the shore over 19.1 to more than 30 km, running swell along the beach past the entry.
* **Fine.** West-northwest clockwise through east (WNW-E), blocked by the island itself, with 1.5 km of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W), over 19.1 to more than 30 km, blows against the west-northwest flood set. East through southeast (E-SE), over 1.5 to 28.2 km, blows against the east-southeast ebb set.

## Depth and tide

The site swings about 2.2 m between high and low water on a typical day, and up to 3.6 m on the year's biggest exchange, a large share of a dive this shallow, so depths are quoted against the MLLW chart datum: a minus tide reads shallower than the datum figure, and any height above MLLW reads deeper.

## Visibility

* **What drives it.** Fine sand stirred up by waves on the beach clouds the shallows after any blow.
* **Season.** Autumn and winter bring the clearest water. Spring and summer plankton blooms cloud the water, worst near the surface.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Sand.** Pacific sand lances in large numbers, especially in summer, emerge in rows from the sandy bottom, and schools of them can surround a diver. Flounder and other flatfish lie on the open sand, and hermit crabs scurry over it.
* **Eelgrass and kelp.** Gunnels hide in the eelgrass and kelp, coloured yellow-green or red-brown by their diet for camouflage. Hooded nudibranchs cling to the eelgrass blades collecting plankton, and decorator crabs disguise their backs with sponges and plants.
* **Fish.** Pacific spiny dogfish cruise the shallows looking for food.
* **Mammals.** Orcas pass in the Strait of Juan de Fuca off the beach.

## Hazards

* **Surf.** The beach faces the open strait, and wind from east-southeast through southwest builds waves that break on the entry. Getting out through the surf, and back in at the end, can knock a diver down and strip loose gear such as weights. Check the waves before kitting up, and dive only when the beach is calm.
* **Drift.** The current along the beach carries a diver east or west of the entry during the dive. Plan to exit downstream along the beach, and expect the walk back on sand in full gear.
* **Shelf edge.** About 450 m out the bottom drops from about 12 m to past 30 m within 150 m, into the open strait, where the tidal stream runs up to 2.47 m/s at the station. Turn back before the edge.
* **Driftwood.** Storms throw large logs onto the beach, and in waves they roll in the surf line.
