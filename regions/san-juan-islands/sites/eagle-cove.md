# Eagle Cove

Shore dive from the beach of Eagle Cove County Park, a wide, secluded south-facing bay on the southwest shore of San Juan Island just north of American Camp, opening onto the junction of Haro Strait and the Strait of Juan de Fuca. Sand, eelgrass, kelp and rock meet in the cove: boulders and ledges along both rocky shores under a summer bull kelp canopy, and out along the west shore to Eagle Point, where the rock drops steeply into deep water. A steep trail down to the beach and a long swim out make it a demanding dive. The headline hazard is the current off Eagle Point, which always sets away from the cove, together with the lost fishing nets that snag on the rock there.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk in off the log-strewn sand beach at the head of the cove and swim about 280 m south-southeast to the dive coordinate. On a low tide the sand flat off the beach dries, out to about 135 m on a minus tide, which adds a walk over wet sand to the entry. The rocks and kelp of the west side lie a short swim from the beach; Eagle Point lies about 550 m southwest of the entry along the west shore, a long swim or a short scooter ride.
* **Parking.** The small public lot of Eagle Cove County Park at 449 Eagle Cove Drive, on the shore side once the road turns to run along the coast. Space is limited. Cost not established.
* **Access.** Eagle Cove County Park is a San Juan County day-use park, open year round during daylight; check current rules with San Juan County Parks, (360) 378-8420. A short trail starts beside the bulletin board at the park entrance and runs about 160 m down a narrow wooded ravine to a stairway onto the beach. It is moderately steep and slippery when wet, and the climb back up in gear is the hardest part of the dive. The beach is busy with swimmers and skimboarders on summer days. Reach the island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes to Friday Harbor, about two hours. From the ferry, turn left up Spring Street for 2.6 km, left on Douglas Road for 2.4 km, left on Little Road for 0.6 km, then right on Cattle Point Road for 4.3 km. Just before the entrance to American Camp, turn right on Eagle Cove Drive and follow it 0.6 km to the parking area.
* **Facilities.** A bulletin board at the trailhead.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach and inner cove.** A sandy beach backed by driftwood under a grassy bluff, with eelgrass and a shallow sand bottom running out from it. The bottom reaches about 4 m below MLLW some 200 m out and 7.5 m at the dive coordinate.
* **West side and the inner point.** Rocks and kelp run along the west side of the inner cove to a small rocky point visible from the beach. Past it the bottom deepens faster than on the east side.
* **West shore to Eagle Point.** Beyond the inner point the rocky shore runs southwest to Eagle Point. Boulders and ledges step down from the shore to sand at about 15 to 18 m below MLLW.
* **Eagle Point.** The cove's western headland, a bluff seen across the cove from the beach. Off its tip the rock falls from about 10 m to past 40 m below MLLW within 40 m, with anemone and sponge walls where the current hits the rock.
* **East side.** Along the east shore the cove rounds an unnamed rocky point, still inside the cove, into a second small, shallow rocky bay. The bottom on this side deepens slowly.
* **Kelp.** In summer bull kelp grows along both sides of the cove over a thick understory of smaller kelps, forming a canopy that dims the light below to twilight.

## Current

The cove sits beside the tidal stream of Haro Strait and the eastern Strait of Juan de Fuca, which drives a clockwise eddy through it on the flood.

* **Flood.** Sets west into the cove and drives onto the western shore between Eagle Point and the inner point. There it turns south-southwest and runs out along the rocky shore, rounding Eagle Point to join the west-northwest flow of Haro Strait. The part that strikes the inner point splits off, runs in along the shore, turns clockwise across the beach and flows out along the eastern shore. A diver swimming out along the west shore on a flood swims against the current until the inner point, then rides an outbound current toward Eagle Point. On days with a flood, daily peaks at the station run 0.43 to 0.84 m/s over 30 days; on some days the station shows no flood at all.
* **Ebb.** Its pattern depends on the size of the exchange. On a tidal exchange of about 1.8 m or less, the ebb inside the cove follows the clockwise flood pattern. On an exchange of about 3 m or more, the ebb sets east-northeast past the mouth of the cove from Eagle Point, and the current inside the cove stays light. Between the two, the ebb can take either pattern. Daily peaks at the station run 0.15 to 0.75 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood runs 84 to 366 minutes, typically about 140, and the slack before ebb 84 to 354 minutes, typically about 155. Around a weak exchange the current stays under the threshold across two slacks, for 210 minutes or more. The inner cove is diveable through more of the tide than Eagle Point, where the stream picks up first.
* **Eagle Point.** The current at the point always sets away from the cove, and picks up there while the cove is still slack. Plan the dive to Eagle Point at slack and turn early: once the flood starts, the return along the west shore runs against the outbound set until the inner point, where the eddy turns back into the cove.
* **East side.** On a flood, swimming east along the shore rides the clockwise eddy out around the unnamed point; slightly deeper water carries the return.
* **Wind.** Strong wind, offshore included, shifts the timing of slack in the cove.

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southeast, 166° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-southwest, 254° |

The beach faces south-southeast out of the cove's mouth, while the dive area's nearest shore is the cove's east side, which faces west-southwest across the cove toward Eagle Point.

* **Bad.** Southeast through southwest (SE-SW), onshore over 21.5 km to more than 30 km of open water across the Strait of Juan de Fuca, driving wind waves and swell into the mouth of the cove and onto the beach.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest (WSW), along the shore over more than 30 km, running swell past Eagle Point into the mouth of the cove.
* **Fine.** West clockwise through east-southeast (W-ESE), blocked by Eagle Point and the island itself, with 510 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** West-southwest (WSW), over more than 30 km, blows against the west flood set. Southeast (SE), over 21.5 km, blows against the east ebb set.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** About 3 to 12 m, lower in the shallows than out on the rock.
* **Season.** Winter and early spring bring the clearest water; late spring and summer plankton blooms cut it, worst in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Kelp and eelgrass.** Bull kelp stands over thick understory kelps along both rocky sides in summer, home to kelp crabs, with a healthy eelgrass bed off the beach. Tube-snouts and perch move through the kelp.
* **Rock and boulders.** Red sea urchins, white sea cucumbers, white plumose anemones and a variety of colorful sea stars, with the occasional sunflower star. Decorated warbonnets and San Diego dorids live in the rock.
* **Eagle Point.** Walls of crimson anemones and sea cucumbers, sulphur sponges and giant plumose anemones. Large schools of yellowtail, black and Puget Sound rockfish gather off the point, and yelloweye rockfish, juveniles and adults, live down deep.
* **Fish and dens.** Painted greenlings and lingcod on the reef, with giant Pacific octopus and wolf eels in the dens and sometimes out in the open.
* **Crabs.** Large Puget Sound king crab on the rock.
* **Sandy bottom.** Scattered rocks out on the sand carry life of their own.
* **Mammals and birds.** Harbor seals rest on nearby rocks, Steller sea lions pass outside summer, and orcas pass the cove in summer. Bald eagles hunt over the bluffs.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Off Eagle Point the stream always sets away from the cove and picks up while the cove is still slack, reaching 0.84 m/s on the largest floods at the station. Dive the point at slack and turn early, since the return along the west shore runs against the outbound flood set.
* **Lost fishing nets.** Gillnets and lines lost from fishing boats snag on the rock off Eagle Point and along this shore, from about 18 m to below 50 m. A monofilament net kills fish, crabs and diving birds, and can entangle a diver. Keep clear of any net, carry a cutting tool, and report it to WDFW's derelict gear line.
* **Bull kelp.** The summer canopy along both sides of the cove is thick. Ascend clear of it, and carry a cutting tool.
* **Wind waves and swell.** Wind from southeast through southwest crosses the open strait straight into the cove and can spoil the entry and exit on the beach.
* **The walk.** The trail down the ravine is moderately steep and slippery when wet, and the climb back up in gear follows a long swim. Save air and energy for the swim back to the beach.
