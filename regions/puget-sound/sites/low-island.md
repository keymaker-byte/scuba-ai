# Low Island (San Juan County Park North Beach)

Shore dive from the north beach of San Juan County Park, on the west side of San Juan Island, across a shallow channel to Low Island, a small rocky islet in Haro Strait ringed by kelp and stepping rock shelves. North-northwest of the island the bottom slopes to the top of a sheer wall that drops into dark water past 30 m, covered in plumose anemones, sponges and crimson anemones, with wolf eels in the open. Low Island is a closed National Wildlife Refuge island. Current is the headline hazard: the water around the island runs hard outside slack, and every dive here is timed to slack before a small exchange.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Carry gear down the staircase from the campground to the foot-only beach in North Bay, the shallow sandy cove north of the park's grass field. Swim 215 m west across the channel to the dive coordinate off the island's east side.
* **Parking.** On the bluff above the north beach, about 58 m from the entry by straight line, with the staircase between. Campers park at their sites. Check current day-use and parking fees with San Juan County Parks.
* **Access.** San Juan County Park is a county campground open year round, with day use allowed. Campsite reservations are strongly recommended from May through September, and from 1 November to 31 March sites go first come, first served, with some closed and staffing limited. Low Island is part of the San Juan Islands National Wildlife Refuge, designated Wilderness and closed to public access: never land on it or climb out onto its rocks. The waters within 183 m of the island are closed to watercraft, and the county asks every visitor to stay at least 183 m away to spare its nesting seabirds and resting harbor seals. The dive around the island and the wall both lie inside that distance, so confirm with park staff that diving there is allowed before entering, and surface away from the island's rocks. Reach the island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes to Friday Harbor, about two hours. From the ferry, turn left up Spring Street, right on Second Street, and follow the main road onto Guard Street and Beaverton Valley Road for about 11 km, then turn left on Mitchell Bay Road for about 5 km, following it right, then left and left again onto West Side Road. The park entrance is on the right.
* **Facilities.** The staircase down to the beach, with flush toilets, drinking water, a small picnic shelter, picnic tables, fire pits and campsites in the park above.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **North Bay.** The cove at the entry is flat and sandy, about 8 m below MLLW where it opens onto the channel.
* **The channel.** A sand and cobble bottom between the beach and the island, about 8 m below MLLW off the bay and about 13 m at its deepest, some 145 m off the beach. The dive coordinate sits on its west side, 8.5 m below MLLW, about 65 m east of the island.
* **Low Island.** A bare, low rock ringed by a kelp bed and a series of rock steps. Its south end is the shallow side, about 7 m below MLLW some 70 m south of the island, falling to about 14 m beyond.
* **North-northwest slope.** From the island's north end a rocky bottom slopes away to the north-northwest, about 12 m below MLLW some 90 m out.
* **The wall.** About 135 m north-northwest of the island, at about 24 m below MLLW, the slope breaks into a near-vertical wall that drops to about 34 m at 175 m out. The bottom keeps falling past 40 m beyond it. The water below about 18 m often runs clearer than the shallows.

## Current

Haro Strait's tidal stream runs past the island, setting northeast on the flood and southwest on the ebb, and the channel between the island and the park carries strong rips when it runs.

* **Flood.** Sets northeast past the island toward the park's north shore. Daily peaks at the station run 0.49 to 1.03 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets southwest past the island, away from the beach and out toward open water. Daily peaks at the station run 0.37 to 1.55 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Dive at slack before a small exchange. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 66 to 132 minutes and the slack before ebb 60 to 108 minutes, the shortest about 25 minutes between two strong exchanges. Where a weak exchange falls between two slacks, the current can stay under the threshold for 3 hours or more.
* **Rips in the channel.** Strong rip currents develop between the park shore and the island once the stream picks up. Make the swim out and back only inside the window.
* **North Bay.** The sandy cove at the entry can hold quieter water during a flood than the open shore.
* **Route on slack before flood.** Swim out early in the window, dive the wall, and work back along the island's north side, so the rising flood sets the return northeast toward the north shore and the beach.
* **Route on slack before ebb.** Leave the wall early: the rising ebb sets southwest, away from the beach and out past the island's west side into open strait.

Low Island stands between the dive coordinate and the open strait to the west, so the fetch measured right behind it reads very short from west-southwest and west. Only the water in the island's lee is sheltered: just off the beach, west-southwest runs 13.6 km of open water past the island's south end.

* **Bad.** Southwest through west-southwest (SW-WSW), onshore over 12.2 to 13.6 km of open water, and west-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), onshore over 14.2 to 20.1 km, driving wind waves across the channel onto the beach.
* **Mixed.** South-southwest (SSW), cross-shore over 13.7 km, running swell past the entry.
* **Fine.** West (W), sheltered by Low Island, with 40 to 240 m of fetch. North-northwest clockwise through south (NNW-S), blocked by San Juan Island, with 920 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), over 12.2 to 13.7 km, blows against the southwest ebb set. No wind with 1 km of fetch opposes the northeast flood set.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** About 3 to 9 m or better at depth, with the shallows often worse.
* **Season.** Spring before the bloom reaches 9 m or more. Summer plankton can cut the shallows to about 1.5 m while the water below about 18 m stays clearer. Winter brings the clearest water.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **The wall.** Densely colored: plumose anemones dot the face, with big crimson anemones, sponges including large funnel sponges, and many candy stripe shrimp.
* **Dens.** Wolf eels, including large males out in the open, and decorated warbonnets.
* **Nudibranchs.** Stubby dendronotids on the rock.
* **Fish.** Black and copper rockfish over the wall and the shelves.
* **Shelves and kelp.** The rock steps around the island carry the attached invertebrates of current-swept rock, with feather stars among them, under a kelp bed.
* **Birds and mammals.** Black oystercatchers, pelagic cormorants, great blue herons and gulls use the island, and harbor seals haul out on it. Orcas pass in Haro Strait.

## Hazards

* **Current.** The island sits in the open stream of Haro Strait, and the channel carries strong rips outside slack. Dive at slack before a small exchange, and look at the water surface before entering to confirm the current has slowed.
* **Drift offshore on the ebb.** A late diver on the rising ebb is carried southwest, away from the beach and past the island's west side into open water.
* **Depth at the wall.** The wall drops from about 24 to 34 m below MLLW and the bottom keeps falling past 40 m, so depth and no-decompression time run out fast; watch both on the wall.
* **Refuge rules.** Low Island is a closed wildlife refuge. Never land on it, keep clear of hauled-out seals and nesting birds, and surface away from its rocks.
* **Boat traffic.** Small boats and kayaks launch from the park, and whale watching brings many more to this shore. Fly a dive flag and ascend along the bottom away from the channel's surface traffic.
* **Kelp.** The kelp bed around the island can entangle a diver at the surface. Ascend clear of it, and carry a cutting tool.
* **Wind swell.** Wind from southwest through west-southwest and from west-northwest through northwest crosses Haro Strait onto the beach and can spoil the entry and exit.
