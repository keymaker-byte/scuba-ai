# Twin Spit

A small public beach at the end of NE Twin Spits Road near Hansville, on the Kitsap shore just inside the entrance to Hood Canal, south of Foulweather Bluff. Eelgrass and silt start in the shallows and the bottom then drops steadily west-northwest into the canal, past 30 m within 200 m of the beach, with sea pens and sea whips on the deeper silt. The beach is sheltered from the strongest water off the bluff, but the slope still carries a tidal current, and a current running down the slope at depth is the headline hazard, along with boat traffic at the canal entrance.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Straight down from the road end onto the small public beach and into the water. The surface swim is short: the bottom reaches 5 m below MLLW about 60 m out and 14.0 m below MLLW at the dive area, 107 m out on a bearing of 302°.
* **Parking.** A few free spaces at the road end, limited, and the beach itself is the only staging area. Read the posted signs on arrival.
* **Access.** From State Route 104, Hansville Road runs north about 13 km toward Hansville; turn left onto NE Twin Spits Road and follow it west past the Foulweather Bluff Preserve trailhead to its end. The public access is a narrow strip between private properties on both sides, so keep gear and people on the access and the beach in front of it. Follow the posted signs for hours.
* **Facilities.** None.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach.** The fixed landmark on the shore. Note its look from the water before descending; the private shoreline on either side looks much the same.
* **The private dock.** A private dock beside the access stands over about 4.5 m below MLLW, with tubesnouts and sculpins gathered in the shallows under it.
* **The slope.** Eelgrass in the shallows, then featureless silt, falling evenly west-northwest: about 5 m at 60 m from the entry, 14 m at 107 m, 20 m at 140 m, 28 m at 180 m and 36 m at 255 m below MLLW. Downhill is out, uphill is home, and depth is position on the return.
* **Sea pen and sea whip zone.** From about 15 to 18 m below MLLW down the slope.

## Depth

* **Typical range.** Median daily 2.93 m, up to 4.60 m on the year's biggest exchange; 2026 span -1.18 m to +3.56 m.
* **Tidal swing.** The tide here swings the depth by nearly 3 m on a typical day, so the depth on the computer changes noticeably between a low and a high slack.

## Current

The site sits inside the Hood Canal entrance, out of the strongest water off Foulweather Bluff, and the current here is mild to moderate. Time the dive to slack, since the floods run past a comfortable speed on the slope.

* **Type.** Tidal.
* **Flood.** Sets south-southeast, 152°, into the canal, at about 0.3 m/s at its peak near the bottom and up to 0.6 m/s on the year's biggest floods, running much the same from the bottom to the surface.
* **Ebb.** Sets north-northwest, 332°, out of the canal. Near the bottom it peaks at about 0.2 m/s and rarely past 0.3 m/s; higher in the water column it runs faster, about 0.35 m/s at its peak and up to 0.6 m/s, so the surface swim and the ascent feel the ebb more than the slope does.
* **Diveable window.** Slack falls a median of about 45 minutes before high or low water at Foulweather Bluff, usually from 75 minutes before to 30 minutes after it. Near the bottom the ebb stays mostly under 0.25 m/s, and most floods close the window for one to four hours around their peak, the biggest for longest. High slack, with the gentle ebb to follow, is the easier slack to dive; a dive at low slack ends before the flood builds.
* **Downslope current.** Late in the slack and past it, a gentle current of about 0.1 to 0.15 m/s can set down the slope at depth and pull a diver deeper. It is felt on the deeper slope; turn up the slope early when it starts, and end the dive inside the window.

The beach faces north-northwest, with land closing it in within about 500 m from north-northwest through northeast, and open water across the canal entrance to the west and northwest.

* **Bad.** West-northwest through northwest (WNW-NW), onshore over 5.4 to 6.7 km of open water across the canal entrance, building chop straight onto the beach.
* **Short fetch.** West (W), onshore over 3.0 km, enough to chop up the entry in a strong blow.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through west-southwest (NNW-WSW): onshore over 410 to 520 m from north-northwest through north-northeast, inside the shelter of the land to the north; cross-shore over short fetch from northeast and east-northeast and from southwest and west-southwest; and offshore from east through south-southwest, off the land behind the beach.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Low through the spring and summer plankton blooms, often under 6 m on the slope, despite the exchange at the canal entrance.
* **Shallows.** The shallows can read worse than the slope on an incoming tide.
* **Silt.** The bottom is fine silt, and fins on it erase the visibility for anyone following.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C through the year. Drysuit year round.

## Marine life

* **Sea pens.** Orange sea pens in a field on the deeper silt, with white sea pens scattered among them, both grazed by the striped nudibranch, Armina californica, which is common here.
* **Sea whips.** Occasional sea whips from about 15 to 18 m below MLLW down the slope.
* **Nudibranchs.** Dendronotus iris on the silt.
* **Sea stars.** Sunflower stars on the slope.
* **Silt dwellers.** Crabs and flatfish across the silt.
* **Under the dock.** Tubesnouts and sculpins around the pilings in the shallows.
* **Water column.** Jellies drifting through on the exchange in spring, among them water jellies, sea gooseberries, lobed sea gooseberries and aggregating jellies.

## Hazards

* **Downslope current.** Late in the slack and past it, a gentle current can set down the slope at depth and pull a diver deeper. Plan the dive inside the window and begin the ascent up the slope early.
* **Boat traffic.** The site sits on the boat route into Hood Canal, with private docks along the shore. Fly a dive flag, listen for engines, and ascend along the slope, close to the bottom.
* **Depth creep.** The slope falls steadily past 30 m with nothing to mark progress; the depth gauge turns the dive.
* **Private property.** The shore on both sides of the access is private, the dock included; stay on the public beach.
