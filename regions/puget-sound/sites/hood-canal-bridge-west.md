# Hood Canal Bridge West Side

The west end of the Hood Canal Bridge meets the Olympic Peninsula shore at Shine Tidelands State Park, at the entrance to Hood Canal. From the park beach a steep sand and shell slope drops into the boating channel beside the bridge. Across it, a pontoon that sank in the 1979 storm lies beside the working bridge support, crusted with anemones and patrolled by big lingcod. The site is a complex jumble of concrete, cable and tangled bridgework in deep, current-swept water. Current, boat traffic in the channel and entanglement are the headline hazards, and signs on the bridge supports prohibit scuba diving below the bridge.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Enter from the park beach north of the bridge and swim south along the shore to the dive area, about 220 m on a bearing of 176°. The bridge is also reached by boat, which removes the shore entry and the channel crossing.
* **Parking.** The Shine Tidelands State Park lot at the end of Shine Tidelands State Park Road, reached heading west across the bridge by turning right onto Paradise Bay Road and immediately right again. A Discover Pass is required.
* **Access.** Shine Tidelands State Park is a day use park, open 8:00 to dusk year round. Signs posted on the bridge supports prohibit scuba diving below the bridge, which takes in the structure this dive visits.
* **Facilities.** A small parking lot, and a boat ramp at the foot of the bridge. No restrooms.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The slope.** The bottom is sand and shell hash, dropping steeply off the beach to about 10 m within 130 m of the entry, then sloping more gently down into the boating channel.
* **The channel crossing.** From the shore by the foot of the bridge, take a compass bearing on the large bridge support across the boating channel, then descend and cross along the bottom. In poor visibility the support is easy to miss, and the bottom between them is featureless sand and shell.
* **The sunken pontoon.** A concrete pontoon of the original bridge, sunk in the February 1979 storm, lies beside the working bridge support. Its sides are covered in life, and twisted metal bridgework lies tangled near its top.
* **The working support.** The in-use bridge structure stands beside the pontoon, with traffic rumbling overhead audible even at depth.
* **The return.** The return to shore runs on the reverse compass bearing. Descending back to the bottom and following it home keeps the diver in the weaker current near the seabed and away from boat traffic at the surface.

## Current

All of Hood Canal fills and drains through the channel beneath the bridge.

* **Flood.** Sets southwest along the channel into Hood Canal, with daily peaks of 0.28 to 0.73 m/s at the station over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets northeast along the channel out of the canal, with daily peaks of 0.34 to 0.76 m/s at the station over the same 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Under 0.25 m/s at the station, slack before ebb typically lasts about 2 to 4 hours and slack before flood about 1 to 3 hours; a large exchange narrows both. Choose a day with a long, flat span of slack, and keep enough gas in reserve to cross the channel back to shore if the current picks up early.
* **Surface and depth differ.** The surface water can turn before the water at depth, ebbing while the bottom is still at slack or in a weak flood. The current near the seabed runs weaker than at the surface, which makes the bottom the safer route back to shore.
* **Bare slope.** The slope between the shore and the structure is bare sand, so a diver caught by the current on the return swims against it the whole way back.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southeast, 127° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southeast, 146° |

The swim out runs 50° off the entry shore's facing, along the shore toward the bridge.

* **Short fetch.** East-northeast through south (ENE-S), onshore over 2.2 to 4.7 km across the canal entrance, enough to raise a chop on the beach.
* **Mixed.** Northeast (NE), along the shore over 5.3 km, and south-southwest through southwest (SSW-SW), along the shore over 7.0 to 12.0 km down the length of the canal, each running a wind swell past the entry.
* **Fine.** West-southwest clockwise through north-northeast (WSW-NNE): offshore from west-southwest through north, with the Olympic Peninsula shore within 600 m upwind from west through north, and cross-shore over 1.9 km from north-northeast.
* **Wind against current.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW) blows against the flood, over 4.1 to 12.0 km, worst from the southwest along the length of the canal. North-northeast through east (NNE-E) blows against the ebb, over 1.9 to 5.3 km.

## Depth and tide

The tide swings over 3 m on a typical day here: the 18.7 m dive area reads about 17.5 m at the year's lowest low and about 22.3 m at its highest high.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** Usually 1.5 to 4.5 m, often snowy with suspended particles, and under 1.5 m at its worst.
* **Current.** The current stirs the bottom, so visibility runs best near slack.
* **Season.** Plankton blooms cut visibility hardest in spring and summer.
* **Light.** Ambient light fades quickly at depth under the bridge; bring a light.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Warms to about 12 to 14 °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **The structure.** Large plumose anemones crowd the sunken pontoon and the bridge supports, with several nudibranch species scattered over the concrete. Mussels, clams, barnacles, anemones and small woody kelp crust the shallower structure, with iridescent polychaete worms in places.
* **Fish.** Big lingcod hold around the pontoon and the supports.
* **Bottom.** Sea stars and abundant crabs, some of them large, on the sand and shell slope.
* **Shallows.** Perch and sole in the eelgrass near shore.

## Hazards

* **Current.** All of Hood Canal's exchange runs through this channel. The site can be an easy dive at slack and go wrong quickly when the current picks up; plan gas for the return crossing and carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Posted prohibition.** Signs on the bridge supports prohibit scuba diving below the bridge.
* **Boat traffic.** Boats run fast through the channel under the bridge and launch from the ramp at the foot of the bridge. Never surface in the channel; ascend only near shore or in a dire emergency, with a surface marker buoy deployed.
* **Entanglement and entrapment.** Steel cables, monofilament, sharp metal and tangled bridgework surround the sunken pontoon, with overhead structure in places. Carry a cutting tool and keep clear of the wreckage's undersides.
* **Navigation.** The bridge support across the channel is easy to miss in poor visibility, and the return runs on a compass bearing across featureless bottom.
* **Submarine transits.** Escorted Navy submarines pass through the bridge's draw span with security vessels.
