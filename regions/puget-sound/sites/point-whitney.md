# Point Whitney

Point Whitney is a shore dive on Hood Canal at the Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife shellfish laboratory near Brinnon, built around an old lab discharge pipe that runs straight out from the beach. The pipe and the concrete blocks and dividers along it are the whole dive for most divers, riddled with giant Pacific octopus dens, and a rare sea whip field continues on the slope beyond the pipe's end, well past recreational limits. Current is negligible at every stage of the tide, so unlike most of the region this is not a slack dive; the entry is easy and the navigation is straight out and straight back, making it a popular beginner and training site as well as a repeat destination for the octopus alone.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Through the gap in the perimeter fence on the lab's beach. Go in on the east side of the pipe, since the pipe itself is buried under algae and sand for its first stretch and easy to miss at the surface; it becomes distinct by about 5.5 m, where turning onto it picks up the route straight out.
* **Parking.** A gravel lot at the lab, close enough to rig at the water's edge. A Discover Pass or WDFW Vehicle Access Pass is required, as at any Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife water access site. No overnight parking or camping.
* **Access.** Open year round. From Highway 101 at Brinnon, head north about 4.8 km, turn right onto Bee Mill Road at the Cove RV Park and Grocery, and continue about 3.2 km to the lab at 1000 Point Whitney Road.
* **Facilities.** A single restroom (flush toilets, no ADA fixtures), open 07:00 to 19:00, plus a picnic table and a concrete boat ramp that silts in enough to want four-wheel drive for launching a boat.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pipe.** The site's one navigational spine, an old lab discharge pipe running straight out from the fence gap. Buried under sand and algae for its first few metres, more distinct by about 5.5 m, then a long shallow stretch before it steepens toward its end.
* **Concrete dividers and blocks.** Flank the pipe from around 9 to 10 m on, the densest stretch of octopus and warbonnet dens on the site.
* **Milk crates.** A cluster of blue plastic milk crates sits off to the side of the pipe, a minor secondary landmark rather than a destination.
* **The pipe's end.** Steepens toward 26 to 30 m, with more den structure right around there, including the deepest of the octopus and warbonnet holes.
* **Sea whip field.** Beyond the pipe's end, from about 30 m, a dense sea whip field runs along the slope. It is well past recreational no-decompression limits and out of scope for a plan built to this workspace's rules; treat the pipe's end as the actual turn point.
* **Getting home.** The pipe holds one heading the whole way out, so following it back is the whole return. There is no second landmark to navigate off, so keep a hand or a light on the pipe through any hazy stretch rather than free-swimming over it.

## Current

Current here is negligible at every stage of the tide, at the surface and depth-averaged alike, which is why the site is dived on any tide rather than timed to a slack.

* **Flood.** Sets NNW into Dabob Bay, negligible in strength.
* **Ebb.** Sets SSE out of the bay toward the main canal, similarly negligible.
* **Slack.** This corner of the canal behaves like the rest of Hood Canal, as a standing wave, so slack falls close to high and low water rather than midway between them. With the whole day already under about 0.25 knots, the distinction rarely matters here.
* **Diveable window.** All day, any tide stage. Current is not the limiting factor at this site; access, visibility and boat traffic are.

## Depth and tide

Hood Canal's tide swings more here than in the main basin of the Sound, and at Point Whitney it moves the pipe and its blocks a long way vertically. A high tide floods the shallow run several metres deeper than the datum figure; a low tide can leave the top of the pipe close to the surface.

## Hazards

* **Depth creep beyond the pipe.** Past the pipe's end the bottom keeps dropping into the sea whip field, 30 m and deeper, well past recreational no-decompression limits; the dives out there are routinely run on closed-circuit rebreathers with staged decompression. Treat the pipe's end as the turn point on open-circuit air or nitrox, not a waypoint to a further destination.
* **Boat traffic.** A working boat ramp sits right at the site, busy with fishing and crabbing traffic year round, and one of the main put-ins for the Hood Canal (Marine Area 12) spot shrimp fishery on its handful of open days each spring. Fly a dive flag and expect boats overhead near the ramp.
* **Losing the pipe in a muck layer.** A band of algae, sediment or bloom settles over the pipe in the shallows on some days, thick enough to make it hard to follow by feel alone; losing contact with it means surfacing to relocate by the buoy marking its end rather than searching blind. Carry a light and keep a hand on the pipe through any hazy stretch.
* **Cold.** Water at depth stays cold year round regardless of how warm the shallows get in summer.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The beach faces north, out across the mouth of Dabob Bay into Hood Canal.
* **Bad.** North through northeast, the open fetch up the canal and across the bay mouth.
* **Fine.** South, offshore and blocked by the high ground inland toward Brinnon.
* **Wind against current.** Not a factor here; the current is too weak to stack against any wind. Read the beach forecast rather than the marine one.

## Visibility

* **Character.** Highly variable day to day rather than a clean seasonal pattern. A haze or bloom layer above roughly 9 m is the norm on most days, and the water below it often opens up considerably, sometimes dramatically, but not always; a handful of days a year the haze runs the whole water column and the dive is called early.
* **Season.** Best on average from about October through January, when clear water can reach close to the surface. Spring and summer bring blooms, including a milky coccolithophore bloom and periodic red tide, that can hold the shallows to a metre or less for weeks at a stretch, even on a day the deep water opens up.
* **Tide.** An incoming or high tide tends to bring clearer water over the shallow run; a low tide, especially after a spell of blooms, tends to leave the shallows their murkiest.
* **Torch.** Carry one regardless of the shallow haze; the pipe's structure and the sea whip field beyond are dim even in clear water.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 6 to 11 °C through the year, coldest in winter and only a little warmer by late summer at the pipe's working depths. Standard drysuit protocol.
* **Surface layer.** Warms sharply in summer, into the high teens Celsius by July over a thermocline that can be abrupt and, on some days, stacked in more than one layer on the way down.

## Marine life

* **Octopus.** The site's headline attraction. Giant Pacific octopus den under the pipe's concrete dividers and supports along its whole length, in enough numbers on a good day to lose count; occasional red octopus as well.
* **Pipe and blocks.** Large moss-headed warbonnets den near the pipe's deeper supports alongside the octopus, with giant and short plumose anemones thick on the structure itself.
* **Fish.** Copper and brown rockfish, lingcod, painted and kelp greenling, pile, striped and shiner perch, buffalo and grunt sculpins, gunnels, sole and sanddab on the sand, blackeye gobies at their holes, and tubesnout in schools; a six-gill shark has turned up at depth.
* **Sea stars.** Sunflower sea stars are a near constant on any dive here, from small juveniles to large adults, alongside giant pink, mottled and leather stars.
* **Nudibranchs and slugs.** A strong slug site: Nanaimo dorid, northern leopard dorid, noble sea lemon, white-lined and golden dirona, Hermissenda, Tritonia and several unidentified aeolids turn up regularly.
* **Crabs and shrimp.** Red rock, Dungeness, kelp and decorator crabs, hermit crabs on the sand, and spot shrimp.
* **Jellies.** Fried egg jellies, lion's mane, tailed jellies and comb jellies drift through, with several smaller unidentified species seasonally abundant.
* **Sea whip field.** Beyond the pipe's end, a dense stand of sea whips on the slope, among the healthiest and most extensive at any shore-accessible site in the region, though at a depth beyond recreational reach.
