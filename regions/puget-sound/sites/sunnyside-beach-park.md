# Sunnyside Beach Park

Shore dive at Sunnyside Beach Park in Steilacoom, on the South Puget Sound shore southwest of the Tacoma Narrows. A shallow sand and eelgrass flat drops down a short, steep slope onto a gently sloping sand bottom. Guide lines there lead to an abandoned metal pipeline that runs offshore past 28 m, and to a group of small sunken boats to the north. The site lies out of the main Narrows stream and runs little current, and its easy entry and open bottom make it one of the South Sound's main training and macro sites. Its headline hazards are the depth along the pipeline, which keeps deepening well past the point most divers turn, and boat traffic over the shallows in summer.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the north end of the parking lot, walk down the paved path, cross the railroad tracks, and walk south along the park to the Wind Pavilion and outdoor shower at the south end of the beach. Enter in front of the pavilion over sand and eelgrass. The water stays shallow for about 70 m, then the bottom drops down a short, steep slope; descend once over it. At low tide the flat in front of the beach is shallow enough to wade a long way out, and fine sediment there clouds easily.
* **Parking.** The park's lot at 2509 Chambers Creek Road, one way in and one way out with angled stalls. A daily fee is charged to non-residents, paid at the pay station by cash or card. The lot fills by mid-morning on warm weekends. Vehicles are not allowed across the railroad tracks, and the walk from the lot to the entry is long enough that a gear cart helps.
* **Access.** From I-5, take Exit 119 (Steilacoom-DuPont) and follow the DuPont-Steilacoom Highway into Steilacoom, then turn right onto Lafayette Street one block before the ferry dock. Lafayette Street becomes Chambers Creek Road, and the lot is on the left. The Town of Steilacoom runs the park, open from half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset. Alcohol is prohibited in the outdoor areas.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms, a cold outdoor shower beside the Wind Pavilion at the entry for rinsing gear (turned off in winter), drinking water, picnic tables and shelters, barbecue grills, a playground and volleyball courts.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The flat.** Sand and eelgrass from the beach out to about 2 m below MLLW, about 70 m out from the pavilion. The eelgrass and the algae on it are where the smallest animals live.
* **The slope.** A short, steep sand slope drops from about 2 m to about 7.5 m below MLLW over about 30 m, then the bottom flattens again.
* **The shore-parallel line.** From the pavilion, swim out and down on a heading of 280°. A guide line runs along the foot of the slope parallel to the shore. Follow it to the right (north) to the line that leads out to the pipeline.
* **The junction box.** A concrete block at the head of the pipeline, about 10.5 m below MLLW. The guide line out from the shore-parallel line ends here.
* **The pipeline.** An abandoned metal outfall pipe about 0.6 m across, running offshore from the junction box and deepening steadily past 28 m below MLLW. It is far too long to swim to the end of, and its undersides hold octopus dens.
* **The sunken boats.** A small boat with life-size Santa and Frosty figures in the stern lies about 6 m northwest of the junction box on a heading of 310°, about 10.5 m below MLLW. From it, a heading of 30° leads to a guide line running north; follow it to its end and continue about 6 m north to the collapsed remains of a second boat, its bow about 11.5 m below MLLW. More small boats lie further north and northwest along the lines.
* **The southern line.** A line running south from the pipeline area ends at a small tire reef. A diver who drops onto it and keeps going south misses the pipeline entirely; the pipeline lies north of it.
* **From the north end of the beach.** Lining up with the northernmost volleyball net and swimming out on 270° meets a guide line running north and south; follow it south to the pipeline.
* **The way back.** Follow the pipeline and its guide line back to the junction box, then the shore-parallel line south and back up the slope on a heading of about 100° to the pavilion. Coming back close to the bottom keeps a diver clear of boats over the flat.

* **The site's own water.** The beach sits out of the main stream between the Narrows and Nisqually Reach, and the water over the slope and the pipeline moves slowly. Along this shore it sets parallel to the beach, northeast and southwest. On most tides it is close to still, and on a large exchange it builds to a mild, noticeable current around the station's maximum.
* **Ebb.** Inshore, the ebb runs opposite to the channel: while the main stream sets north toward the Narrows, the water along the beach sets south. At depth on an ebb it can also set gently offshore. Daily peaks run 0.11 to 0.28 m/s at the station over 30 days.
* **Flood.** Early in the flood the inshore water keeps setting south, then it turns and runs north along the beach for the rest of the flood. The inshore current can switch direction more than once during a dive. Daily peaks run 0.09 to 0.24 m/s at the station over the same 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Most tides. Current at the station stays under 0.25 m/s for about 1.5 to 21 hours at a time. On a big exchange, dive close to the station's slack for the stillest water and the best visibility; the bottom silts up as the flood builds toward its maximum.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 300° |

The beach faces west-northwest across the South Sound toward Fox Island and Ketron Island. Chop stirs up the shallow flat and cuts the visibility at the entry.

* **Bad.** Southwest through north (SW-N), onshore over 5.2 to 17.3 km from west through north, the strong northerlies coming down out of the Narrows. Southwesterlies come up Nisqually Reach over a long fetch and push chop onto the beach.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest (NNE-SSW), offshore off the bluff behind the park, or cross-shore over 1.6 km or less.
* **Wind against current.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W) blows against the ebb, and north-northeast (NNE) against the flood. The inshore current is weak and raises no chop of its own; the concern is offshore in the main stream.

## Depth and tide

South Sound tides are large, and the tide swings the depth here by up to about 6 m. At a big minus tide the flat in front of the beach is nearly dry and the slope begins in barely a metre of water.

## Visibility

* **Character.** Usually 3 to 6 m, often less in the shallows. Best from autumn through early spring, with stretches of 9 m or more, and worst in the spring and summer plankton blooms, when a dark algae layer can sit over the deeper water. Low tide and wind chop cloud the flat, and visibility tends to drop as a big flood builds.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 8 to 9 C from winter into early spring, rising to about 13 C in midsummer. Drysuit year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warmer than depth over the shallow flat from spring through summer.

## Marine life

* **On the pipeline and junction box.** Giant Pacific octopus in dens under the pipe, white and orange plumose anemones, encrusting sponges, tube worms and feather duster worms, and large California sea cucumbers. Red rock, Dungeness, helmet and decorator crabs sit on top of the pipe. Copper, quillback and brown rockfish, cabezon, buffalo, grunt, sailfin and great sculpins, lingcod, whitespotted greenlings and gunnels shelter along it, with pile, striped and kelp perch alongside and schools of tubesnouts over it.
* **On the boats and lines.** Giant Pacific octopus under the northern wrecks, decorator crabs and skeleton shrimp on the lines, small nudibranchs including Ancula on the ropes, and clusters of market squid eggs tied to the lines in winter and spring.
* **On the sand.** Orange sea pens, often with striped nudibranchs grazing on them, flounder, sole and sanddabs, moon snails, geoduck clams, hermit crabs, graceful crabs, sea stars including sunflower, leather, pink, rose, morning sun and Stimpson's sun stars, snake pricklebacks in summer, sturgeon poachers, and the occasional skate or dogfish.
* **In the eelgrass.** The shallows are the site's macro highlight: Pacific spiny lumpsuckers, including tiny juveniles on the red and brown algae from late winter into spring, juvenile snailfish, Moss Landing aeolids, spotted aglajas, winged sea slugs and their egg masses, hooded nudibranchs, bay pipefish and crescent gunnels. The bed has thinned in recent years.
* **Nudibranchs throughout.** Striped (Armina), Tritonia festiva, diamondback, frosted, white-lined and golden dironas, barnacle-eating dorids and opalescent nudibranchs.
* **In the water column.** Moon, egg yolk, red sausage and water jellies, comb jellies, and opalescent squid mating in summer.

## Hazards

* **Depth along the pipeline.** The pipeline keeps deepening steadily past 28 m below MLLW, and at high water it passes 30 m on the gauge. A diver following it out on an easy gradient can run deep without noticing. Set the turn depth before the dive and watch the computer.
* **Boat traffic.** Small boats run close to shore in summer, and the Steilacoom ferry dock is nearby. Fly a dive flag, listen for engines, and come back along the bottom.
* **Railroad tracks.** An active rail line runs between the parking lot and the beach. Cross carefully with gear. The path down can be slippery in freezing weather.
* **Silt.** The fine sediment on the flat and around the boats clouds easily, worst at low tide and in wind chop. Keep off the bottom near the wrecks.
* **Jellies.** Large egg yolk jellies with long tentacles drift through in summer. Check each other for tentacles before taking off masks and gloves.
