# Fort Ward Park

Shore dive on the southwest shore of Bainbridge Island, on Rich Passage between Bainbridge and the Kitsap Peninsula, a stretch of water that carries most of the tidal exchange in and out of Sinclair Inlet and the Bremerton Navy Yard. A gently sloping sand and cobble bottom, scattered with boulders that give sea anemones, sponges and kelp something to hold onto, runs out from the beach past the site's usual working depth before continuing to drop well beyond it. The old submarine net anchors that once closed the passage during the Second World War still sit in the shallower part of that slope, now a reef of their own, and a resident giant Pacific octopus or a mated pair of wolf eels turn up in them often enough to be the site's main draw. Ferry and boat traffic through the passage overhead is the site's headline hazard.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down the beach a short distance from the boat ramp, an easy gravel and sand approach below a low bank. Wade or snorkel out on a bearing toward the dive site before descending; the slope drops steadily the whole way.
* **Parking.** A free lot at the day use area and boat ramp, room for about 25 cars and boat trailers; it shares the lot with boaters launching.
* **Access.** Bainbridge Island Metro Park and Recreation District land, transferred from Washington State Parks in 2011. Day use is free year round, 8 a.m. to dusk, no pass required. 2241 Pleasant Beach Drive NE, Bainbridge Island.
* **Facilities.** Picnic tables and grills, a beachside fire ring, vault toilets open year round, walking trails through the old fort grounds, bird blinds, and the two lane boat ramp. Poison oak grows in the undergrowth along the trails and beach access.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Descent.** No reef or buoy to follow down; head out from the beach on a bearing toward the dive site and let the sand slope take you down.
* **The slope.** Sand and cobble, gently graded, with scattered boulders and rock the further out you go. Most of the life is worked in the upper part of this slope, roughly the top 18 m.
* **The Cribs.** Old submarine net anchors from the fort's WWII defenses, a jumble of metal now grown over with plumose anemones, sit within this same depth band off the park's shore. Worth searching out for the octopus and wolf eel den sites in and around the structure.

* **Flood.** Sets northwest, parallel to the shore, up the passage toward Sinclair Inlet.
* **Ebb.** Sets southeast, parallel to the shore, back out toward the main basin past Rockaway Beach and Alki Point. Peak speeds at the station commonly run in the 0.4 to 0.7 m/s range each cycle, a real current to plan around rather than the negligible flow older references describe.
* **Diveable window.** Varies by exchange, from a little over an hour on a big exchange to several hours on a small one. Always check the window for the specific day rather than assuming it is wide.

## Hazards

* **Ferry and boat traffic.** The Seattle to Bremerton passenger ferry transits the length of Rich Passage, alongside recreational, fishing and naval vessel traffic. Fly a marker buoy and listen for engine noise before ascending.
* **Current.** Real, not negligible; a genuine slack tide dive rather than a forgiving one. Time entry and exit to the window, and expect it to build through the dive away from slack.
* **Depth creep.** The sand and cobble keeps dropping well past the site's usual 18 m working depth. Watch depth on the way out past the Cribs.
* **Poison oak.** Present in the undergrowth along the beach access trails.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The beach faces roughly west-southwest across Rich Passage toward the Kitsap Peninsula shore, with the passage itself running northwest to Sinclair Inlet and Bremerton and southeast out to the main basin past Rockaway Beach and Alki Point.
* **Bad.** Southwest through northwest, which blows either straight onshore or down the length of the passage from Sinclair Inlet; either way it builds meaningful chop here.
* **Fine.** Northeast through east, blocked by Bainbridge Island's own landmass behind the beach.
* **Wind against current.** A west to northwest wind opposing the flood's northwest set is the roughest combination. The ebb's southeast set runs with an easterly wind and stays calmer even when the passage is choppy elsewhere.

## Visibility

* **Seasonal.** Follows the same broad pattern as the rest of the Sound: worst through spring and summer plankton blooms, better outside bloom season.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11°C through the year, in line with the wider central Sound. Standard drysuit protocol year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Sand and cobble.** Tube-dwelling anemones, selective feeders that will accept or reject a small offered animal through an inner ring of tentacles, sea pens, flounder, hermit crabs, kelp crabs, moon snails, sea cucumbers, gumboot chitons, nudibranchs, ratfish, and shrimp.
* **The Cribs and scattered rock.** Plumose anemones covering the old net anchors in orange and white, yellow sponges, sea squirts, rockfish, lingcod, kelp greenlings and perch. A resident giant Pacific octopus is close to a sure sighting here, and a mated pair of wolf eels den in the structure.
* **Algae.** Sea lettuce, brown algae and bull kelp attached to the scattered rock and boulders across the sand.
