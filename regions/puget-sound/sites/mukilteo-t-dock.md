# Mukilteo T-Dock

Shore dive on the Mukilteo waterfront, on the south shore of Possession Sound at its mouth. It is sheltered and, for a shore dive, unusually deep, with a bottom that drops away fast and steeply from the beach. Its one hard hazard is the Mukilteo to Clinton ferry lane, which since the terminal moved in December 2020 now lies east of the entry.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the beach on Front Street, next to the Silver Cloud Inn. Concrete stairs run down from the parking lot to the beach, beside a sign bearing a scuba diver logo. No boat needed.
* **Parking.** Paid parking in the lot beside the Silver Cloud Inn, the same lot the entry stairs come off. It is a popular training site for local dive shops, so the lot is often crowded and can be tight on weekends.
* **Facilities.** No restroom on site. Portable toilets are in the ferry terminal parking area nearby, for emergency use; the nearest proper restroom is at the ferry terminal.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Depth is the compass.** The bottom drops away fast and steeply, so depth alone tells you where the beach is: shallow is shoreward, and you can always find your way in by going up. No compass is needed, in any visibility.
* **Guidelines.** The site is strung with lines and artificial markers, running both parallel to the beach along the contour and straight down the slope. A line runs from the beach out to the geodome and back, and it is the spine of the dive.
* **Road sign.** A large road sign stands upright on the bottom straight out from the entry, its lettering no longer legible, a marker on the way out toward the geodome.
* **The geodome.** A geodesic dome about 6 m across, built of PVC, decades old and heavily encrusted with jingle shells. Its top sits about 15.4 m below MLLW datum, reached on a straight descent a few minutes off the beach. Copper rockfish shelter inside it, around a statue of an angel set at its centre.
* **Ferry terminal.** 47.9503, -122.2964, about 470 m east of the entry on a bearing of 080°. Opened December 2020.

* **Flood.** Sets north-northeast at the station, with daily peaks of 0.17 to 0.40 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets south-southwest at the station, with daily peaks of 0.09 to 0.28 m/s over the same 30 days.
* **Deep layer.** The deep water goes properly slack on the tidal cycle and can be planned from the current station.
* **Shallow layer.** Along this shore the shallow water shows a persistent eastward drift that does not follow the tide. Whether it ever reverses is not established, so treat the shallows as potentially always setting east.
* **Diveable window.** Current at the station stays under 0.25 m/s for about 1.5 to 15 hours at a time; the shallow eastward drift runs regardless.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | north-northwest, 347° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | north-northwest, 347° |

The beach faces north to northwest, into Possession Sound.

* **Bad.** Northwest through northeast (NW-NE), onshore over 6.2 to 12.7 km, with open fetch up the Sound onto the entry.
* **Short fetch.** West-northwest (WNW), onshore over 3.8 km.
* **Mixed.** East-northeast (ENE), over 5.8 km, and west-southwest (WSW), over 5.2 km, cross-shore.
* **Fine.** East through southwest (E-SW): offshore from east-southeast through southwest, where the beach stays flat regardless of speed, and cross-shore over 460 m from the east; and west (W), cross-shore over 3.7 km.
* **Wind against current.** North through east-northeast (N-ENE), over 5.8 to 10.3 km, against the flood is the combination to avoid. West-southwest (WSW), over 5.2 km, opposes the ebb.

## Depth and tide

Possession Sound swings up to about 5 m between high and low water here, so depths are relative to the tide and given against the MLLW chart datum.

|---|---|
| **Tide station** | NOAA Glendale, Whidbey Island (9447814), about 4.2 km west-southwest across Possession Sound |
| **Series** | High and low water only |
| **Typical range** | Median daily 3.32 m, up to 4.94 m on the year's biggest exchange; 2026 span -1.30 m to +3.85 m |

The geodome top, at about 15.4 m below datum, reads a metre or so shallower at a low near -1 m, and several metres deeper at a high near +3.5 m.

## Visibility

* **Best on the flood.** The flood brings cleaner water in from Admiralty Inlet, so an early-flood dive is usually the clearest the site offers on a given day.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 14 °C through the year, and around 12 °C in July. Standard drysuit protocol.
* **Surface layer.** In summer the surface runs much warmer than the dive, up to about 17 °C over 12 °C at depth. Dress for the depth and expect to be warm on the walk in.

## Marine life

* **Character.** Typical rich cold-water Salish Sea reef life, concentrated on the geodome, pilings, lines and scattered debris. Hard structure set into an otherwise soft, sloping bottom is what packs the life in, so the animals cluster where the man-made cover is rather than spreading evenly across the site.
* **Octopus.** Both local octopus occur. The giant Pacific octopus, the largest octopus species in the world, ranges throughout Puget Sound and dens in structure of exactly this kind, and larger animals are found back in the pipe and debris at depth. The smaller Pacific red octopus, active and inquisitive, is the one that turns up out on the rocks, once seen among a cluster of red sea cucumbers. The site has a reputation for octopus after dark, when they come out to hunt in the open.
* **Wolf eels.** A classic Mukilteo animal and one of the draws. Wolf eels are resident in the rock and piling structure, where mated pairs hold a den in a crevice for years, and juveniles are seen regularly, including around the geodome and the banded pipes just north of it.
* **Fish.** The usual Sound reef fish: copper, quillback and brown rockfish, some of them decades old, long-lived and slow to replace. Lingcod and cabezon lie camouflaged on the structure and guard nests in late winter and spring. Kelp and painted greenlings, and many sculpins including the small, characterful grunt sculpin, work the bottom, with ratfish cruising the deeper soft ground.
* **Nudibranchs.** The Salish Sea is a world centre of nudibranch diversity, and the site shows it. Hooded nudibranchs ride the large red-bladed kelp on the west side of the ferry in season, and other species, among them a white-and-orange-tipped one, are found across the structure.
* **Invertebrates.** Giant plumose anemones and other anemones on the pilings and hard structure, red sea cucumbers on the rocks, rough patch shrimp, and Dungeness and red rock crab. Comb jellies such as the lobed sea gooseberry drift through the water column.
* **West of the ferry.** The rocky ground opposite the ferry lane carries large red-bladed kelp and is the most productive patch, where the nudibranchs, kelp and octopus concentrate.

## Hazards

* **Ferry lane.** The Mukilteo to Clinton ferry lane lies immediately east, and ferries run constantly.
* **Pilings and fishing line.** Around the dock. Carry a knife.
* **Boat traffic.** Working ferry and small-boat water. Fly a marker buoy.
