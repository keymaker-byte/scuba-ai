# Spencer Spit State Park (Frost Island)

Rock wall along the west side of Frost Island, reached from the tip of Spencer Spit on the northeast shore of Lopez Island across a narrow channel that separates the spit from the island. A long walk out the sand spit with all the gear leads to an entry at the point, a descent down a sandy slope across the channel, and a colorful wall of ledges and cracks crowded with sea cucumbers, tunicates, sea stars and small fish, with less silt on the stretch north of the spit. Tidal current through the channel and small boats using it as a shortcut are the headline hazards.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk or swim to the sandy point at the tip of the spit and enter there. The point faces north, with Frost Island across the channel to the east-northeast. A replica log cabin stands on the outer part of the spit, and the water can also be entered on either side of the spit beside it, with the rest of the way to the point covered by wading or a surface swim.
* **Parking.** The base of the spit, where the access road meets the beach, lies about 560 m from the tip, the length of the walk out with the gear. A Discover Pass is required for parking in Washington State Parks ($10 for a day, $45 for a year). The steep gravel road from the ranger station down to the beach is restricted to load and unload use; ask at the ranger station for permission to drive gear down, unload at the bottom, and return the car to the parking area above the spit. The park keeps carts that make the walk out the spit manageable, and a wagon of your own serves as well.
* **Access.** The park is open 8 a.m. to dusk year round, at 521 A Bakerview Road. From the Lopez ferry landing drive south 3.2 km, turn left onto Center Road for 1.1 km, left onto Cross Road for 0.6 km, right onto Port Stanley Road for 0.5 km, then left onto Bakerview Road for the last 0.8 km to the park gate. Past the second gate, the first road on the right leads to the ranger station and the access road down to the beach. Frost Island is private land with no public access; stay off its shore and keep every surface stop in the water.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms at the upper parking area, a picnic shelter and picnic tables at the base of the spit, a picnic table about a third of the way out the spit and another beside the cabin, a composting toilet near the beach campsites, and park mooring buoys on both sides of the spit.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The channel.** From the point, descend and follow the sandy bottom down the slope and across the channel toward Frost Island, about 210 m east-northeast to the base of the rock. The channel floor runs about 23 m below MLLW at its deepest on this crossing. Crossing on the bottom keeps the swim clear of the boats that run through the channel overhead.
* **The far side.** The bottom rises as a sandy slope on the Frost Island side, and dark shapes appear ahead as the slope reaches the rock base of the island.
* **The wall.** Rock ledges and short walls run in both directions along the base of Frost Island. The stretch north of the spit carries less sediment and more life. The dive coordinate sits at the foot of this wall about 150 m north of the crossing, 17.2 m below MLLW, and the foot of the wall runs 20 to 23 m below MLLW for 100 to 150 m further north.
* **The return.** Turn back south along the wall to the crossing, then recross the channel on the bottom to the point. On the slack before ebb, the building ebb sets southeast and carries the return; on the slack before flood, the building flood sets northwest, away from the crossing, so turn early.

## Current

The channel between the spit and Frost Island carries the exchange between Lopez Sound and the water north of Lopez Island, and sets northwest on the flood and southeast on the ebb.

* **Flood.** Sets west-northwest through the channel and past the north end of the wall. Daily peaks at the station run 0.11 to 0.66 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets east-southeast through the channel, from the north end of the wall back toward the crossing. Daily peaks at the station run 0.11 to 1.44 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 85 to 175 minutes and shrinks to under an hour ahead of the strongest floods. The slack before ebb typically runs 2.8 to 7.8 hours, because the weaker flood often stays near the threshold for hours, and shrinks to under an hour ahead of the strongest ebbs.
* **In the channel.** Current runs strongly through the narrow channel on both tides, hardest on the ebb, and scours its floor. The slack before ebb gives a return with the current.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | north, 4° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northwest, 312° |

The entry is the point of the spit, with water on three sides; the dive area facing is the Frost Island wall's own, across the channel.

* **Bad.** North (N), onshore over 9.4 km of open water up the channel and beyond it, building chop on the point and across the crossing.
* **Short fetch.** North-northeast (NNE), onshore over 3.8 km, and northwest (NW), onshore over 2.2 km, raising chop on the point.
* **Fine.** Northeast clockwise through west-northwest (NE-WNW), blocked by Frost Island across the channel and by Lopez Island and the spit itself, and north-northwest (NNW), with 740 m of fetch.
* **Wind against current.** Northwest (NW), over 2.2 km, blows against the west-northwest flood set.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Best in winter and early spring before the bloom. Spring and summer plankton blooms bring it down to 3 to 4 m.
* **Silt.** The bottom along the base of Frost Island is muddy and silts up at the least touch of a fin. The wall north of the spit carries less sediment.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **The wall.** Orange tentacles of burrowing sea cucumbers reach out of cracks in the ledges and walls. Glassy tunicates and small groups of plump, waxy red tunicates stand out on the dark rock, with pink coralline algae, yellow cup corals, bright red colonial tunicates and colorful urchins. A dive light brings back the reds, oranges and yellows the depth takes out.
* **Sea stars.** Sunflower stars, blood stars, leather stars, morning sun stars, Stimpson's sun stars and slime stars.
* **Crevices and ledges.** Hairy yellow helmet crabs, giant Pacific octopus, grunt sculpins, penpoint gunnels, painted greenlings, decorated warbonnets and orange-spotted nudibranchs.
* **Sponges and fish.** Small stalked vase sponges grow in large numbers, with small rockfish along the wall and rocky outcroppings to the north.
* **Silty bottom.** Small round sculpins such as the tadpole sculpin.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Strong tidal current runs through the narrow channel on both tides, hardest on the ebb. Time the dive for slack, and on the slack before flood turn back early, before the flood builds northwest away from the crossing.
* **Boat traffic.** Small boats run through the channel between the spit and Frost Island, most often around high tide, and powerboat wakes reach the channel from Lopez Sound. Cross the channel on the bottom, fly a dive flag and keep surface time in the channel short.
* **Long walk.** About 560 m each way along the spit with full gear, on sand. Use a cart or wagon and save effort for the return.
* **Private island.** Frost Island is private property with no public access; stay off its shore.
* **Silt.** The muddy bottom along the base of the wall clouds with the least stirring. Keep off it and stay on the wall.
* **Wind chop.** North wind builds chop on the point and across the crossing, and north-northeast and northwest wind raise it too.
