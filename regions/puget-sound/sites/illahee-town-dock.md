# Illahee Town Dock

Illahee Town Dock is a Port of Illahee community pier and boat ramp on the Kitsap Peninsula's Port Orchard shore, at the foot of Ocean View Boulevard NE in the small community of Illahee. A short swim out from the dock reaches a sunken tire reef, dozens of clumps of bound tires laid down in the 1970s and 80s and still connected along the bottom by rope lines, before the sand continues out onto a gentler outer slope. The reef itself carries little life beyond anemones and algae on the tires, though the connecting ropes support more growth and can catch a fin or a hose. Boat and fishing traffic at the dock, and the old tires' chemical leaching, are the site's main considerations.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down the boat ramp at the Port of Illahee dock. Swim out along the pier's south side, away from the boat launch lane, then turn to pick up the tire reef once the bottom reaches the 6 to 9 m range.
* **Parking.** A small public lot at the dock, rebuilt along with the pier and floats in 2025. Port of Illahee land, free, no pass required.
* **Access.** Port of Illahee public tidelands and upland property, off Illahee Road NE at Ocean View Boulevard NE, between Bremerton and Silverdale. The pier, floats and gangways were closed for a full rebuild starting April 2025, substantially complete by the end of that year.
* **Facilities.** A vault toilet added as part of the 2025 rebuild, the pier and its recreational floats and gangways, and the boat ramp itself.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The dock and pilings.** Freshly rebuilt in 2025. Swim out along its south side, clear of fishing lines from anglers on the pier above.
* **The tire reef.** Sits roughly 100 to 160 m out, where the bottom runs about 6 to 9 m below MLLW. Bound clumps of tires, a dozen or two tires each, run in lines connected by rope, many of them partly buried in the sand.
* **Past the reef.** The sand continues out past the tires to the site's usual working depth, about 14 m, roughly 210 m out, then keeps dropping more gently from there.

## Current

Current here is negligible at every stage of the tide, at the surface and depth-averaged alike, so this is not a slack-tide dive.

* **Flood.** Sets north, up the Port Orchard reach, negligible in strength and staying under about 0.15 m/s at any stage of the tide.
* **Ebb.** Sets south, down the reach toward Rich Passage and the main basin, similarly weak.
* **Diveable window.** All day, any tide stage. Current is not the limiting factor at this site; boat traffic, visibility and the reef's own rope lines are.

## Hazards

* **Boats and fishing line.** An active community dock with a boat ramp, recreational floats and a fishing pier overhead; monofilament line and lures collect on the bottom near the pilings. Stay clear of the boat launch lane and any moored boat, and listen for engine noise before ascending.
* **Rope lines on the reef.** The tire clumps are strung together along the bottom by rope, an entanglement risk in low visibility. Carry a cutting tool and keep clear contact with a buddy while working the reef.
* **The tires themselves.** A December 2023 survey found at least 138 distinct tire clumps here, 12 to 20 tires each, still largely in place. The material leaches 6PPD-quinone, formaldehyde and other petroleum-based compounds, and the site is a named target of a state tire-pile removal program expected to continue through the 2025 to 2029 bienniums, so the reef's extent and layout can change from one season to the next.
* **Depth creep.** The sand keeps dropping past the site's usual working depth, at a gentle grade. Watch depth on any push beyond the reef.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The site faces roughly east, across the Port Orchard reach toward Bainbridge Island.
* **Bad.** Northeast through southeast, the open fetch across the reach to Bainbridge Island.
* **Fine.** Southwest through northwest, blocked by the Kitsap Peninsula landmass behind the site.
* **Wind against current.** Not a real factor here; the current stays too weak at every stage of the tide to stack meaningfully against any wind.

## Visibility

* **Character.** Follows the same broad pattern as the rest of central Puget Sound, worst through spring and summer plankton blooms and better outside that season. Visibility here can run poor enough that the reef's own rope lines, not sight, are what keeps a diver oriented on the tire clumps.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11°C through the year, in line with the wider central Sound. Standard drysuit protocol year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Tire reef.** Sparse compared to a natural reef; mainly anemones and algae on the tires themselves, with rockfish, lingcod and sculpin turning up around the structure. The connecting ropes carry more growth than the tires do, including tube worms.
* **Dock and pilings.** Fry and other small fish shelter under the floats and pilings.
