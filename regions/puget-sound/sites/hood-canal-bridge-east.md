# Hood Canal Bridge East Side

The east end of the Hood Canal Bridge stands on a line of concrete piers running out from the Kitsap shore at the entrance to Hood Canal, a few hundred metres southwest of Salsbury Point County Park near Port Gamble. The outer piers stand in current-swept water down a smooth sand slope, and their concrete disappears under white plumose anemones, giant purple feather duster worms and sea stars. The dive is a 430 m swim from the park timed so the flood carries the diver to the bridge and the new ebb carries the diver back. Current is the headline hazard: all of Hood Canal's exchange runs through this channel, and signs on the bridge supports prohibit scuba diving below the bridge.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Enter from the beach at the southwest boundary of Salsbury Point County Park and swim out into the channel, then southwest along the shore to the bridge, about 430 m on a bearing of 237°. The swim takes up to half an hour each way. The bridge is also reached by boat from nearby ramps, which removes the swim.
* **Parking.** Free, in the boat ramp lot at the end of Whitford Road NE, reached from SR 104 by Wheeler Street just east of the bridge.
* **Access.** Kitsap County parks are open during daylight hours only. The beach south of the park is private and posted No Trespassing, so stay in the water once past the park boundary. Fishing and crabbing from the floating dock are prohibited. Signs posted on the bridge supports prohibit scuba diving below the bridge, which takes in the piers this dive visits.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms with pay showers (open April through September), a covered picnic shelter, picnic tables, a playground, a boat ramp and a seasonal floating dock, in place from April to October.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The swim out.** Swim out from shore into the main channel until the flood is felt setting toward the bridge, then turn and ride it southwest. Inshore, eelgrass grows between Salsbury Point and the bridge, holding perch, sole and shiners.
* **Old pilings.** A group of old pilings stands about halfway between the boat ramp and the bridge. The inshore reverse current during the flood runs from here back toward Salsbury Point.
* **The piers.** The east approach stands on a line of concrete piers counted out from shore. The inner ones stand in the shallows, the outer two down the slope in deeper, current-swept water. On a sunny day the dry concrete columns above the surface are visible from the bottom and lead from one pier to the next; otherwise a compass bearing carries the diver between them.
* **Bottom.** A smooth, silty sand slope runs gradually down to the piers and steepens under the bridge. Each of the outer two piers stands on the slope, with its uphill side about 3 m shallower than its downhill side. The outer two piers each carry a ledge part way down the column.
* **Old bridge debris.** Pieces of old bridge structure lie scattered on the bottom around the piers.
* **Descent.** Submerge on the upstream side of a pier, so the surface current holds the diver against the pier.

All of Hood Canal fills and drains through the channel beneath the bridge.

* **Flood.** Sets south-southwest in mid-channel, past the park toward the bridge and into Hood Canal, with daily peaks of 0.28 to 0.73 m/s at the station over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets north-northeast in mid-channel, from the bridge back toward the park and out of the canal, with daily peaks of 0.34 to 0.76 m/s at the station over the same 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** The site is dived on slack before ebb, the high water slack. Under 0.25 m/s at the station it typically lasts about 2 to 4 hours, and slack before flood about 1 to 3 hours; a large exchange narrows both. At the outer piers the slack runs much shorter than the station window, and the current builds quickly once it turns. Enter the water 45 to 60 minutes ahead of slack before ebb, ride the last of the flood to the bridge, explore the piers through slack, and return to the park with the new ebb.
* **Inshore reverse current.** From about two and a half hours into the flood, a reverse current runs along the shoreline from the old pilings back toward Salsbury Point, against the mid-channel flood.
* **Surface and depth differ.** The surface water can turn before the water at depth, ebbing while the bottom is still at slack or in a weak flood, so the current felt on the surface swim can differ from the current at the piers.
* **No handholds.** The smooth sand bottom offers nothing to grip, so a diver caught by the current cannot pull along the bottom against it.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 293° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northwest, 308° |

The swim out runs 57° off the entry shore's facing, along the shore to the bridge.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through west (WSW-W), onshore over 5.4 to 5.9 km of open water across the canal entrance, building chop straight onto the beach and the swim.
* **Short fetch.** West-northwest through north (WNW-N), onshore over 2.2 to 3.6 km, enough to raise a chop on the entry.
* **Mixed.** North-northeast (NNE), along the shore over 5.6 km, and southwest (SW), along the shore over 15.2 km along the length of the canal, each running a wind swell past the entry.
* **Fine.** Northeast through south-southwest (NE-SSW): offshore from east-northeast through south, where the Kitsap shore lies within 300 m upwind, and cross-shore over less than 5 km of fetch at either end.
* **Wind against current.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW) blows against the flood, over 2.2 to 15.2 km, worst from the southwest along the length of the canal. North through northeast (N-NE) blows against the ebb, over 3.6 to 5.6 km.

The tide swings over 3 m on a typical day here: the 9.9 m dive area reads about 8.7 m at the year's lowest low and about 13.5 m at its highest high.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** Usually 1.5 to 4.5 m, often snowy with suspended particles, and under 1.5 m at its worst.
* **Silt.** The current stirs the fine silt off the sand slope, so visibility is often poor at the piers and best once the water settles near slack.
* **Season.** Plankton blooms cut visibility hardest in spring and summer.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Warms to about 12 to 14 °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **The outer piers.** White plumose anemones cover the deeper bases of the outer two piers, with giant purple feather duster worms fed by the current. White compound ascidians, small red sea squirts and sharp-nosed crabs decorated with sponge live on the concrete, and sunflower stars, morning sun stars and blood stars gather around the bases.
* **Encrusting growth.** Mussels, clams, barnacles, anemones and small woody kelp crust the shallower structure, with iridescent polychaete worms in places.
* **Nudibranchs.** Several species scattered over the structure, among them yellow nudibranchs grazing on sponge, clown nudibranchs and frosted nudibranchs.
* **Sand.** Ranks of dark purple sand dollars stand on edge in the sand to feed on plankton and debris, beside amber diatom mats in the grooves of the sand. The sand dollars are alive and easily damaged. Jackknife clams, kelp crabs, sea pens and sea stars live on the sand flats.
* **Eelgrass and dock.** Perch, sole and shiners in the eelgrass between the park and the bridge, and schools of small fish under the park dock.

## Hazards

* **Current.** All of Hood Canal's exchange runs through this channel. A mistimed dive at the piers can carry a diver well past the bridge, and the smooth sand offers nothing to hold. Carry a surface marker buoy.
* **Posted prohibition.** Signs on the bridge supports prohibit scuba diving below the bridge.
* **Long swim.** About 430 m each way, up to half an hour, with the return relying on the new ebb.
* **Boat traffic.** The park's boat ramp and dock see heavy use by recreational and commercial boats, including geoduck harvesters, and boats run the channel under the bridge. Surface away from the dock and use a dive flag.
* **Submarine transits.** Escorted Navy submarines pass through the bridge's draw span with security vessels.
* **Lost fishing gear and debris.** Nets, lures, line and anchors lie on the bottom near the dock and the bridge, among scattered old bridge debris. Carry a cutting tool.
* **Navigation.** In poor visibility the piers are easy to miss on the swim out; a compass is essential.
* **Private beach.** The shore south of the park is private; stay in the water past the park boundary.
