# Ed Munro Seahurst Park

Shore dive off the long sand and cobble beach of Ed Munro Seahurst Park in Burien, south of Seattle, on the main basin of Puget Sound facing the north end of Vashon Island. The bottom is a gentle slope of sand and silt with a small eelgrass bed in front of the stream that crosses the beach, the scattered remains of an old wooden barge, and a field of large orange sea pens to the north of it. The beach sits on a wide stretch of the Sound, so current is usually minimal, and the dive suits every level. The standing challenge is navigation: the bottom has little structure, and finding the barge depends on a compass and the depth contour.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk from the lower lot to the beach where the stream crosses it, finding a way through the line of drift logs at the top of the beach, and wade in over sand and slick cobble. The beach is long and flat, so on a low tide it is a long walk across wet sand to swimming depth.
* **Parking.** Free. The lower lot beside the beach is small and fills early on a fine day; a much larger upper lot sits about a ten minute walk uphill, a long carry in a drysuit. A vehicle turnaround at the beach allows gear to be dropped off before parking.
* **Access.** The park is open 8 a.m. to dusk, which rules out night dives. About 16 km south of downtown Seattle: take Highway 518 west into Burien, where it becomes SW 148th Street, turn right on Ambaum Boulevard SW, left on SW 144th Street, then right on 13th Avenue SW, which winds about 1.3 km downhill into the park.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms with drinking fountains along the path beside the beach lot, reservable picnic shelters, a playground, and forest trails above the beach.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Out from the stream.** Swim directly out, due west, from where the stream crosses the beach. A small eelgrass bed lies on the gentle sand slope in front of the stream mouth.
* **To the barge.** Descend to about 11 m below MLLW and turn north, following that contour. The remains of the barge lie north of the stream, the shallow end at about 9 m and the deep end at about 12 m. The barge has largely rotted away, so look for low timbers and debris on the sand rather than a hull.
* **If it is not found.** Reaching about 12 m without sighting the barge means it lies inshore or behind; turn back along the contour or surface and reset from the stream mouth.
* **Sea pen field.** North of the barge the sand carries a field of large orange sea pens, the best ground on the site.
* **The deep slope.** North of the eelgrass the sand descends steadily, and seaward of the barge it steepens, passing 20 m within a short swim.
* **Return.** Head east back up the slope and follow the shallows south to the stream mouth.

| **Recommended bin** | bin 35, at 14.5 m, matching the working depth. The other published bin, at 42 m, sits well below the site |

| **Flood axis** | South, 186° |
| **Ebb axis** | North-northwest, 343° |

* **Local flow.** The water here is weak and runs along the shore, north-northeast and south-southwest (about 18° and 198°). Out in the channel the station's currents reach a few tenths of a metre per second; along this beach the flow stays a fraction of that on most days.
* **Flood.** Sets south-southwest along the shore.
* **Ebb.** Out in the channel the ebb sets north, but close in front of the entry an eddy keeps the near-shore water setting south on the ebb as well, so expect a southward set on either exchange.
* **Diveable window.** Current is minimal for most of the day, picking up to moderate only on the larger exchanges. Slack along the beach turns later than at the station, typically by 40 minutes to over an hour, and on weak exchanges the turn is so gentle its timing is ill defined. On a big exchange, plan the dive around the station's slack and expect a southward drift; finishing south of the entry is an easy walk back along the beach.

## Hazards

* **Current on large exchanges.** Usually minimal, but moderate during the bigger exchanges, and near the entry it sets south on both the flood and the ebb.
* **Navigation.** The bottom is open sand with little relief, and the barge is low and easy to pass. Carry a compass and use the depth contour.
* **Boat traffic.** Small boats pass along the beach. Fly a marker buoy and listen before surfacing.
* **Beach.** Slick cobble and wet sand on a long, flat beach, with a line of drift logs to climb through at the top; footing is poor carrying gear on a low tide.
* **Silt.** The soft bottom stirs easily; careful finning keeps the water clear around the barge.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The beach faces west-northwest across the main basin toward Vashon Island.
* **Bad.** West through northwest blow onshore, with fetch down the main basin from the northwest, and put chop on the entry. Southerlies run along the shore with fetch up East Passage and roughen the beach as well.
* **Fine.** East and northeast are offshore, blowing off the wooded bluff behind the beach.
* **Wind against current.** The water close in sets south on both exchanges, so a south wind opposing it steepens the chop over the entry. That is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

* **Blooms.** Spring and summer plankton blooms are the usual limit, with clearer water from late autumn through winter.
* **Silt and runoff.** The sand and silt bottom clouds easily, and the stream and winter rain carry runoff into the shallows near the entry; visibility usually improves away from the stream mouth and with depth.
* **Wind.** Onshore chop stirs the shallow flat and clouds the first part of the swim.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 8 to 9 °C in winter and spring, rising to about 12 to 13 °C in late summer. Standard drysuit protocol.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the mid teens over the shallow flat in summer.

## Marine life

* **Sea pens.** The field of large orange sea pens north of the barge is the signature of the site. Brown and white striped nudibranchs move through the top layer of sand among them and feed on the sea pens, and their pale yellow egg coils lie partly buried in the sand nearby.
* **On the sand.** Flounder and C-O sole lie hidden on the bottom, moon snails plough the sand, and hermit crabs, burrowing sea cucumbers, sea anemones and sea stars dot the slope.
* **Around the barge and eelgrass.** Perch gather over the barge remains and the eelgrass bed, and stubby squid turn up on the sand.
* **Overhead.** Harbor seals, sea lions and harbor porpoises pass along the beach.
