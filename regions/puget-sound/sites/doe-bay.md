# Doe Bay

Shore dive from the beach of Doe Bay Resort and Retreat on the southeast shore of Orcas Island, where a shallow, silty bay with a tidal estuary at its head opens onto the north end of Rosario Strait. Contorted rocky shorelines frame both sides of the bay, bending inward into small coves and blind pockets, with a submerged ledge and wall along the southeast shore and an open slope falling past 30 m beyond it. The inner bay holds little current, while the points and the slope outside them take the full Rosario Strait exchange, so dives beyond the points are timed to slack. The beach is private resort property.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** An easy walk into the water off the narrow sand and cobble beach at the head of the bay, below the resort buildings. The dive coordinate lies 244 m south-southeast of the entry, a surface swim down the middle of the bay, and the southeast and southwest shorelines both lead out from the beach.
* **Parking.** Resort parking above the beach, about 120 m north of the entry. Offsite visitors receive a visitor's parking pass at the front desk, valid for 45 minutes, to display on the dashboard; cost not established. Parking for the length of a dive is arranged at the front desk.
* **Access.** The resort is private property, and its beach is reserved for onsite guests. Offsite visitors check in at the front desk (open daily 9 am to 5 pm, 360-376-2291) before walking the grounds, and offsite kayakers pay $25 to launch from the beach; a diver not staying at the resort arranges access with the front desk before entering the water. Reach Orcas Island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes. From the ferry landing take Orcas Road north to Eastsound, then follow Olga Road through Moran State Park to Olga, turn left on Point Lawrence Road and continue about 5 km to Doe Bay Road and the resort at 107 Doe Bay Road, about 45 minutes from the ferry.
* **Facilities.** The resort store and front office, a café open to the public, restrooms and hot showers, and a spa with soaking tubs and sauna overlooking the estuary.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The beach.** The narrow sand and cobble beach shelves gently onto a silty bottom, about 3.5 m below MLLW some 80 m out.
* **Estuary mouth.** A tidal estuary opens into the head of the bay about 80 m east of the entry. Its mouth has a mud bottom, and the route to the southeast shore crosses it.
* **Southeast shoreline.** East of the estuary mouth the rocky shoreline drops to a sand and cobble bottom covered in shell fragments.
* **The ledge and wall.** A submerged rock ledge and wall about 3 m high sits at the second outcrop of rock just past the estuary mouth, 145 m southeast of the entry. Its base lies about 8 m below MLLW, its holes and crevices lined with invertebrates.
* **The shelf.** Farther along the southeast shore, beyond the ledge, the rock steps down as a larger shelf to its foot at the dive coordinate, 105 m south-southeast of the ledge.
* **Open slope.** Beyond the southeast shore's rock the bottom falls toward Rosario Strait, passing 20 m below MLLW 80 m east-southeast of the dive coordinate and 30 m some 180 m to the southeast.
* **Southwest shoreline.** The rocks along the southwest shore meet a silty sand bottom, with a large eelgrass bed in slightly deeper water beyond and silty mud past that. The bottom off this shore lies about 6.8 m below MLLW some 250 m south of the entry. The shallower rock formations on this side are full of holes and crevices, and a dive light shows the animals sheltering in them.

## Current

The inner bay is sheltered, and the Rosario Strait exchange sets past the points and over the open slope.

* **Flood.** Runs north-northeast up Rosario Strait past the mouth of the bay and sets north into the bay at the dive coordinate. Daily peaks at the station run 0.71 to 2.30 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Runs southwest down Rosario Strait and sets south out of the bay at the dive coordinate. Some ebbs run long, with two peaks and no slack between them. Daily peaks at the station run 0.57 to 2.15 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 36 to 90 minutes and the slack before ebb 54 to 84 minutes, with the shortest windows near 30 minutes between strong exchanges. Where an exchange stays weak, two slacks merge into a single quiet spell of 4 to nearly 6 hours.
* **Inside the bay.** The current inshore, over the beach shallows, the estuary mouth and the inner shorelines, is minimal.
* **At the points.** The current strengthens once outside the bay, at the points and over the open slope beyond them.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southeast, 154° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 171° |

* **Bad.** East through south (E-S), onshore over 5.7 to 22.1 km of open water across Rosario Strait, building wind swell on the beach and the swim out.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest (SSW), onshore over 4.1 km, enough to raise chop in the bay.
* **Fine.** Southwest clockwise through east-northeast (SW-ENE), blocked by the bay's own shores and Orcas Island, with 480 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** South-southeast through south-southwest (SSE-SSW), over 4.1 to 8.0 km, blows against the south ebb set. Every wind opposing the north flood set, north-northwest through northeast (NNW-NE), crosses under 1 km of fetch, too short to stack.

## Depth and tide

## Visibility

* **Silt.** A fine silt covers every structure in the bay and lifts at a careless fin kick, so good buoyancy and a gentle kick keep the water clear for the diver behind.
* **Season.** Plankton blooms bring it down in spring and summer; it runs clearest in winter, before the spring bloom.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Rocky shorelines.** Animal life is sparse under the silt but varied: orange and California sea cucumbers, frosted nudibranchs, purple and sunflower sea stars, Christmas anemones, gumboot chitons, red sea squirts, snails, limpets and shrimp.
* **Ledge and wall.** Large red sea urchins and other colourful invertebrates and sponges live along the southeast wall.
* **Dens.** Boulders and crevices along the rock on both sides of the bay hold a variety of fish and invertebrates, and giant Pacific octopus.
* **Fish.** Small sculpins, rockfish, perch, sand lances, kelp greenling and blackeye gobies.
* **Mud bottom at the estuary mouth.** Hermit crabs, kelp crabs, hooded nudibranchs, flounder and tube-dwelling anemones.
* **Kelp and eelgrass.** Kelp grows along the southeast shore in summer, and a large eelgrass bed lies off the rocks on the southwest side.

## Hazards

* **Current past the points.** Rosario Strait peaks at the station exceed 2 m/s on the largest exchanges, and the current builds quickly once a diver leaves the shelter of the bay. Dive the points and the slope at slack.
* **Depth off the southeast shore.** The slope beyond the southeast shore's rock passes 30 m within 180 m of the dive coordinate. A diver drifting offshore loses the rock to open water, so keep the shoreline on one side.
* **Boats.** Occasional small boats use the bay, the resort keeps private mooring buoys in it, and guided kayak tours launch from the beach in summer. Fly a dive flag and ascend along the shoreline.
* **Silt.** The silty bottom clouds quickly, and a stirred-up bay loses its visibility for the rest of the dive.
* **Wind swell.** Wind from east through south-southwest raises swell and chop on the open beach and can spoil the entry and exit.
