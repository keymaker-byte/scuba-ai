# Smallpox Bay (San Juan County Park)

Shore dive from the boat ramp in Smallpox Bay, the small sandy cove at the south end of San Juan County Park on the west side of San Juan Island, facing Haro Strait. Divers swim out of the shallow, silty bay and around either of its two rocky points, where ledges, large boulders and undercuts step down a reef slope under understory and bull kelp. The campground sits steps from the water, so campers can dive straight from their sites. Current along the outer shore is the headline hazard, and every dive past the points is timed to slack.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk in down the boat ramp at the head of the bay onto a shallow sandy bottom, then swim 187 m west-southwest across the bay to the dive coordinate at its mouth. Descend at the mouth to save air for the reef outside.
* **Parking.** The lot across from the boat ramp, about 46 m from the water. Campers park at their sites, many of them a short walk from the ramp. Check current day-use and parking fees with San Juan County Parks.
* **Access.** San Juan County Park is a county campground open year round, with day use allowed; campers can dive straight from their sites. Campsite reservations are strongly recommended from May through September, and from 1 November to 31 March sites go first come, first served, with some closed and staffing limited. Divers coming for the day should check with the park office ahead of time. Reach the island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes to Friday Harbor, about two hours. From the ferry, turn left up Spring Street, right on Second Street, and follow the main road onto Guard Street and Beaverton Valley Road for about 11 km, then turn left on Mitchell Bay Road for about 5 km, following it right, then left and left again onto West Side Road. The park entrance is on the right.
* **Facilities.** Flush toilets, drinking water, a small picnic shelter, picnic tables, fire pits, campsites, kayak racks and the boat ramp. The upper restroom closes in winter.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The bay.** A narrow cove opening west-southwest, its inner half shallow sand and silt that dries in places at low water. The bottom deepens to about 3 m below MLLW across the mouth.
* **South point.** Swimming out along the south shore keeps a diver clear of boats using the launch. Rock along this shore drops to the silty sand of the bay, and the structure grows and deepens around the point.
* **North point.** The headland below the park's grass field and viewpoint. Rounding it from the bay, the reef runs north along its west face past boulders and kelp toward North Bay, the next cove north, where a staircase climbs from a foot-only beach back to the campground. On a flood, the northeast set allows a one-way dive from the ramp to the stair beach.
* **Around the points.** From about 3 m below MLLW at the points the rock drops in ledges past large boulders and undercuts, to about 18 m some 75 m west of the dive coordinate, then eases to about 19 m at 150 m.
* **Low Island.** The small rocky island about 350 m north-northwest, off North Bay, is a National Wildlife Refuge island closed to landing.

## Current

Haro Strait's tidal stream runs along this shore, setting northeast on the flood and southwest on the ebb past the mouth of the bay.

* **Flood.** Sets northeast along the shore, past the north point toward North Bay. Daily peaks at the station run 0.49 to 1.03 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets southwest along the shore, past the south point and away down the island. Daily peaks at the station run 0.37 to 1.55 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before flood typically runs 66 to 132 minutes and the slack before ebb 60 to 108 minutes, the shortest about 25 minutes between two strong exchanges. Where a weak exchange falls between two slacks, the current can stay under the threshold for 3 hours or more. Slack followed by a small exchange gives the most forgiving dive.
* **Inside the bay.** The bay itself is sandy and sheltered. The current picks up at the points and along the outer shore.
* **Route on slack before flood.** Dive the south point first and let the rising flood carry the return northeast into the bay, or dive north along the north point's west face and ride the flood on to the stair beach in North Bay.
* **Route on slack before ebb.** Dive north along the north point's west face first, so the rising ebb carries the return southwest toward the mouth of the bay.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-southwest, 250° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 272° |

* **Bad.** South-southwest through northwest (SSW-NW), onshore over 8.2 to 21.9 km of open water across Haro Strait, driving wind waves into the mouth of the bay and onto the ramp.
* **Fine.** North-northwest clockwise through south (NNW-S), blocked by the island itself and the bay's two points, with 1.3 km of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** South-southwest through west-southwest (SSW-WSW), over 12.0 to 13.2 km, blows against the southwest ebb set. No wind with 1 km of fetch opposes the northeast flood set.

## Visibility

* **Typical range.** About 3 to 9 m, often silty, especially over the soft bottom of the bay.
* **Season.** Winter brings the clearest water; spring and summer plankton blooms cut it, worst in the shallows.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Bay floor.** Purple sea stars, orange sea cucumbers and pink anemones on the sand, with tall eelgrass and swarms of hatchling shrimp in season.
* **Rock and boulders.** Dense invertebrate cover around the points: giant barnacles, yellow sponges, yellow cup corals, small colorful tube worms, sea anemones, chitons, limpets, turban snails, sea urchins, sea cucumbers, sharp-nosed crabs and feather stars. Blood stars, Stimpson's sun stars and sunflower stars work the rock, and nudibranchs include clown nudibranchs. Swimming scallops and shrimp fill the cracks.
* **Fish.** Lingcod, blackeye gobies and other reef fish among the boulders.
* **Kelp.** Understory kelp blankets the boulders and exposed reef, with patchy bull kelp along the outer shore.
* **Mammals.** Harbor seals frequent the shore, and orcas pass offshore in Haro Strait.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Outside the bay the stream runs along the shore at up to 1.55 m/s on the largest ebbs at the station. Dive at slack, ideally before a small exchange, and look at the water surface before entering to confirm the current has slowed.
* **Boat traffic.** The ramp is the park's boat and kayak launch, and whale watching brings many small boats to this shore. Swim out along the south shore clear of the launch, fly a dive flag, and ascend along the rock.
* **Bull kelp.** Bull kelp off the points can entangle a diver at the surface. Ascend clear of it, and carry a cutting tool.
* **Wind swell.** Wind from south-southwest through northwest crosses Haro Strait straight into the bay and can spoil the entry and exit.
