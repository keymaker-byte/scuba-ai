# Odlin County Park (North)

Rocky shoreline at the north end of Odlin County Park on Lopez Island, entered beside the park's small fishing pier and followed north along boulders and crevices toward Upright Head, where the bottom falls away as a steep cobble slope into Upright Channel. Fish gather under the pier, and wolf eels and giant Pacific octopus den among the boulders. Current is the headline hazard: it runs along the shoreline and strengthens away from the bay, and on larger ebbs it reverses direction partway through the exchange.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Step in beside the fishing pier at the north end of the beach, about 5 m from the parking. The dive coordinate lies about 323 m north-northwest of the entry, along the rocky shoreline.
* **Parking.** The parking beside the pier at the north end of the beach. Daytime parking is free; overnight parking carries a fee.
* **Access.** Reach Lopez Island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes, about 40 minutes. From the ferry landing drive south on Ferry Road for 1.8 km and turn right into Odlin County Park; the beach parking lies about 0.5 km inside the entrance. San Juan County Parks runs the park year round, with quiet hours from 10 pm to 7 am.
* **Facilities.** The fishing pier, a day-use dock with a two-hour limit, a free boat and kayak launch, mooring buoys, vault toilets, a picnic area and pavilion, and a campground with potable water from April through October.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pier.** A short pier stands off the rocky shore at the north end of the beach. Explore underneath it when no one is fishing; schools of small fish and slender pipefish gather in its shade.
* **The rocky shoreline.** Head north from the pier with the shore on the right. Submerged boulders and rock crevices line the shallows, the dens of the site's octopus and wolf eels.
* **The slope.** The bottom drops steeply away from the shoreline: about 4.3 m below MLLW some 110 m out from the entry, 11.5 m at 160 m, 20.9 m at 240 m and 27.3 m at the dive coordinate. North toward Upright Head it becomes a steep cobble slope with the occasional large boulder.
* **The return.** Turn back along the shoreline toward the pier, keeping the rock on the left, and surface inside the bay.

## Current

The site sits on the east shore of Upright Channel, which joins Harney Channel to the north with San Juan Channel to the southwest. The water off the shoreline runs with the channel, and the bay to the south holds slacker water.

* **Flood.** Sets northeast up the channel past the shoreline toward Upright Head on every exchange. Daily peaks at the station run 0.58 to 1.29 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** On exchanges of 1.2 m or less the ebb also sets northeast past the park, with the flood. On larger exchanges it sets southwest first, then reverses and runs northeast while the tide is still falling; the reversal comes up to three hours into the ebb, and its timing varies with the size of the exchange. The ebb flowing east through San Juan Channel is deflected off the west shore of Lopez into the south end of Upright Channel, and once it outweighs the ebb entering from the north, the flow past the park turns northeast. Daily peaks at the station run 0.38 to 0.70 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** The slack before ebb is the one that serves. Under 0.25 m/s at the station it typically runs 102 to 138 minutes, shrinks to about 12 minutes ahead of the strongest ebbs, and stretches to about five hours on days when the weaker ebb stays under the threshold. A second slack follows on a larger ebb, as the southwest ebb weakens against the northeast flow and reverses.
* **No slack before flood at the site.** The station turns, but the water off the shoreline is already running northeast by the end of the ebb, and it only slows at minimum current before picking up again as the flood.
* **Outside the bay.** Current is mild close to the pier and strengthens along the shoreline toward Upright Head, where it can be strong enough to end a dive away from slack.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west, 278° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west, 262° |

The shoreline faces straight across Upright Channel toward Shaw Island, with Upright Head and the body of Lopez Island at its back.

* **Bad.** West-southwest (WSW), onshore over 7.4 km of open water down Upright Channel, building chop on the entry and along the shoreline.
* **Short fetch.** Southwest (SW), onshore over 1.9 km, and west through north-northwest (W-NNW), onshore over 1.6 to 3.5 km across Upright Channel, raising chop on the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south-southwest (N-SSW). North and north-northeast run along the shore; northeast through south-southeast blow off Upright Head and Lopez Island with 100 to 420 m of fetch; south and south-southwest run along the shore with under 600 m of fetch.
* **Wind against current.** Southwest (SW) over 1.9 km, west-southwest (WSW) over 7.4 km and west (W) over 3.5 km blow against the southwest ebb set. The winds opposing the northeast flood set, and the ebb once it reverses onto that set, blow off the land with under 250 m of fetch.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Best from autumn through spring. Plankton blooms in spring and summer bring it down hard, though clear spells still come in summer.
* **Current.** Running water along the shoreline stirs the bottom and lowers it.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Under the pier.** Schools of small fish and slender pipefish.
* **Boulders and crevices.** Wolf eels, giant Pacific octopus and rockfish in the dens along the shoreline, with purple sea stars on the rocks in their purple, orange, yellow and brown forms.
* **The slope toward Upright Head.** Brown hydroids on the larger boulders, with giant Pacific octopus, rockfish and wolf eels, young ones among them.

## Hazards

* **Current.** It strengthens away from the bay along the shoreline, and on larger ebbs it reverses partway through the exchange, so the direction can change partway through a dive. Plan around the slack before ebb, and turn back toward the pier when the current builds.
* **Fishing line.** Monofilament and hooks hang from the pier and lie on the bottom around it. Carry a cutting tool and stay clear of lines in the water when people are fishing.
* **Boat traffic.** The boat launch, the day-use dock and the mooring buoys bring small boats across the entry, and the ferries and other traffic in Upright Channel throw wakes onto the shore. Fly a dive flag and surface close to the rock.
* **Depth.** The slope continues steeply past the dive coordinate, which reaches about 30 m below the surface at the year's highest tides. Watch depth and no-decompression time on the descent.
* **Wind chop.** Wind from southwest through north-northwest raises chop on the entry and along the shoreline.
