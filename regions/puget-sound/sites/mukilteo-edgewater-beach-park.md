# Mukilteo Edgewater Beach Park

A Port of Everett beach on the Mukilteo waterfront, on the south shore of Possession Sound east of the ferry terminal, beside the Mount Baker Terminal, a working barge-to-rail pier that lands oversize aircraft parts. The dive is a long walk and swim out along the pier over a shallow flat to its outer end, where the bottom falls away steeply and the pilings, a tire pile and the debris under the pier hold octopus and crab. The pier is a working one: tugs land and move barges there, and a berthed barge is an overhead.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the beach beside the landward end of the pier, 50 m east of the lot. The swim out runs 222 m at 012° along the pier to the dive area, the first 140 m of it over a flat no deeper than 2.2 m below MLLW.
* **Parking.** Free, in the lot of about 30 spaces at the end of First Street. Reached from Mukilteo Boulevard by Mukilteo Lane and Mount Baker Avenue, across the BNSF grade crossing, then right on First Street. When the lot is closed, free parking lies across the street near the Japanese Gulch trailhead. Parking along the railroad tracks on Mukilteo Lane is prohibited.
* **Access.** Open dawn to dusk year round. The Port of Everett built the beach for habitat mitigation and public access beside the terminal, adding 340 m of beach material along the shore. The pier itself is closed to the public, its landward end fenced and posted no trespassing; the water beside and under it is dived from the beach.
* **Facilities.** Paths, benches, picnic tables, the parking lot, and portable toilets.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The pier.** The Mount Baker Terminal, 275 m long, completed in 2007, runs out north-northeast from the beach and is the spine of the dive: follow its pilings out and back.
* **The flat.** For the first 140 m from the entry the bottom stays within 2.2 m of MLLW, a long walk or shallow swim at low water.
* **The drop.** Past the flat the slope steepens quickly, to 6 m below MLLW at 180 m out and 13 m at the dive area near the pier's outer end, then on down at about 15%, reaching 21 m at 50 m past it and 30 m at 120 m past it.
* **The old reef.** The pier was built over an older artificial reef of debris and small steel-hulled boat wrecks, mostly under the berth where barges tie up.
* **Tire pile and bottle fields.** A pile of large tractor tires lies off the end of the pilings, with old bottle fields on the soft bottom around the pier's outer end.
* **Ferry terminal.** About 560 m west-southwest of the entry, on a bearing of 248°.

## Depth

* **Typical range.** Median daily 3.24 m, up to 5.14 m on the year's biggest exchange; 2026 span -1.30 to +3.99 m.
* **Tidal swing.** Possession Sound swings up to about 5 m between high and low water here, which decides how far the flat is walked and how deep the pier's end reads.
* **At low and high.** At the year's lowest low of -1.30 m the flat dries for about 110 m out from the entry and the dive area reads 11.7 m below the surface; at its highest high of +3.99 m the whole flat is covered and the dive area reads 17.0 m.

## Current

Possession Sound's tidal exchange: weak over the flat, stronger at the pier's outer end.

* **Type.** Tidal.
* **Flood.** Sets east-southeast, 102°, along the shore toward Port Gardner, at peaks of about 0.1 m/s.
* **Ebb.** Sets west-northwest, 282°, at about the same strength.
* **Diveable window.** The predicted current stays under 0.25 m/s through the whole tidal cycle on every day of the year.
* **Outer end.** The water off the pier's outer end can run strong on an ebb, well above the predicted speed; time the dive for a slack.

* **Bad.** Northwest through northeast (NW-NE), onshore over 6.3 to 10.9 km of open water up Possession Sound, building chop onto the beach and across the long swim out.
* **Short fetch.** West-northwest (WNW), onshore over 4.96 km, enough for a short chop on the entry.
* **Mixed.** West-southwest (WSW), cross-shore over 6.4 km, running a chop along the beach past the entry.
* **Fine.** East-northeast clockwise through southwest (ENE-SW): cross-shore over 380 m to 4.6 km from the east-northeast and east, and offshore from east-southeast through southwest, where the beach lies under the Mukilteo shore 200 to 390 m upwind. West (W) is also fine, cross-shore over 4.6 km.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Best in winter, when the water runs clear; worst in spring and summer through the plankton blooms, when it can close down to a few metres at depth.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 8 °C in winter and 13 to 14 °C in late summer. Drysuit year round.
* **Surface layer.** In summer the shallow flat warms well above the water at depth, to 18 to 19 °C.

## Marine life

* **Crab.** Dungeness and red rock crab are numerous on the bottom around the pier, and the site is dived for them in the open crab season.
* **Octopus.** Octopus den in the tire pile off the end of the pilings and in the debris under the pier.
* **Moon snails.** Moon snails are abundant on the soft bottom around the pier's outer end.
* **Shallows.** Juvenile salmon and forage fish use the restored beach and the flat, with river otters along the shore.

## Hazards

* **Working pier.** Tugs land, berth and move barges at the terminal. Stay clear of the pier while a tug is working a barge.
* **Berthed barge.** A barge tied up at the pier is an overhead environment over the old reef beneath it.
* **The flat at low water.** At a low tide the flat dries for up to about 110 m out from the entry, a long walk with gear on at the start and the end of the dive.
* **Railroad.** The BNSF line runs between the road and the beach, with commuter, freight and Amtrak trains. Cross only at the Mount Baker Avenue grade crossing.
* **Boat traffic.** The Mukilteo to Clinton ferry lane lies to the west, and small boats work the water off the pier. Fly a marker buoy.
