# Camano Island State Park

Camano Island State Park sits at the southwest tip of Camano Island, facing Saratoga Passage across to Whidbey Island, close to Camano Head where the passage meets the channel around to Port Susan. It is a shore dive with genuine current, unlike the flat water on the island's other shores, and its draw is a slope that steepens into a wall as the shoreline curves south toward the point. Divers describe it as a fish geek's site, better known for what is on the bottom than for scenery, and best planned tightly around slack.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Off the beach at Lowell Point, the park's boat launch beach, or from North Beach further along. The slope that makes the site worth diving lies a few hundred metres south of the Lowell Point access, where the shore curves toward Camano Head; getting to it is a swim or a walk down the beach, not a dive straight off the sand.
* **Parking.** Day-use lots above both beaches. A Discover Pass is required, an annual pass or a one-day pass bought on site. The Lowell Point boat launch carries its own daily fee, not needed for a shore entry.
* **Access.** Park hours run 6:30 a.m. to dusk, year round. North Beach's north staircase is closed; the south trail beach access serves that end of the park instead. Beach fires are prohibited under the park's current burn restriction.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms and showers near the campground, picnic and kitchen shelters, fire pits away from the beach, and a developed campground with cabins.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The slope.** Depth builds gradually off the main beach, then steepens into the site's wall as the shore curves south toward Camano Head, the island's southern tip.
* **Camano Head.** The point itself sits just south of the site, where Saratoga Passage meets the channel that runs around to Port Susan. That confluence is the source of the current here.
* **Kelp.** A summer kelp bed marks the shallower structure and is worth working before heading down the slope.

* **Flood.** Sets northwest, with daily peaks of 0.23 to 0.38 m/s at the station over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets southeast, with daily peaks of 0.08 to 0.27 m/s at the station over the same 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Current here runs genuinely strong for a shore site, the confluence off Camano Head pulls harder than the open passage further north along the island. Plan the entry around slack at the governing station and build in a wide margin either side rather than trusting the station's time directly. Current at the site does not track the station's slack on a fixed lag. Current at the station stays under 0.25 m/s for about 2 to 15 hours at a time, and the strongest water sits off Camano Head.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south-southwest, 195° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south-southwest, 196° |

The beach faces across Saratoga Passage toward Whidbey Island.

* **Bad.** Southeast through south-southeast (SE-SSE), onshore over 6.5 to 25.1 km up Possession Sound, and west-southwest (WSW), onshore over 6.2 km across to Whidbey, building chop on the beach.
* **Short fetch.** South through southwest (S-SW), onshore over 2.7 to 3.4 km.
* **Mixed.** East-southeast (ESE), over 5.8 km, and west through west-northwest (W-WNW), over 5.0 to 6.1 km, cross-shore.
* **Fine.** Northwest clockwise through east (NW-E): offshore from northwest through east-northeast, blocked by the island itself, and cross-shore over 2.7 km from the east.
* **Wind against current.** Right off Camano Head the current sets northwest on the flood and southeast on the ebb. West-southwest through northwest (WSW-NW) against the flood, over 5.0 to 9.9 km, and east-northeast through southeast (ENE-SE) against the ebb, over 2.3 to 25.1 km, both stack wind and current where the current is already strongest.

## Visibility

* **Best in summer.** Best when the kelp is in bloom through summer, with better conditions and more to see than the rest of the year.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11 °C year round, typical for the Sound. Standard drysuit protocol.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens in late summer, above the water at depth.

## Marine life

* **Fish.** A reputation as a fish geek's site: bottom fish reward divers who know where to look, more than the site offers at a glance.
* **Invertebrates.** Starfish and other invertebrates work the bottom and the structure.
* **Octopus.** Reported at the site, in keeping with the rocky structure the slope offers.
* **Kelp.** A summer bed over the shallower structure, thinner or absent the rest of the year.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Strong for a shore site, worst right off Camano Head where the two channels meet. Plan tightly around slack.
* **Boat traffic.** The Lowell Point boat launch sits at the main beach; fly a marker buoy on the swim south.
* **Kelp.** The summer bed is thick enough to foul a line or a fin if not respected.
