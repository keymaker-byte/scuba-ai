# Old Man House Park

Old Man House Park is a small Suquamish Tribe park on the Kitsap Peninsula shore, at the point where Agate Passage, the tidal channel separating Bainbridge Island from the Kitsap Peninsula, opens north into Port Madison. It sits at the historical site of Old Man House, once the largest longhouse on Puget Sound. The park works as a shore dive in its own right, in real but moderate current, since it sits only a short distance inside the passage's mouth; well up the channel to the south, at the Agate Pass Bridge, the same tide runs much harder, over 1 m/s at times. Divers also use the park as the take-out for a longer dive, a 1.6 km drift with the ebb current that starts at the bridge and rides the tide down the length of the passage to this beach.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Old Man House Park, on Division Avenue in Suquamish, fronts a gentle sandy beach right at the pass mouth. A short walk from the parking lot reaches the water.
* **Parking.** A designated lot at the park, free, with room for a handful of vehicles.
* **Access.** Day use park, Suquamish Tribe land. The park was a Washington state park until 2004, when it was returned to the Tribe.
* **Facilities.** Restroom and picnic area at the park. No lifeguard.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The point.** The park sits right at the corner where the pass widens into Port Madison, so the current runs along the shore here rather than straight out from the beach as it does further into the pass.
* **Up the channel.** Following the shore south and slightly east leads back up the passage toward the Agate Pass Bridge, about 1.6 km away. This runs against the ebb, the direction a drift dive rides down to this beach, so it is a swim rather than a drift.
* **Eelgrass.** An eelgrass bed grows off the park, thinning out into open sand and gravel further from shore.

| **Flood axis** | SW, about 218 to 238° |
| **Ebb axis** | NE, about 38 to 58° |

* **Flood.** Sets southwest, back up the channel. Peaks roughly 0.6 to 0.7 m/s here.
* **Ebb.** Sets northeast, out into Port Madison. Peaks roughly 0.4 to 0.7 m/s; this is the direction a drift dive rides down from the bridge to this beach.
* **Diveable window.** Typically an hour and a half or more around each slack, a broader window than the fast water further up the channel near the bridge.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Real at every stage but slack; this is the mouth of a tidal passage, not a sheltered cove. Plan the dive around the slack window above.
* **Boat traffic.** Port Madison carries boat traffic in and out past the park, on top of whatever runs through the passage itself.
* **Missing the entry.** A diver riding the ebb drift down from the bridge who misses this take-out is carried on past the park into the open water of Port Madison.

## Marine life

* **Eelgrass and open sand.** The eelgrass bed off the park gives way to sand and gravel further out. Seals are seen working the water beyond the grass.
