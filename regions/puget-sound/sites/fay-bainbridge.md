# Fay Bainbridge Park

Sand and eelgrass shore dive at the northeast tip of Bainbridge Island, facing open water on the Sound's main basin toward Possession Sound and Whidbey Island. A gentle, featureless slope runs from the beach out past the working depth and keeps dropping well beyond it, so the dive is built around watching animal behaviour over open sand rather than around any reef or wreck structure. The current here runs harder than the plain sand terrain suggests, and it is a genuine slack-tide dive rather than a forgiving one.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down to the beach at the south end of the day-use area. Swim out toward where two round white buoys sit anchored on the slope, then descend and follow the sand down.
* **Parking.** A free day-use lot at the park; it fills alongside the campground on summer weekends.
* **Access.** Bainbridge Island Metro Park & Recreation District land, transferred from Washington State Parks in 2011. Day use is free, dawn to dusk year round, no pass required. 15446 Sunrise Drive, Bainbridge Island.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms, including a building with showers, picnic shelters and open tables, a playground, and an accessible boardwalk and beach viewing platform. Restrooms can close intermittently in winter during high water.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Descent.** No reef or artificial structure to navigate by. An eelgrass band runs along the shallows, thinning into open sand as the bottom drops away.
* **The slope.** Follow the sand straight down from the entry to the usual working depth, about 22 m below MLLW. Beyond that the same slope keeps dropping, well past 55 m, outside NDL range.

| **Recommended bin** | bin 1, at 21.6 m, matching the working depth |
| **Time offset** | Not established; slack should be confirmed in the water |
| **Flood axis** | SSW, 212° at the station |
| **Ebb axis** | NNE, 14° at the station |

* **Flood.** In the open channel the station reads a south-southwest set. Along this shore itself the flood runs parallel to the beach instead, setting southeast.
* **Ebb.** The station's channel axis is north-northeast, but along the shore in front of the site the ebb sets northwest, parallel to the beach, then bends west as it rounds Point Monroe into Port Madison.
* **Diveable window.** Wide on a small exchange, several hours under 0.25 m/s. On a larger exchange the window narrows to a little over an hour either side of slack, with flood and ebb both running past 0.5 m/s; treat this as current sensitive on any exchange over about 3 m, not just the big ones.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Stronger than the open, featureless bottom suggests. Plan and time this as a real slack-tide dive rather than assuming the plain sand terrain means mild water.
* **Boat traffic.** Recreational and fishing boats work this stretch of the main basin. Fly a marker buoy and listen before ascending.
* **Featureless bottom.** Open sand with no landmarks makes it easy to lose the return line; carry a compass or a reel.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The beach faces roughly east-northeast, open across the main basin toward Possession Sound and Whidbey Island.
* **Bad.** Northeast through east, which runs a long fetch up the main basin onto this beach.
* **Fine.** Southwest through west, blocked by Bainbridge Island's own landmass behind the beach.
* **Wind against current.** An easterly wind opposing the ebb's northwest set along the shore is the roughest combination; the same wind runs with the flood's southeast set and stays calmer.

## Visibility

* **Seasonal.** Open to the main basin rather than sheltered in a cove, so it follows the same broad seasonal pattern as the rest of the Sound: worst through spring and summer plankton blooms, better outside bloom season.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Typically 7 to 11°C across the year, in line with the wider central Sound. In an unusually warm year the water column can run mixed rather than layered, up to about 14°C from the surface down through the working depth around 22 m, with no thermocline.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens in a normal summer, and can match the deep reading entirely in a mixed, warm year.

## Marine life

* **Character.** A sand and eelgrass site rather than a reef, sparser than the region's other junk and rock sites; the interest here is animal behaviour over open bottom rather than density of life.
* **Behaviour to watch for.** A male hermit crab dragging a female across the sand, a beaded anemone disgorging the remains of a crab, nudibranchs feeding on a sea pen, a skate lying still on the bottom, or a flounder lunging out of the sand to take a passing animal.
* **Fish and other animals.** Perch, flounder and other flatfish, skates, moon snails, geoducks, sea anemones, sea stars, and comb jellies and jellyfish through the water column.
* **Nudibranchs.** Frosted and striped nudibranchs work the sand and the sea pens.
* **Eelgrass.** A thin band along the shallows rather than a dense meadow.
