# Golden Gardens Park

A shore dive from the sand beach of Golden Gardens Park in Ballard, Seattle, on the east shore of Puget Sound's main basin just north of Shilshole Bay Marina. A shallow eelgrass flat off the beach gives way to a steep, even slope of sand and silt that passes 19 m below MLLW about 120 m out and keeps falling into the deep basin. The current is gentle, and the life is the small kind of a sand and eelgrass bottom: crabs, flatfish, gunnels and nudibranchs, with ratfish on occasion and harbor seals often about. The headline hazard is boat traffic from the marina and the Eddie Vine boat ramp to the south, and in summer from windsurfers, kiteboarders and personal watercraft launching off the beach.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Across the sand beach and straight out west into the water. The eelgrass flat sits at about 1 m below MLLW for the first 50 m from the entry, then the bottom drops quickly: about 3 m at 60 m out, 10 m at 80 m, 15 m at 100 m and 18.9 m at the dive area, 121 m out on a bearing of 270°.
* **Parking.** Free, in the lower lot off Golden Gardens Drive NW, about 120 m east-northeast of the entry. The lot fills by mid-morning on summer weekends; an overflow lot sits up the hill in the upper park. The lot gates open at 7 a.m.; before then, park along Seaview Avenue NW.
* **Access.** A Seattle Parks and Recreation park at 8498 Seaview Place NW, open 4 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. from May 1 to September 30 and 4 a.m. to 11:30 p.m. the rest of the year. Dogs are banned from the beach; the park's off-leash area is up the hill in the upper park. After heavy rain or a sewer overflow, Public Health Seattle and King County can close the beach to all water contact; check the Department of Ecology's beach closure map before diving.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms near the playground, the historic brick bathhouse, drinking fountains, picnic shelters and tables, and beach fire pits. The Eddie Vine boat ramp and a fishing pier on the marina breakwater sit at the south end of the park.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Meadow Point.** The low point just north of the entry, with a shallow flat running out from it at about 0 to 1 m below MLLW. North of the point the beach turns to cobble and larger rocks.
* **The eelgrass flat.** Covers the shallow shelf off the sand beach, and holds most of the site's small life.
* **The slope.** Plain sand and silt falling steadily west: about 19 m below MLLW at 120 m from the entry, 29 m at 170 m, 36 m at 220 m and past 50 m at 370 m. The bottom is bare and featureless, so depth is position: downhill is out, uphill is home.
* **The marina breakwater.** The north end of Shilshole Bay Marina's breakwater and the Eddie Vine boat ramp lie about 500 m south of the entry; the boat channel at the marina's north entrance is the edge of the site.

## Depth

* **Typical range.** Median daily 3.35 m, up to 4.98 m on the year's biggest exchange; 2026 span -1.30 m to +3.89 m.
* **Tidal swing.** The tide swings up to about 5 m here, so depths are given against MLLW.

## Current

The beach lies off the main tidal stream of the Sound, and the current here is gentle on every exchange.

* **Type.** Tidal.
* **Flood.** Sets south, 186°, along the shore toward the marina, at about 0.1 m/s near the bottom at its peak and rarely past 0.15 m/s; higher in the water column it reaches about 0.2 m/s.
* **Ebb.** Sets north, 6°, along the shore toward Meadow Point, at about 0.05 to 0.1 m/s near the bottom and up to about 0.2 m/s higher in the water column.
* **Diveable window.** The current stays under 0.25 m/s through the whole day on every exchange, so timing is forgiving and either slack works. Slack still gives the calmest water and the best visibility.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southwest, 232° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 214° |

The beach faces southwest across Puget Sound toward Bainbridge Island, with Meadow Point just north of the dive area and the marina breakwater to the south.

* **Bad.** Southwest through west-northwest (SW-WNW), onshore over 7.4 to 11.6 km of open water across the Sound, building chop straight onto the beach.
* **Short fetch.** South-southwest (SSW), onshore over 2.8 km across Shilshole Bay, enough for a short chop on the entry.
* **Mixed.** Northwest through north-northwest (NW-NNW), along the shore past Meadow Point over 8.3 to 21.2 km. North-northwest is the beach's strongest sailing wind, running chop down the length of the shore past the entry.
* **Fine.** North clockwise through south (N-S): offshore from north through east-southeast, north quartering off the beach past Meadow Point and the rest off the park and the bluff behind it; cross-shore from southeast and south-southeast over less than 1 km; and onshore from south over 710 m, sheltered by the marina breakwater and the bluff to the south.

## Visibility

* **Usually limited.** The sand and silt bottom stirs easily, and visibility typically runs 3 to 6 m, hazy to silty.
* **The shallows.** The eelgrass flat runs murkier than the slope, worst on an outgoing tide.
* **Seasons.** Spring and summer plankton blooms cut visibility, and winter is usually the clearest.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11 °C through the year. Drysuit year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms to about 12 to 13 °C in late summer over the shallow flat.

## Marine life

* **Eelgrass flat.** Juvenile kelp crabs, crescent gunnels, flatfish, perch, moon snails, hermit crabs and clingfish, with geoducks in the sand of the shallows.
* **The slope.** Nudibranchs of several species, sea pens, crabs, skates, and ratfish on occasion.
* **Mammals.** Harbor seals are common, and California sea lions pass offshore.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Shilshole Bay Marina's north entrance and the Eddie Vine boat ramp lie about 500 m south of the entry, with boats running in and out of the marina all day. Fly a dive flag on a float, and stay north of the marina entrance.
* **Beach launches.** In summer, windsurfers, kiteboarders and personal watercraft launch from the beach, and kiteboarders work the water off Meadow Point in a north to northwest wind. Surface close to shore along the slope.
* **The steep slope.** The bottom drops from the flat to 19 m below MLLW in about 70 m and keeps falling, bare and unbroken, into the deep basin. Watch the depth gauge on the descent.
* **Water quality.** The beach closes to water contact after sewer overflows and heavy rain. Check for a closure before diving.
* **Cold.** The water is cold all year, even after a warm summer day on the beach.
