# Seacrest Cove 1

Shore dive at the southeast end of Seacrest Park on the West Seattle waterfront, also known as Alki Cove 1, beside Salty's restaurant and facing the downtown Seattle skyline across Elliott Bay. It is a quiet training and night site on a gentle muddy slope, with old pilings, a broken pipeline and a storm outfall in the shallows, small critters throughout the shallows, and a concrete block called the monolith on the deep slope. The headline hazard is the slope itself: past the shallows it drops at a steady grade into deep water with little to stop a descent, and the deep landmarks sit near 30 m.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down from the concrete seawall ledge onto the rip-rap and cobble beach; the east end of the ledge is the easiest way down. The beach faces northeast, and the swim out runs straight off it, northeast. On a large minus tide the walk crosses a long stretch of steep, uneven rock, and the footing is tricky.
* **Parking.** Free curbside parking along Harbor Avenue SW beside the cove, and across the street along the condominium fence line. Salty's private lot is off limits to divers and tickets or tows. Overnight parking is prohibited. Weekends fill early with classes.
* **Access.** Seacrest Park, 1660 Harbor Avenue SW, is open 4 a.m. to 11:30 p.m. From I-5, exit 163 to the West Seattle Bridge, then the Harbor Avenue exit, turn right and follow the waterfront to the park. A two-way bike lane runs between the parking and the seawall; cross it with care in gear. Restrooms, a drinking fountain, a seasonal diver rinse station and an AED are at the Marination Ma Kai building, about 200 m northwest along the waterfront path.
* **Facilities.** The seawall ledge, wide and flat, serves as the staging area for gearing up.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Layout.** The cove runs from the public fishing pier at its northwest end to Salty's at its southeast end. A no-dive zone extends 46 m around the fishing pier, marked by yellow buoys at the surface and a perimeter line on the bottom; the next cove lies beyond it to the northwest.
* **Shallow reef.** A low rock reef lies just out from the beach in the first few metres of depth, and a band of rocks extends from shore near the northwest side of the cove. Both hold most of the shallow life.
* **Pilings and small pier.** A row of old pilings stands out in front of Salty's, the tallest showing at the surface, with a small pier beside them. The pilings run from the shallows out onto the upper slope and are grown over top to bottom.
* **Pipeline and storm outfall.** An old pipeline runs out from the shore across the shallows, and a storm outfall still in use discharges fresh water near the entry; the fresh water rolls out and rises toward the surface. The outfall keeps the bottom around it silty.
* **Trombones.** A structure divers call the trombones sits on the slope, crowded with growth.
* **The slope.** Gentle for the first 50 m out from the beach, then a short steep drop to about 11 m below MLLW 75 m out, and from there a steady grade of about 1 m in 6: 21 m at the site coordinate 130 m out, 30 m around 190 m out and 40 m around 250 m out on the swim-out bearing. Depth reads as distance from shore, and up the slope is home.
* **The monolith.** A large concrete block on the deep slope near the 30 m contour. A guide line runs to it from a can buoy in the cove; without the line it is easily missed on the featureless silt.
* **Deep wreckage.** A large piece of steel boat hull lies on the deep silt beyond the pilings, near 28 m, to the northwest when heading straight out from them. Beyond the hull the slope continues down over open silt.
* **Debris.** Old dinghies, tires and scattered junk lie across the northwest half of the cove toward the fishing pier; individual pieces shift and silt over through the years.

## Current

Current is negligible here. The cove sits deep inside Elliott Bay, sheltered from the main tidal streams, and stays diveable through the whole cycle on any exchange; pick the entry time for tide height, daylight and visibility.

* **Flood.** Sets south-southwest, onto the beach and toward Salty's. Daily peak at the station runs 0.10 to 0.22 m/s over 30 days, and the water at the cove itself moves far less.
* **Ebb.** Sets north, off the beach and toward the fishing pier. Daily peak at the station runs 0.07 to 0.14 m/s on the days it ebbs over 30 days; on 2 days in 30 the station predicts no ebb.
* **Diveable window.** Any tide. The station stays under 0.25 m/s every hour of every day across 30 days, so the whole day is one window.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | northeast, 49° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | northeast, 46° |

The beach faces northeast across Elliott Bay toward downtown Seattle.

* **Short fetch.** North through east-southeast (N-ESE), onshore over 1.1 to 4.3 km across the bay onto the entry, which builds chop on the rocks; a northerly has the longest fetch, in through the bay mouth.
* **Mixed.** North-northwest (NNW), cross-shore over 5.6 km down the Sound, running chop along the beach.
* **Fine.** Southeast clockwise through northwest (SE-NW): offshore from south through west-northwest, blocked by the West Seattle hill behind the park, and cross-shore under 1 km from southeast, south-southeast and northwest.
* **Wind against current.** The current is too weak to stack against any wind.

## Visibility

* **Range.** From under 1.5 m to 9 m or more. Typical days give 3 to 6 m, and late autumn and winter bring the clearest water.
* **Shallows.** Often the worst layer: the outfall, the fine silt and a turbid band from plankton or runoff can hold the shallows near 1.5 m while the water below 10 m opens up. A murky mid-water layer sometimes sits between clear shallows and clear depth.
* **Blooms and particulate.** Spring and summer blooms cut the visibility and fill the water with drifting marine snow and mucus strands.
* **Silt.** The muddy bottom stirs instantly under classes and careless fins, and weekend training churns the shallows by late morning.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** 8 to 13 °C through the year. Drysuit year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the mid teens in late summer while the slope stays cooler; dress for the depth.

## Marine life

* **Character.** A critter site on mud and silt: the life concentrates on the rocks, pilings, pipeline and debris in the shallows, more of it than on the open deep slope.
* **Squid and octopus.** Stubby squid in numbers, especially in late winter. Pacific red octopus turn up in bottles, pipes and debris, and giant Pacific octopus den under the pilings and the larger debris. The cove lies inside a WDFW octopus preserve, closed to octopus harvest.
* **Fish.** Sculpins of many kinds, red Irish lords, great and staghorn sculpins, cabezon, northern clingfish, gunnels and pricklebacks, poachers, plainfin midshipman, painted, kelp and whitespotted greenlings, copper rockfish, lingcod, ratfish, bay pipefish, schools of tubesnouts and needlefish, and striped and pile perch in summer. Starry flounder, sand dabs and skates lie on the mud and cobble.
* **Invertebrates.** Nudibranchs in variety (opalescent, leopard dorids, diamondback tritonia, sea lemons, white-lined dirona, striped and freckled dorids), orange sea pens, plumose anemones, the occasional painted anemone, creeping pedal sea cucumbers, brittle stars, hermit and kelp crabs, spot prawns and other shrimp, sea stars, and swarms of Pacific winged snails and jellies in season.
* **Seals.** Harbor seals frequent the cove and join night dives, hunting by divers' lights.

## Hazards

* **The slope.** Past the shallows the bottom drops at a steady grade and keeps going past 40 m on featureless silt. The monolith sits near 30 m and the hull near 28 m, where a long search off the line eats into the no-decompression limit quickly. Follow the guide line, watch the gauge, and turn on depth and time.
* **Boat traffic.** Small boats, jet skis and kayaks work this shoreline, heaviest in summer. City code requires the red diver down flag with a white diagonal stripe on the surface. Listen before any ascent and come up along the bottom to shore or up a line.
* **Fishing pier.** Diving is prohibited within 46 m of the fishing pier at the northwest end of the cove, inside the yellow buoys and the bottom perimeter line. Fishing line drapes the pilings and debris around it; carry a cutting tool.
* **Debris and garbage.** Monofilament, old fishing gear and trash cover parts of the slope. Watch for entanglement around the pilings and junk.
* **Entry at low tide.** On a large minus tide the steep rip-rap and loose rock make a long, awkward walk in gear.
* **Bike lane and road.** Fast bikes use the lane between the parking and the seawall, and Harbor Avenue carries steady traffic; take extra care crossing in gear, especially from parking across the street.
* **Silt.** The fine bottom fogs at a touch; keep off it, especially near the outfall and the pilings.
