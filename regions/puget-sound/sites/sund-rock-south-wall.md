# Sund Rock South Wall

Sund Rock South Wall is a boulder-strewn rock wall on the west shore of Hood Canal, between Hoodsport and Lilliwaup, reached through its own footpath entry with no swim needed. Boulder formations from about 6 to 15 m below MLLW hold some of the best wolf eel and giant Pacific octopus dens on this stretch of the canal, tame after years of protection in a marine preserve closed to harvesting. Current is negligible at every stage of the tide, so the dive is planned around visibility and access rather than water movement.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** A footpath drops down to a small, almost hidden beach, right onto the wall with no swim needed.
* **Reservation.** The private access is reservation only, booked in advance at sundrock.com. Entry is about 23 US dollars per diver including tax, with a reduced rate for veterans and first responders, and a dive card covering six dives for 100 US dollars bought on site. Reservations start at 08:00, or 09:00 on Mondays and Fridays, the last reservation is taken at 12:30, and the property closes to everyone at 17:00 sharp.
* **Parking.** Space for 10 to 12 vehicles at the gated ramp, which fills on a good weekend.
* **Access.** Beside Highway 101 about 3.5 km north of Hoodsport, roughly 60 km north of Olympia and 115 km from Seattle by road. The tidelands above the extreme low water line are private and posted.
* **Facilities.** Chemical toilet, gear-up benches and a rinse area.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Boulder formations.** Reached directly from the footpath entry, right onto the wall with no swim needed. Boulder formations between 6 and 15 m, holding some of the best wolf eel and octopus dens on the property.
* **Getting home.** The wall itself is the landmark. Keep it on one shoulder outbound and the other inbound, and the shallow water over the boulder formations leads back to the entry.

## Current

Sund Rock South Wall is dived independently of the tide. The current in the channel peaks around 0.25 m/s on a large exchange and is weaker again inshore in the bay, so there is no slack window to hit and no exchange size that closes the site.

* **Flood.** Sets southwest, filling the canal toward the Great Bend, and peaks around 0.25 m/s mid-channel.
* **Ebb.** Sets northeast, draining toward the entrance, and runs through the bay toward Sund Rock at its northeast point. Similar strength to the flood.
* **Slack.** This reach of the canal behaves as a standing wave, so slack falls close to high and low water rather than midway between them. Slack at the site falls close to high and low water at Union, which makes the tide table a usable guide to it here; the exact offset is not established and is confirmed in the water. This is a local exception, and it does not hold elsewhere in the Sound.
* **Diveable window.** All day. What little current there is rarely matters at this contained, shallower depth range.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | east-northeast, 70° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | north-northeast, 30° |

The bay opens east and northeast onto Hood Canal, backed by the steep ground of the Olympic foothills. The canal is only about 2 km wide here, so cross-canal fetch is short, but it runs a long way northeast to southwest.

* **Bad.** Northeast (NE), onshore over 9.0 km, both onshore into the bay and carrying the full down-canal fetch.
* **Short fetch.** North-northeast (NNE), onshore over 3.4 km, and east-northeast through southeast (ENE-SE), onshore over 2.0 to 3.1 km across the canal; though the fetch is short, they blow straight into the entry.
* **Mixed.** South (S), cross-shore over 10.5 km up the canal, roughly parallel to the shore, so it builds a swell that passes the entry rather than driving into it.
* **Fine.** South-southwest clockwise through north (SSW-N), offshore and blocked by the high ground behind the site or under 600 m of fetch; and south-southeast (SSE), cross-shore over 3.0 km.
* **Wind against current.** North-northeast through east-northeast (NNE-ENE) blows against the ebb, but the current is too weak to stack against a wind. Wind alone is what decides the surface, so read the beach forecast rather than the marine one.

## Depth and tide

Hood Canal swings up to about 5.6 m in a day here, and the tide is the single biggest control on how the site reads. A high tide adds several metres to every feature and floods the shallow bench, which is why the site is usually dived on a tide of 3 m or more. Those high daytime tides fall in winter, which is also when the visibility is best.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Best from October through March, when the water is cold, the plankton is down and the tides run high in daylight. A good winter day is clear enough to make the wall worth the drive.
* **Worst case.** Spring and summer plankton blooms and river runoff drop it sharply, and a bad summer day can be a couple of metres or less. Summer viz is usually better at depth than in the shallows, where the warm surface layer holds the bloom.
* **Torch.** Carry one on every dive. The colour in the crevices and the fish holding in them do not show without it.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 6 to 12 °C through the year, coldest in January and around 11 to 12 °C at the end of summer. Standard drysuit protocol.
* **Surface layer.** Hood Canal warms far more than the main Sound in summer, reaching 21 to 22 °C in July over water that is still around 11 °C at the wall base. The thermocline is sharp and the descent is abrupt.

## Marine life

* **Character.** A marine preserve closed to harvesting, and the density and tameness of the fish reflect it. Boulder formations against silty sand put a compact stretch of habitat within reach of a single dive.
* **Headliners.** Giant Pacific octopus, wolf eels in numbers, and lingcod tame enough to hold station beside a diver. Copper rockfish and other rockfish work the boulders.
* **Rock and wall.** White and orange plumose anemones, green and red Christmas anemones, swimming anemones, yellow branching sponge, bryozoans, sea cucumbers including white and California, and nudibranchs including giant rainbow and yellow-rimmed.
* **Crabs.** Red rock crabs backing out of cracks, and Dungeness, kelp, decorator, graceful decorator, sharp-nosed and galatheid crabs, with black-eyed hermit crabs on the sand.
* **Fish.** Pile perch, striped seaperch and shiner perch, grunt and fringed sculpins, crescent gunnels, ratfish and dogfish.
* **Sea stars and drifters.** Sunflower and velcro stars on the rock. Moon jellies, cross jellies and lion's mane drift through open water, and shrimp are everywhere on the bottom.
* **Mammals.** Harbour seals visit the site.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Small boats work this stretch of the canal all year. Listen throughout the dive, ascend up the wall or in the shallows rather than in open water, and look up the whole way. Fly a marker buoy.
* **Silt.** The bottom below the wall is fine silt over mud and shale, and a fin kick will erase the visibility for everyone behind you. Establish neutral buoyancy at each new depth and stay off the bottom.
* **Closing time.** The gate shuts at 17:00 sharp, which caps the second dive and the surface interval. Plan the day backward from it.
* **Private property.** The tidelands above the extreme low water line are private and posted.
* **Marine preserve.** No taking of marine life of any kind.
* **Cold.** Water at depth stays cold year round regardless of how warm the surface is in summer.
