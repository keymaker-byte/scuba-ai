# Three Tree Point (South)

Shore dive off the Maplewild Avenue street-end beach on the south side of Three Tree Point in Burien, facing East Passage and Vashon Island. A sandy bottom with eelgrass in the shallows steepens quickly into a slope scattered with tires, cinderblock anchors, the decayed remains of old cars, a canoe and an aircraft drop tank, with three small sunken boats further east, all of it grown over with plumose anemones and good for small critters. The current runs strong along this shore, with a westward set toward the point, and the slope continues past 30 m. The site is no longer accessible: there is no parking for divers anywhere near the beach access.

## Getting there

* **Status.** No longer accessible. Parking near the beach access is gone, so the site cannot be dived from shore.
* **Entry.** From the small public beach at 3599 SW 172nd Street, beside the guard rail and concrete barrier at the sharp bend where Maplewild Avenue SW reaches the shoreline at SW 172nd Street. Gear drops at the two benches in front of the beach. The beaches on either side are private; enter only from the public access.
* **Parking.** None for divers. The street end at the access is posted No Parking, reserved for water rescue and for unloading kayaks and paddleboards, and the residential streets around it hold no space set aside for diving. The parking at the coffee shop up the road is for its customers only.
* **Access.** Sunrise to sunset. From Highway 518 west through Burien, then Ambaum Boulevard SW, SW 152nd Street and the long, winding descent of Maplewild Avenue SW to the water; the access is on the right at that sharp bend.
* **Facilities.** Two benches and small trash bins put out by the neighbours, and nothing else; bring your own first aid kit and oxygen.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The shallows.** The sand and cobble beach slopes gently to about 1 m below MLLW, with eelgrass beds from there to about 4.5 m and fields of driftwood on the bottom, then the sand steepens and drops away fast.
* **Straight out.** The dive area lies about 77 m south of the entry. A lone tire sits at about 5.5 m, and a stack of tires at about 9 m lies almost straight out from the entry, close to the buoys off the beach. Swimming to the buoys on the surface and descending there saves gas.
* **East along the slope.** Most of the debris lies east of the entry. An overturned canoe sits at about 20 m and the remains of an aircraft drop tank at about 18 m, then further east the remains of an old car beside a square gas tank at about 14 m, another old car among a cluster of barrels at about 18 m, a log and a stump at 18 to 20 m, and piles of tires and a tire tangled in rope deeper still, at 24 to 27 m.
* **The three boats.** About fifteen minutes east by scooter along the 9 to 11 m contour lie three small boats within a minute of each other: an overturned white boat of about 3 m, a rowboat of about 2.5 m with a buoy line tied to it, and a bluish cabin boat of about 6 m that holds schools of fish. They are a scooter dive.
* **West of the entry.** A sunken circular plate at about 15 m, and a bucket and sunken cylinder at about 27 m.
* **The older wreck line.** A compass heading of 135° magnetic from the access leads southeast down the slope toward the car remains, dumped decades ago and now in advanced decay and hard to recognise.
* **Return.** Work back up the slope to the eelgrass and swim the shallows to the entry, finishing with a safety stop over the sand.

The current runs strong along this shore, and the shoreline set runs west toward the point on both exchanges.

* **Flood.** Sets southeast down East Passage off the slope, while along the shore the water turns counterclockwise and runs west toward the point. Daily peaks of 0.19 to 0.42 m/s at the station over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Sets northwest off the slope, and along the shore it also runs west toward the point. At the site the ebb runs noticeably stronger than a flood of comparable size. Daily peaks of 0.12 to 0.25 m/s at the station over the same 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Slack before the flood and a mild flood are the calmer times here, and slack before the flood also brings the best visibility. Current at the station stays under 0.25 m/s for about 10 to 23 hours at a time, broken by 1 to 4 hours around the stronger daily floods.
* **Where the current sits.** It runs strongest near the surface and in the shallows, pushing west toward the point.

## Wind

|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | south, 180° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | south, 180° |

The beach faces due south across East Passage toward Vashon Island, with the point itself running out to the west.

* **Bad.** South-southeast through southwest (SSE-SW), onshore over 5.9 to 10.8 km of open water down East Passage and across to Vashon, which builds chop on the entry.
* **Short fetch.** East-southeast through southeast (ESE-SE), onshore over 1.7 to 2.9 km.
* **Fine.** West-southwest clockwise through east (WSW-E): offshore from west-northwest through northeast, west-northwest over 4.5 km of water past the point and the rest over 140 m or less off the land behind the beach, and cross-shore over 4.8 km or less from west-southwest and west, and 380 m or less from east-northeast and east.
* **Wind against current.** East-southeast through south (ESE-S) blows against the southeast setting flood, and west-northwest (WNW), over 4.5 km, against the northwest setting ebb; either steepens the surface chop.

## Visibility

* **Character.** Silty, often only a couple of metres in the shallows and clearer deeper on the slope.
* **Tide.** Slack before the flood brings the clearest water.
* **Blooms.** Spring and summer plankton blooms are the usual spoiler, with clearer water returning in autumn.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 8 °C in late winter to about 12 °C in late summer. Standard drysuit protocol.

## Marine life

* **Character.** A critter site: the debris on an otherwise plain sand slope concentrates fish and invertebrates, and a slow search turns up far more than a quick swim.
* **On the structure.** Plumose anemones cover the tires, cinderblocks, car remains and boats, with tube worms on the tires. Red octopus and wolf eels den in the debris, and copper rockfish, young-of-the-year rockfish, kelp greenling and schools of pile perch hold to it. The cabin boat in the east carries the densest schools.
* **On the sand.** Orange sea pens, California sea cucumbers, tunicates, heart cockles, hermit crabs, red rock crabs and shrimp, with C-O sole, speckled sanddabs and other flatfish lying in the sand and ratfish cruising the deeper ground.
* **Nudibranchs.** Striped nudibranchs on the sea pens, alabaster, opalescent, hooded and diamondback nudibranchs, and sea lemons.
* **Sea stars.** Vermilion, spiny and leather stars.
* **Sculpins and small fish.** Pacific staghorn sculpins, gunnels, snake pricklebacks and spearnose poachers along the bottom, and an occasional Pacific spiny lumpsucker in the eelgrass.
* **Water column.** Moon jellies and egg yolk jellies drift through, and at night ratfish, skates and dogfish come up the slope.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Strong along this shore, and both exchanges set west toward the point. Turn back well short of the tip of the point; past it the set carries a diver into the shipping lane between the point and Vashon Island.
* **Steep slope.** The sand drops away quickly past the eelgrass and continues past 30 m; watch depth and the no-decompression limit.
* **Boats and crab pots.** Small boats work the water off the beach, and crab pots and their lines are set across the site. Fly a surface marker buoy for any open water ascent.
* **Entanglement.** Tires, rope, barrels and car remains all snag. Carry a cutting tool.
* **Access and neighbours.** Private beaches on both sides of the access and a quiet residential street; stay on the public beach.
