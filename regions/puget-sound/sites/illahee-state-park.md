# Illahee State Park

Illahee State Park sits on the Kitsap Peninsula's east shore in Bremerton, on the Port Orchard reach directly across from Bainbridge Island. A public fishing pier and small boat dock run out from the beach, giving an easy shore entry straight down to wharf pilings thick with the small, colorful life that collects on a dock's underside. Past the pilings a smooth sand bottom slopes gradually out to the site's usual working depth before continuing to drop well beyond it. Boats mooring and launching at the dock, and fishing line left on the bottom near the pier, are the site's main hazard.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down a short flight of stairs beside the fishing pier, at the boat launch and pier day use area. Enter on the pier's far side and swim out along the pilings.
* **Parking.** A lot at the boat launch and pier day use area, right next to the water. A Discover Pass is required at the automated pay station, $10 for a single day or $45 annual.
* **Access.** Washington State Parks land, 3540 NE Sylvan Way, Bremerton. Open 8 a.m. to dusk year round.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms at the boat launch and pier day use area in the lower park, hot showers up in the camping area, picnic tables, a covered eating area and barbecue stands, the fishing pier and small boat dock themselves, a boat launch, and a campground with 23 standard sites and one full hookup site.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Entry and pilings.** From the stairs, cross to the pier's far side and follow the wharf pilings straight out. The dock overhangs the shallow first stretch, thick with the small life that collects on the underside of a floating structure.
* **Past the pilings.** Beyond the pier, the sand bottom drops gradually, reaching the site's usual working depth, about 13.6 m, roughly 200 m out.
* **Depth creep.** The same sand slope keeps dropping past that point, reaching roughly 25 to 30 m within another 100 to 200 m. Most of the life described below sits well inside that, above the working depth.

## Current

Current here is negligible at every stage of the tide, at the surface and depth-averaged alike, so this is not a slack-tide dive.

* **Flood.** Sets NNW, up the Port Orchard reach toward Agate Passage and Brownsville, negligible in strength and staying under about 0.15 m/s at any stage of the tide.
* **Ebb.** Sets SSE, down the reach toward Rich Passage and the main basin, similarly weak.
* **Shallows.** The water above the pilings can push harder on an incoming tide, enough to work against right at the dock, even with the rest of the site sitting under 0.15 m/s. Treat the top few metres near the pier with more caution than the open current axis alone would suggest.
* **Diveable window.** All day, any tide stage. Current is not the limiting factor at this site; boat traffic and visibility are.

## Hazards

* **Boats and fishing line.** An active boat launch and small boat moorage dock share the site with the diver, and the pier draws steady fishing traffic; monofilament line and lures collect on the bottom near the pilings. Stay clear of the underside of any moored boat, and listen for engine noise before ascending, surfacing between the pilings rather than in open water while boats are active.
* **Depth creep.** The sand slope keeps dropping well past the site's usual working depth. Watch depth on any push beyond the pilings.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The site faces roughly northeast, across the Port Orchard reach toward Bainbridge Island.
* **Bad.** North through east, the open fetch down the reach from Agate Passage and across the water to Bainbridge Island.
* **Fine.** South through west, blocked by the Kitsap Peninsula landmass behind the site.
* **Wind against current.** Not a real factor here; the current stays too weak at every stage of the tide to stack meaningfully against any wind.

## Visibility

* **Character.** Follows the same broad pattern as the rest of central Puget Sound, worst through spring and summer plankton blooms and better outside that season.
* **Tide.** Opens up toward 6 m near low slack and drops toward 3 m within a couple of hours as the tide starts moving; the upper few metres turn turbulent on an incoming tide even while the bottom stays comparatively clear.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11°C through the year, in line with the wider central Sound. Standard drysuit protocol year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Pilings and dock.** Colorful sea anemones, tube worms, sea squirts, pile perch and striped seaperch, hermit crabs and kelp crabs, and an abundance of nudibranchs on the structure itself.
* **Sand slope.** Flounder, blood stars, leather stars, Stimpson's sun stars, sunflower stars, sea cucumbers, Dungeness crab, moon snails, and geoduck.
* **Open water.** Jellyfish drift through over the sand.
