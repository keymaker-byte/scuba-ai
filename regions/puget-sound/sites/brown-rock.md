# Brown Rock

Small rock formation in the cove on the Orcas Island side of Obstruction Pass, reached by a long surface swim from the beach beside the Obstruction Pass county pier near Olga. Its outer side is crowded with colorful invertebrates, jagged ledges step down to a shale, sand and mud bottom, and a narrow, shallow channel cuts through the rock itself. Current is the headline hazard: the inshore water runs along the shore toward the pier on both the flood and the ebb and never truly slacks, so the swim out and back is planned around it.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk in off the beach between the county pier and the Obstruction Pass boat ramp, about 45 m from the lot. The dive area off the rock's west side lies about 380 m east-southeast of the entry, a long surface swim across the cove.
* **Parking.** The small public lot beside the county pier and Lieber Haven Resort and Marina, with limited spaces. Cost not established.
* **Access.** Reach Orcas Island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes. From the Orcas ferry landing turn left on Horseshoe Highway for 13.0 km, turn right into Eastsound and drive 1.9 km to a tee, then turn right again and follow Horseshoe Highway 10.5 km past the Rosario turnoff and through Moran State Park. Turn left at Café Olga onto Point Lawrence Road, then right on Obstruction Pass Road for 4.0 km to the marina and the county pier, about 29 km from the ferry in all. Brown Rock is a unit of the San Juan Islands National Wildlife Refuge: landing on the rock is prohibited, and the waters within about 180 m of it are closed to watercraft, which keeps boats and kayaks well off the rock and makes the dive a swim from shore. Access hours not established.
* **Facilities.** The county pier with its float, the boat ramp and a portable toilet.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The swim out.** The rock is the obvious rock in the cove, east-southeast of the entry. On the flood, head straight out from the county pier through the inshore current running toward the pier, into the main channel flow, which carries the rest of the swim to the rock. On a small exchange a direct swim is possible, starting shortly before slack in the main channel. The water under the swim runs about 2 to 6 m below MLLW, and mooring buoys in the cove make rest stops.
* **West side.** The dive coordinate sits about 50 m off the rock's west side, 14.5 m below MLLW. Descend here and circle the rock along its angular faces.
* **Outer side.** The south and west faces drop as a series of jagged ledges to the base of the rock, and a moderate slope continues to a shale, sand and mud bottom 19 m below MLLW, some 60 to 150 m south and southwest of the rock.
* **Inside channel.** A narrow, shallow channel runs through the rock formation on its inshore side, about 1 to 5 m below MLLW, its smooth rock sides covered in limpets and chitons.
* **The return.** Move to the inshore side of the rock before starting back, where the inshore current carries the swim toward the county pier.

## Current

The rock sits in the cove off the main channel of Obstruction Pass, and the channel flow wraps into the cove as a counter-clockwise eddy along the Orcas shore.

* **Flood.** Enters Obstruction Pass from its southwest end and drives onto Brown Rock, where it splits in two. Inshore of the rock the flood turns counter-clockwise and runs back west along the shore toward the county pier and the boat ramp. Daily peaks at the station run 0.45 to 1.19 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** Enters Obstruction Pass at its east end and follows the curve of the Orcas shore west past Brown Rock, the county pier and the boat ramp, then out along the south-southwest shore of the cove. Daily peaks at the station run 0.40 to 0.92 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** Both slacks serve. Under 0.25 m/s at the station, the slack before ebb typically runs 72 to 138 minutes. The slack before flood typically runs 60 to 120 minutes, shrinks to about 40 minutes ahead of the strongest floods, and stretches to several hours on days when the weaker of the two daily floods stays under the threshold.
* **No slack inshore.** Along the shore between the rock and the pier the water runs west toward the pier on both the flood and the ebb. It slows at mid-channel slack and picks up again in the same direction.
* **At the rock.** The flow splits around the rock and shifts direction around it, pushing a diver back and forth among the boulders.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | southeast, 142° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | southwest, 223° |

The entry shore faces the cove and Obstruction Island across the pass; the dive area facing is the rock's own southwest face.

* **Bad.** East-southeast through southeast (ESE-SE), onshore over 5.7 to 9.4 km of open water down the east entrance of the pass, building chop on the beach and across the swim out.
* **Mixed.** Southwest (SW), cross-shore over 7.3 km down the west entrance of the pass, running swell past the entry.
* **Fine.** South-southeast through south-southwest (SSE-SSW), blocked by Obstruction Island 410 to 670 m across the pass, and west-southwest clockwise through east (WSW-E), blocked by Orcas Island itself, with 570 m of fetch at most.
* **Wind against current.** East-southeast (ESE), over 5.7 km, blows against the east flood set. Southwest (SW), over 7.3 km, blows against the west ebb set and the west inshore set along the shore.

## Depth and tide

The site swings about 2.6 m on a median day, and up to 3.9 m on the year's biggest exchange, so depths are quoted against the MLLW chart datum: a minus tide reads shallower than the datum figure, and any height above MLLW reads deeper. The top of the rock stands above the water at low tide.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Best in winter and early spring before the bloom. Spring and summer plankton blooms bring it down hard.
* **Current.** Running water through the pass stirs the bottom and lowers it.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Outer side of the rock.** Colorful invertebrates crowd the outer faces. Orange sea cucumbers, white sea cucumbers, red sea urchins and giant acorn barnacles dominate, with golden staghorn bryozoans, orange cup corals, chunks of white sponge and anemones attached to the rock.
* **Grazers and stars.** Gumboot chitons, lined chitons, limpets and green urchins, with purple sea stars, Stimpson's sun stars, blood stars and six-rayed stars.
* **Crevices and boulders.** Nudibranchs and hermit crabs among the ledges and the boulders at the base.
* **Inside channel.** Limpets and chitons cover the smooth sides of the rocks.

## Hazards

* **Current.** The inshore water never slacks: it runs west toward the pier on both tides and fights the swim out to the rock. Keep the dive to days with exchanges under about 1.7 m between successive tides, and to divers fit for a long swim in current.
* **Long surface swim.** About 380 m each way across open water. Mooring buoys in the cove make rest stops on the way out; the return rides the inshore current from the inshore side of the rock.
* **Boat traffic.** Small boats use the boat ramp, the county pier, the marina next door and the pass itself, all across the swim route. Fly a dive flag and keep a tight group at the surface.
* **Wildlife refuge.** The rock is closed to landing and the waters within about 180 m are closed to watercraft. Surface and rest in the water, clear of the rock and the wildlife on it.
* **Wind chop.** Wind from east-southeast through southeast raises chop on the beach and along the whole swim, and southwest wind runs swell past the entry.
