# Burrows Pass (Skyline Wall)

Shore dive on the north side of Burrows Pass, between Fidalgo Head and Burrows Island, near Skyline Marina in Anacortes. The site is a run of rock ledges dropping into a tidal channel, reached by a surface swim of about ten minutes from the beach. Current is the defining feature: the pass runs fast, and the only reliable window is the slack before the ebb. It is an advanced dive.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** At the end of Cabana Way by Skyline Marina, where the public road ends at a fence marked "Private Property". The beach north of the fence is private.
* **Parking.** On the street, or in the adjacent marina lot, which is free but limited.
* **Access.** Open 24 hours. About 75 mi from Seattle and 43 mi from Bellingham.
* **Facilities.** None on site. Washington Park, 0.9 mi away, has restrooms, picnic tables and barbecues. Air fills are available in Anacortes and Oak Harbor.

## Navigation and landmarks

The dive runs out along the ledges and returns the same way. Working from the entry:

* **Entry.** Shallow sand with an eelgrass meadow and wide-blade kelp.
* **Surface swim.** South-southwest for about ten minutes on the last of the flood, past a house on a concrete bulkhead, to the kelp bed.
* **The slope.** A sandy cobblestone slope below the kelp, a barren transition.
* **First landmarks.** A first kelp stipe on a lone rock, then more stipes on more rocks, then the corner of a larger rock.
* **The ledges.** They begin past that corner. The near, north-northeast end sits 7.9 m below datum and runs southwest, deepening to 17.4 m.
* **Outer structure.** Beyond a loose-rock slope, smaller rock structures lie at 22.3 m and 23.5 m below datum.

Finding the ledge from the surface:

* **In the growing season.** Kelp marks it; submerge at the north-northeast end of the kelp bed.
* **In winter, with no kelp.** Look for a cut in the upper bank, just before the shoreline turns continuously rocky. The ledge is directly offshore of the cut.
* **Bearings from the near end of the ledge.** 020° to the outer edge of the concrete bulkhead, and 285° to the south-southwest side of the bank cut.

Safety stops work at the shallow north-northeast end of the ledge, or on the sandy slope.

## Current

Diveable on the slack before the ebb only. Do not dive on a flood exchange or at the slack before a flood.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Governing station** | NOAA Burrows Pass current station (PUG1738), at the site |
| **Recommended bin** | bin 23, at 8.5 m. NOAA's default bin is shallower, at 4.6 m |
| **Time offset** | None; the station sits at the site |
| **Flood axis** | west-northwest, 283° |
| **Ebb axis** | east, 89° |

* **Flood.** Sets southwest past the entry and out of the pass, around Fidalgo Head and into Rosario Strait. Strong, peaking around 1.8 to 2.1 m/s.
* **Ebb.** Sets northeast, back into the pass toward the entry and exit. Gentler, peaking around 0.9 to 1.3 m/s.
* **Layered reversal.** The ebb begins at the surface and works downward. Early in a low-volume ebb, water above about 13.7 m can already run northeast toward the exit while water below still runs southwest, outbound, on the tail of the flood. The transition band sits roughly 12 to 18 m below the surface. Ascend to about 12 m or shallower to catch the homeward ebb.
* **Exchange size.** A small ebb may never reverse the deep flow, and the back eddy that carries you home may not form; too large an exchange is also unwelcome. A moderate ebb is the target. On a failed reversal the flow can sweep west onto the rocks and cliffs at the point.

## Depth and tide

The pass swings roughly 2 to 4 m between high and low water, so depths are given relative to the MLLW chart datum. A low tide reads shallower than the datum figure; a high tide reads deeper.

## Hazards

* **Swift current.** A flood, or an ebb that fails to reverse, sets west out of the pass onto the rocks and cliffs at the point, then into Rosario Strait.
* **Back eddies.** Form on several sides of the site, running counter to the main flow.
* **Kelp.** Entanglement risk; the outbound leg runs under the canopy.
* **Boat traffic.** A working channel with the marina adjacent.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The entry beach is on the north shore of the pass, facing south-southwest toward Burrows Island.
* **Bad.** Southwest through west, straight up the axis of the pass from its Rosario Strait opening.
* **Fine.** North, northeast and east are offshore.
* **Wind against current.** The homeward ebb runs east, so a westerly opposes it on the leg spent at the surface. That is the combination to avoid.

## Visibility

* **Water.** Well-flushed Rosario Strait water, generally good.
* **Blooms.** Spring and summer plankton blooms are the usual spoiler.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 8 to 12 °C year round, and 11 to 12 °C in summer. Standard drysuit protocol.

## Marine life

* **Character.** This is filter-feeder country. The same current that makes the site demanding pours cold, clean, nutrient-rich Rosario Strait water past fixed rock, so the ledges are carpeted with encrusting life on every surface. A current-swept wall in the San Juans, and it dives like one, with the invertebrate cover as the reward for the timing it takes to get in.
* **The rock.** Yellow and other sponges, orange cup corals, northern staghorn bryozoans and giant acorn barnacles encrust the ledges, with green and red urchins and gumboot chitons grazing among them. Sea cucumbers in orange, white, red-tentacled and faintly purple forms cover much of the shallow rock, and scallops sit on the ledges.
* **Anemones and their guests.** Christmas anemones and crimson rose anemones stud the wall. The rose anemones are worth a close look, as candy-stripe shrimp shelter among their tentacles unharmed, one of the classic Salish Sea partnerships.
* **Sea stars.** Blood, brittle, Stimpson's, sun, slime and six-rayed stars work the rock, part of the diverse sea-star fauna these cold, well-flushed waters are known for.
* **Crabs.** Decorator, kelp, helmet, hermit, umbrella and sharp-nosed crabs live on and around the structure, and the Puget Sound king crab, a large, armoured red-and-orange animal of the San Juans and Rosario Strait, is a prized find here.
* **Fish and cephalopods.** Octopus, wolf eels (juveniles are seen shallow, above about 11 m), rockfish, perch, red Irish lords, kelp greenlings, grunt and buffalo sculpins, sand lances, plainfin midshipmen in the sand, and tube-snouts beneath the kelp canopy.
* **In the shallows.** An eelgrass meadow and wide-blade kelp lie between the entry and the kelp beds, nursery habitat that contrasts with the invertebrate-covered rock below.
