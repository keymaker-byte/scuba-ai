# Rockaway Beach (Norrander's Reef)

Natural rock reef shore dive on the southeast shore of Bainbridge Island, at the mouth of Blakely Harbor where it opens onto the main basin of Puget Sound across from Alki Point and West Seattle. A narrow, craggy rib of Seattle fault line boulders and outcrops, some standing nearly vertical, runs roughly west to east from about 8 to 26 m below MLLW, packed with life in every crack and crevice. It is the only shore accessible piece of a wider reef system reaching south toward Blakely Harbor and Blakely Rock, the rest of it a boat or kayak dive. Current at the reef itself is usually mild, and a small park with room for only a handful of cars is the main practical constraint on a visit.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Down the park's stairway at the north end, over large rocks that run slippery, then a gentle slope to gear up at the waterline. Take care with the footing, especially carrying kit.
* **Parking.** Limited street parking along Rockaway Beach Road NE, room for only a handful of cars, among private houses; it fills fast.
* **Access.** A quiet residential street on the southeast side of Bainbridge Island, off Eagle Harbor Drive NE continuing onto Rockaway Beach Road NE, about 15 minutes from the Winslow ferry terminal.
* **Facilities.** A picnic table and benches in the small park; no restrooms. Winslow is the nearest town.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Descent line.** A small buoy, when present, marks a fixed line running straight out from the north end of the park to the top of the reef. Follow it down.
* **Turn point.** Descend to about 17 m, then turn north onto the reef. Without the buoy, snorkel out about 15 m, descend, and hold a bearing of about 060° to reach the shallow ledges at 8 to 11 m, then about 090° on to the deeper reef at 15 to 18 m.
* **The reef.** Narrow and easy to miss in a single pass: a rock rib running roughly west to east, outcrops and cracks rising up to about 3 m off the surrounding sand, in places extending further south than older maps of the site show.
* **South along the rib.** Ropes at the far end of the reef lead toward two more fingers of the same system, known locally as Metridium Wall and Leiker's Reef. Both are a real surface swim from this entry, or better reached by boat or kayak, and not worth pushing to except in calm, settled conditions.

* **Flood.** Sets south, into the sound, and runs with a northwest wind rather than against it.
* **Ebb.** Sets north-northeast, back out toward the strait. It is the stronger, more troublesome phase at this site.
* **Diveable window.** Best on the flood or the slack ahead of the ebb. On a small exchange, under about 2 m of tidal range, the ebb itself stays manageable; on a larger exchange, plan to be out of the water before it builds.

## Depth and tide

The site sits on the open main basin, and tide swings the depth here by several metres, enough to change which parts of the reef are comfortably worked at a given hour.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** Recreational and fishing boats work this stretch, and the Bremerton fast ferry transits Rich Passage nearby. Fly a marker buoy and listen before ascending.
* **Northwest wind.** Builds surf directly on this beach and is the condition to check before committing to the entry.
* **Slippery entry.** The stairway lands on large rocks before the gentler slope to the water; footing is the main risk carrying gear in.
* **Harbor seals.** Sometimes hauled out or working the area; keep at least 90 m away.
* **No collection.** Marine life may not be taken at the park.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** The beach faces east-northeast across the main basin toward Alki Point and West Seattle, tucked into a small cove.
* **Bad.** Northwest, which runs a long fetch down the main basin and lands as surf directly on this beach.
* **Fine.** Wind out of the south is comparatively sheltered, blocked by the point and the wooded shore behind the cove.
* **Wind against current.** The ebb sets north-northeast, out of the sound. A northwest wind opposing that ebb is the combination to avoid; the same northwest wind runs with the flood and stays calmer.

## Visibility

* **Tide dependent.** Best on a calm day at high tide; low water adds a longer walk and swim and stirs the shallows.
* **Variable.** Conditions swing widely between visits, from very good to disappointing, more than at most central Sound sites.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 8 to 14°C through the year, in line with the wider central Sound, coldest in late winter and spring, warmest at the end of summer. Standard drysuit protocol year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms into the low teens in summer.

## Marine life

* **Fish.** Lingcod, sometimes guarding egg clutches on the reef in late winter and spring and reluctant to leave the nest, quillback rockfish, cabezon, tube-snouts, blue-striped and pile perch, painted greenling (which turn black and brown rather than purple and gray while mating), scalyhead sculpin, and the occasional wolf eel.
* **Octopus.** Small octopus on the reef, sometimes with a midden of crab shells outside the den; giant Pacific octopus turns up occasionally.
* **Invertebrates.** Dense white plumose anemones, orange sea cucumbers wedged between the rocks, red rock crabs in the kelp (mating when the timing is right), kelp crabs, coonstripe shrimp, and shrimp colonies lining crevice walls.
* **Sea stars.** Orange sun stars the size of a dinner plate, purple striped stars, and blood stars work the rock.
* **Nudibranchs.** Alabaster, lemon peel (frequent here), brown-striped, and leopard nudibranchs.
* **Snails.** Moon snail egg cases on the bottom, the snails themselves seen more often on a high tide.
* **Other.** Ratfish, harbor seals hauled out or working the area, and cormorants hunting underwater at the reef.
