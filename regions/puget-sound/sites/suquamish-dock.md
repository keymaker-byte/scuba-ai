# Suquamish Dock

Suquamish Dock is a long public dock and boat ramp on the Suquamish waterfront, in Port Madison proper rather than in Agate Passage, the fast tidal channel between Bainbridge Island and the Kitsap Peninsula that opens into Port Madison a couple of kilometres south. Where that channel runs hard on the tide, this stretch of harbor is sheltered and close to slack all the time, a low key, shallow dive over a gently sloping sandy bottom scattered with old bottles and glass from the site's long history as a village and trading spot.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** The Suquamish waterfront dock, or the Charles Lawrence Memorial Boat Ramp immediately beside it. The dock has ladders of a sort, but the boat ramp is the easier way in and out.
* **Parking.** Free, with room for vehicles and trailers at the ramp; the preferred spot is across the road on Suquamish Way.
* **Access.** Usable at any tide level. This is Port Madison Indian Reservation land, not a designated public dive site, and diving here is by the Suquamish Tribe's ongoing tolerance rather than a formal right; ask first. The Tribe's Community Development department, (360) 394-8423, manages the dock; the general administration line, (360) 598-3311, is the fallback if that doesn't reach the right person. Be respectful and low key about it.
* **Facilities.** Seasonal portable restrooms nearby. Nothing at the dock itself.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The dock.** Metal pilings, thick with kelp, run the length of the dock, which is unusually long because the bottom underneath it slopes so gently.
* **Bottle ground.** The sandy bottom near the dock carries old bottles and glass, remnants of the site's long use as a village and trading location going back to well before the dock itself; much of it is heavily barnacle encrusted.

## Current

Negligible at every stage of the tide. This stretch of Port Madison sits off to the side of Agate Passage's main flow, and it shows: the site's own ENPAC15 extract predicts a peak of only about 0.14 m/s all day, against well over 1 m/s in the passage itself, a couple of kilometres south.

## Hazards

* **Boat traffic.** A working dock and boat ramp, with launch traffic to watch for on the surface.
* **Glass and metal debris.** The old bottles and dock hardware on the bottom are part of the draw, but broken glass and rusted metal are both real cut hazards.
* **Access is informal.** Diving here rests on the Tribe continuing to permit it rather than on any formal designation; a low profile keeps it that way.

## Visibility

* **Not current flushed.** Sheltered from the passage's flow, so this stretch of harbor is not scoured clear the way a fast tidal channel is. Expect the same broader seasonal swings as the rest of Puget Sound, worst through spring and summer plankton blooms, rather than the current driven clarity pattern of a genuinely current-swept site.

## Marine life

* **Pilings.** Kelp grows thick on the dock's metal pilings, the main marine growth documented here.
