# Redondo

Shore dive at Redondo Beach in Des Moines, on the mainland shore of East Passage, the most popular shore dive in the South Sound and the place Des Moines takes its title of Sixgill Shark Capital of the World from. Highline College's MaST Center Aquarium stands on the MaST pier in the middle of the site, and heavy rope lines run from it down a steep slope through a field of sunken boats, statues, pipes, rock piles and old bottles to a silty plain at the foot of the slope. Easy stairs, a lot across the street and life everywhere on the structure make it a favourite for classes, night dives and photographers. The headline hazards are the plain itself, where uphill is not always the way home and the bottom falls away again past its outer edge, and fishing lines and boat traffic from the pier and the launch beside the site.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Cross Redondo Beach Drive S from the lot and take the main stairs down to the beach, just south of the MaST pier. A shallow flat runs out to the end of the pier, where the bottom drops away; swim out on the surface at high water, or walk most of the way at low water. Descend toward the middle of the end of the pier, clear of its southwest corner. Two other entries serve the edges of the site: the beach north of the aquarium, shallower and lined with structure, the gentlest start for a new diver, and the shore south of the boat launch, which deepens faster and is the better choice when wind waves are running onto the main stairs. On the south side, enter well clear of the ramp.
* **Parking.** The gated pay lot at 28280 Redondo Beach Drive S, across the road from the beach, with restrooms and showers in it. Rates are $2.50 for the first 2 hours, $1.50 for each hour after that, and $10 for 6 hours or more, $12 for a truck and trailer, and $10 for a lost ticket. Payment is at the exit gate, by card or Apple Pay only; no cash. Street parking along Redondo Beach Drive S takes the overflow and suits a larger group.
* **Access.** The lot is open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily, and overnight parking in it is prohibited; check the posted street signs before a late night dive. The city closes the lot for resurfacing and other works from time to time, announced in advance. The Redondo Fishing Pier beside the site reopened in 2026 after a full rebuild.
* **Facilities.** Restrooms and diver showers in the pay lot, rinse hoses at the boat launch, a two-lane launch ramp with seasonal floats, and the MaST Center Aquarium on its pier, open to the public on Saturdays. An underwater camera at the old observation pipe streams the site live, lit for night viewing, and shows the visibility before you kit up.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The flat and the drop.** From the stairs the bottom stays nearly level, close to datum, for about 80 m out to the end of the MaST pier. There it breaks over steeply, about 30°, and falls to around 21 m within another 40 m. The aquarium's intake pipework and lines hang off the southwest corner of the pier end.
* **The deep line.** A heavy rope starts a little way down the slope from the middle of the pier end and runs generally west down the slope. A statue stands beside it partway down, and the line ends at the Carousel boat, named for the carousel horse on its bow, near the foot of the slope where the plain begins, at the head of a trough in the bottom. The stern points down the trough, and a trail of cinder blocks leads on down it to scattered bottles and a toilet. A decrepit rowboat lies a few metres past the stern, to the right looking aft.
* **The Black Pearl.** A small runabout north of the Carousel boat, reached by a thin line from near it. A larger rope beyond it leads to another small boat, deeper still.
* **The pipe.** From the statue, head north past an overturned bathtub-like structure to the pipe, an old aquarium observation tube about 1 m across, with the live camera at its north end beside a pair of shark statues. A small boat lies just below the pipe. This is the most popular stretch of the site for a new diver.
* **The shallow line.** A rope runs north from the pier area, roughly parallel to the shore, past rock piles, stacked pipes, PVC diamond swim-throughs and the remains of a Volkswagen Beetle, corroded down to its chassis and barely recognisable, with a collapsed PVC dome near it. Northeast of the line's north end, an eelgrass bed fills the shallows.
* **South of the launch.** The shallows south of the boat launch are known as Lumpy Land. Downslope lie Bob's Reef, three rock piles, with Fort Dondo, a newer reef build, upslope of it, and an engine block below it. Deeper again is the south bottle field. Yard waste dumped off the ramp has buried its northern part under bark; its southern part remains in good shape. Loose ropes lie across it.
* **The plain.** Below the boats the slope eases into a silty plain at about 28 to 31 m below MLLW, which reaches about 300 m from the stairs before the bottom falls away again, past 40 m within another 100 m. It holds scattered bottles and debris, and it is a long swim back to the slope from its outer edge.
* **Finding the way back.** The bottom has humps and hollows that mislead: the bottom rises in places away from shore, so uphill can lead the wrong way. Southeast generally leads back toward the pier. Take a bearing before leaving the deep line, and check the compass before starting back up from the deep end of the site.

## Current

The station describes East Passage; the water on the slope moves far less than the station, and the current that matters here comes in short bursts along the shallows on the biggest exchanges.

* **Flood.** Sets east toward the slope at the foot of the deep line, at only 0.01 to 0.02 m/s near the bottom. Daily peaks run 0.18 to 0.34 m/s at the station over 30 days. On a large flood an unpredictable current runs along the shore in the shallows, strongest toward the north eelgrass.
* **Ebb.** Sets west-southwest at the foot of the deep line, just as weakly. Daily peaks run 0.06 to 0.22 m/s at the station over the same 30 days. On a large ebb, the shallows in front of the aquarium can run hard to the north, enough to pin a diver to the bottom and force a crawl; a few metres down the slope it eases. The same ebbs can also set up downwellings along the drop-off in front of the aquarium and down to the bottle field.
* **Diveable window.** The site is dived at any state of the tide on most days. Over 30 days the station stays under 0.25 m/s all day on about half the days; on the rest, a flood peak over it near the spring tides breaks the day into windows of about 10 hours. The times to avoid, or to dive with extra gas, are the beginning and end of the largest ebbs, when the shallows run hard.
* **The Redondo River.** The local name for the bursty shore-parallel current. It arrives without warning, rarely lasts long, and turns the visibility to silt within minutes. It shows most at the start and end of the largest ebbs, and also at the start of a flood following a very low tide. A diver caught in it stays low and works along the bottom, down the slope and out of the shallows' set.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | west-northwest, 285° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | west-northwest, 283° |

The beach faces west-northwest across East Passage toward Vashon Island, with open water up the passage to the north-northwest.

* **Bad.** West-southwest through north-northwest (WSW-NNW), onshore over 5.6 to more than 30 km of open water. Wind waves on the long, flat approach to the pier make the main stairs entry and exit hard work; the shore south of the boat launch deepens faster and handles waves better.
* **Short fetch.** Southwest (SW), onshore over 1.4 km, enough to put a short chop on the flat in front of the stairs.
* **Mixed.** North (N), cross-shore over 5.2 km, running chop along the beach.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through south-southwest (NNE-SSW): offshore from northeast through south-southeast, off the hillside behind the beach, and cross-shore over under 1 km from north-northeast, south and south-southwest.
* **Wind against current.** No wind with 1 km or more of fetch opposes the flood's easterly set; the winds that do, east-northeast through southeast, are offshore over under 1 km. The ebb's peaks stay under the 0.25 m/s threshold, too weak to stack against wind.

## Depth and tide

## Visibility

* **Silt.** The bottom is fine silt and sand that clouds at a fin stroke and takes several minutes to settle. Careful trim keeps the visibility for the divers behind you.
* **Depth and tide.** The shallows usually run murkier than the slope, and murkiest at low water. Bands and clouds of marine snow drift through the site in summer, so the clearest water is often a matter of location as much as depth.
* **Season.** Ranges from under 1.5 to over 9 m. Plankton blooms in spring and summer bring the worst of it, with clear spells in late summer and autumn; the large ebbs that wake the Redondo River also stir up the shallows. The live camera shows the day's water before you go.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11 °C through the year, standard for Puget Sound. Drysuit year round.
* **Surface layer.** Warms to about 13 to 16 °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Sixgill sharks.** Bluntnose sixgills rise onto the lower slope here from about June to September, mostly juveniles of around 1.5 to 2.5 m. They cruise mainly at night, around the Carousel boat, the trough, the bottle field and the plain beyond, slow and curious, and sometimes come close enough to bump a diver. Keep hands off them.
* **On the structure.** Small red octopus by the dozen, especially in autumn, and giant Pacific octopus under the boats, the pipe and the Beetle. Juvenile wolf eels in old tires and rock piles on the upper slope and around the engine block. Penpoint, saddleback, crescent, longfin and green gunnels cluster under the pipe and in the rock piles. Grunt, sailfin, buffalo, roughback and scalyhead sculpins, red Irish lords and cabezon; copper and quillback rockfish with young of the year, and lingcod. Plumose anemones and sea whips grow on every hard surface.
* **In the eelgrass and shallows.** Pacific spiny lumpsuckers in Lumpy Land and in the north eelgrass, best looked for at dusk in late winter and early spring, with tiny ones turning up in autumn. Bay pipefish standing upright in the grass, tubesnouts, shiner, pile and striped perch, Pacific tomcod, hooded nudibranchs on the eelgrass and kelp, plainfin midshipman, poachers and stubby squid.
* **On the sand and the plain.** Flounder and sole, Dungeness and graceful crabs, hosts of hermit crabs, sand dollars, painted anemones, spiny dogfish, sometimes in packs late in summer, and ratfish and skates at night.
* **Visitors.** Harbor seals hunt perch through the site and play with divers. Ocean sunfish, Pacific electric rays, egg yolk jellies and mosshead warbonnets turn up from time to time, and ruby octopus rarely.

## Hazards

* **The plain and the drop beyond it.** The plain at the foot of the slope is a long swim from the shore, and past its outer edge the bottom falls away past 40 m. Running low on gas or falling ill on the lower slope leaves a long ascent and a long swim home. Plan gas for the swim back to the slope, and watch the depth on the slope, which drops fast.
* **Navigation.** The humps and hollows on the bottom lead divers astray; going uphill is not always going home. Check the compass before heading back from the deep end, and turn toward the southeast for the pier.
* **Fishing pier.** Anglers cast from the fishing pier beside the site. Keep to the slope, clear of the shallows in front of it, and carry a cutting tool for line and hooks.
* **Aquarium pipework.** Intake pipework and lines hang off the southwest corner of the end of the MaST pier, an entanglement hazard. Descend toward the middle of the pier end.
* **Boat launch.** The public ramp between the main stairs and the south entry is busy in fishing season. Fly a surface marker, ascend well away from the ramp, and keep clear of the ramp itself on the south side.
* **Ropes and debris.** Guide lines, stray ropes in the bottle field and the debris on the bottom all snag.
* **Current on large ebbs.** The shallows in front of the aquarium can run hard to the north, with downwellings along the drop-off, at the beginning and end of the largest ebbs. Dive those windows only if comfortable in current, and leave extra gas.
* **The stairs and the south entry.** The bottom step of the main stairs is awkward, scattered boulders sit below it, and at high water the stairs lead straight into the water. Large rocks at the south entry trip divers; keep the regulator in on entering. Watch for traffic crossing Redondo Beach Drive S with gear.
* **Wind waves.** Waves running onto the long flat in front of the main stairs make the entry and exit difficult; call the dive, or use the south entry, when they are up.
