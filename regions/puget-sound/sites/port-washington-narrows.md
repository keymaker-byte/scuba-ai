# Port Washington Narrows

Port Washington Narrows is the tidal cut through Bremerton joining Dyes Inlet to Sinclair Inlet, dived as a one way flood drift of about 2.6 km from beneath the Manette Bridge, under the Warren Avenue Bridge, to a beach take-out beside the fishing pier at Lions Park. The bottom is a current swept cobble plain with occasional large boulders, covering a lot of ground for little effort. It is a two car shore dive, and only on the flood: the ebb runs the other way, toward Sinclair Inlet, with no reliable exit. Strong current, boulders looming out of the drift, and small boat traffic overhead are the hazards.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** From the upper lot, walk down the wooden stairs to the beach beneath the Manette Bridge. Swim out along the bridge to one of its piers in deeper water, current permitting, and descend to begin the drift.
* **Take-out.** The beach beside the fishing pier at the west end of Lions Park, on the north shore of the narrows about 1.1 km past the Warren Avenue Bridge, just past a long row of trees along the shore.
* **Parking.** The upper lot above the Boat Shed Restaurant, reached by turning right immediately after crossing the Manette Bridge toward Manette and right again. The lower lot beside the restaurant is small and fills quickly; leave it for diners. The drift is one way, so stage a second car in the Lions Park lot beside the pier and boat ramp, or have a non-diver move the car from the Manette Bridge to the park.
* **Access.** Shore access at the Manette Bridge is open 24 hours a day. Lions Park is a City of Bremerton park at 408 Lebo Boulevard, reached from the Manette Bridge by driving 1.4 km west on Wheaton Way, turning left onto Lebo Boulevard and continuing 1.6 km to the lot beside the pier.
* **Facilities.** None at the entry. Lions Park has restrooms, a boat ramp and the fishing pier.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **Manette Bridge.** The start of the drift, where the narrows opens into Sinclair Inlet. The bridge dates from 2011.
* **The drift, Manette to Warren Avenue.** About 1.5 km of cobble bottom at 6 to 9 m below MLLW, the channel bending from north northwest to west northwest along the way. Large boulders rise without warning out of the bottom; the work is steering clear of them, holding neutral buoyancy, and keeping a buddy in sight.
* **Warren Avenue Bridge.** The midpoint, and the deepest water of the drift, about 9 m below MLLW between its pylons. Passing beneath it is the cue to start working toward the north shore.
* **The approach to the exit.** Past the Warren Avenue Bridge the channel shoals quickly to a shallow flat along the north shore. Begin angling toward the north shore within about 300 m of the bridge so the current carries you onto the take-out beach rather than past it.

| **Time offset** | None. Slack at the Manette Bridge falls within about 10 minutes of slack at the station |

| **Ebb axis** | ESE, about 102° |

* **Flood.** Sets up the narrows from Sinclair Inlet toward Dyes Inlet, north northwest (about 333°) beneath the Manette Bridge and swinging west northwest by the Warren Avenue Bridge. This is the drift direction. Peak flood at the station commonly reaches 1.1 to 1.25 m/s; at the Manette end it runs about 80 percent of that.
* **Ebb.** Sets down the narrows from Dyes Inlet toward Sinclair Inlet, out past the Manette Bridge into open water. Peak ebb at the station can exceed 1.4 m/s. Never drift the ebb: the only take-out is at the Manette Bridge itself, easily missed, with Sinclair Inlet beyond it.
* **Diveable window.** The flood, not a slack. Entering at the turn makes for a long dive, since the first hour of the flood carries a diver well under a kilometre. Entering roughly an hour after the slack before flood, as the current builds, brings the full 2.6 km drift to about an hour to an hour and a quarter on an average exchange; a weak flood takes longer and a strong one less. Check the flood's peak speed for the day and size the entry time and gas to it.

## Depth and tide

The drift runs through shallow water relative to the tidal range, so the tide changes the working depth substantially: a 6 m point at MLLW reads under 5 m on a big low and over 10 m on a big high. On a low tide the shallow flat at the exit becomes a long wade to the beach.

## Hazards

* **Strong current.** The narrows runs as a fast drift through most of the flood, with no stopping to look at anything and real effort needed to stay with a buddy.
* **Boulders.** Large rocks appear suddenly out of the drift; keep enough height off the bottom to steer around them.
* **Missing the exit.** A diver who stays mid-channel past the Warren Avenue Bridge is carried on toward Dyes Inlet. Start for the north shore early.
* **Ebb drift.** Drifting the ebb leads to a missed take-out at the Manette Bridge and a ride out into Sinclair Inlet. Dive the flood only.
* **Small boat traffic.** The narrows is the only water route between Sinclair Inlet and Dyes Inlet and carries steady small boat traffic directly overhead. Stay at least 3 m deep for the whole drift and surface only at the take-out, ideally beside the shore.
* **Shallow exit.** The take-out sits on a tideflat; on a low tide expect a long, slow wade in full gear.

## Wind

* **Orientation.** A narrow inland cut through residential Bremerton, with land close on both sides. The entry beach faces west across the narrows and the exit beach faces southwest.
* **Bad.** A strong southerly up Sinclair Inlet is the one direction with any fetch to the Manette entry. Little else reaches the narrows.
* **Fine.** Effectively every other direction; the surrounding shore leaves little open water for wind to build across.
* **Wind against current.** A northerly runs against the flood and can raise a short chop in the channel, worth noting for a surface swim to the take-out but rarely enough to cancel the dive.

## Visibility

* **Current and season.** Swings with the regional plankton blooms, worst through spring and summer and best in winter. The cobble bottom holds little silt, so the current stirs less sediment here than over a muddier floor, but water drawn in from Sinclair Inlet on the flood carries whatever the inlet holds that week.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** Roughly 7 to 11 °C year round, the regional figure for Puget Sound. Standard drysuit protocol.

## Marine life

* **Sea stars.** Five-rayed pink stars and multi-rayed sunflower stars dig into the cobble after clams, with smaller, slower red and white mottled stars, reddish-brown leather stars and orange-red blood stars scattered across the bottom.
* **Bottom.** Piddock clams, whelks, hermit crabs, kelp crabs, orange sea cucumbers, beaded sea anemones and white plumose anemones, with giant acorn barnacles on the rocks.
* **Fish and octopus.** Flounder and C-O sole on the cobble, and octopus among the larger boulders.
