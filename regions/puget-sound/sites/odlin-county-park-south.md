# Odlin County Park (South)

Sandy bay fronting Odlin County Park on Lopez Island, entered from the middle of the beach and swum out across a shallow flat and eelgrass onto an open slope of sand and mud facing Upright Channel. The soft bottom holds tube-dwelling anemones, orange sea pens and sturgeon poachers, and a low rocky shoreline with a kelp bed borders the bay to the south. It is a gentle site inside the bay; the current builds toward the bay mouth, and on larger ebbs it reverses direction partway through the exchange.

## Getting there

* **Entry.** Walk across the sand to the middle of the beach, about 73 m from the parking, and wade in. The dive coordinate lies about 323 m west-northwest of the entry, across the flat.
* **Parking.** The day-use parking behind the beach. Daytime parking is free; overnight parking carries a fee.
* **Access.** Reach Lopez Island on the Washington State Ferry from Anacortes, about 40 minutes. From the ferry landing drive south on Ferry Road for 1.8 km and turn right into Odlin County Park; the beach parking lies about 0.5 km inside the entrance. San Juan County Parks runs the park year round, with quiet hours from 10 pm to 7 am.
* **Facilities.** The sandy beach, a free boat and kayak launch, a day-use dock with a two-hour limit and a small fishing pier at the north end of the beach, mooring buoys, vault toilets, a picnic area and pavilion, and a campground with potable water from April through October.

## Navigation and landmarks

* **The flat.** The beach shelves very gently: the first 100 m out sit at about the MLLW line and dry on a minus tide. Swim it at the surface toward the west-northwest.
* **Eelgrass.** An eelgrass band grows on the shallow bottom past the flat.
* **The slope.** Past the eelgrass the bottom opens out and slopes evenly away: about 4 m below MLLW some 160 m from the entry, 9.6 m at 240 m and 11.7 m at the dive coordinate. Sand gives way to mud partway down, and the slope continues to 17.5 to 21.4 m below MLLW about 100 to 180 m farther northwest.
* **The south shoreline.** A low rocky shoreline borders the bay about 180 m south of the dive coordinate, with a kelp bed along it, for a second dive over rock.
* **The return.** Head back east-southeast up the slope to the eelgrass and the beach.

## Current

The bay opens onto the east shore of Upright Channel, which joins Harney Channel to the north with San Juan Channel to the southwest. The inner bay holds slacker water; the flow builds toward the bay mouth and the channel.

* **Flood.** Sets north past the park on every exchange, turning east into the bay. Daily peaks at the station run 0.54 to 1.32 m/s over 30 days.
* **Ebb.** On exchanges of 1.2 m or less the ebb also sets north past the park. On larger exchanges it sets out of the bay to the west and south first, then reverses and runs north while the tide is still falling; the reversal comes up to three hours into the ebb, and its timing varies with the size of the exchange. The ebb flowing east through San Juan Channel is deflected off the west shore of Lopez into the south end of Upright Channel, and once it outweighs the ebb entering from the north, the flow past the park turns north. Daily peaks at the station run 0.42 to 0.73 m/s over 30 days.
* **Diveable window.** The slack before ebb is the one that serves. Under 0.25 m/s at the station it typically runs 96 to 138 minutes, shrinks to about 24 minutes ahead of the strongest ebbs, and stretches to about five hours on days when the weaker ebb stays under the threshold. A second slack follows on a larger ebb, as the outgoing ebb weakens against the northbound flow and reverses.
* **No slack before flood at the site.** The station turns, but the water past the park is already running north by the end of the ebb, and it only slows at minimum current before picking up again as the flood.
* **Toward the bay mouth.** Current is mild over the inner bay and builds toward the outside of the bay.

## Wind

| | |
|---|---|
| **Entry shore facing** | northwest, 311° |
| **Dive area shore facing** | north, 350° |

The beach faces out of the bay across Upright Channel toward Shaw Island; the dive area facing is the south shoreline of the bay.

* **Bad.** West-southwest (WSW), onshore over 6.8 km of open water down Upright Channel, building chop and small surf on the beach.
* **Short fetch.** West through north (W-N), onshore over 1.7 to 3.6 km across Upright Channel, raising chop on the beach and across the swim out.
* **Fine.** North-northeast clockwise through southwest (NNE-SW). North-northeast and northeast run along the beach with under 800 m of fetch, blocked by Upright Head; east-northeast through south blow off Lopez Island with 180 to 390 m of fetch; south-southwest and southwest run along the beach over 210 m to 1.4 km.
* **Wind against current.** Southwest (SW) over 1.4 km, west-southwest (WSW) over 6.8 km, west (W) over 2.0 km and west-northwest (WNW) over 1.8 km blow against the west ebb set. The winds opposing the east flood set blow off the land with under 500 m of fetch.

## Depth and tide

The site swings about 2.4 m on a median day, and up to 3.9 m on the year's biggest exchange, so depths are quoted against the MLLW chart datum: a minus tide reads shallower than the datum figure, and any height above MLLW reads deeper. The flat off the beach dries on a minus tide.

## Visibility

* **Season.** Best from autumn through spring. Plankton blooms in spring and summer bring it down hard.
* **Soft bottom.** The sand and mud silt up quickly under careless fins, especially on the mud below the sand.

## Temperature

* **At depth.** About 7 to 11 °C year round. Drysuit.
* **Surface layer.** Can warm into the low teens °C late in summer.

## Marine life

* **Sand and mud.** Tube-dwelling anemones and large orange sea pens, with California and orange sea cucumbers, geoducks, hermit crabs, sharp-nosed crabs and striped nudibranchs. Sturgeon poachers rest on the sand and mud.
* **Sea stars.** Morning sun stars, purple stars, red-orange blood stars and leather stars.
* **Eelgrass and water column.** Slender pipefish and kelp crabs in the eelgrass, and delicate comb jellies in the water above.

## Hazards

* **Current.** Mild over the inner bay and stronger toward the bay mouth, and on larger ebbs it reverses partway through the exchange. Plan around the slack before ebb and stay inside the bay when the current builds.
* **Boat traffic.** The boat launch at the beach, the day-use dock and the mooring buoys bring small boats and kayaks across the swim out, and the ferries and other traffic in Upright Channel throw wakes into the bay. Fly a dive flag and keep a tight group at the surface.
* **Kelp.** The kelp bed along the south shoreline can snag gear; swim through it slowly and surface in open water.
* **Long surface swim.** The flat makes the first 100 m a surface swim, and a walk across dry sand on a minus tide.
* **Wind chop.** Wind from west-southwest through north raises chop and small surf on the beach.
