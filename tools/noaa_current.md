# NOAA CO-OPS currents (slack and max flood/ebb)

<https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/mdapi/prod/>

The slack time and direction, on the day, for the station that governs the site: the number the whole plan hangs off. Computed on this machine from the harmonic constants NOAA publishes for each current station, the same constants NOAA's own predictions come from. A station is downloaded once, the first time it is asked for, and every prediction after that runs with no request at all, for any date.

- **Stations it serves.** Every current station NOAA lists, on every US coast, about 2,800 of them: the harmonic stations with their depth bins, and the subordinate stations with their corrections. A station row in a site file whose ID is a NOAA ID is read with this tool; `stations --near` lists what NOAA carries, with each station's type. The ID prefix names a survey project or a set of tables (PUG Puget Sound, SFB San Francisco Bay, LIS Long Island Sound, PCT and ACT the Pacific and Atlantic current tables); the type names the kind of station. A retired station, one missing from NOAA's list, is read with `noaa_legacy_current`.
- **Use it for.** Two jobs. Writing a site file's Current section, by the region's current method: finding the governing station, its published bins, and the axes, peak speeds and typical window at the recommended bin. Planning a dive: the slack time and direction on the day at that station, plus the max flood and ebb speeds bracketing the window.
- **Read the whole day.** Slacks are unevenly spaced and unequal: a slack between two weak maxes is a wide window, between two strong maxes a narrow one. The max flood and ebb speeds either side of the window bracket how fast the site turns on a late diver.
- **Watch for.** Diurnal inequality (the two daily exchanges differ in size), and big exchanges around new and full moon.
- **Station types.** H, harmonic: the station's own constants, usually from an instrument survey that publishes depth bins. S, subordinate: time and speed corrections to a reference station. W, weak and variable: no predictions at all.
- **The data.** `tools/db/noaa_current/` holds NOAA's station list and one small file per station: every bin's constants, its mean current, its flood and ebb directions, and a subordinate bin's corrections. Each is downloaded when missing. Deleting the folder, or one station's file, fetches NOAA's current constants on the next run, which is how a re-surveyed station is picked up. Every header prints the date its constants were fetched.
- **How it predicts.** The speed along a bin's major axis is its mean current plus the sum of its constituents, up to 37 of them, each with the node factor and phase of its year. The astronomy is `tools/lib/tidal_astronomy.py`, shared with `noaa_tide`: Schureman's Manual of Harmonic Analysis and Prediction of Tides with NOAA's own conventions, the node factors held at mid-year and changed at the first hour of the UTC year. A change to it is followed by `verify` on a few stations of each tool.
- **How close it is to NOAA.** `verify --all` compared every published bin of every station with NOAA's live API, 4,169 bins over 30 days each, in November 2026. On the 2,281 harmonic bins that carry constants, the speed every six minutes agrees within 0.004 m/s on all of them, and every slack and maximum within 3 minutes and 0.013 m/s on 2,279; the other two differ by one event on a flat curve. On all 1,466 single-bin subordinate stations every slack and maximum agrees within 2 minutes and 0.02 m/s. The station bins the Pacific Northwest site files plan on also agree within 0.002 m/s and 2.4 minutes in 2031 and 2036.
- **Mind the bin.** The stations are ADCPs with many depth bins, and NOAA publishes predictions for only a few. The site file's Recommended bin, the published bin nearest the dive area's seabed depth, is the one every figure and every plan uses, and the offset is derived against it; an offset against one bin is a different number against another. At a fast-moving pass the shallowest bin might sit at 4.6 m and the deepest published bin at 30.6 m; on a test day the deep slack ran 20 minutes earlier than the surface slack, with max flood weaker (1.41 vs 1.61 m/s) and the flood axis rotated from 286° to 275°. A multi-bin station needs `--bin`; the tool stops without one. `bins` lists every bin with its depth and kind; a wide spread between bins is itself a warning that the offset is depth-sensitive.
- **Unpublished bins.** Some stations carry constants for more bins than NOAA publishes predictions for, 754 bins in all. The tool predicts those too and says so in the header; they have no NOAA prediction to verify against, so a published bin stays the one a site file names.
- **Bins with no constants.** NOAA lists three harmonic bins and publishes neither constants nor predictions for them (g06010 bin 5, kb0201 bin 8, kb0401 bin 6). The tool stops on such a bin, and `bins` labels it.
- **Weak and variable bins.** A bin NOAA lists as weak and variable stops the run, with nothing to plan on there.
- **Subordinate bins.** A subordinate bin has no constants. The tool predicts its reference station's slacks and maxima and moves each by NOAA's own correction for it, four time corrections and two speed ratios, which is how NOAA defines it. `predict` works; `window` stops, since a subordinate has events only and no speed curve between them. The reference station is downloaded with it.
- **Subordinate bins of a multi-bin station.** NOAA publishes separate corrections for each bin of these stations, and its prediction API answers every bin with the corrections of the station's first bin. The tool applies each bin's own published corrections, so it matches NOAA on the first bin and differs on the others: of 419 such bins, 189 match and 226 differ in exactly this way, by up to hours and by the ratio between the bins' speeds. The header says so on every such bin, and `verify` reports which bin's corrections NOAA used. Two stations stand apart: at Waldron Island (PUG1716) NOAA's predictions match no bin's published corrections, and at PCT0336 bin 2 NOAA publishes corrections and no predictions. A plan built on a subordinate bin of a multi-bin station states which of the two readings it uses.
- **Minimums are named.** A weak point between two floods prints as `min flood`, between two ebbs as `min ebb`, with its speed. NOAA prints the same event as a second flood or ebb, or as a slack when it is near zero. NOAA also leaves out a maximum too small to round above zero; the tool prints it.
- **The first hours of a new year.** NOAA's slack and maximum listing keeps the old year's node factors for a few hours into January 1 UTC, while its own speed series changes at the first hour. The tool follows the speed series, so an event in those hours can sit a few minutes and up to 0.08 m/s from NOAA's listing, and the change itself can print as a small extra maximum at 00:00 UTC. January 1 UTC begins on the afternoon or evening of December 31 local time; on a subordinate bin the affected hours move with its time correction.
- **Every time it prints is at the station, un-offset.** The site file's Observed offset is applied to it, where established.
- **Times.** `--tz` is required on every command that prints a time: the site's own IANA zone, the `tz` in its JSON. Every time prints in that zone, named in the header, daylight time included.
- **A failed download stops the run.** A refusal, an empty or non-JSON answer, or an unreachable API ends the run with what failed and the URL asked for, and leaves no partial station file behind. A cached station needs no network.

## Before relying on it

- **It is NOAA's prediction, reproduced.** It gives the current at the station's own point and bin, un-offset; the site's own water is the site file's business.
- **Constants go stale silently.** NOAA replaces a station's constants when it re-surveys, and a cached file keeps the old ones. The header prints the fetch date. Run `verify` on a site's governing station and bin whenever its file is older than a year or a plan matters more than usual; on a failure, delete the station's file, run `verify` again, and report it if it still fails.
- **A `verify` failure is a stop.** It is the same kind of failure as an unreachable API: report it and wait, with no second station or bin substituted.
- **Harmonic bins and single-bin subordinates are proven; the rest carry a caveat.** Unpublished bins have nothing to be verified against. Subordinate bins past the first on a multi-bin station differ from NOAA's API by design, as above.
- **`window` works on harmonic bins.** A subordinate bin has slacks and maxima only.
- **One process, many stations.** The prepared constants of a bin are cached for the run and tied to that bin; a script that imports the tool and builds its own bin dictionaries passes each one whole to `speed`, `series` and `events`, as the tool does.

## `tools/noaa_current.py`

Standard library only.

```sh
python3 tools/noaa_current.py stations --near <lat> <lon>                                    # site file: stations near the site
python3 tools/noaa_current.py bins STATION_ID                                                # site file: bins, their depths and kinds
python3 tools/noaa_current.py predict STATION_ID --bin B --date 2026-07-12 --tz <IANA_ZONE>  # site file and planning: slack, max flood, max ebb
python3 tools/noaa_current.py window STATION_ID --bin B --date 2026-07-12 --tz <IANA_ZONE>   # site file and planning: diveable windows
python3 tools/noaa_current.py fetch --near <lat> <lon> --km 50                               # cache every station within a radius
python3 tools/noaa_current.py fetch STATION_ID [STATION_ID ...]                              # cache named stations
python3 tools/noaa_current.py fetch --all                                                    # cache every station NOAA lists
python3 tools/noaa_current.py verify STATION_ID --bin B --days 30                            # compare one bin with NOAA's live API
python3 tools/noaa_current.py verify --all                                                   # compare every published bin of every cached station
```

`predict` prints the day's slacks and maxima under a header naming the station, bin, time zone and the date its constants were fetched. `window` is the planning command: it reports every span where the current stays under `--max-speed`, to the minute, with its duration and peak, so a 72-minute window and a 20-minute one stop looking alike. The threshold defaults to `noaa_current.max_speed_ms` in `tool-config.json`, 0.25 m/s where the key is absent, a placeholder for what is comfortable in a drysuit, worth setting from experience. A site file's peak speeds and typical window come from `predict` and `window` run across 30 consecutive days, which takes in both spring tides of the lunar month, the larger perigean one included.

`fetch` caches stations ahead of time, for working with no connection. It skips what is already cached, so a run that NOAA's rate limit stops picks up where it left off. `--all` caches all 2,758 stations in about 85 minutes and 12 MB; `--near` covers a region in a minute or two.

`verify` is the proof and the staleness check. It reads NOAA's predictions for the same station, bin and dates and reports the worst difference in speed and in the time and size of every slack and maximum, passing within 0.02 m/s and 3 minutes. A slack or a maximum on a flat curve, where minutes of time cost a hair of speed, is judged on the speed, and events in the first hours of a new year are left out and counted. A failure on a station that used to pass means NOAA replaced its constants: delete the station's file and run it again.

`verify --all` runs the same comparison on every published harmonic and subordinate bin of every cached station and writes one JSON line per bin to `tools/db/noaa_current/_verify.jsonl`: the differences, `ok`, and on a failed subordinate bin `noaa_used_bin`, the bin whose corrections NOAA answered with. It makes about 6,500 requests and takes about six hours. A bin already in the file is skipped, so a stopped run resumes; deleting the file starts it over. Run it after deleting the cache to refresh every station.
