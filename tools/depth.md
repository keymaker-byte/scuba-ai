# Depth (seabed depth at any point)

<https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/coastal-elevation-models>, <https://vdatum.noaa.gov>, <https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/e6e11b99-f0cc-44f7-f5eb-3b995fb1637e>, <https://www.dcbd.nl/resource/2869>, <https://cdi-bathymetry.seadatanet.org/search>

The seabed depth at a point, below the datum of the water it lies in: MLLW in US tidal water, CHS Chart Datum in Canadian tidal water, the lake's own surface in a lake, and a plain observed depth around Bonaire. The tool picks the bathymetry that covers the point and names it in every answer, so a site file and a plan read one tool wherever the site is. Every source keeps its data on this machine: each piece is downloaded once, the first time a point inside it is asked for, and every depth after that is read with no request at all. The Navy's surveys of Bonaire are ordered by hand from SeaDataNet, and a missing one stops the run with the steps to order it and the folder to save it in.

- **Use it for.** The water depth at a dive point, which goes in the site file's Coordinates row and in the site JSON's `seabed_m`, with the datum it is on in `seabed_datum`, the seabed the current tool's height above the bottom is measured from. A spatial current model's own mesh cannot supply it: its elements can span from the beach to the deep basin, so its nearshore depth can be wrong by tens of metres.
- **Also a sanity check on a coordinate.** Is this point underwater at all, and at a divable depth? It reads the actual bottom where a nautical chart gives scattered soundings between wide contours.
- **Which source answers.** The first source in the tool's list that covers the point, or with `--datum K` the first one whose answer is on that datum. A source with no survey at the point passes it to the next one that covers it; any other answer with no depth, such as land or a shore, stands. A point no source covers, or none on the datum asked for, stops the run naming what each source gave.
- **Datum.** MLLW in US water, the datum NOAA's charts and its tide and current predictions use; CHS Chart Datum in Canadian water, the datum Canadian charts and CHS's tide predictions use, Lower Low Water, Large Tide on the Pacific coast, which sits 0.8 to 1.3 m below MLLW in the Salish Sea; the lake's surface in a lake, which has no tide; none on Bonaire's reef, whose tide of about 30 cm sits inside the grid's own precision. Every answer names its datum by its name from `tools/lib/datums.py`, `[datum MLLW]`, `[datum CHS]`, `[datum LAKE]` or `[datum NONE]`, the name a site JSON's `seabed_datum` and the tide tool use. The two tidal datums are different surfaces, so a depth from one is never compared with a depth from the other, nor added to a tide above the other.
- **Across the border.** The NCEI tiles along the 49th parallel reach into Canadian water, and wherever they hold data inside a VDatum sub-region they answer first, below MLLW. Around Saturna and the Pender Islands the tiles hold data where VDatum has no sub-region, so no MLLW offset exists there, and the GSC DEM answers below Chart Datum, joined to the NCEI DEM by the NCEI to GSC seam. A Canadian site's depth is read with `--datum CHS` (or `--file`, whose `seabed_datum` is `CHS`), which skips NCEI and answers from the GSC DEM, so the depth sits on the datum of the CHS tide station it is planned with. In that water the NCEI DEM is also the weaker reading: across Boundary Pass and Haro Strait, against CHS's own Chart Datum bathymetry (NONNA-10), the GSC DEM reads 0.33 m deeper at the median and the NCEI DEM 1.45 m deeper, where a true MLLW depth would read about 0.9 m shallower. A point in a border tile with no NCEI data there, or outside every VDatum sub-region, and every point north of the tiles, falls to the GSC West Coast DEM, below Chart Datum, through the seam's band where it lies within 100 m of the NCEI DEM's water. The GSC DEM also covers the Washington side of the Salish Sea, where NCEI answers first.
- **No depth.** A point on land, on the shore, inland of the coastline in water no lake holds, or in a lake whose water level the source cannot find, gives no depth and the reason. Walk the coordinate out and ask again.
- **A site JSON.** `at --file` reads the JSON's `lat`, `lon` and `seabed_datum`, answers on that datum, and checks its `seabed_m` against the answer at the 0.1 m it is written to, exiting non-zero on a difference or on a missing `seabed_datum`, with the datum to add. A site on `NONE`, a plain observed depth such as Bonaire's, is checked the same way, against Bonaire's surveys.
- **A failed download stops the run.** A refusal, a short or unreadable file or an unreachable server ends the run with what failed and the URL asked for, and leaves no partial file behind. A point whose data is already on this machine needs no network.

## Sources

### NCEI coastal DEM

NOAA NCEI's coastal digital elevation model, at about 3 m resolution (1/9 arc-second), on NAVD 88, with MLLW from NOAA VDatum's grids.

- **Coverage.** The ninth arc-second tiles NOAA publishes, about 930 of them along the US coasts: Washington, Oregon, California, the Gulf, the Atlantic seaboard and part of Alaska, with the lakes inside them.
- **The data.** `tools/db/depth/ncei/` holds NOAA's tile list, one GeoTIFF per quarter-degree tile, and each lake's water level once read (`lake_levels.json`; deleting it reads them again). A tile is about 200 MB, so its first use takes a few minutes on a fast connection and much longer on a slow one. `fetch` downloads a whole area ahead of time. Deleting a tile, or the folder, fetches NOAA's current data on the next run, which is how a re-surveyed tile is picked up.
- **Shared code.** The tile list, the downloads and the lakes are `tools/lib/ncei.py`, the GeoTIFF reader `tools/lib/dem_tiles.py`, and the MLLW offset `tools/lib/vdatum.py`, shared libraries the map's depth layer and the current tool's seabed read the same way.
- **The value is the pixel the point falls in.** The tile's own georeferencing places each 3 m pixel, and the tool reads the one containing the coordinate. NCEI's live map service answers with a pixel half a cell to the south-east, 1.7 m away, so the two agree within a few centimetres on a gentle bottom and differ by up to 1.4 m on a steep wall.
- **MLLW comes from VDatum's grids, on this machine.** The height of NAVD 88 above MLLW runs from a few centimetres in some areas to more than a metre in others. The tool computes it at the exact point from NOAA VDatum's own transformation grids, the chain VDatum's conversion service runs: GEOID18 from NAVD 88 to the NAD83(2011) ellipsoid, HTDP's frame transformation to IGS14 at the epoch 2026.0, xGEOID20B to IGS14's gravimetric geoid, then the sub-region's `tss` grid to mean sea level and its `mllw` grid to MLLW. It reproduces answers VDatum's own service gave within 2 mm. The answer names the offset and the VDatum sub-region it came from.
- **It answers inside VDatum's sub-regions, and inland.** The NCEI DEM covers a point where its tiles hold data and either a VDatum sub-region holds the point, the water its MLLW offset is defined over, or the point lies inland of the coastline, where a lake is read below its own level with no VDatum; at sea past VDatum's sub-regions, the next source answers.
- **The sub-region is read from the coordinate.** VDatum divides tidal water into sub-regions, each with its own polygons, and the tool picks the one VDatum itself picks: the highest polygon type holding the point (a priority area over tidal water), the first in VDatum's own order on a tie. A sea point in no sub-region, in a non-tidal area, or on the land side of the grids' shoreline stops the run with the reason.
- **Where two sub-regions meet.** Neighbouring sub-regions split the water along a stairstep of whole grid cells, each cell holding a value in one sub-region's grids alone, so along the join neither grid has all four nodes around a point. A node empty in the sub-region's own grid takes the value a neighbouring sub-region's grid gives at that node, and the offset runs across the join with no step: the two grids agree there within a millimetre. A node no grid holds is the grids' shoreline, where the point has no MLLW offset.
- **The grids.** `tools/db/vdatum/` holds VDatum's full package as it downloads (`vdatum_all_<date>.zip`, about 3 GB), its index of sub-regions (`tidal_area.inf` and `tidal_area.dat`), and one folder per sub-region extracted from the package: the `tss` and `mllw` grids, and GEOID18 and xGEOID20B cut to the sub-region's box. A sub-region is extracted the first time a point inside it is asked for, in a second or two; after that it needs neither the package nor the network. The Washington sub-regions are about 250 MB. Deleting a folder extracts it again, which is how a new package is picked up.
- **The package is downloaded by hand.** VDatum's download page refuses automated clients, so a missing package stops the run with the page to fetch it from: <https://vdatum.noaa.gov/download.php>, the full package, saved unopened into `tools/db/vdatum/`. The newest package there by its date is the one read.
- **Sub-regions referenced to IGS14.** Every Washington sub-region, and 20 of VDatum's 52, carry their tidal grids on IGS14, the frame the chain is built for. A sub-region on NAD83 or IGS08 stops the run with its frame named.
- **Lakes.** A lake bed is an elevation above NAVD 88, with no tide and no MLLW. Its depth is the lake's water level minus that elevation. The tool reads the level from the DEM: where the survey never reached the bed, the DEM holds the flat water surface on the day it was flown, so one height repeats along the shore. A level counts when it shows along at least 10 of 12 stretches of the shore and sits within 10 m of HydroLAKES' own rough level; a lake with none, such as one surveyed right up to its walls, gives no depth. The level moves with the seasons, so a depth holds to the survey day's water, usually within a metre.
- **Where the sea ends.** The sea ends at OpenStreetMap's coastline, and a lake at its HydroLAKES outline, both read through `tools/lib/shapes.py`, a shared library: a point inland of it, behind a lock or a dike, is never seabed. Water joined to a lake belongs to it: inland ground more than 1 m below the lake's level that also looks like water (at or below sea level, inside a lake with no level of its own, or within 100 m of the outline) takes the lake's level when it is reached from the outline without leaving such ground. That brings in the shallow shore the outline misses, a lake with no level of its own, and a canal held at the lake's level up to its lock, and stops at the coastline, at another lake, and at dry ground, such as the valley below a lake on a plateau. A point query traces it on the map's own 6 m grid, so both read the same water.
- **`--verify`.** Compares the tile with NCEI's live service at the point, passing when the service answers from the same tile and its value matches one of the nine pixels at and around the point within 0.01 m.
- **`selftest`.** Computes the MLLW offset at a dozen points across the Washington sub-regions and compares each with the answer VDatum's own conversion service gave there in November 2026, passing within 3 mm. Run it after a new VDatum package, or a change to `tools/lib/vdatum.py`.

### GSC West Coast DEM

The Geological Survey of Canada's Canada West Coast topo-bathymetric digital elevation model (Open File 8963), Version 2 of 2025, gridded at 10 m, with its sea depths below CHS Chart Datum.

- **Coverage.** 47.2° to 56.2° N and 134.8° to 120.9° W: the whole British Columbia coast and its continental shelf, the Canadian side of the Salish Sea (the Strait of Georgia, Haro Strait, the north shore of Juan de Fuca Strait, Howe Sound, Burrard Inlet and the Gulf Islands), and the Washington side, where NCEI answers first.
- **What it is built from.** CHS multibeam surveys from 1997 to 2024 at 1 to 5 m, DFO's coastal elevation model, NRCan's regional bathymetry from CHS contours, Ocean Networks Canada's Lower Mainland DEM and Washington DNR lidar, all resampled to one 10 m grid on BC Albers. Where multibeam covers the bottom the grid follows it closely; between surveys it is interpolated from older soundings and contours.
- **The data.** `tools/db/depth/gsc/` holds `index.json` (where the GeoTIFF sits inside NRCan's zip, and the zip's date), the GeoTIFF `WestCoastDEM_Version2_Elevation.tif` (7.9 GB, a BigTIFF) and its world file. It is one file, downloaded the first time a point inside its extent is asked for: 7.3 GB of NRCan's 12.2 GB zip, read out of the zip with byte range requests and unpacked as it arrives, with the zip's own size and CRC checked; the relief shading, source map and overviews in the zip stay on the server. A stopped download starts over. Deleting the folder fetches NRCan's current package on the next run.
- **Shared code.** The download, the projection and the reading are `tools/lib/gsc.py`, the GeoTIFF reader `tools/lib/dem_tiles.py`, the coastline and lakes `tools/lib/shapes.py`.
- **The value is the pixel the point falls in,** found by projecting the coordinate to BC Albers (EPSG:3005, NAD83 on GRS 80) and placing it on the grid by the world file. The coordinate is taken as NAD83; WGS 84 sits about 1 m from it in British Columbia, a tenth of a pixel.
- **Datum.** CHS Chart Datum in Canadian water: the compilers reduced every bathymetric source to it, and on flat bottom in Boundary Bay, the Strait of Georgia, Howe Sound, Saanich Inlet, the Gulf Islands and Burrard Inlet it agrees with CHS's own Chart Datum bathymetry (NONNA-10) within 0.01 to 0.30 m at the median of each area. In US water it sits on another surface: on flat bottom from Boundary Pass to Semiahmoo Bay it reads about 0.5 m shallower than the NCEI DEM's depth below MLLW, where Chart Datum would read about 1 m deeper, so its Washington side is read only as a placeholder; NCEI answers first there. The tool holds no line for the maritime boundary, so `--datum CHS` at a point in US water still answers from this DEM, on that other surface: a US site is read on `MLLW`, its region's datum. The land heights keep each land source's own datum, so a value above zero is read only as dry ground.
- **Drying ground.** A value at or above Chart Datum dries at low water and gives no depth, with the drying height.
- **Where the sea ends.** OpenStreetMap's coastline, as for NCEI: a point inland of it is never seabed. The DEM carries a lake as land surface, so a point in a HydroLAKES lake gives no depth.
- **`--verify`.** Reads the pixel value from NRCan's map service, its West Coast DEM Elevations layer, over a window a few metres across so it answers at full resolution, and passes when it matches one of the nine pixels at and around the point within 0.01 m.
- **`selftest`.** Projects five coordinates to BC Albers and compares each with the figure Esri's geometry service gave for NAD83 to EPSG:3005 in November 2026, passing within 1 mm. Run it after a change to `tools/lib/gsc.py`.

### The NCEI to GSC seam

The transition from the NCEI DEM to the GSC West Coast DEM, wherever the NCEI DEM's water ends against the GSC DEM's: at the edge of the NCEI tiles' data, and where a VDatum sub-region ends while the tiles go on. It answers over a band of 100 m past that edge: the GSC DEM's depth below Chart Datum, plus the two DEMs' difference at the NCEI DEM's nearest point with data, applied in full at that point and fading to none at the band's end. The depth runs on from the NCEI DEM's last value to the GSC DEM's with no step, and answers on `CHS`, as the GSC DEM does.

- **The NCEI depth on Chart Datum.** The edge point's depth below MLLW, from VDatum as the NCEI DEM gives it, moved onto Chart Datum by MLLW's height above Chart Datum there: CHS's own MLLW figure at the three nearest CHS stations that publish one, weighted by the inverse square of the distance. Each station's file is downloaded the first time it is needed.
- **Where it answers.** Where the GSC DEM answers sea within 100 m of the NCEI DEM's water and outside it. A point the GSC DEM calls drying ground or land keeps that answer.
- **The answer.** Names the distance past the edge, the NCEI DEM's depth at the edge point on both datums, the GSC DEM's depth at the point and under the edge point, and the share of the difference added.
- **`selftest`.** Points 10 m past the NCEI DEM's edge at random in two boxes, from Haro Strait through Boundary Pass to the border's east end, and along Juan de Fuca Strait from Pysht to Port Angeles, their step against the edge point's own depth, and the step the GSC DEM alone would leave there, passing within 1.0 m at the median and below the GSC DEM alone at the median and p90. In November 2026, over 60 points, the seam left 0.09 m at the median and 0.25 m at p90, against 0.68 m and 1.18 m for the GSC DEM alone.

### DCBD coastal bathymetry of Bonaire

E. Meesters' 2020 coastal bathymetry of Bonaire and Klein Bonaire, published on the Dutch Caribbean Biodiversity Database, gridded at 2 m, a plain observed depth (`NONE`).

- **Coverage.** Both islands from the shoreline out as deep as the survey reached: 20 m at most, often about 10 to 12 m. The grid's extent runs 12.01° to 12.34° N and 68.44° to 68.19° W. Land, water deeper than the survey and anything unsurveyed hold no data. At 2 m it resolves the terrace, the sand and the reef crest a coarse grid averages into one figure.
- **The data.** `tools/db/depth/dcbd/` holds the GeoTIFF `bonaire_depth_hires2.tif` (48 MB), `index.json`, the date it was fetched and the zip's size and the grid's size and CRC as DCBD served them, and `wet_mask.bin`, one byte per 20 m block marking where the grid holds water, read once from the whole grid in about 30 seconds and again whenever the grid changes, which tells the Navy's surveys where DCBD's water lies around a point. It is one file, taken out of DCBD's 34 MB zip the first time a point inside the extent is asked for, its CRC checked against the zip's own. Deleting the folder fetches DCBD's current package on the next run.
- **Shared code.** The download, the projection and the reading are `tools/lib/dcbd.py`, the GeoTIFF reader `tools/lib/dem_tiles.py`. The current tool's seabed reads Bonaire through the depth tool's own chain, this grid, the Navy's surveys and the seams between them, so the map's current layer covers the same water as its depth layer.
- **The value is the pixel the point falls in,** found by projecting the coordinate to WGS 84 / UTM zone 19N (EPSG:32619), the grid's own projection, and placing it by the GeoTIFF's tags.
- **Datum.** The dataset states none; its values sit at about sea level, with the shoreline at zero. Bonaire's tidal range of about 30 cm sits inside the grid's precision, so the depth is a plain observed one, on `NONE`.
- **The spread around a point.** Each depth also gives the shallowest and deepest pixel within 10 m, the relief right around the point: a narrow spread on the terrace or sand, a wide one on the drop-off.
- **No data at a pixel.** The answer gives no depth and names the nearest pixel with data within 100 m, with its distance, bearing and depth, so the edge of the survey shows where the reef keeps going deeper. A pixel with no data between the grid's water, its pixels under water within 30 m lying on opposite sides of it with no empty angle of 180° or more, answers from that water by the inverse square of the distance: a small gap in the survey, or the narrow tip of a mangrove finger in Lac Bay. It does so at sea and where the 2019 survey holds no cell, since a notch in DCBD's deep edge lies deeper than the edge around it and the 2019 survey reads it. Past that edge the Navy's surveys answer, joined to it by the Bonaire seams, and in the strip along the shore it never reached, where the 2019 survey holds no cell, Bonaire's shore strip answers. The grid starts a few metres off the waterline, where the water is already 2 to 5 m deep off a rocky shore, so an entry point on the rocks reads no data, with the first water beside it.
- **`--verify`.** DCBD serves the grid as a file, with no depth at a point, so the check is on the file: one byte range request reads the directory of the zip DCBD serves now, and passes when the zip's size and the grid's size and CRC match the ones in `index.json`.
- **`selftest`.** Projects five coordinates to UTM zone 19N and compares each with the figure Esri's geometry service gave for WGS 84 to EPSG:32619 in November 2026, passing within 1 mm. Run it after a change to `tools/lib/dcbd.py`.

### Royal Netherlands Navy survey of Bonaire, 2019

The Royal Netherlands Navy Hydrographic Service's multibeam survey of Bonaire's leeward coast, its south tip and the south half of its east coast, made from 19 to 22 November 2019 to the IHO S-44 standard (survey `HY191121-22-23-24`), a 20 m grid of mean depths below LAT, lined up with the DCBD coastal bathymetry and answering as a plain observed depth (`NONE`). Published under CC BY 4.0: every answer credits the Hydrographic Service and SeaDataNet.

- **Coverage.** The leeward coast from Karpata to the Invisibles, around the south tip, and up the east coast past Lac Bay to about 12.16° N, with the east side of Klein Bonaire, from about 1 m to 230 m deep. From Jeff Davis Memorial south it holds the whole wall and the slope below it, to 55 to 137 m; from Karpata to Weber's Joy a strip from the shore to about 30 m; along the east coast the wall from about 15 m to 100 to 150 m. The north coast, the east coast north of 12.16° N, and the west and north of Klein Bonaire lie outside it.
- **Where it answers.** Where the DCBD grid holds no data, past its edge on the wall and in the shallow strip along the shore it never reached. A DCBD pixel at or above the surface stays DCBD's.
- **The data.** `tools/db/depth/nlhs/` holds SeaDataNet's order zip as it downloads (`order_<number>_unrestricted.zip`), with `000574_XYZ_HY191121-22-23-24_V<version>.txt` inside it: 45,766 cells, each a latitude, a longitude and the mean depth of the soundings in it. The newest version found there is the one read, out of the zip or as the text file itself. `offset.json` holds the measured offset to DCBD and the two files it was measured on.
- **Ordered by hand.** SeaDataNet hands the survey out only behind a free login and an order, so a missing file stops the run with the steps to order it from <https://cdi-bathymetry.seadatanet.org/search> and the folder to save it in. The order is unrestricted and is ready within minutes.
- **Shared code.** The reading, the offset and the depth at a point are `tools/lib/nlhs.py`; the projection is `tools/lib/dcbd.py`'s.
- **The value between cell centres.** Every cell centre sits on a multiple of 20 m in WGS 84 / UTM zone 19N, the projection DCBD is on. A point is read between the four cell centres around it, bilinearly, so the depth runs smoothly down a wall; at the survey's edge, where one of the four is missing, it takes the cell the point falls in.
- **Gaps between the swaths.** The survey holds strips with no cells, 20 to 120 m wide, between its multibeam swaths, most of them on the deep slope of the channel between the islands. A point with no cell reads as a gap where the finer surveys' data, the survey's own cells or DCBD's pixels under water, lies within 100 m in at least three of the four quadrants around it; it takes the depth of the survey's cells within 100 m by the inverse square of the distance, and the answer says so, with the nearest cell's distance. The strip between the shore and the survey has only land on its landward side, so it reads as no gap and takes no depth.
- **Lined up with DCBD.** On bottom flat in both surveys this one reads 2.2 m shallower than DCBD, all along the coast, where LAT accounts for about 0.2 m. The Navy's own single-beam soundings of 1970 to 1972 agree with DCBD there within 0.13 m, so every depth here is moved onto DCBD's reference by the median difference over the cells flat in both: a Navy cell and its 8 neighbours within 3 m of each other, no deeper than 22 m, and DCBD's pixels over the 40 m square around it within 3 m of each other. Every answer prints the offset, the cells it was measured on and their spread. It is measured again whenever either survey's file changes.
- **The spread around a point.** Each depth also gives the shallowest and deepest cell within 20 m.
- **`--verify`.** SeaDataNet serves the survey only behind a login, so there is no live service to compare with: the check prints the version held and the record to compare it with by hand, <https://cdi-bathymetry.seadatanet.org/report/edmo/574/hy191121-22-23-24>, and reads NOT CHECKED.
- **`selftest`.** Every sounding on a cell centre of the 20 m grid within 5 cm, and the depth read back at each centre equal to its own.

### Royal Netherlands Navy soundings of Bonaire, 1970 to 1972

The Royal Netherlands Navy Hydrographic Service's single-beam surveys of the waters around Bonaire and Klein Bonaire from 1970 to 1972, digitized from their fair sheets (surveys `HY1024lm1(digi)`, `HY1024lm2(digi)`, `HY1024lm6(digi)`, `HY1024lm7(digi)` and `HY1026lm11(digi)`), soundings below LAT answering as a plain observed depth (`NONE`), a rough depth beyond diving limits. Published under CC BY 4.0: every answer credits the Hydrographic Service and SeaDataNet.

- **Coverage.** The waters around both islands, from about 1 m to 2,600 m deep: 8,518 soundings 20 m apart along tracks a few hundred metres apart. A point off the reef lies a median of 360 m from the nearest sounding, and 1.2 km at p90.
- **Where it answers.** Where neither DCBD nor the 2019 survey holds data, at sea, and seaward of them: a point within 100 m of their nearest data answers only when it lies farther from OpenStreetMap's coastline than that data does, or 100 m or more from the coastline with the finer surveys' water crossed by the straight line to it (a notch in their deep edge, wherever it sits along that edge), and any other point only 100 m or more from the coastline. A patch the finer surveys left unsurveyed answers too, where their data lies within 300 m in at least three of the four quadrants around the point. The strip between the shore and DCBD's first pixel, which the soundings never resolved, has only land on its landward side and takes no depth from them: Bonaire's shore strip answers there. Nor does water behind the finer surveys, a lagoon or mangrove wetland the open sea reaches only through their water or over land, such as the north and east of Lac Bay: on a 25 m grid over DCBD's extent, the open sea is flooded from the extent's edge through water holding neither the finer surveys' water nor land, and a patch it never reaches that touches land lies behind them, kept in `behind.bin` beside the surveys and worked out again when a survey, the DCBD grid or the coastline changes. A patch it never reaches away from land, a hole in the finer surveys out on the reef, keeps its depth.
- **The data.** `tools/db/depth/nlhs/` holds SeaDataNet's order zip with the five surveys inside it, `000574_XYZ_<survey>_V<version>.txt`, beside the 2019 survey's, ordered by hand the same way: a missing survey stops the run with the steps and the folder.
- **Shared code.** `tools/lib/nlhs_1970.py`.
- **The value at a point.** Interpolated by inverse distance squared from the 8 nearest soundings within 1.5 km, where the point lies beside a track or among the soundings: within 500 m of a sounding, about the spacing of the tracks, or with no empty angle of 180° or more around it between the soundings within 1.5 km and the finer surveys' water within 500 m. Past the last track, farther than 500 m from every sounding with the soundings all to one side, it gives no depth. Inside the soundings' coverage it answers wherever the tracks lie: a patch of sea the covered water rings on every side, or a strip of it narrower than 200 m, takes the 8 nearest soundings however far they lie, so where tracks fan out or run far apart the map draws no hole between them. The coverage is worked out once on a 50 m grid, a cell covered where the soundings answer all through it, beside a track, by the empty angle, or in the finer surveys' water, and the open sea flooded from the coast and the extent's edge; it is kept in `enclosed.bin` beside the surveys, about 20 seconds, and worked out again when a survey, the DCBD grid or the coastline changes. Every answer names the distance to the nearest sounding.
- **Read as published.** On flat bottom the soundings agree with DCBD within 0.13 m, so they take no offset.
- **Rough.** Their positions date from 1970 to 1972, and the tracks lie far apart over a steep bottom: a sounding left out and answered by its neighbours is 38% off at the median, to 200 m. Read a depth from them as how deep the water is out there, never as a dive figure.
- **`--verify`.** As for the 2019 survey: it prints the versions held and reads NOT CHECKED.
- **`selftest`.** Every sounding read back at its own position, with the leave-one-out spread above reported beside it.

### The Bonaire seams

The transitions between Bonaire's surveys, finest first: from DCBD to the 2019 survey, and from either of them to the 1970s soundings. Each answers over a band past the finer surveys' edge: the coarser survey's depth there, plus the two's difference at the finer surveys' nearest edge point, applied in full at that point and fading to none at the band's end. The depth runs on from the finer survey's last value to the coarser one's with no step, and a depth line drawn from the tool crosses the join cleanly.

- **DCBD to the 2019 survey.** A band of 40 m, two of the survey's cells. DCBD's edge on the wall sits at the lip of the drop-off, at about 10 m, where the 2019 survey's 20 m cells already average the wall below; the Navy's 1970s soundings read 24 to 60 m within 5 to 25 m past that edge, so the steep drop the seam draws there is the wall itself.
- **To the 1970s soundings.** A band of 100 m, past the edge of DCBD or the 2019 survey, whichever is nearer, its depth at the edge point the one the finer surveys answer there.
- **The answer.** Names the distance past the edge, the finer surveys' depth at the edge point, the coarser survey's depth at the point and under the edge point, and the share of the difference added.
- **`selftest`.** For each seam, the step left a short way past the edge point (2 m for the first, 5 m for the second) against the edge point's own depth, and the step the coarser survey alone would leave there: passing within the drop of a steep wall over that distance at the median (2.5 m and 6 m), and below the coarser survey alone at the median and p90.

### Bonaire's shore strip

The water between OpenStreetMap's coastline and the finer surveys' first pixel, which no survey reached: DCBD's grid starts a few metres off the waterline, and the strip between runs from a few metres to tens of metres wide. Its depth runs from nothing at the coastline, the waterline by definition, to the depth DCBD or the 2019 survey reads at their nearest edge point, in proportion to the distance across, a plain observed depth (`NONE`).

- **Where it answers.** At sea, within 60 m across from the coastline on one side to the finer surveys' edge on the other, where neither DCBD (its pixels, or a gap between its water) nor the 2019 survey answers. It answers before the 1970s soundings, whose tracks never reached the shore.
- **Lagoons and wetlands.** Water behind the finer surveys in a patch three 25 m cells wide or more, such as Lac Bay's mangrove wetland, takes no depth from it, read from the same grid as the 1970s soundings' (`behind.bin`); the narrower pockets of the strip that DCBD's edge closes off from the open sea answer as the rest of it.
- **The answer.** Names the distance to the coastline, the distance to the finer surveys' edge and their depth there.
- **The map.** It is drawn at DCBD's zoom, beside DCBD's own pixels, so a strip a few metres wide shows.
- **A shape, not a survey.** It draws the slope from the waterline to the reef's first measured depth as a straight line; a shore that drops at the waterline reads shallower here than it is.

## Before relying on it

- **Data goes stale silently.** A source replaces its data as new surveys arrive, and the copy on this machine keeps the old. `--verify` compares it with the source's live service at the point; run it when a site's depth matters more than usual.
- **A `--verify` failure is a stop.** Report it and wait. For NCEI, delete the tile and the tile list; for the GSC DEM, delete `tools/db/depth/gsc/`; for DCBD, delete `tools/db/depth/dcbd/`; for the Navy's surveys, delete the order zip and order them again; run it again, and report it if it still fails.
- **A bathymetry is a model of the bottom.** It blends surveys of different ages and densities, and a narrow wall or a pinnacle smaller than a few pixels is smoothed. Good to a metre or two at most dive sites.

## Adding a source

A source is a class in `tools/lib/depth.py`'s `SOURCES`, with its name and coverage, whether it covers a point, the depth there below the water's datum with the reading behind it and the datum's name from `tools/lib/datums.py` as `key`, a download of an area ahead of time, a check against its live service, and its own self-test, plus a section above. Its place in the list sets which source answers where two overlap: the more detailed one goes first.

## `tools/depth.py`

Standard library only.

```sh
python3 tools/depth.py at <lat> <lon>                          # depth below the water's datum, and the source
python3 tools/depth.py at <lat> <lon> --datum CHS              # the depth on one datum: a Canadian site inside the NCEI border tiles
python3 tools/depth.py at <lat> <lon> --verify                 # also compare the source's data with its live service
python3 tools/depth.py at --file <path/to/slug.json>           # the same at a site JSON's coordinate, on its seabed_datum, checked against its seabed_m
python3 tools/depth.py transect <lat1> <lon1> <lat2> <lon2> --step 10   # the depth every 10 m from entry to dive area
python3 tools/depth.py fetch <S> <W> <N> <E> [<S> <W> <N> <E> ...]   # download every source's data touching the boxes, ahead of time
python3 tools/depth.py selftest                                # each source's own checks
```

Each answer prints the depth below the water's datum with the datum's name, the source's own figure and the conversion between them, and the source with its tile or grid.

`transect` answers each point along the line as `at` does, every `--step` metres (default 10), and takes `--datum` the same way: walking a coordinate across the terrace to the drop-off.

`fetch` caches data ahead of time, for working with no connection: every source's data touching the boxes of south, west, north and east edges, six downloads at a time (`--workers`). It skips what is already here, so a stopped run picks up where it left off. The US side of the Salish Sea is 45 NCEI tiles and 8.4 GB; any box touching British Columbia also brings the GSC DEM, 7.3 GB, and one touching Bonaire the DCBD grid, 34 MB:

```sh
python3 tools/depth.py fetch 47.0 -123.5 49.0 -122.0 48.0 -124.75 48.5 -123.5
```

`depth.user_agent_email` in `tool-config.json` sets the contact address sent in the User-Agent of a download.
