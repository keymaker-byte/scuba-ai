# Tide (tide height at a station, and therefore depth)

<https://api.tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/mdapi/prod/>, <https://api-iwls.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/swagger-ui/index.html>

The predicted tide across the entry window: how deep the site is that day. This is what turns a depth reading into a number that means something on any other day, and the tool behind the workspace's depth convention: metres above the datum of the station's source, MLLW at a NOAA station and CHS Chart Datum at a CHS station. The tool reads every tide source it knows through one set of commands, and names the source and its datum in every answer, so a site file and a plan read one tool wherever the site is, on either side of the border. Every source keeps its data on this machine: a station is downloaded the first time it is asked for, and every prediction after that runs with no request at all.

- **Use it for.** The tide height across the entry window, and the day's high and low water, at a station in the same body of water as the site.
- **A site's median daily tidal range, its largest daily range, and its high/low span across the year.** `range` works through a full year of highs and lows in about a second and reports the median and max daily range and the year's span. The largest and the smallest daily ranges both cluster near the solstices, tracking diurnal inequality, which peaks near the solstices and is smallest near the equinoxes; the exact dates shift from year to year with the moon's cycle.
- **Pick a station in the same body of water.** Water level varies smoothly, so the nearest station is usually right, but "near" has to mean hydraulically near, not near on a map. Verify the station's name and position before trusting the label; a mislabelled tide station once cost about 1 m at high water.
- **Naming a station.** A station is named by its source's own ID, `9447130`. Where two sources share an ID the tool stops and asks for it as `<source>:<id>`, `noaa:9447130`, which every command also accepts. `stations --near` lists every source's stations around a point, nearest first, each with its source and datum.
- **Datum.** Each source's heights are above its own datum, named in every header by its name from `tools/lib/datums.py`: `MLLW` at a NOAA station, `CHS` at a CHS station. Two datums are different surfaces, about a metre apart in the Salish Sea, so a depth below one plus a tide above the other is wrong by that much. The Datums section below is how the tool keeps them apart.
- **Times.** `--tz` is required on every command that prints a time: the site's own IANA zone, the `tz` in its JSON. Every time prints in that zone, named in the header, daylight time included.
- **Data downloads on first use.** Each source keeps its station list and one small file per station under `tools/db/tide/<source>/`, each downloaded when missing. Deleting a station's file, or the source's folder, fetches the source's current values on the next run, which is how a re-analysed station is picked up. Every header prints the date its data was fetched.
- **A failed download stops the run.** A refusal, an empty or unreadable answer, or an unreachable service ends the run with what failed and the URL asked for, and leaves no partial station file behind. A cached station needs no network.
- **Shared code.** The datum names are `tools/lib/datums.py`, the sources `tools/lib/tide.py`, the harmonic prediction every source and the map's tide layer sum with is `tools/lib/tide_harmonics.py`, and the astronomy `tools/lib/tidal_astronomy.py`: Schureman's Manual of Harmonic Analysis and Prediction of Tides with NOAA's own conventions, the node factors held at mid-year and changed at the first hour of the UTC year.

## Datums

A datum is named the same way in the tide tool, the depth tool and a site JSON's `seabed_datum`, from `tools/lib/datums.py`, and the tide tool checks a station's datum against the site's before it answers. Depth below the surface is the datum depth plus the tide height, on every datum.

- **`MLLW`.** Mean Lower Low Water, NOAA's tidal datum in US water. The tide tool reads it at NOAA stations alone.
- **`CHS`.** CHS Chart Datum, the datum of Canadian charts and CHS's tide predictions: Lower Low Water, Large Tide on the Pacific coast, which sits 0.8 to 1.3 m below MLLW in the Salish Sea. The tide tool reads it at CHS stations alone.
- **`LAKE`.** A lake's own surface. A lake has no tide, so the tide height above it is 0 by definition and no station is named; the header says the lake's level moves with the seasons.
- **`NONE`.** No datum: a plain observed depth, in water whose tide is too small to normalize against, such as Bonaire's. The tide height is 0 by definition and no station is named.
- **The check.** Every station command takes `--file`, a site JSON, whose `seabed_datum` names the datum its depths are on, or `--datum K` with the same names. A station on another datum stops the run, naming the stations to choose from; on `LAKE` or `NONE` a named station stops the run, and with none named every height is 0. `project` always takes one of the two, since its depth means nothing without its datum. `stations --datum K` lists only the stations on that datum.

## Sources

### NOAA CO-OPS

NOAA's tide prediction stations, computed on this machine from the harmonic constants and datums NOAA publishes for each station, the same constants NOAA's own predictions come from. Heights above MLLW.

- **Stations it serves.** Every tide prediction station NOAA lists, about 3,500 of them on every US coast and a scattering abroad: the reference stations with their own constants, and the subordinate stations with their corrections.
- **The data.** `tools/db/tide/noaa/` holds NOAA's station list and one file per station: a reference station's constants and its MSL, MLLW and NAVD 88 datums (NAVD 88 where NOAA ties the station to it), or a subordinate station's corrections. The source is `tools/lib/noaa_tides.py`.
- **How it predicts.** The height at a reference station is its mean sea level above MLLW plus the sum of its constituents, 37 at most stations and up to 120 where NOAA publishes the shallow-water compounds, each with the node factor and phase of its year.
- **How close it is to NOAA.** `verify --all` compared every station with NOAA's live API over 30 days each, in November 2026. On all 1,255 reference stations that carry constants and datums, the height every six minutes agrees within 0.002 m and every high and low within 3 minutes and 0.02 m. On all 2,241 subordinate stations with a usable reference, every high and low agrees within 2 minutes and 0.02 m.
- **Subordinate stations.** Many stations in enclosed or nearshore waters are subordinate: NOAA defines each by its highs and lows alone, the reference station's moved by two time corrections and two height corrections (a ratio or a fixed offset). The tool applies those, and the reference station is downloaded with it. Between a high and a low NOAA publishes nothing; the tool follows the reference station's own curve between the same two extremes, stretched in time and height to fit, so `at`, `normalize` and `project` work at any station. That in-between height is an estimate, closest where the corrections are small.
- **Stations with nothing to predict from.** NOAA lists six stations the tool stops on, with a message: four reference stations with no constants (8660754, 8762483, 9450623, 9751388), one with no MSL or MLLW datum (Eugene Island, 8764311), and one subordinate station that names itself as its reference (Malakal Harbor, 1841275).
- **The turn of the year.** The change of node factors at 00:00 UTC on January 1 puts a small step in the curve, a few centimetres at most, in NOAA's predictions and here alike. A high or low within the hour either side of it can sit minutes and a few centimetres from NOAA's listing. January 1 UTC begins on the afternoon or evening of December 31 local time.
- **`verify`.** Reads NOAA's predictions for the same station and dates and reports the worst difference in the six-minute height, at a reference station, and in the time and height of every high and low, passing within 0.02 m and 3 minutes. A high or low on a flat top, where minutes of time cost a hair of height, is judged on the height, and one at the turn of the UTC year is left out and counted. `verify --all` over every NOAA station makes about 4,800 requests and takes about three hours.

### CHS

The Canadian Hydrographic Service's water level prediction stations, read from CHS's IWLS API and computed on this machine from constants fitted by Foreman's method, the method CHS predicts with, to CHS's own predicted heights, which they reproduce to the millimetre. Heights above CHS Chart Datum (`CHS`).

- **Stations it serves.** Every station CHS lists with water level predictions, about 1,150 on every Canadian coast, 139 of them in the Canadian Salish Sea from Sooke to Chatham Point, up Howe Sound and the Fraser to New Westminster. CHS publishes a full predicted series at its secondary stations as well as at its reference ports, so every station is fitted the same way; the header names which it is. A station CHS lists as non-tidal stops the run.
- **The data.** `tools/db/tide/chs/` holds CHS's station list with its height types, one file per station (position, CHS's characteristic heights such as MWL, MLLW, LLWLT, HAT and LAT above Chart Datum, its reference port, and the span CHS holds), and one fit per station, `<id>_fit.json`: its constants, the years they were fitted to, the span they were tested on, and both checks. CHS's predictions are read only while a fit is made, held in memory and dropped after it. The source is `tools/lib/chs_tides.py`.
- **The fit.** CHS keeps its harmonic constants to itself, so the tool fits its own, by least squares, to CHS's heights every 15 minutes over the newest two complete years CHS holds: Foreman's 68 standard constituents, the ones two years resolve, joined by every shallow water compound of Foreman's table that the station's own series calls for, those standing 0.5 mm or more in what the 68 leave unexplained, in up to three rounds. A reference port takes the 68 alone; a secondary station in shallow or enclosed water takes a few more, 71 to 79 in the Gulf Islands and 109 at Port Moody. Foreman's node factors come from his satellite table and the station's latitude, moved day by day as CHS moves them. Foreman's table, `tide3.dat`, comes inside R. Pawlowicz's t_tide distribution and is downloaded into `tools/db/tide/foreman/` the first time a fit is made. The method is `tools/lib/foreman.py`.
- **The test.** The fit is then tested on predictions it never saw: the months CHS holds after the two years, or the complete year before them where those months are fewer than 90 days. A station passes when both the years it was fitted to and the span it was tested on keep within 2 mm rms and 0.02 m at worst on the height every hour, and every high and low within 3 minutes and 0.02 m. A station is fitted once, the first time it is asked for, in about three minutes: its downloads at the 30 requests a minute CHS allows, shared with the current tool's CHS client, and the fit itself.
- **How close it is to CHS.** It is CHS's own prediction, at reference ports and secondary stations alike. Across 20 stations from Sooke and Victoria to Campbell River, Squamish, Port Moody and the Fraser at New Westminster and Steveston, constants fitted to 2027 and 2028 reproduce those years and CHS's January to November 2029, the span they were tested on, within 0.3 mm rms and 0.7 mm at worst, and every high and low within 3 minutes and 1 mm. A high or low on a flat stand, where minutes of time cost a hair of height, is judged on the height: one whose fitted height at CHS's moment sits within 3 mm of the fit's own extreme. `verify` against CHS's live service over 30 days at Narvaez Bay, Victoria Harbour and Campbell River in November 2026 found every 15-minute height within 1 mm and every high and low within 1.3 minutes and 1 mm.
- **A station that falls short stops the run.** Like NOAA's stations with nothing to predict from, a station whose fit misses the limits answers nothing, with its figures and the limits it missed; choose another station with `stations --near`. The usual cause is a revision: CHS revises a station's predictions from time to time, from a given date on, and the series it holds carries the step, which no set of constants follows. Fulford Harbour's series steps by 2 cm on 15 September 2026 and sits 22 mm low from 14 to 30 September 2029; its fit reproduces 2027 and 2028 within 0.3 mm and misses its test by 5 mm rms and 22 mm at worst, so it is refused.
- **The span CHS holds.** CHS holds predictions from January 2018 to the end of November 2029, extended a year at a time. A date outside it answers from the same constants, carried forward or back, and the header names the span.
- **CHS's highs and lows.** CHS's published highs and lows mostly agree with its own published series within 1 mm. Some differ: for 2019 by about 3 cm either way, and at Campbell River in January 2025 by 2.5 to 5 cm on the small-range lows. A fit follows CHS's series, and its check leaves out and counts a high or low CHS lists more than 0.01 m from its own series at that moment. Each listed event is told high or low by CHS's own heights an hour either side of it, so a gap in CHS's list of highs and lows leaves every event it does list read correctly: CHS's 2029 list lacks most events from 13 September to 1 October.
- **The map.** The map's tide layer carries each station's fitted constants in Foreman's form, with the part of Foreman's table they need, and moves the node factors day by day in the browser as `tools/lib/foreman.py` does, reproducing the tool's heights at sample moments before it draws. A station that answers nothing is left out of the map and named.
- **A gap in CHS's series.** A fit reads CHS's heights unbroken over the span it is made from: a station whose series has a hole inside the span CHS says it holds stops the run with the moment of the hole, and nothing is cached. The map leaves it out and names it, and takes it back on the first export after a fit can be made. Bamberton's (07279) series lacks its heights at 00:00 UTC on 8 October 2029.
- **`verify`.** Reads CHS's live predictions for the station and dates, its heights every 15 minutes and its highs and lows, and passes when every height is within 0.02 m of the fit's and every high and low within 3 minutes and 0.02 m, a flat stand judged on its height and a high or low off CHS's own series left out and counted. A fit is made again by deleting `<id>_fit.json`; do that and run `verify` in January, when CHS adds a year, so the fit reads CHS's newest predictions.

## Before relying on it

- **It is the source's prediction, reproduced.** It is the astronomical tide at the station; wind, pressure and river flow move the real water level around it.
- **Data goes stale silently.** A source replaces a station's constants, datums or published predictions from time to time, and a cached file keeps the old ones. The header prints the fetch date. Run `verify` on a site's tide station whenever its file is older than a year; on a failure, delete the station's files (and its reference station's, for a NOAA subordinate; the station's file and its `<id>_fit.json`, for CHS), run `verify` again, and report it if it still fails.
- **A `verify` failure is a stop.** It is the same kind of failure as an unreachable service: report it and wait, with no second station substituted.
- **Highs and lows are proven everywhere; a subordinate station's in-between height has nothing to be verified against.** A depth normalized at a subordinate station mid-tide carries that estimate; a reference station in the same water gives a proven height at any moment.
- **The shared libraries are shared.** A change to `tools/lib/tide_harmonics.py`, or to `tools/lib/tidal_astronomy.py`'s `longitudes`, `nodal` or constituent table, is a change to every source, the current tool and the map, to be followed by `verify` on a few stations of each source.

## Adding a source

A source is a class in `tools/lib/tide.py`'s `SOURCES`, with its key, name, datum (a name from `tools/lib/datums.py`, added there first when it is a new surface) and cache folder under `tools/db/tide/`, its station list, whether an ID is one of its stations, a station downloaded when missing, the height at a moment and the highs and lows between two moments, its harmonic constants where a station has its own, and a check against its live service; the interface is written out at the top of that file. A source whose stations carry harmonic constants predicts with `tools/lib/tide_harmonics.py`, so its constituents are summed as NOAA's are. It also takes a section above. The map's tide layer takes every source's stations that carry harmonic constants, grouped by datum, each family summed in `map/tides.js` in its own form (NOAA's held per year, Foreman's moved day by day), and leaves the rest out with the reason; a source in a new form needs its family there.

## `tools/tide.py`

Standard library only.

```sh
python3 tools/tide.py stations --near <lat> <lon> --datum <K>                                       # find a station on the site's datum, then verify its name
python3 tools/tide.py predict STATION_ID --date 2026-07-12 --tz <IANA_ZONE> --file <slug.json>      # high/low water and the day's range
python3 tools/tide.py at STATION_ID --time "2026-07-12 09:18" --tz <IANA_ZONE> --file <slug.json>   # tide height at a moment
python3 tools/tide.py range STATION_ID --year 2026 --tz <IANA_ZONE> --file <slug.json>              # site file: the "Typical range" bullet
python3 tools/tide.py project STATION_ID --date 2026-07-12 --tz <IANA_ZONE> --file <slug.json>      # planning: the site's seabed_m to depth below surface, all day
python3 tools/tide.py project STATION_ID --datum-depth 15.4 --datum <K> --date 2026-07-12 --tz <IANA_ZONE>   # planning: any datum depth
python3 tools/tide.py normalize STATION_ID --time "2026-07-12 09:21" --depth 14.5 --tz <IANA_ZONE> --file <slug.json>  # logging: observed depth to depth below the datum
python3 tools/tide.py project --date 2026-07-12 --tz <IANA_ZONE> --file <lake-or-bonaire-site.json>  # LAKE or NONE: no station, height 0
python3 tools/tide.py fetch --near <lat> <lon> --km 50                                              # cache every station within a radius
python3 tools/tide.py fetch STATION_ID [STATION_ID ...]                                             # cache named stations
python3 tools/tide.py fetch --all [--source noaa]                                                   # cache every station a source lists
python3 tools/tide.py verify STATION_ID --days 30                                                   # compare one station with its source's live predictions
python3 tools/tide.py verify --all [--source noaa]                                                  # compare every cached station
```

`project` and `normalize` are inverses. Depth below the datum is the observed depth minus the tide height; depth below the surface is the datum depth plus the tide height. Tide heights are signed, so a dive on a minus tide reads shallower than the datum depth. The arithmetic is the same on every datum: on `LAKE` and `NONE` the height is 0, so the two depths are equal. `project --file` takes the site JSON's `seabed_m` unless `--datum-depth` gives another depth on the same datum.

`stations` and `fetch --near` read every source; `--source` narrows either to one, and `stations --datum` to the sources on that datum. `fetch` caches stations ahead of time, for working with no connection, with each subordinate station's reference. It skips what is already cached, so a stopped run picks up where it left off. `fetch --all --source noaa` caches all 3,502 NOAA stations in about an hour and 14 MB; `--near` covers a region in a minute or two. For a CHS station `fetch` keeps the station's file and its fit, which is all it needs to answer with no connection, at about three minutes a station, so the Canadian Salish Sea, 141 stations within 150 km of the Strait of Georgia's middle, takes about 7 hours (`fetch --near 49.3 -124.0 --km 150 --source chs`). A station whose fit falls short is kept too, with its fit, and `fetch` lists it with the reason it answers nothing. Without `fetch`, each piece is downloaded the first time it is asked for. A new year is fetched by running `fetch` again in January, after deleting the stations' `_fit.json` files so their fits are made from CHS's newest two years.

`verify --all` runs each source's comparison on every cached station of it and writes one JSON line per station to the source's `_verify.jsonl`, `tools/db/tide/noaa/_verify.jsonl` for NOAA: the differences and `ok`. A station already in the file is skipped, so a stopped run resumes; deleting the file starts it over. Run it after deleting a cache to refresh every station.
